Decision log — GBGPeriodicFields

Chronological and append-only. Each entry records a decision, what it replaced, and the evidence that settled it; a later entry supersedes an earlier one's "remaining" or "deferred" note rather than contradicting it. Every entry reads on its own, without opening the source or another document.

This log lived as §13 of the architecture until 2026-09-19, where it was three quarters of that file and pushed the design it documents into the last quarter. It is cited throughout the package as "decision N", and those citations are unchanged.

Chronological and append-only — later entries supersede earlier "remaining"/"deferred" notes (e.g. the CPU threading and the interpolation layer) rather than contradicting them.

  4. Docstring policy: one docstring per operation on the bare binding, curated signatures + validity prose; generators never emit docstrings.

  5. Operators defined in function op(...) ... end form (parse-proven house style); short form only for unary +.

  7. Refactor complete: FieldAlgebra loads and is parity-verified (M2 + M3, 198 methods); DifferentialOperators rewired to import FieldSpectralMultipliers and its helpers from Fields, with the umbrella re-export corrected (the old five-argument constructor removed by design); reference test suite in place (test/, Pkg.test() green) including the method-parity baseline.   10. GPU testing is opt-in and dependency-free for the package: the data layer is generic over AbstractArray, FFT plans are built through AbstractFFTs (backend-dispatched on the array type) and allocations inherit their backend via similar, so a GPU backend is only ever a test dependency. The default Pkg.test() stays backend-free, so it is portable across CI and every machine.

  11. Test-suite buildout (beyond the GPU work in item 10): element-type genericity (Float32 alongside Float64), anisotropic-domain operators (the $2\pi/L$ scaling), in-place and alias-guard coverage, a Nyquist end-to-end test, and the operator adjointness / structural identities on randomized band-limited fields ($\nabla\cdot = -\nabla^\top$, self-adjoint curl, negative-semidefinite Laplacian — negative-definite on the zero-mean subspace, its kernel being the constant mode — exactly idempotent Helmholtz projection (decision 42) — the discrete energy/entropy structure). Coverage detail lives in docs/TESTING.md.

  12. CPU threading implemented (§9), realizing the design formerly deferred in items 6/9. A Fields._assign! primitive threads elementwise/fiberwise assignments on CPU (Base.Threads, contiguous chunks, bitwise-identical to serial), routed in via the field broadcast copyto! and the kernel assignments (codegen _to_broadcasted plus the @_assign! macro for hand-written kernels); FFTW threads are matched to Julia's at load. Hybrid policy: on when nthreads() > 1 and above a $2^{14}$ serial cutoff, with a task-local with_threading ScopedValue override; the GPU path stays on the materialize! fallback. [compat] julia was raised to 1.11 here (for ScopedValue); the floor has since moved again, to 1.12 (decision 31). Reductions remain deferred (separate FP-ordering design).

  13. Interpolation-layer design fixed (§10): grid→point interpolation as the callable f(x) / f.(xs) plus the named interpolate / interpolate!; point→grid anterpolation as anterpolate / anterpolate! — the symmetric named pair, each with allocating and in-place forms, and the α/β-accumulate form on the scatter only (the mul! convention, since they are the linear maps $W$ / $W^\top$); only interpolation also has the pointwise callable f(x) (the adjoint of interpolation; synonyms — spreading, deposition, scatter, projection/PSI-Cell, gridding, two-way-coupling feedback — listed on the binding; "extrapolation" rejected as a misnomer). Adjointness is explained in the module docstring via the function–measure duality (Dirac evaluation vs. pushforward of point masses). Method: the Lalescu local splines (Lalescu, Teaca & Carati 2010) as in TurTLE (Lalescu et al. 2022) — local Hermite-with-finite-difference derivatives, tunable (neighbours n, smoothness m), single (physical) representation, GPU-friendly; van Hinsberg B-spline (2012) and NUFFT exact-spectral are the higher-accuracy / reference alternatives held in reserve. Interpolation and anterpolation share the same partition-of-unity weights, so anterpolation is the conservative transpose (Sundaram & Collins 1996) — required to conserve total water and latent heat in the two-way-coupled parcel DNS. Implemented (item 17).

  14. Point-data types fixed (§10) and the math-backend policy recorded. The unstructured base reuses the base/fiber split: Point{T} = SVector{3,T} (a position; an alias of FiberVector, not a wrapper); PointSet (M positions, SoA (M,3), grid-free); and PointValues as three concrete types mirroring the field zoo — ScalarPointValues / VectorPointValues / TensorPointValues{R} under AbstractPointValues, SoA, representation-free. Naming is domain-neutral by policy: the package is a math backend, so no particle / tracer type and no concept of time (no advection / integration / temporal state) — those are the consumer's. The types live in Interpolation, importing Fields helpers; they are implemented and tested (only the transfer kernels of item 13 remain future work — see item 15 for the algebra).

  15. Point-value algebra implemented (§10): FieldAlgebra's code generators are reused verbatim over an extended point-value section interface, mirroring the full physical field surface (M2 + M3, storage-wise broadcasting) with no duplicated formula; locked by a separate 118-method baseline (the 198-method field surface byte-unchanged), zero ambiguities, tested on CPU (test/pointvalues_algebra.jl) and GPU (ROCm). The shared AbstractFiberSection{T, R} supertype the fiber-bundle model (§2) invites — and the lift of the inlined norm/sym/skew/dev formulas into a builder — is the planned next step, not yet undertaken.

  16. Phase A consolidation done (§2, §10). The foundation module FiberSections holds the base-agnostic supertype AbstractFiberSection{T,R} (the field and point-value types subtype it), the _assign! threading primitive, and the vertical-algebra code generators — the single home for every fiberwise formula (norm/sym/skew/dev included, via _section_scalar_type), reused verbatim by FieldAlgebra and Points. The former Interpolation module is split into Points (unstructured-base section objects + their generated algebra — a noun peer of Fields) and Interpolation (the transfer maps — an operation peer of DifferentialOperators). The field/point asymmetry is math-mandated (no point differential operators, no second representation; the point algebra is a small generated emission folded into Points). Both parity baselines (198 + 118) held byte-for-byte through every step, zero ambiguities. (The transfer kernels themselves followed — item 17.)

  17. Transfer kernels implemented (§10): interpolation (gather $W$, also the callable f(x)) and anterpolation (scatter $W^\top$, with the α/β mul! convention) as the Lalescu local splines. Both hierarchies are provided: the smooth LalescuSpline(n, m) — a two-point Hermite of degree $2m{+}1$ matching the value and the order-$1, \dots, m$ derivatives (centred Fornberg finite differences) at the two bracketing nodes, $C^m$ with $0 \le m \le 2n$ (CatmullRom, the cubic $n=1, m=1$, is the default; $m=0$ is linear) — and the $C^0$ LagrangeSpline(n), the degree-$2n{+}1$ interpolant of all $2n{+}2$ nodes. The smooth weights are pinned to a tolerance against TurTLE's beta_n{n}_m{m} (n ↔ interp_neighbours, m ↔ smoothness; the pin asserts $\le$ 1e-12, test/interpolation_transfer.jl) — an earlier "interpolate the central $2(n{-}m){+}2$ nodes" draft was corrected after reading the TurTLE source, Lalescu's smooth spline being a two-point Hermite, not a central-node hybrid. Reproduction is $\min(2m{+}1, 2n)$ for $n \ge 1$ (smooth) / $2n{+}1$ (Lagrange); the closed-form Catmull–Rom and the Lagrange cardinal basis are independent anchors, with partition of unity (⇒ conservation). Kernels are portable across CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU via KernelAbstractions (new dep) with the atomic scatter via Atomix (new dep); both index the native arrays linearly, so one kernel serves scalar/vector/tensor orders. Adjointness in the counting pairing — $\sum_p (Wf)_p \cdot q_p = \sum_n f_n \cdot (W^\top q)_n$, the unnormalized sums over the $M$ points and the $N$ grid nodes — and conservation hold to roundoff; tested on CPU (test/interpolation_transfer.jl, Float64/Float32, incl. the TurTLE pin) and GPU (ROCm). Physical-space only. The kernels loop with the contiguous (x) axis innermost (stride-1 stencil access) — a measured GPU-first choice (≈1.6× at 256³, ≈3.9× for the 512³ Lalescu on a since-retired card), re-affirmed on CPU in the decision-37 production band: the 512³ Zen 5 A/B found z-inner ~10% ahead only on the narrow Catmull–Rom stencil and 23% behind on the wide Lalescu one, so x-inner stands on both platforms and the reopened tiebreak is closed (PERFORMANCE.md §4b). The measure-consistent adjoint against the two normalized pairings is $(N/M) W^\top$ ($N$ grid points, $M$ sample points; the factor is the ratio of the $1/N$ and $1/M$ normalizations, not of a cell volume) — both base measures are uniform, so it is a scalar rescaling of the counting-transpose anterpolation, not a new operation — and is verified via inner as $M \texttt{inner}(Wf, q) = N \texttt{inner}(f, W^\top q)$ (test/interpolation_transfer.jl, decision 19).

  18. The L² / measure structure on sections — the third operation family (done). Advances the reduction deferral of items 6/11/§9. The package's operations split into vertical (fiberwise, pointwise — dot, norm, cross; return sections), horizontal (differential — gradient, …; return sections), and now base reductions — integrate the base away against a measure it carries; return a fiber value or a scalar. Math grounding (§2): a section lives in $L^2(\mathrm{base}, \mu) \otimes V$ — the fiber $V$ is a tensor factor, not flattened away — and the inner product pairs the two factors — the fiber contraction is the metric pairing on $V$ (cf. Manifolds.jl inner(M,p,X,Y), a fiber-level pairing) and the base integral is the $L^2(\mathrm{base}, \mu)$ factor — the measure is an explicit object (cf. Gridap.jl Measure, MeasureTheory.jl), and the moments are the canonical reductions (cf. Statistics mean/std/var). All dispatch on AbstractFiberSection, so fields and point-values share one set of formulas (FiberSections "write it once").

    The measure is a property of the base. Grid3D → uniform Lebesgue quadrature (cell volume $\prod_i \Delta x_i$); PointSet → counting measure (unit weight per point) — and this is forced, not a choice: a PointSet is grid-free (§10, carries no domain), so the only well-defined measure is counting. Consequences:

    integral(f) is the extensive sum against the measure, mean(f) the intensive average $\texttt{mean}(f) = \tfrac{1}{\mathrm{count}}\sum f$ — per base:

    • Grid3D ($N$ cells): $\texttt{integral}(f) = \int_\Omega f\,\mathrm{d}x = (\lvert\Omega\rvert/N)\sum f$, $\texttt{mean}(f) = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum f$.
    • PointSet ($M$ points): $\texttt{integral}(f) = \sum f$, $\texttt{mean}(f) = \tfrac{1}{M}\sum f$.

    So mean is the arithmetic average over base elements in both cases — $\tfrac{1}{M}\sum$ over points (a plain sample mean), $\tfrac{1}{N}\sum$ over the grid (which, the grid being uniform, equals the domain average $\langle\cdot\rangle$). Names and conventions:

    • integral(f) — the extensive $\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ (the one quantity that scales with the base measure).
    • mean(f), std(f), var(f) — extend Statistics (we own the types ⇒ not piracy); intensive, fiber-preserving (mean(VectorField) → FiberVector, base reduced).
    • inner(f, g) — the normalized L² inner product $\langle f, g\rangle = \texttt{mean}(\texttt{dot}(f, g))$ → scalar (fiber-contracting). Normalized (intensive) by deliberate choice: it matches the mean convention above and the intensive quantities of homogeneous turbulence (kinetic energy $\tfrac{1}{2} \texttt{inner}(u,u) = \tfrac{1}{2}\langle |u|^2\rangle$); for points it is $\tfrac{1}{M}\sum \texttt{dot}$, consistent with mean. Not the textbook extensive $\int f\cdot g\,\mathrm{d}x$ — that is integral(dot(f,g)) if ever needed.
    • $\texttt{l2norm}(f) = \sqrt{\texttt{inner}(f,f)}$, generalized as lpnorm(f, p) ($p=2$ default; $p=\infty$ ⇒ sup of the fiber magnitude — the CFL case). Chosen over norm_L2; does not touch the vertical norm.
    • maximum/minimum/extrema — extend Base for scalar sections; vector/tensor sup of magnitude via lpnorm at $p = \infty$.
    • No LinearAlgebra overloading: dot/norm stay the vertical pointwise ops (return sections, scalar fields); the global inner/l2norm are package generics returning numbers.

    inner/l2norm are representation-invariant (Parseval): the physical reductions came first, and the spectral inner/l2norm are now implemented — for two fields in spectral representation $\texttt{inner}(\hat f, \hat g)$ equals the physical inner via the rfft half-spectrum Parseval weighting (each mode counts twice except those on the $k_x=0$ plane and, for even $N_x$, the Nyquist plane — the planes the rfft maps to themselves under conjugation, whose partners are already stored, so they count once), computed as a dot over the array plus a plane correction per self-conjugate plane — allocation-free in the inner(f, f)/l2norm case, while the mixed inner(f, g) materializes those planes: the folded axis's zero plane always, and its Nyquist plane as well when that axis has even length, so an odd folded axis pays for one and an even one for two (decision 26). The integral and mean reductions are representation-invariant too, by a different route — the zero (DC) Fourier mode: under the unnormalized-forward convention the DC coefficient is $\hat f(0) = \sum_x f(x)$ (real for a real field), so the mean is $\hat f(0)/N$ and the integral $(|\Omega|/N) \hat f(0)$ with no transform, read from the same zero-mode slot the constant-add writes (invariant 4). The lpnorm reduction and maximum/minimum/extrema have no single-coefficient form and remain physical-only — lpnorm at every exponent, $p = 2$ included, that case being served in spectral representation by l2norm rather than by an lpnorm method. (This entry's original wording also grouped std/var with them; decision 49 later found those two physical-only by scoping rather than by validity, and gave them their spectral form.) Physics stays consumer-side: energy $\tfrac{1}{2} \texttt{inner}(u,u)$, enstrophy $\tfrac{1}{2} \texttt{inner}(\omega,\omega)$, dissipation $\nu \texttt{inner}(\omega,\omega)$, rms $\sqrt{\texttt{inner}}$, intensities from std. The FP-ordering concern that deferred reductions (item 11) is real — so they are implemented with a defined summation order and a tolerance-based test (not the bitwise one used for the threaded assignments).

  19. The measure taxonomy — one L²/integration structure, three roles for "measure"; the recorded foundation for the spectral-multiplier family later realized as Convolution (decision 20). The word measure recurs across the package (reductions, interpolation, and convolution) not by coincidence but because all three are facets of the same L²/integration structure on sections (§2). This entry fixes the taxonomy so the three never get conflated and so the multiplier family starts from a recorded design. The math anchor (Grafakos, Classical Fourier Analysis, 3rd ed., Graduate Texts in Mathematics 249, Springer, 2014, Thm 2.5.10): on a uniform periodic box every homogeneous (translation-invariant) linear operator is a Fourier multiplier — a coefficientwise $m(k)$ — and the bounded ones are exactly those with symbol $m(k) \in L^\infty$, where $\lVert T\rVert_{L^2 \to L^2} = \lVert m\rVert_\infty$. So "apply $m(k)$" is not a convenience — it is the complete representation of the translation commutant. The existing operators are all such multipliers: helmholtz_projection (§4), the consumer's 2/3 de-alias mask, and the integrating factor $\exp(-\nu k^2 \Delta t)$ are the bounded ones ($m \in L^\infty$); gradient/curl/laplacian are the unbounded multipliers (symbols $\mathrm{i}a$, $-|k|^2 \notin L^\infty$), diagonalized identically but outside the norm identity above.

    The roles — each as what it is; operation(s); the map it performs; status:

    • base-quadrature: Grid3D cell volume $\prod_i \Delta x_i$ / PointSet counting; ops integral, inner, mean, std, lpnorm, radial_spectrum (the $|k|$-resolved inner); section → scalar / fiber / radial profile; done (decisions 18, 21).
    • kernel — multiplier: translation-invariant convolution, symbol $m(k) \in L^\infty$; convolve / convolve! plus the builders spectral_cutoff / spectral_band / radial_kernel (any real symbol of $|k|^2$); grid → grid (a Fourier multiplier); done (Convolution, decisions 20, 21).
    • kernel — transfer: off-grid partition-of-unity convolution (unit mass; signed or positive); interpolate ($W$) / anterpolate ($W^\top$); grid ↔ point (base-change, not a multiplier); done (decisions 13/17).
    • sample-space (FDF): spatially-filtered fine-grained Dirac density, over the velocity/composition sample space; no package operation; out (physics; invariant 12).

    Interpolation is the base-changing sibling of the multiplier, not a multiplier. The gather weights {W(x_p, x_g)}_g are a discrete grid measure of total mass 1 (partition of unity), so $u(x_p) = \int u\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x_p}$ is integration of the field against a unit-mass kernel measure centered at the point — the same species as a homogeneous filter. Two differences make it its own operation (§10, in Interpolation, not under the multiplier family): (i) its codomain is the unstructured point base, which has no wavenumbers, so it is a base-change (grid↔point), not a Fourier multiplier (it degenerates to one only when points coincide with nodes); (ii) the default CatmullRom/Lagrange kernels have negative side-lobes — a signed unit-mass measure (higher reproduction order, but overshoot), while only the linear m=0 / positive-B-spline end is a genuine probability measure (monotone). Reproduction order = number of matched moments of the kernel measure (0th moment =1 = partition of unity = constants reproduced). This signed/positive split is the same dichotomy the LES research surfaces below — evidence the kernel measure is one concept.

    The transfer adjoint is a row-1 (base-measure) statement, and that is why reductions came first. Anterpolation $W^\top$ is the adjoint of $W$; "adjoint w.r.t. which pairing?" is answered by the base measures. As shipped (decision 17) the adjointness $\sum_p (Wf)_p \cdot q_p = \sum_n f_n \cdot (W^\top q)_n$ (the unnormalized counting pairing) and conservation $\sum_g (W^\top q)_g = \sum_p q_p$ hold in the counting/transpose pairing (the extensive facet of row 1; conservation = partition of unity). The measure-weighted L² adjoint — $W$, $W^\top$ adjoint with both bases carrying their normalized (total-mass-one) measures, $\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_n$ on the grid and $\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_p$ on the points, which is what inner computes — is the bridge from row 1 to the transfer kernel, and is now done: both measures being uniform, it is $(N/M) W^\top$ (a scalar rescaling, no new operation). The $N/M$ is exactly the ratio of those two normalizations; pairing an unnormalized cell-volume grid measure against counting points would instead give $(N/|\Omega|) W^\top$, verified via inner as $M \texttt{inner}(Wf, q) = N \texttt{inner}(f, W^\top q)$ (test/interpolation_transfer.jl). That the reductions had to precede it is recorded here.

    The multiplier family (designed here; realized as Convolution, decision 20) carries three research-grounded guardrails. The deep-research findings on LES filtering do not change the design — they justify it and bound it:

    • The symbol is data, never parameters — this is how the family stays inside invariant 12 (no time, no physics). The applicator multiplies a field-in-spectral by a precomputed $k$-array; $\nu$, $\Delta t$, filter width, and positivity all live in how the consumer builds that array from the FieldSpectralMultipliers wavenumbers (§4). The viscous factor $\exp(-\nu k^2 \Delta t)$ is literally the heat-kernel probability measure, yet the package never sees $\nu$ or $\Delta t$.
    • Do not restrict to positive ("measure") symbols. $L^\infty$-symbol is the right generality; convolution-with-a-finite-measure is a strict subclass whose symbol must be continuous, and the 2/3 mask and sharp spectral cutoff have discontinuous symbols (kernels not in $L^1$) — so they are not such convolutions yet are valid multipliers we rely on. Positivity of the kernel ⟺ positive-definite symbol (Bochner) ⟹ probability measure ⟹ realizable SGS stress (Vreman, Geurts & Kuerten 1994, realizability iff positive filter) ⟹ bona-fide filtered PDF (Colucci et al. 1998, the FDF); all of that is consumer semantics. No PositiveFilter type, no realizability checks. (Interpolation already ships signed kernels by default — consistent.)
    • Homogeneous-only scope. Variable-width filters are not multipliers (commutation error; Ghosal & Moin 1995, Vasilyev, Lund & Moin 1998) — and the uniform torus admits only homogeneous operators anyway, so this is a clarifying boundary, not a gap. Projections are the idempotent multipliers (the sharp cutoff; helmholtz_projection is the matrix-valued one we already ship); smoothings are the non-idempotent ones — same family, no special case.

    Open at the time (untouched by the research; all resolved in decision 20 — the Convolution module, the convolve op, the spectral_cutoff builder): a name distinct from the existing FieldSpectralMultipliers data (e.g. a FourierSymbol operator or a generic apply_multiplier!); placement (extend DifferentialOperators, which owns the fixed diagonal symbols, vs. a new module); and how many neutral builders to ship (bare applicator vs. a spectral_cutoff helper — never a physics-named gaussian_les_filter). v1 is scalar symbols; matrix-valued symbols are deferred (helmholtz_projection covers the only case currently needed). The Germano differential filter $1/(1 + \Delta^2 k^2)$ is architecturally inert — whatever its symbol, it is one more scalar multiplier the consumer supplies.

  20. Convolution — the kernel-multiplier family realized (§2; the new Convolution module). Implements the multiplier designed in decision 19 as periodic (circular) convolution of a field with a kernel, in spectral representation: by the convolution theorem on the torus, f ⋆ g is the coefficientwise spectral product $\hat f \cdot \hat g$. The kernel g is supplied by its transfer function $\hat g$ as a ScalarFieldSpectral (decision 19's open "kernel as a field" call, chosen for elegance); a scalar kernel broadcasts over the fiber, so one method serves scalar/vector/tensor fields, and because a kernel is itself a scalar field in spectral representation, convolve(g₁, g₂) composes two kernels for free (the R = 0 case). (Superseded by decision 60: a kernel is now its own type, so a ScalarFieldSpectral is wrapped in a StoredKernel to declare that role and composition is unambiguous by type rather than by argument position.) convolve / convolve! mirror laplacian / laplacian!: coefficientwise ⇒ alias-safe (no _guard_noalias!; destination may alias the field), @_assign!-routed (threads on CPU), with the five-argument α/β accumulate form (invariant 10). Spectral representation only — there is no physical-space convolve (the differential-operator precedent).

    This completes invariant 1 rather than weakening it. The coefficientwise spectral product is the operation invariant 1 names but bans as * (because * would read as the spectral pointwise product, which it is not). It is offered here under its true name — convolve, never * — and is linear in the field for a fixed kernel; the spectral pointwise product, division, and norms remain absent. The convolve / convolve! / spectral_cutoff family are separate generics, so the FieldAlgebra 198-method and point-value 118-method parity baselines are unchanged.

    One neutral builder ships: spectral_cutoff(multipliers, k) — the isotropic sharp low-pass (idempotent projection), the basis of de-aliasing / spectral truncation; physics (an LES filter, an integrating factor $\exp(-\nu k^2 \Delta t)$) stays a set of coefficients the consumer builds from the wavenumbers, wrapped as a StoredKernel since decision 60 (invariant 12). Naming follows the §2 convention (the verb convolve for a generic transformation; the module noun Convolution, parallel to Interpolation / interpolate). Tested CPU (test/convolution.jl, Float64/Float32 — the translation kernel $\exp(-\mathrm{i} k_x m \Delta x) \Leftrightarrow$ circshift is the independent physical oracle, plus identity/scaling/ cutoff-idempotence/composition/α-β/alias) and GPU (ROCm device-vs-CPU parity in test/gpu/suite.jl); benchmarked (the convolution class). The L²-weighted and matrix-valued (anisotropic) kernels remain future.

  21. Shell-space spectral primitives — the radial coordinate, the band-pass, and the shell-resolved inner (extending Convolution + Reductions). Three neutral primitives surfaced by the examples/HIST_* demos, each the code image of an established object and each slotting into an existing family — no new module, no parity-baseline change (separate generics), physics names stay consumer-side (invariant 12). They close the largest hand-rolled pieces of a real pseudospectral consumer (the energy spectrum, the forcing band, the viscous integrating factor):

    • squared_wavenumber_field(reference, multipliers) (Convolution; REMOVED by decision 60 — radial_kernel(identity, …) is the same symbol as a closure) — the radial coordinate $|k|^2$ of spectral space as a ScalarFieldSpectral (real, stored complex, like a cutoff mask). Decision 19 fixed that "the symbol is data": the consumer builds $\exp(-\nu |k|^2 \Delta t)$, a smooth envelope, or a Germano filter $1/(1 + \Delta^2 |k|^2)$ by broadcasting over its field_data, then applies it with convolve. The package hands over the coordinate; $\nu$, $\Delta t$, the width stay consumer-side. It wraps the existing _squared_wavenumber_broadcast (the Laplacian's $|k|^2$, true value at Nyquist — invariant 5).
    • spectral_band(multipliers, wavenumber_low, wavenumber_high) (Convolution) — the isotropic annular band-pass, the generalization of spectral_cutoff (now its wavenumber_low = 0 special case, refactored to delegate). Idempotent ⇒ a projection; it selects a wavenumber shell — a de-alias band, a forcing band, a filter ring. With it the demos' low-shell forcing becomes pure package ops: band energy = inner(u_band, u_band) with u_band = convolve(u, band), then a rescaling convolve — no scalar indexing, and the inner(f, f) form is allocation-free (its fused-abs2 fast path).
    • radial_spectrum(f, multipliers) (Reductions; introduced as shell_spectrum, renamed in decision 22) — the radial (shell-binned) power spectrum: inner decomposed into isotropic $|k|$-shells. Returns (shells, spectrum) with the exact refinement sum(spectrum) == inner(f, f) (same rfft half-spectrum Parseval weighting, now resolved per mode). This is the genuinely new reduction — spectral-representation only, like the Parseval inner — and the architectural framing is precisely "the energy spectrum is the radially-resolved inner" (decision 19's L²/integration structure, refined by the spectral radial coordinate). The consumer's energy spectrum over the shell wavenumber $\kappa$ ($= |k|$) is $E(\kappa) = \tfrac{1}{2} \texttt{spectrum}$; the neutral noun keeps physics out (invariant 12). Shells run to the corner wavenumber so the refinement is exact; on a de-aliased field the high-$|k|$ shells are empty.

    Device-clean by construction. radial_kernel / spectral_band are broadcast kernel builders (the spectral_cutoff pattern); radial_spectrum bins via a single scatter-add pass — one portable atomic-histogram kernel over the modes (no scalar indexing), an occasional diagnostic. (Originally a sequence of masked reductions, one per shell, with the histogram scatter deferred as "unneeded"; decision 25 lifted the deferral — the per-shell masks allocated $O(N)$ full-grid temporaries per call ($N$ the points per axis), which on the GPU the garbage collector cannot reclaim promptly, so a single $256^3$ diagnostic wedged a 12 GB card.) Tested CPU (test/convolution.jl band/coordinate vs the $|k|^2$ oracle + cutoff equivalence; test/reductions.jl sum(radial_spectrum) == inner, the single-mode shell placement) and GPU (ROCm device-vs-CPU parity in test/gpu/suite.jl); benchmarked (the reductions / convolution classes).   22. Radial statistics — the spectrum↔correlation duality (Wiener–Khinchin), and the FFT route to point pair-statistics (RDF). [Phase 1 (radial_correlation), Phase 2 (assignment_window + the pair-correlation recipe), and the two-field co-spectra (radial_spectrum(f, g) / radial_correlation(f, g)) all DONE.]

    The object. A field's two-point correlation function and its power spectrum are one object in two representations, linked by the Wiener–Khinchin theorem: on the periodic grid the circular autocorrelation $C(r) = \langle f(x) \cdot f(x+r)\rangle$ equals $\mathcal F^{-1}(|\hat f|^2)/N$ exactly — the inverse transform of the power spectrum, normalized by the grid-point count $N$ (the cross-correlation theorem). Radially averaging gives the isotropic radial statistics, a Fourier-dual pair — average $|\hat f|^2$ over $|k|$-shells (the spectrum), or $\mathcal F^{-1}(|\hat f|^2)$ over $|r|$-shells (the correlation). This is invariant 2 — physical and spectral are representations of one object — raised to second-order statistics: the spectrum and the correlation function are not two diagnostics but one, viewed in the two representations. The radial_spectrum reduction (decision 21, introduced as shell_spectrum) is the spectral half; this entry designs the physical half and the point application, and fixes the terminology so the family is coherent.

    Terminology (decided; the rename is done). shell_spectrum is renamed radial_spectrum, and the physical dual radial_correlation is added (Phase 1, done). "Radial" is the isotropic-average qualifier common to both spaces — and to the radial distribution function of point statistics; "shell" named the binning implementation and does not pair with the physical-space correlation. The two are the radial reductions: a Fourier-dual pair in Reductions, each mapping a field to a 1-D radial profile, named by the quantity's noun per §2 (spectrum / correlation) under the radial qualifier. The radial_spectrum math is unchanged (sum == inner). Physics names stay consumer-side: the energy spectrum $E(\kappa) = \tfrac{1}{2} \texttt{radial\_spectrum}$, the integral/Taylor scales from radial_correlation, the RDF g(r) from radial_correlation of a density field. (The rename was safe — the API is not frozen and shell_spectrum had shipped the same day with no external users.)

    The physical dual — radial_correlation (done). $C(r)$ = radially-averaged $\langle f(x) \cdot f(x+r)\rangle$ (full fiber contraction — the trace of the correlation tensor, mirroring inner) $= \mathcal F^{-1}(|\hat f|^2)/N$ binned over minimum-image $|r|$ shells. The zero-separation value is $C(0) = \langle |f|^2\rangle = \texttt{inner}(f, f)$ (the second moment — the variance only when $f$ is mean-free); and for a zero-mean field $C(r) \to 0$ at large $r$. The open design point was resolved as the user chose: radial_correlation(f, plans) takes the field in spectral representation plus its transform plans (symmetric with how the differential operators take multipliers) — Reductions imports to_physical from Fields to do the one inverse rfft, then bins with the same scatter-add machinery as radial_spectrum (the single-pass shell histogram; decision 25), in $r$ instead of $|k|$. Verified against an independent binned-circshift autocorrelation (CPU test/reductions.jl, GPU test/gpu/suite.jl) and C(0) = inner.

    The two-field co-spectra — radial_spectrum(f, g) / radial_correlation(f, g) (done). The mixed second-order statistics of two compatible, equal-order fields, refining the mixed inner(f, g) exactly as the single-field forms refine inner(f, f). The co-spectrum bins the Parseval-weighted cross-power density $\sum_c \mathrm{Re}(\hat f_c \overline{\hat g_c})$ ($c$ indexing the fiber components) over $|k|$-shells ($\sum_\kappa P_{fg} = \texttt{inner}(f, g)$); the cross-correlation $C_{fg}(r) = \langle f(x)\cdot g(x+r)\rangle = \mathcal F^{-1}(\overline{\hat f} \hat g)/N$ binned over $|r|$-shells ($C_{fg}(0) = \texttt{inner}(f, g)$). Two properties fall out of the math and are pinned by tests: each is symmetric in $f, g$ — $\mathrm{Re}$ is conjugation-symmetric for the spectrum, and the $\pm r$ folding of the min-image shells symmetrizes the correlation ($C_{fg}(-r) = C_{gf}(r)$, so the radial average is $\tfrac{1}{2}(C_{fg}+C_{gf})$) — and the single-field forms are exactly the $g = f$ case ($\mathrm{Re}(\hat f \overline{\hat f}) = |\hat f|^2$; asserted bit-identical). Unlike the power spectrum the co-spectrum may be negative in a shell (anticorrelated modes — the canonical use is a momentum/scalar flux co-spectrum, where sign is physical). Implementation reuses the single-field code: the binning helpers (_radial_spectrum_bin, _correlation_field_from + _radial_average) are shared, the two-field methods only swap the per-mode density ($|\hat f|^2 \to \mathrm{Re}(\hat f \overline{\hat g})$) / the per-component spectrum ($|\hat f|^2 \to \overline{\hat f} \hat g$, Hermitian for real $f, g$ ⇒ the c2r reconstruction is exactly real). (The helper set has since been consolidated: the staging fused into the binning kernels themselves and _correlation_field_from folded into the correlation methods — decision 34; the sharing structure is unchanged.) These are new methods on the existing Reductions generics — no export change, the 198/118 parity baselines are unchanged. Verified CPU (test/reductions.jl: analytical oracles — an orthogonal pair $\cos x \perp \sin x$ giving zero, and $\cos x \pm \cos 2x$ giving $\pm\tfrac{1}{2}$ in adjacent shells that cancel to $\texttt{inner} = 0$; the $g = f$ consistency; the $f \leftrightarrow g$ symmetry; and an independent binned-circshift cross-correlation on a random pair) + GPU (ROCm device-vs-CPU parity, test/gpu/suite.jl); benchmarked (reductions class).

    Points → RDF (Phase 2, done). The pair correlation $g(r)$ of $M$ points is computable in $O(N \log N + M)$ (vs $O(M^2)$ direct counting) via the FFT structure-factor estimator — exactly the implemented recipe: anterpolate the points to a grid density (positive/linear assignment), form the overdensity $\delta = \rho/\bar\rho - 1$, FFT, deconvolve $\hat\delta$ by the assignment window $\hat W$, then radial_correlation of the deconvolved field gives $\xi(r) = \langle\delta(x)\delta(x+r)\rangle$ and $g(r) = 1 + \xi(r)$ (the $i=j$ self term is the shot noise, isolated in the $r=0$ shell, so $g$ at $r>0$ is clean). With $\hat\rho(k) = \sum_i e^{-\mathrm{i} k \cdot x_i}$, $S(k) = |\hat\rho|^2/M = 1 + \bar n \hat h(k)$ (Ornstein–Zernike) and $g = 1 + h$. We already owned anterpolate + the transforms + both radial reductions; the one new neutral primitive is the assignment-window deconvolution, the rest is the consumer recipe (invariant 12). Three corrections, all handled:

    • Assignment-window deconvolution (assignment_window, Convolution). Scattering with kernel $W$ gives $\hat\rho_g = \hat W(k) \hat\rho$, so the measured spectrum is suppressed by $|\hat W(k)|^2$ — divide it out (a convolve by $1/\hat W$). Shipped for the linear (CIC) positive assignment: $\hat W(k) = \prod_a \operatorname{sinc}^2(k_a\Delta x_a/2)$, no in-band zeros. (Higher positive B-splines / the signed-kernel closed forms remain future.)
    • Shot noise. The $i=j$ self term is a constant $+1$ floor in $S(k)$ (a $\delta(r)$ at the origin): in the binned $g(r)$ it lands entirely in the $r=0$ shell, so evaluating $g$ at $r>0$ is the subtraction (no extra step). Consumer-side by convention.
    • Aliasing / resolution. The grid folds high-$k$ at Nyquist — trust $k \lesssim \tfrac{2}{3} k_\mathrm{Nyq}$, and $g(r)$ below a few grid cells is unreliable regardless of corrections. FFT-RDF resolves intermediate / large-scale clustering (e.g. inertial-range preferential concentration), not a sharp contact peak (interlacing — Sefusatti et al. 2016 — is a future refinement). This matches the intended consumer use; it is a documented boundary, not a defect.

    Verified (test/rdf.jl): assignment_window against its $\prod \operatorname{sinc}^2$ formula (even/odd grids, both element types), and the full recipe against an independent direct $O(M^2)$ physical-space pair count on a clustered point set — the recipe recovers the clustering peak (within ~10% of direct counting) and the $g \to 1$ baseline (the normalization).

    Decision-19 tie-in. A density estimate wants the positive (probability-measure) end of the kernel taxonomy (the linear $m = 0$ / a B-spline), not the signed default CatmullRom, so $g(r) \ge 0$ and $\hat W(k)$ has no in-band zeros (no division blow-up in the deconvolution). Decision 19's recorded signed/positive split becomes a concrete, motivated choice here.

    Invariant 12. radial_spectrum / radial_correlation and assignment_window are neutral (harmonic analysis / spatial statistics). The names RDF, structure factor, pair correlation, and the shot-noise / normalization conventions (which encode "what a particle is") stay consumer-side — the package ships the primitives and a documented recipe (test/rdf.jl, validated against direct pair counting), not a radial_distribution_function function: that scoping held.

    Phasing — all done. Phase 1: radial_correlation(f, plans) (the field duality; neutral, no new math), $\Delta r$ = the finest grid spacing, minimum-image $|r|$ binning, $C(0) = \texttt{inner}$ exact. Phase 2: assignment_window (the linear/CIC $\prod \operatorname{sinc}^2$ deconvolution window, Convolution) + the documented point-RDF recipe, validated against direct $O(M^2)$ counting. Co-spectra (done): the two-field radial_spectrum(f, g) / radial_correlation(f, g) (the cross-power / cross-correlation pair — momentum/scalar fluxes; new methods on the existing generics that reuse the single-field binning, refining the mixed inner(f, g); symmetric, may be negative — see above). Future (deferred until a consumer makes them concrete): higher positive-B-spline / signed-kernel window transforms and interlacing (sharper small-$r$ $g$); and — the "meaningful fibers over the radial coordinate" — the anisotropic / tensor-valued radial spectra $E_{ij}(\kappa)$ and the Craya–Herring longitudinal/transverse split, which only earn their structure under anisotropy (e.g. shear flow), per the §2 rule against speculative structure.

  23. Spectral-domain transfer — DirectDFT (exact) and the native NUFFT (fast). [Both DONE.]

    The object. The transfer layer (§10) had one scheme family — the physical-domain local splines. This adds the spectral-domain transfer: evaluation/deposition between Fourier coefficients and scattered points, the exact off-grid form of the field. Both families are unified under a new supertype AbstractTransferScheme, sharing the interpolate/anterpolate verbs and the adjoint contract (the counting-transpose pairing, which the $N/M$ rescaling above turns into the exact L² adjoint); a scheme is a choice of (kernel, representation). The spectral schemes dispatch on a field in spectral representation (invariant 2: hand $W$ whichever representation it acts on — a spectral solver hands $\hat f$ for free); anterpolate returns a section, here in spectral representation (the Fourier coefficients of $\sum_p q_p\delta_{x_p}$).

    DirectDFT (done). The literal $O(NM)$ sum ($N$ the grid-point count, $o$ the grid origin): type-2 $f(x_p) = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{\text{stored }k} \alpha(k) \mathrm{Re}(\hat f(k) e^{\mathrm{i} k\cdot(x_p-o)})$ (the rfft half-spectrum reconstruction, with the Parseval weight $\alpha$ of decision 18 — $1$ on the $k_x=0$/even-Nyquist planes, $2$ else), type-1 $(W^\top q)(k) = \sum_p q_p e^{-\mathrm{i} k\cdot(x_p-o)}$. It is the definition of the spectral-domain pair, so it is at once a transparent exact baseline, the divergence-free reference (it reproduces the band-limited field exactly — verified $\sim 10^{-16}$ off-grid — where the splines break $\nabla\cdot u=0$), and the oracle that will validate NUFFT. It matches to_physical at the grid nodes and to_spectral from node samples (both exact to round-off), and satisfies $M \langle W\hat f, q\rangle_{\mathrm{pts}} = N \langle \hat f, W^\top q\rangle_{\mathrm{grid}}$ (the measure factor, as for the splines — decision 17). Two simple KernelAbstractions kernels (type-2 over points, type-1 over modes — no atomics), the per-axis wavenumbers + $\alpha$ built host→device like the multipliers; no FFT, window, or oversampling. Stateless DirectDFT(); CPU + GPU-portable. Standing principle (beyond this pair): when a fast or approximate method is added, its exact reference ships as a first-class sibling scheme, built first — at once the definition a user can reach for at small sizes and the oracle the fast form is validated against — never a test/-only helper.

    Native NUFFT (decided: own it, no dependency — done). The fast $O(N\log N + M)$ windowed gridding form of DirectDFT: spread → FFT → deconvolve, all reusing existing machinery — the transfer kernels (spread/gather with an exponential-of-semicircle window in place of the spline coefficients), a FieldTransformPlans on an oversampled ($\sigma=2$) grid, and a $1/\hat\psi$ deconvolution ScalarFieldSpectral — $\psi$ the smooth compact spreading window, $\hat\psi$ its Fourier transform — built like assignment_window (which is the $\hat\psi$ of the linear/CIC window — so NUFFT generalizes the decision-22 RDF deconvolution). The one new ingredient is that window $\psi$ (exp-of-semicircle, GPU-trivial) and its transform $\hat\psi$ (host quadrature, the $z=\sin\theta$ substitution regularizing the endpoint); the stencil width and the shape parameter $\beta$ (the ES-kernel constant of the NUFFT literature — unrelated to the package's $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulation weights) follow the Barnett–Magland–af Klinteberg ES rule for the tolerance. Verified against DirectDFT (and to_physical) to the requested tolerance $\varepsilon$ (the default requests tol = 1e-10; the exponential-of-semicircle window over-delivers, $\sim 10^{-12}$ measured there) for scalar and vector fields, with the NUFFT pair an exact adjoint ($\sim 10^{-15}$).

    The Nyquist edge. The one subtlety: the rfft half-spectrum Nyquist mode of an even axis cannot be split losslessly into the finer oversampled grid (zero-padding a real spectrum). It is projected out in the deconvolution, so NUFFT's type-1/type-2 remain exact adjoints on the resolved band — exactly the band a spectral solver uses (de-aliasing, or any natural spectral decay, zeros the Nyquist), where the transfer is exact to $\varepsilon$. DirectDFT (no oversampling) carries the Nyquist exactly; the two agree on the sub-Nyquist band.

    NonuniformFFTs.jl was evaluated (it fits — real-data rfft, AMDGPU/KA, batched) but not adopted: owning it keeps the package self-contained and makes the spline transfer and the NUFFT share one spread machinery, with DirectDFT and a direct $O(NM)$ DFT as independent ground truth. Tested CPU (test/transfer_spectral.jl) + GPU parity; method parity 198/118 unchanged (the transfer verbs are not in those surfaces).

  24. Allocation-free in-place spectral operators (the GPU hot-loop discipline). A solver runs the operators thousands of times in its time loop. On the GPU, any scratch an operator allocates internally is invisible to Julia's garbage collector — the collector triggers on host memory pressure, but the scratch lives in device memory — so the device memory pool grows every step until it fills VRAM, and the run then wedges (the allocator spins, GPU idle) or hipMalloc aborts. The discipline this forces: the in-place $!$ operators must allocate nothing, writing only into their preallocated destination. Two changes brought the spectral operators into line. (a) _squared_wavenumber_broadcast — the radial coordinate $|k|^2 = k_1^2 + k_2^2 + k_3^2$ — now returns a lazy Broadcasted over the three per-axis squared-wavenumber vectors instead of a materialized $O(N^3)$ array ($N$ the points per axis), so the Laplacian ($-|k|^2\hat f$), inverse Laplacian ($-\hat f/|k|^2$) and Helmholtz projection ($\hat f_i - k_i (k_j\hat f_j)/|k|^2$) fuse it into their own broadcasts without ever allocating it. (b) The laplacian!, inverse_laplacian! and helmholtz_projection! kernels were rewritten so their former per-call temporaries (the longitudinal coefficient $k_j\hat f_j/|k|^2$, the safe $1/|k|^2$ that is $1$ at the zero mode) are fused into the component assignments rather than materialized; the projection recomputes the longitudinal part inline for each component, which is safe because the kernel always receives a destination distinct from the source — the aliased helmholtz_projection!(x, x) allocates its guard temporary upstream (§7). (The projection has since moved on from both details: its one-pass kernel computes the longitudinal part once per mode and serves the aliased call directly, guard retired — decision 32.) Net: each of the three now allocates $\sim 0$ bytes in place (measured), with identical arithmetic and results (the full suite is unchanged) — so a preallocated, in-place pseudospectral step allocates nothing per step and runs on a 12 GB consumer GPU with no manual GC.gc() / reclaim, at flat VRAM (the examples/ HIST solvers demonstrate this — $192^3$ Float32 at $\sim\!34$ ms/step on a since-retired card). Guarded by test/allocations.jl (every in-place spectral operator, transform, convolve!, and fiber product/shuffle, both element types). The fiber norm, lpnorm and dev! deliberately materialize one field (a pointwise magnitude, or the trace they subtract) and are the documented exceptions — a consumer's CFL speed uses maximum(abs, …) rather than lpnorm.

  25. Allocation-bounded radial diagnostics — the shell binning as a single scatter-add (the GPU-GC corollary of decision 24). Decision 24 made the time loop allocation-free; this brings the diagnostics into the same discipline, for the same reason. The radial statistics — radial_spectrum (the $|k|$-shell power spectrum) and radial_correlation (the $|r|$-shell two-point correlation) — binned by looping over the shells and, for each shell $s$, reducing a masked broadcast (sum of the per-mode value over the modes whose rounded shell index equals $s$). The number of shells scales with the grid — $\lceil \sqrt{3} N/2\rceil$ ($N$ the points per axis), $\approx 220$ at $N = 256$ — and each iteration allocates a full-grid temporary: the Boolean shell mask, and (because on the GPU stack a mapreduce over a Broadcasted materializes its argument rather than fusing) the masked product too. That is $O(N)$ full-grid temporaries per single diagnostic call. On the GPU these are dead immediately but not reclaimed: Julia's garbage collector triggers on host memory pressure, and a dead device array is a tiny host wrapper owning a large device buffer (the mechanism of decision 24), so the pool climbs. One $256^3$ correlation snapshot churned several GB; with the live working set already $\sim\!6$ GB, the first snapshot wedged a 12 GB card (the allocator spinning, GPU idle).

    The fix replaces the per-shell loop with a single scatter-add pass — one portable KernelAbstractions + Atomix histogram kernel (_shell_bin_kernel!, driven by _bin_by_shell) that sweeps the array once and, per element, atomically adds the value (and a unit population count) into the accumulator of its shell, keyed by a precomputed integer shell index. (Decision 34 later fused even that staging away: the shell index and the binned quantity are now derived per element inside the kernels, and the precomputed-index pair retired.) Per-call full-grid temporaries drop from $O(N)$ to $O(1)$ (the shell-index array, the binned quantity, and the tiny length-$(\text{number of shells})$ accumulators): the spectrum keeps the value sum (normalized by $N^2$), the correlation divides it by the per-shell population. This is the same atomic scatter the point→grid anterpolate already uses (§10), now depositing mode/point energies into radial shells instead of point quantities onto the grid; it lifts the "histogram scatter deferred as unneeded" sub-decision of decision 21. The trade-off — contention on the small accumulator set — is acceptable for an occasional diagnostic (every snap_stride steps in a solver), and the old form was itself $O(\mathrm{shells}\times N^3)$ work; a privatized/shared-memory histogram is a possible future optimization, unneeded now.

    Correctness is unchanged (the scatter sums the same per-shell quantities as the masked reductions): CPU test/reductions.jl (the analytic $\langle\sin^2\rangle$ and independent binned-circshift oracles, both element types) and GPU parity test/gpu/suite.jl (allowscalar(false) — the scatter is device-clean, no scalar indexing) both hold. End to end, the $256^3$ Float32 forced-HIT example on a 12 GB card ($\approx 87$ ms/step) now runs to completion at a flat $\sim\!6$ GB ($\approx 8$ GB peak) through its radial_correlation snapshots, where the per-shell form wedged at the first one.

  26. Allocation-free in-place operators are a standing, exhaustively-enforced contract (generalizing 24 and 25). Decisions 24 (the spectral operators) and 25 (the radial binning) each fixed one family that allocated a per-call temporary the GPU could not afford. This decision promotes the property from a sequence of point fixes to a package-wide invariant: every in-place ! operator allocates no field-sized scratch when called on preallocated buffers with a destination distinct from its sources — kernels fuse their temporaries into the assignment, never materialize them. The motivation is the one from decision 24: a solver applies these operators thousands of times per step, and on the GPU a per-call temporary escapes Julia's garbage collector (which triggers on host memory pressure, while the temporary is a device buffer behind a tiny host wrapper), so the device pool grows every step until the run wedges or hipMalloc aborts.

    The enforcement is an exhaustive test, test/allocations.jl. It warms one in-place call per signature and asserts a second @allocated call stays below a small fraction of one field — across both element types, both representations, tensor orders $R = 0, 1, 2$, the field/constant/reversed and α/β argument branches, the point-value algebra, and the spline transfer (the whole in-place surface, not a sample). It runs on the CPU on purpose: there a materialized field is a host Array and @allocated counts its full bytes, so a stray temporary trips the threshold; on the GPU the same similar/broadcast allocates a device buffer whose host wrapper is only a few hundred bytes, so GPU @allocated would report $\sim 0$ and miss the leak. The operator code is generic over the array type, so a field-sized materialization — a similar or a broadcast landing in a temporary — is backend-symmetric: seeing it on the CPU is seeing it on the GPU, and the CPU is simply where it is visible. That symmetry covers the materialization class, not every byte a device run allocates: a KernelAbstractions launch also takes a small, size-independent host-side bookkeeping allocation (finding (j), ~1.5–7 KB, thread-count-dependent) with no CPU-serial counterpart, which the audit's fraction-of-a-field threshold absorbs rather than measures — sound, because the leak this contract exists to stop scales with the grid and bookkeeping does not. The contract binds new code: a new in-place operator (or any function documented allocation-free) must be added to the audit (invariant 13; the §4 coverage contract in docs/TESTING.md).

    Documented exceptions — bounded, not zero. A few operations materialize one field by design and are excluded from the zero assertion, getting a bounded guard instead (catching a regression to $O(N)$ — or $O(\mathrm{shells})$ — extra while allowing the legitimate one-field temporary): the fiber norm!/lpnorm (a pointwise magnitude) and the radial diagnostics ($O(1)$-bounded); dev! left this list when decision 39 fused its trace into a register, and std/var when decision 49 rewrote them as two fused passes — measured at 16 bytes rather than a field. The spectral inner is the subtle case ($N$ the points per axis): inner(f, f) — an energy or l2norm — is allocation-free, since $\overline{\hat f} \hat f = |\hat f|^2$ is real and the whole Parseval sum, correction planes included, is a fused sum(abs2, …); but the mixed inner(f, g) of two distinct fields allocates an $O(N^2)$ kₓ-plane correction, because a dot over the strided plane view (which would fuse, no temporary) has no method on the AMD path (rocBLAS dotc takes no strided argument — verified on the device), and one route serving every backend is preferred to a per-vendor split. Consumers form a hot-loop energy as inner(f, f) or the physical inner (a contiguous dot); the examples/ forcing computes its band energy as inner(u_band, u_band) for exactly this reason. The audit found the whole in-place surface already clean; the only allocation it surfaced was the mixed spectral inner, resolved by the inner(f, f) fast path above.

  27. Consumer-driven fused and missing field-math operations — combine!, det, symmetric_gradient, weighted_inner (done); a quadratic-form reduction deferred. The sibling consumer GBGHomogeneousTurbulence (a thin physics solver built on this field layer) asked the package to absorb the field-math it hand-rolls hottest, on the principle that a field library should serve its consumer the way numpy/BLAS serve scientific Python — not only composable primitives, but fused kernels for the recurring composites. The reason is concrete: Julia fuses within a single broadcast or mapreduce but not across function-call boundaries, so a chain like gradient! → sym! → inner is three separate passes that each materialize a tensor-sized intermediate — and on a memory-bound GPU consumer that intermediate's peak footprint, not the bandwidth, is the binding constraint. The rule for what to absorb: fuse away the intermediate the consumer never needs, or supply a genuinely missing primitive — never add an operation "for completeness" (invariant 1). An adversarial triage kept four and deferred one; each kept operation is general field math with no time, particles, or physics (invariant 12 — the physics names stay consumer-side).

    The four, by family.

    • combine!(dest, α, a, β, b) (FieldAlgebra) writes the real-weighted combination $\alpha a + \beta b$ of two same-representation fields $a, b$ with real weights $\alpha, \beta$, in one fused pass. It was a genuine gap: add! is unit-weight ($a+b$) and multiply! scales a single field, so the weighted combination (an axpby) had no form and the consumer reached into field storage with hand-rolled broadcasts at nine call sites. It completes the package's own $\alpha/\beta$ accumulation convention (already on mul!/convolve!); being linear in the fields it is defined in both representations, and being coefficientwise it is alias-safe with no guard ($a$ or $b$ may be dest). It is a new verb rather than a five-argument add!, to keep add!'s constant-variant dispatch clean. It stays field-only — the consumers are field updates, and the package does not add a point-value method for symmetry alone.
    • det/det! (FieldAlgebra) is the fiberwise determinant, the third principal invariant $I_3$ (the signed factor by which the tensor scales volumes as a linear map) — the sibling of the trace $\operatorname{tr}$ (the first invariant $I_1$) the package already carried; the gap was the missing invariant, not a completeness fill. The second invariant $I_2$ stays absent, having no consumer — and a consumer that wants it can build it from the two that ship, as $I_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(I_1^2 - \operatorname{tr}(A^2))$. Being cubic in the components it is nonlinear, so it is physical-space only (there is no spectral determinant), exactly like norm. It reuses the shared per-component formula builder in FiberSections, so it lands on both fields and point-values (mirroring $\operatorname{tr}$, which already lived on both) with the formula written once.
    • symmetric_gradient/! (DifferentialOperators) forms the symmetric part of a vector field's gradient, $S_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2} (\partial_{x_j} u_i + \partial_{x_i} u_j)$ for a vector field $u$, directly — spectrally $\hat S_{ij} = \tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2} (k_j \hat u_i + k_i \hat u_j)$ with the same Nyquist-correct derivative wavenumbers $k$ as gradient — skipping the full nine-component gradient tensor $\nabla u$ that sym(gradient(u)) builds only to symmetrize. It is the fused symmetric peer of curl, which already extracts the antisymmetric part of $\nabla u$ (as a vector). The name is the charter-neutral mathematical one; the fluids name "strain rate" stays in the consumer, exactly as the package says curl where the consumer says "vorticity".
    • weighted_inner(f, w) / weighted_inner(f, g, w) (Reductions) is the multiplier-weighted L² inner product of two fields $f, g$ in spectral representation against a real Fourier multiplier $w(k)$, $\langle f, w g\rangle = \tfrac{1}{N^2}\sum_{\text{stored }k} \alpha(k) w(k)\sum_c \mathrm{Re}(\hat f_c \overline{\hat g_c})$ (with $\alpha(k)$ the rfft half-spectrum Parseval weight — a fixed per-mode function, distinct from the scalar accumulation weight $\alpha$ of combine! above — $N$ the grid-point count, and $c$ the fiber index) — the field $g$ filtered by $w$ and paired with $f$ in one fused pass with no filtered field materialized. It is the reduction-dual of convolve (apply a spectral symbol, then reduce) and generalizes inner (the $w \equiv 1$ case); $w = |k|^2$ gives the $H^1$/Dirichlet energy $\langle f, -\Delta g\rangle$. Spectral representation only, like the Parseval inner. The consumer formed in-band energy every step as convolve! into scratch then inner; the fused form removes that scratch field.

  28. Spectral translation — translation_kernel, and the reality condition a complex kernel must meet. The sibling consumer GBGHomogeneousTurbulence asked for a fifth kernel builder: the multiplier that translates a field by a physical displacement $h = (h_1, h_2, h_3)$ (a length per axis, in the box's units), carrying a field $f$ at position $x$ to the displaced field $f(x - h)$. Its consumer is phase-shift de-aliasing, where the nonlinear product is evaluated a second time on a half-cell-displaced grid and averaged with the unshifted evaluation — the memory-lean alternative to padding the grid; it also serves shift-and-average filters and the relocation of a probe.

    Why the package owns it. Translation is a coordinate-only operation on the field, with no physics in it — the exact sibling of the derivative and cutoff multipliers the package already owns — and by the shift theorem it is a convolution: with the Dirac delta displaced to $h$, whose transfer function is the pure phase $\hat g(k) = e^{-\mathrm{i} k\cdot h}$ at wavenumber $k$. So it needs no new operation, only a new kernel (decision 20): convolve! already applies a scalar kernel to any tensor order, with its accumulation weights, alias-safely. Only the builder was missing — the package exposes no function-of-wavenumbers constructor, and the allocation helper similar_field is internal, so a consumer cannot build the kernel without reaching into the package's internals.

    The correctness trap — the real reason it belongs here. A kernel must leave a real field real. The rfft half-spectrum stores only the modes whose first wavenumber component is non-negative; the rest are implied by the reality of the field, $\hat f(-k) = \overline{\hat f(k)}$. Multiplying by a kernel preserves that symmetry — and so returns a field rather than a complex impostor — exactly when the kernel is itself Hermitian, $\hat g(-k) = \overline{\hat g(k)}$, equivalently when its physical-space form $g$ is real. The four kernels shipped before this one are real and even in $k$, so they satisfy the condition without anyone having to think about it. The translation phase does not, at one frequency per axis: the Nyquist frequency of an even-length axis (with $N_a$ points and spacing $\Delta x_a$ along axis $a$), the mode that is its own conjugate partner and where the kernel is therefore forced to be real. The two obvious candidates each fail, and they fail differently:

    • The true phase $e^{-\mathrm{i} k\cdot h}$ built from the signed wavenumbers is complex at Nyquist for a fractional displacement, so it returns coefficients that are no real field's — the inverse transform then silently keeps only their Hermitian part. (Whole-cell displacements are safe: the factor there is $(-1)^m$ for a displacement of $m$ cells, which is real.)
    • The Nyquist-zeroed derivative wavenumbers — the ones gradient uses, and the fix the request itself proposed — do keep the kernel real (the factor becomes $1$), but the value is wrong: they leave the Nyquist mode unmoved, where a one-cell shift must flip its sign.

    The correct multiplier follows from what the Nyquist mode is. It carries a single real amplitude standing for both of the wavenumbers $\pm\pi/\Delta x_a$, so its band-limited form is the cosine $\cos(\pi x_a/\Delta x_a)$, and displacing that cosine and re-sampling it on the grid multiplies it by the real factor $\cos(\pi h_a/\Delta x_a)$ — equal to $(-1)^m$ for a whole-cell displacement of $m$ cells, and to $0$ for a half-cell one, a displaced Nyquist mode being genuinely not representable on the same grid. Splitting the phase into its even and odd parts, $e^{-\mathrm{i} k_a h_a} = \cos(k_a h_a) - \mathrm{i} \sin(k_a h_a)$, states the rule in one line: the even (cosine) part takes the true wavenumber, the odd (sine) part takes the derivative wavenumber. Those are exactly the package's two multiplier families (invariant 5: squared wavenumbers keep the true value at Nyquist, derivative wavenumbers are zeroed there), so the builder is a three-factor broadcast and the Nyquist correction falls out of a convention already in place for the very same reason — an odd function of $k$ must vanish at Nyquist to keep a real field real, which is why the derivative multiplier is zeroed there. The translation_kernel builder is the first operation to use both families, and it confirms that split rather than excepting it.

    Consequences, documented on the operation. The kernel is a pure phase of unit modulus on every resolved mode, so it moves the field without touching any amplitude; at Nyquist it is a real attenuation instead. Translations therefore compose and invert exactly on the resolved band but not at Nyquist (displacing by $h$ and back scales that one mode by $\cos^2$). None of this reaches a field already truncated by a de-aliasing cutoff, which is the consumer's case.

    Scope: the builder only. No fused translate! — a convolve! that builds its own kernel — although the request offered one. The kernel depends on nothing but the displacement, so building it per call would allocate a field-sized array on every invocation, exactly the allocation the package forbids in place (decisions 24, 26). The family's build-once-apply-many pattern is already the right one: a displaced evaluation builds the two kernels (for $+h$ and $-h$) once and applies them with convolve! at zero allocation.

    Verification. Two independent oracles pin the operator, and each rejects one of the wrong kernels: a whole-cell displacement equals circshift on a random field, which carries content at every mode including Nyquist; and a sub-cell displacement equals the exact DirectDFT evaluation of the field at the displaced nodes $x_n - h$ (decision 23). The random field matters — the band-limited fields the older convolution tests use pass with any of the three candidate kernels, which is precisely how a hand-rolled kernel gets away with being wrong. Reality is checked directly (transforming the displaced field out and back is the identity, the inverse transform being the Hermitian projector, so a non-Hermitian result would move), alongside the analytic displaced cosine, the Nyquist attenuation, and unit modulus on the resolved band — over both element types, a mixed-parity grid, GPU device-parity, and a benchmark. The method-set baselines (206 field / 122 point-value methods) are untouched: like convolve itself, this is a new generic in Convolution.

  29. HDF5 field persistence — write_field / read_field, a weak-dependency extension. The sibling consumer GBGStreamingStatistics ships write_histogram / read_histogram to persist its durable summaries to HDF5; the request was the field analogue, so a solver can checkpoint a field for restart, dump a full-resolution snapshot for later analysis, or hand a field to another tool as easily as it dumps statistics. The demos already hand-rolled HDF5 for their plotted curves; this makes the fields themselves first-class.

    Persistence, not a mathematical operation — so it does not touch the math API. Serializing a field is I/O, not an operation on sections, so the rule that design changes be justified by established mathematics (§2), which governs the operation set, does not bar it — and the mechanism keeps it out of the way: write_field / read_field are a weak-dependency package extension ([weakdeps] HDF5 + [extensions], methods in ext/GBGPeriodicFieldsHDF5Ext.jl), so the HDF5 binary never enters the core and a user who does not persist pays nothing. Bare function write_field end stubs and their docstrings live in Fields, so ?write_field and the manual work without HDF5 loaded; the methods appear only when it is. This mirrors the sibling exactly.

    The line held (invariant 12 — no time). A field is a self-describing object — its numeric array plus its Grid3D geometry, Fourier layout, element type, and tensor order — so it maps onto one HDF5 group: the array is the dataset data, and everything else is attributes (a type tag, a schema version, the grid geometry, the fft_dims, the element type, the tensor order, the representation). The primitive is one field at a time, and — the load-bearing choice — it takes an open File or Group, never a path. So the consumer bundles a whole iteration snapshot (velocity, vorticity, …) into one file under separate names and attaches its own time / iteration attributes: the package serializes the fields, the consumer owns the bundling and every notion of time. There is no Snapshot type and no clock in the field layer. A field group and a GBGStreamingStatistics histogram group share the same self-describing-group convention, so a run's fields and its statistics can live in one file.

    Both representations, host and device. A field in physical representation stores a real array; a field in spectral representation stores its complex half-spectrum (HDF5 round-trips complex arrays). Device (GPU) arrays are copied to the host on write via Array(…) — backend-agnostic, a no-op on the CPU — and the reload is a host field the consumer moves to a device if wanted. On read, read_field dispatches on the stored type tag and reconstructs through the public constructor: the element type follows the stored array (real or complex), the tensor order follows its dimensionality, and the grid spacing is recomputed bit-for-bit, so the reload is exact. The sugar parent[name] = field is setindex! for write_field, made strictly more specific than HDF5's own array-writing setindex! so it never clashes.

    Scope. Every field type (the six: scalar / vector / tensor × physical / spectral); no compression or chunking options until a consumer needs them; no snapshot / time type (invariant 12).

    Verification. The round-trip test test/hdf5.jl covers every field type over both element types, asserting the data (real and complex), the full Grid3D geometry, the element type, the tensor order, and the layout are recovered bit-for-bit; it exercises the setindex! sugar, a snapshot bundle carrying consumer-owned time / iteration attributes, the self-describing attributes read back as plain HDF5, and the error paths (a dataset instead of a group, an absent name, a group with no type tag). The method-set baselines (206 field / 122 point-value methods) are untouched — write_field / read_field are new generics, and their methods exist only under the extension.

  30. Mapped reductions — a leading function on five of the measure reductions. The consumer GBGHomogeneousTurbulence audited its memory and found a recurring shape: to average a nonlinear invariant of a tensor field — the determinant of a strain tensor, the magnitude of a velocity — it had to build that invariant as a whole section first and then reduce it, paying one field of scratch for a single number. Here a section is a field or a set of point-values, and its fiber value at a base point is the scalar, vector, or $3 \times 3$ tensor sitting over that point. So five reductions gained a form taking a leading function, applied to the fiber value at every base point: mean(det, A) averages a determinant, maximum(norm, u) takes a supremum of magnitude, and the mapped quantity is consumed as it is formed rather than stored.

    The standard library sets the surface, including where it stops. The mapped spellings mirror Statistics' mean(f, itr) and Base's sum/maximum/minimum/extrema with a leading function, so mean, maximum, minimum, extrema, and the package's own integral (the extensive peer of mean) take one. Statistics gives var and std no such method, so neither does this package: inventing a spelling the standard library declines to have would be exactly the "for completeness" addition invariant 1 exists to refuse. Being a nonlinear map of the fiber, the mapped forms are physical-only, like lpnorm and std before them.

    It reconciles an older deliberate absence rather than overturning it. The bare maximum/minimum/extrema are scalar-section-only on purpose: the supremum of a vector or tensor section is ambiguous between the components and the magnitude, and the package refuses to guess (§12). The mapped form does not guess — the caller names the map, so maximum(norm, u) is well posed where maximum(u) is not. The absence was of an ambiguous operation, and naming the map is precisely the condition that removes the ambiguity, so the mapped forms carry no ambiguity restriction. That is the restriction this entry lifts, and it is not the same as carrying no ceiling at all: decision 56 later fixed the supported surface at $R < 3$, and the fiber-preserving and mapped reductions throw above it rather than return a wrong answer. They also give a fused peer to lpnorm(u, Inf), which computes the same supremum of magnitude by materializing a magnitude section first.

    Fiber-last layout dictates the implementation, and hides two allocation traps. Because components are stored last (invariant 3), a base point's components sit a whole base apart, so a fiber cannot be reinterpreted into a StaticArray in place; the fiber is instead gathered through a lazy Broadcasted over the component arrays, which reduces in one pass on both backends. Two spellings that look equivalent are not, and each silently allocates:

    • the multi-argument mapreduce(fiber_map, combine, components...) is fused by GPUArrays but falls back on the CPU to a route that materializes a full section-sized array;
    • an anonymous (values...) -> fiber_map(V(values)) leaves the component count uninferrable, so the fiber cannot be built in registers and the reduction allocates per base point — measured at about four and a half times the tensor section's own size. A callable struct carrying the fiber type restores the inference.

    A third trap sits in the public signatures: Julia declines to specialize on an argument of Function type that a method only forwards instead of calling, so each mapped form names its map's type as a parameter. Without that, the map arrives boxed and every call allocates. All three are guarded by the @allocated audit, which pins the whole family at zero bytes.

    The reduction is split by backend, so the fused route wins on both. How a reduction consumes its input is where the backends disagree, and a single formulation cannot serve both. Reducing a contiguous array uses a vectorized blocked loop; reducing a lazy Broadcasted forgoes that loop, at a measured $5.6\times$ on the reduction alone — more than materializing an extra section costs in traffic. A GPU has no such loop to forfeit: its reduction is thread-parallel and bandwidth-bound, so index arithmetic hides behind memory latency and the section write and read-back that fusing skips is pure profit. The implementation therefore reduces through a vectorized blocked loop over the component arrays on a CPU and one fused pass over a lazy Broadcasted on a GPU. Measured with the same map on both sides (mean(det, A) against mean(det(A)), $N = 128$, Float64), the fused route then costs 5.1 ms against 13.0 ms on a CPU and 0.55 ms against 0.69 ms on a GPU — cheaper on both, allocating nothing against one full section. The CPU loop blocks pairwise like Base's own reduction, which also makes it the most accurate of the routes (relative error $1.2\times10^{-16}$ against the lazy form's $3.7\times10^{-15}$). The strided gather that fiber-last layout forces costs only about $2\times$, and building the StaticArray per base point is free.

    A caveat that outranks the route, recorded because it misled this analysis twice. The fiber map's own cost can outweigh the choice of reduction entirely. LinearAlgebra.norm applied to a fiber uses the overflow-safe scaled 2-norm, measured at $4.3\times$ the plain $\sqrt{\sum_i u_i^2}$ that the fieldwise norm evaluates. Comparing maximum(norm, u) against lpnorm(u, Inf) therefore varies the algorithm and the route at once, and reads as a fusion penalty that is not the fusion at all — given the same formula, the fused form is 1.8 ms against 4.2 ms. Vary one thing at a time when measuring this family. The integral docstring carries the practical form of both points for the reader who never opens this file.

  32. The one-pass Helmholtz kernel, and a convolution fused into the projection and the divergence (the operator ∘ kernel family). Two requests from the consumer package GBGHomogeneousTurbulence ("IT") — items 1 and 6 of its 2026-07 memory and compute audits — triaged adversarially and landed as one kernel rewrite, because they touch the same code.

    The one-pass projection. helmholtz_projection! — the Leray/Helmholtz projection $\mathbb P$, which removes a vector field's curl-free part — used to run as three fused broadcasts, one per output component, each recomputing the longitudinal factor $k_j \hat u_j / |k|^2$ (the wavenumber-parallel part of the field, $k$ the wavenumber vector and $\hat u$ the field's spectral coefficients) from all three source components: nine component reads per mode for three writes, and an in-place call (destination aliasing the source) was detected and routed through a guard temporary costing one full vector field per call — the single largest transient in IT's measured budget, and the one op whose natural call is in place. It is now a single portable KernelAbstractions kernel (the package's third KA site, after the transfer kernels and the shell binning): per mode, the three source values are read into registers, the longitudinal factor is computed once, and the three destinations are written. Three reads for three writes, and alias-safe by construction — all of a mode's reads precede its writes, and modes are disjoint — so the guard and its temporary are retired (§7) and the aliased call is bitwise-identical to the non-aliased one. Two implementation notes worth recording: the sources are deliberately not marked @Const, whose no-other-writes promise the aliased call would break; and the KA launch keeps a small size-independent host-side bookkeeping allocation (measured $\sim$1.5–7 KB, thread-count-dependent), which the allocation audit's fraction-of-a-field threshold absorbs — the field-sized temporary is what is gone. Measured on the 12700K, the rewrite is 2.7–6.5× faster than the three-broadcast form ($64^3$–$128^3$ Float64, one and four threads) — the memory fix paid compute, as predicted: the projection is bandwidth-bound, and nine reads became three.

    The kernel-composed forms. helmholtz_projection! and divergence! gain trailing kernel[, α, β] arities: op!(dest, f, multipliers, kernel) and op!(dest, f, multipliers, kernel, α, β), with the four-argument form delegating with one(T), zero(T) (invariant 10) and allocating op(f, multipliers, kernel) wrappers. The mathematics is the §4 multiplier picture closing under composition: a coefficientwise kernel $\hat g$ (a Fourier symbol, as convolve! takes it) commutes through both operators — scalar times matrix symbol for $\mathbb P$, scalar times the contraction $\mathrm{i} a \cdot$ for the divergence — so "operator then convolve" is itself one Fourier multiplier, and the fused form computes it in the operator's own pass. The consumer pattern it serves is mask-de-aliasing: every de-aliased right-hand side in a pseudospectral solver is the operator followed immediately by convolve! with the de-alias kernel, so fusing saves that pass's full read and write of the destination (two passes of the field's class per evaluation). The α/β pair is load-bearing, not completeness: the fused form replaces a convolve! that carries α/β — IT already rides the $-\nabla\cdot$ sign of its scalar advection on convolve!'s α — so shipping less would strand the very chains being fused; and β collapses IT's phase-shift de-aliasing tail entirely, as two projection calls — the primary with the de-alias kernel at $\alpha = 1/2$, the displaced evaluation with the composed kernel (de-alias ∘ shift-back, built once at setup by the kernel composition convolve(g₁, g₂)) at $\alpha = 1/2$, $\beta = 1$ — eliminating the standalone shift-back, average, and truncation passes (four passes per evaluation at wiring time; two more were already banked consumer-side by rewriting the average as convolve!'s five-argument form). The curl! operator deliberately does not get the kernel arity: IT's curls feed transforms directly, so there is no consumer — the absence is the §2 no-speculative-structure rule, not invariant 1. The distinction matters and the two must not be blurred: invariant 1's absences are mathematical statements a user can rely on (a spectral * is invalid and always will be), while this one is merely unbuilt — curl! is exactly as composable with a kernel as divergence!, and the arity would be added the day a consumer wants it. Recorded here. Validity is unchanged: spectral-only, the kernel under convolve!'s Hermitian reality condition, and the Nyquist split untouched (per-mode scalars multiply regardless of their Nyquist values). Guards: the fused-equals-sequential equalities, the α/β references, the bitwise aliased projection, and the kernel-aliasing case in test/differentialoperators.jl; zero-allocation pins for every new arity and the aliased call in test/allocations.jl; device-vs-host parity in test/gpu/suite.jl; the fused-vs-sequential benchmark rows in the operators class.

  33. The exponential radial kernel and the kernel-composed combination (the lazy-kernel pair). Two requests from GBGHomogeneousTurbulence's audits — items 2 and 7 — landed as one broadcast-builder family in Convolution, because the second is the first with a two-field prologue.

    The exponential family. exponential_convolve! (allocating exponential_convolve) applies the exponential radial kernel $\exp(c |k|^2 + s \hat w)$ — $c$ a real coefficient on the squared wavenumber $|k|^2$, $\hat w$ an optional real per-mode exponent field with real scale $s$ — evaluated lazily from the three one-dimensional wavenumber arrays inside the fused assignment. The kernel is never built, so against the stored-kernel route (radial_kernel → consumer broadcast → convolve!) it removes the kernel's residency, its build pass, and its full field read per application, reading $O(N)$ wavenumber rows instead ($N$ the points per axis). The measured trade is a reuse question (PERFORMANCE.md finding (k)): with a varying exponent — the consumer's case, whose stage substeps differ — the lazy form beats rebuild-then-apply by $\approx 1.4\times$ on the GPU; with one fixed kernel applied many times the stored route keeps a $\approx 1.1\times$ (GPU) to $\approx 1.7\times$ (CPU) edge, the per-mode exponential costing more than the field read it saves. The two-operand fused forms below win on both backends regardless ($\approx 1.1$–$1.5\times$, the CPU two-operand case the low end — the finding-(k) table of PERFORMANCE.md is the source of record). For $c \le 0$ the kernel is, as a real physical object, the periodic Gaussian: the operator $e^{-c\Delta}$, smoothing at length $\sqrt{-2c}$ — and the identification is exact on the grid because the exponent uses the same true-Nyquist squared-wavenumber family as the Laplacian's own symbol (invariant 5).

    The boundary, and two signature decisions made against the request's framing. The consumer framed the exponent as built from a diffusivity and a substep; neither crosses PF's boundary (invariant 12) — the consumer folds them into the dimensionless $c$, and the name "rate" was declined (a rate is per-unit-time, and the array that would cross is the dimensionless product, not the rate). Working the consumer's stage structure sharpened the offset into its final shape: a scalar exponent offset does not exist, because $\exp(c|k|^2 + s_0) = e^{s_0} \exp(c|k|^2)$ folds into the accumulate weight $\alpha$ — a deliberate absence; and the per-mode offset enters as scale times field ($s \hat w$, not a pre-scaled array), because the consumer's substeps vary per stage — with a bare offset array every stage would pay a full array rebuild, while the scalar $s$ is free and the resident $\hat w$ is built once.

    The kernel-composed combination. combine! gains the method combine!(dest, α, a, β, b, kernel) — the weighted combination convolved with a stored kernel, $\hat g \cdot (\alpha \hat a + \beta \hat b)$, in one coefficientwise pass — and exponential_convolve! carries the same two-operand form with its lazy kernel. The consumer evidence is the integrating-factor state update (a combine! followed immediately by a convolve! with the factor, per stage per field — two full passes of the destination saved per site, about six vector plus six scalar passes per three-stage step); the lazy twin additionally retires the factor's per-stage build. A third proposed consumer — the phase-shift average — was found not to be an instance of this form at all (its kernel lands on one operand), and is served instead by decision 32's $\beta$ accumulate; the correction is recorded there. With $b === \texttt{dest}$ the method computes the kernel-filtered accumulate in place (coefficientwise, hence alias-safe with no guard), which is why it carries no second trailing accumulate pair — two $\alpha$/$\beta$ roles in one signature is the glyph-collision failure mode, and the aliasing provides the accumulate for free.

    Naming and the method-set bookkeeping. The kerneled combination is a method of combine! with a trailing kernel argument — the decision-32 composition idiom (divergence! and helmholtz_projection! compose the same way) — not a new verb; the earlier weighted_inner naming (decision 27) is the reductions family's convention and stands there. The method is defined in Convolution, and the honest reason is narrower than "include order": DifferentialOperators precedes Convolution too and kept its own kernel-composed divergence!/helmholtz_projection! arities (decision 32), so precedence alone does not decide it. What differs is that those two are operators whose symbol the kernel multiplies, defined where the operator lives, whereas combine!'s kernel form is a convolution applied to a combination — a Convolution operation taking a FieldAlgebra verb's arguments. Placing it here happens to leave the FieldAlgebra method-parity baseline untouched, the parity count filtering on the defining module. That is an observation about a consequence and never a reason: a baseline exists to notice movement, so avoiding movement is not a design goal, and placement follows the operation's mathematics. A change that moves a count refreezes it with the reason (decision 53's protocol) rather than being reshaped to avoid it. Validity: spectral-only (a kernel is a spectral object; the physical absence is a validity absence), the exponential kernel real, even, and positive — Hermitian-safe by construction — and a complex-valued $\hat w$ breaks reality exactly as any consumer-built kernel can under convolve! (the docstring warns; the trust model is unchanged). Guards: stored-kernel equality oracles, the single-mode eigenvalue oracle $e^{c}\cos x$, fused-equals-sequential for both two-operand forms, the in-place filtered accumulate, and the absences in test/convolution.jl; zero-allocation pins in test/allocations.jl; device parity in test/gpu/suite.jl; and the convolution benchmark rows whose baseline is the sequential combine! + convolve! pair.

  34. The radial diagnostics fuse their staging into the binning kernels. The audits' item 5: each radial_spectrum call staged three full spectral planes — a fiber-contracted magnitude array, an integer shell-index array, and a Parseval-weighted array — before the decision-25 scatter-add pass consumed them (measured 1.6 of the consuming solver GBGHomogeneousTurbulence's units at $256^3$, reproduced here; the cross-power form staged a fourth, fiber-shaped, plane), and radial_correlation staged a separation-magnitude and a shell-index array of its own. All of it is now derived per element inside the kernels: the spectral kernel computes a mode's $|k|$ from the three one-dimensional squared-wavenumber arrays (the same left-associated sum the lazy $|k|^2$ broadcast evaluates, so shell assignment is bitwise-unchanged), contracts the fiber inline ($\sum_c |\hat f_c|^2$, or $\sum_c \mathrm{Re}(\hat f_c \overline{\hat g_c})$ for the cross form — one kernel, the second field nothing-dispatched away), and reads the rfft Parseval weight off the plane index; the physical kernel derives the minimum-image separation from the grid index and folds the $1/N$ correlation normalization into the tiny per-shell average. The shell count is computed analytically from the corner: the separable $|k|^2$ (and the min-image $|r|$) is maximized componentwise, so the corner value is the same floating-point sum the kernel forms for that element — three $O(N)$ reductions over one-dimensional arrays (spectrum) or pure host arithmetic (correlation), and no bin can overflow by construction. Each kernel allocates only the accumulators it uses (the spectrum drops the population counter the correlation needs).

  35. The audit-response declines, recorded: no general fusion API, two memory items deferred, and the counterparty's standing reservation. The 2026-07 audit exchange with GBGHomogeneousTurbulence ("IT") shipped five items (decisions 32–34). This entry records what was declined or deferred, because a decline is a decision, not a gap.

    No general fusion API. After two rounds of fusion requests the natural follow-up was "expose a general fusion mechanism and stop shipping one-offs." Declined, on five grounds. (a) The general mechanism already exists de facto — Julia's dot-broadcast fuses over fields — and it is precisely the problem: it is unguarded, able to express what the method set deliberately withholds (a .* of two fields in spectral representation is the banned coefficientwise product), and no type system can help, since a legitimate kernel is a ScalarFieldSpectral — kernel application and the banned field product are indistinguishable at the type level. (Decision 60 later made a kernel its own type, so that particular pair is now distinguishable; the ground stands anyway, since a .* of two fields remains expressible and unguarded.) The broadcast layer is therefore internal machinery, not a public contract (§8). (b) The public method set is the spec (invariant 1); a fusion surface erases it. (c) Verification does not fuse: each named operation carries alias analysis, the α/β branch, Nyquist reasoning (decision 28 needed both wavenumber families to keep a displaced field real), the Hermitian reality condition, allocation pins, device parity, and bitwise threading identity — a client-composed kernel carries none of them, and the consumer's own architecture ("raise, don't reimplement") exists because it does not want to own kernel correctness. (d) The triage protocol is where correctness happens: across two rounds it caught a wrong signature, a missing β, an invariant-12 boundary violation, and produced two fusions larger than requested — a compiler would have delivered none of that. (e) Demand is measurably exhausted: everything that survived triage instantiates one structure — sections in spectral representation as a module over the ring of Fourier symbols — whose operations the named set now covers (ring multiplication is kernel composition, the ring-weighted combination is decision 33, operator ∘ symbol is decision 32), and with the five items landed IT's frozen pass ledger sits at its fused ideal. Two validity-closed escalation paths are recorded for when demand materializes, not built: radial real symbols $\sigma(|k|^2)$ as closure kernels (real and even by construction, hence Hermitian-safe and Nyquist-unambiguous; exponential_convolve! is the family's first instance, and a third functional form — a Gaussian filter, hyperviscosity $\exp(-\nu (|k|^2)^p \tau)$ — is the trigger to generalize); and a stdlib-shaped physical map!(φ, dest, sections...) (since built — decision 48), the section-valued sibling of the mapped reductions (every pointwise fiber map is valid in physical representation), waiting for a consumer needing a fiber map outside the shipped algebra.

    Deferred: symmetric second-order tensor storage (IT item 3). Mathematically legitimate — the symmetric tensors $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)$ are a genuine subbundle, so a six-component storage type clears the §2 bar — but it is the largest surface on the table (two field types, a symmetric fiber type, six-component transform plans, and a combinatorial decision over the 206-method algebra), and a symmetric type wired into only a few operations is exactly the partial surface invariant 1 exists to prevent. It is also dominated on its own motivating episode by item 4 below, which composes with it. Revisit when a consumer needs the symmetric type as a first-class algebra citizen, not as storage for one diagnostic.

    Deferred: an in-place real-to-complex transform mode (IT item 4). Requested "when convenient" with its consumer archived, so the no-speculative-structure rule settles it — and the design cost is real: in-place r2c needs the padded first dimension $2(N_1/2+1)$ ($N_1$ the first-axis points), making the physical data a non-contiguous view, which collides with invariant 3's contiguity guarantee (relied on by FFT batching and the threading chunking) and needs a rocFFT/cuFFT in-place story. It is, though, the largest-reach memory lever on the table; if memory genuinely binds, un-archiving the consumer to make this concrete beats pursuing the symmetric storage. The wider byte-level buffer-sharing framing is a scratch-arena design, and the package's stance is that the consumer owns buffers.

    The counterparty's standing reservation, quoted so ground (e) stays honest. IT pre-registered — deliberately not raising it now — that its post-wiring ledger sits near but not at the fused ideal: the residual is the two-output stage update $(\hat h, \hat u) \leftarrow (\hat N + a \hat h,\ \mathcal E \odot (\hat u + b \hat h))$ — a module map with two outputs sharing an intermediate, outside the ring structure the shipped set closes (not ring multiplication, not the ring-weighted combination, not operator ∘ symbol) — of order 13 % of its pass traffic under the mask strategies. If the regenerated ledger confirms it once the five items are wired, it returns as a round-3 request with consumer evidence, per the demand protocol. It would not fail the §2 bar (a map into a direct sum of section spaces with ring-weighted entries is honest structure); the questions it will face are surface shape — the package has no two-destination operation — and whether the measured share justifies one.

  36. The pass audit: element traffic measured per operation against a frozen fused ideal. The audit exchange proved a hole in the verification stool: an unfused operation passes every existing layer — the correctness oracles (right values), the allocation audit (recomputation allocates nothing), static analysis — and only a benchmark number drifts, with no ideal beside it to notice. The three-broadcast Helmholtz projection read every source component three times per mode from decision 24 until decision 32, and no test failed. Since the pointwise operations are bandwidth-bound streaming (PERFORMANCE.md §2), their element traffic is their cost — so test/pass_audit.jl now measures it and pins it exactly.

    Method — measured, not transcribed. the consuming solver GBGHomogeneousTurbulence's compute audit (its decision 34) freezes an analytically transcribed per-step pass ledger with a fused-ideal bound (live-ins read once, live-outs written once, intermediates free) and a named-holder gap register; that is the honest form for a schedule its author owns. The package has no schedule (invariant 12), so its unit is the operation — and, per its own testing philosophy (verify against ground truth, never the code's own account), it measures rather than transcribes: a test-local CountingArray (getindex/setindex! under atomic counters) slides beneath the field types through the generic storage of invariant 6, flows through the field broadcast, _assign!, and the KernelAbstractions CPU kernels alike, and every audited call asserts each participating array's exact absolute element counts — absolute, not per-array multiples, because legitimate patterns break multiples (sym! reads an off-diagonal twice and a diagonal once). Beside each frozen count sits the fused ideal, and a count above its ideal must carry a named holder or the suite fails — the gap register transplanted intact. CPU-only, like the allocation audit and for the same reason: the code is generic, so the pass structure is backend-independent, while launch counts and achieved bandwidth stay benchmark-side. Excluded with reasons — each exclusion cut to its true width: the transform interiors (FFTW's internal passes are FFTW's; plans reject wrapped storage — but an operation containing a transform still audits its countable stages, and radial_correlation's input read and its decision-34 physical binning kernel are audited, the wrapper never reaching the FFT because similar on it yields plain storage); the anterpolation scatter (its Atomix accumulate needs pointer atomics a wrapper cannot provide — the gather is audited at its exact per-point stencil arithmetic, $M (2n+2)^3$ field reads per component, and extending the atomics onto the wrapper is the recorded route to the scatter); and O(N) configuration arrays and point positions (only field- and point-value-shaped storage is wrapped, its own scope line).

    What the first measurement found. The decision-32/33/34 kernels sit at their ideals — the one-pass projection reads 3 and writes 3 (the audit would have failed the old 9), its $\beta = 0$ forms provably never read the destination, the KA kernel reads a composed ĝ once per mode where the broadcast family re-reads a scalar kernel once per fiber component, and the fused radial binning reads the field once. (Since retired: decisions 38/39 took the whole family to its fused ideals; the one holder standing is the exponential ŵ fiber extrusion.) Two curiosities the exact counters caught: LinearAlgebra's generic dot (and the component reduction under mean) peeks one element to type its accumulator (+1 read, frozen with its holder), while the fused mapped reductions peek nothing.

  37. The CPU path's performance target is one cluster node, not small problems — and that reopens the GPU-first tiebreaks. The performance model (§9, PERFORMANCE.md §1) recorded the CPU path as serving "development, testing, and small problems", and every platform tiebreak below was licensed by that sentence. It understated the role. The CPU path does serve development and small tests — but at those grids performance seldom decides anything, so that role sets no tuning target. The role that does is the opposite of small: a single large CPU node, running the resolutions that exceed one GPU's memory. Since distributed and multi-GPU execution are out of scope (§1), the memory ceiling of a run is whatever one node holds, and a CPU node holds far more than a GPU. On NAISS Arrhenius — the concrete target — a thin CPU node pairs two 128-core AMD EPYC 9755 processors with 768 GB of DDR5 and a fat node carries 3 TB, while the GPU partition's GH200 offers 96 GB of HBM. Taking one scalar field in either representation as 8 bytes per grid point in Float64 (the physical array and the rfft half-spectrum agree to within a part in the per-axis size) and a solver's live set at order 25 fields, one GH200 stops near $768^3$ where a thin CPU node reaches about $1536^3$. The two partitions are also distinct machines, so the split is clean: GPU work runs on GPU nodes, CPU work on CPU nodes.

    What this reopens. Three recorded results rest on the understated premise, and each was measured outside the regime that now matters. The loop order (PERFORMANCE.md §4b) kept the $x$-inner stencil traversal for the transfer kernels at a measured $0.75\times$ CPU regression, justified on two grounds — that "the CPU's large caches absorb the strided access", and that the GPU is the production platform. At $1536^3$ a single Float64 vector field is 87 GB against 512 MB of L3 per socket, so the first ground fails outright, and the second no longer holds in this band; the regression there is plausibly worse than the measured figure, not better. The pass audit's standing gaps (decision 36; since retired by decisions 38/39) — gradient! re-reading its source $3\times$, symmetric_gradient! $5\times$ — are close to free while a field is cache-resident and become pure DRAM traffic once it is not, so they price very differently here. And NUMA first-touch, which §9 sets aside as a consumer concern, is a two-socket node's dominant risk: similar reserves address space without touching pages, so the placement is decided by whichever code writes first, and a threaded _assign! write distributes while a serial fill concentrates. Whether that lands well is a measurement nobody has taken.

    The measurement gap this exposes. benchmark/characterize.jl sweeps $64^3$–$256^3$ by default, and no CPU column above $256^3$ has ever been recorded — so the entire CPU production band is unmeasured, and the tiebreaks above were settled two to four octaves below it. Closing that is a prerequisite for revisiting them, and it is only partly possible off-cluster: a single-socket workstation can extend the grid sweep but cannot exhibit a NUMA effect at all.

    The same target fixes a portability bar: aarch64. Arrhenius's GPU nodes are GH200 superchips, so the CUDA device is hosted by a Grace CPU — an ARM (aarch64) host — and production will eventually run on both partitions. The package must therefore stay buildable on ARM, and no design decision or dependency may be adopted that is x86-only — the canonical tempting example is swapping the FFT provider to MKL, which has no aarch64 build; any x86-intrinsic tuning falls under the same bar. Nothing in the current dependency tree is known to be x86-only (pure Julia plus JLL artifacts published for aarch64-linux), but nothing has ever been built or tested on ARM either — a coverage gap recorded in PERFORMANCE.md §7.

  45. Grid3D enforces its own uniformity (decided 2026-07-24; a /check-consistency panel finding). The type is documented as a uniform grid, so its spacing is not independent data — it is domain_size ./ grid_size along every axis. But grid_spacing is stored (the transfer kernels read it directly, and recomputing it per launch buys nothing), and Julia's generated four-argument constructor is public, so a caller could hand in a spacing bearing no relation to the domain and get a silently self-contradictory grid. Nothing in the tree did: the only four-argument call site was the two-argument convenience constructor itself, with the HDF5 reader, the NUFFT oversampled grid and the tests all going through the two-argument form — and the reader in fact re-derives the spacing on load, so a persisted inconsistent grid would not have survived its own round trip. The failure it invites is the quiet kind: the spline transfer kernels turn a position into a cell index with the stored spacing, so a wrong value mislocates every interpolated point instead of raising anything. An inner constructor now rejects a spacing that contradicts the domain, and a non-positive grid size with it. Decision 29 gave fields a serializer and stopped there, which left a plain asymmetry: a solver could checkpoint the velocity field it was integrating but not the point set it was advecting through that field, so a restart could not reconstruct the state it had. Since the point layer exists for consumer-side Lagrangian methods (§10), the half that cannot be persisted is the half a restart most needs. The pair mirrors the field pair exactly, in the same weak-dependency extension, with the same bare generics and docstrings in the core so ?write_points works without HDF5 loaded.

    Why decision 29's reasoning carries over unchanged. Serializing a section is I/O, not an operation on sections, so the rule that design changes be justified by established mathematics (§2) — which governs the operation set — does not bar it, exactly as for fields. Invariant 12 is untouched as well: a PointSet is a set of positions, not a particle with a history, and the package still attaches no notion of time. The consumer owns the file, bundles a snapshot, and writes its own time / iteration attributes.

    The group is simpler than a field's, because a point object carries less. A field group must describe its Grid3D geometry, its Fourier layout and its representation; a point object has none of those. So the group holds the array as the dataset data plus four attributes — a type tag, a schema version, the element type, and the point count — with a fifth, tensor_order, on a point-value section but not on a PointSet, which is a base and has no fiber. The tensor order is recovered on read from the array's dimensionality, as a field's is.

    Positions and values are separate groups, deliberately. They are separate objects — the base, and sections over it — and one stored set of positions is often shared by several value sets, so embedding the coordinates in every value group would duplicate the largest array in the file. A consumer that wants them bundled writes both under one parent group, which costs nothing and keeps the choice with the consumer.

  48. map! / map — the arbitrary-fiber-map escape hatch, physical-only by construction (2026-07-28). The section-valued sibling of the mapped reductions (decision 30) and decision-35's first map! instance: map!(φ, destination, sources...) applies an arbitrary fiber map φ to every base point and writes a section, where a mapped mean reduces it. It reuses the _fiberwise_pass! executor unchanged — that executor already takes a runtime values function and keeps its targets and arrays tuples separate, so an order-changing map (exp ∘ det : tensor → scalar) works with no new machinery — so map! inherits the fused pass's parallelism (a device kernel on a GPU, a slab-threaded loop on a CPU) and its allocation-free contract, and is embarrassingly parallel (disjoint writes, no atomics), bitwise thread-count-invariant on the CPU.

    The only φ-specific piece is a small adapter in FiberSections (_MapValues): it groups the flat component scalars a fused pass hands it back into fibers (column-major, so assembly is a bare SMatrix constructor and the flatten a bare Tuple), applies φ, and flattens the result — a struct, not a closure, so the input count stays type-level, exactly _FiberGather's reasoning for the reductions. The public methods live where the vertical algebra lives: FieldAlgebra for physical fields, Points for point-values (both over the shared _map_pass! driver), extending Base.map! / Base.map (not piracy — the types are ours). It is physical-only for fields: an arbitrary φ is nonlinear, so by invariant 1 there is no spectral method, and the result is aliased in spectral representation (the FieldAlgebra module warning). Point-values are representation-free and carry no such restriction. The allocating map infers the output tensor order from φ's return type (Base.promote_op); when it cannot, it errors and steers to map! with a preallocated destination.

    Why an escape hatch is allowed at all, against invariant 1's "the method set is the spec": map! is validity-closed — physical-only-by-construction is its type signature, so it can only ever produce a valid operation, never forge an invalid spectral one. It does not subsume the named algebra: the linear fiber operations (tr, sym, skew, dev, transpose) exist in both representations and so cannot route through a physical-only map, and the named nonlinear ones (det, norm) already share the same executor a level down — map! serves the un-named maps, and the named operations stay named to keep the representation-validity encoding intact.

  49. var/std are scalar-only, gain a spectral form, and the vector second central moment is the new covariance cov (2026-07-28). The fiber-preserving var/std — a per-component variance / standard deviation for a vector or tensor section — were two mistakes wearing one API. First, they were physical-only by scoping, not validity: $\operatorname{var}(f) = \langle f, f\rangle - \langle f\rangle^2 = N^{-2}\sum_{\text{stored }k \ne 0} \alpha(k) |\hat f_k|^2$ (the fluctuation energy, with $\alpha(k)$ the rfft half-spectrum multiplicity and the $N^{-2}$ the normalization the inner product itself carries; that displayed form is the population moment — corrected = false — and var's default corrected = true returns it scaled by $N/(N-1)$), both terms already Parseval-available, so they were wrongly grouped with the genuinely physical-only lpnorm/maximum. Second, the per-component value is only the basis-dependent diagonal of the covariance tensor $R_{ij} = \langle u'_i u'_j\rangle \in \operatorname{Sym}^2(V)$ — the coordinate-free second central moment, the primes marking fluctuations about the mean — and $\operatorname{std} = \sqrt{\operatorname{var}}$ has no canonical vector form at all: the matrix square root of the covariance is not a standard deviation, the per-component root is basis-dependent, and $\sqrt{\operatorname{tr} R}$ is a scalar RMS — three different objects. And var-as-covariance could not even be uniform (a vector's covariance has tensor order 2, a tensor's order 4; the result would be an order-4 fiber value, not a section, so the $R < 3$ section ceiling is not what stands in the way — FiberTensor4 exists as a type. The actual limit is narrower: the fiber assembly in FiberSections (_fiber_type/_assemble_fiber) is enumerated through order 2, so an order-4 result has nowhere to be built. The cov reduction is therefore defined on vector sections alone, which is where its consumer is).

    So var/std are now scalar-only — the one representation where each is unambiguous — physical and spectral (the Parseval form, equal to the physical value; the spectral one-pass loses precision to cancellation when the mean dominates its fluctuations, which the docstring notes). A vector or tensor section raises a clear error pointing to cov. The new cov (extending Statistics.cov; not re-exported at the time, like mean/var/std — all four have been re-exported by the umbrella since 2026-08-13, per the naming rule's export policy in §2) returns the symmetric covariance FiberTensor2 of a vector section — its diagonal the per-component variances (the normal stresses, in a turbulence reading), its off-diagonal the covariances (the shear stresses) a per-component var discarded — computed in one fused mapped pass as $\texttt{mean}(u \otimes u) - \bar u \otimes \bar u$ (reusing decision 48's map! machinery through the mapped mean), Bessel-scaled. It is physical-only (the outer product is nonlinear). The 206/122 parity baselines are unchanged — these are Statistics extensions, outside the M2 + M3 surface.

  50. The separable kernels are stored as their per-axis factors, not as fields (2026-07-28). translation_kernel and assignment_window build a kernel that factors over the axes, $\hat g(k) = \prod_a g_a(k_a)$, and both already computed the three one-dimensional factors internally before materializing their product into a full field on the last line. That last line is now gone: both return a SeparableKernel holding the three factors, and the product is evaluated one mode at a time inside the pass that consumes it.

    The saving is a representation change with no numerical content — every result is bitwise identical to the materialized kernel, at every tensor order and on both the overwrite and the $\alpha$/$\beta$ branch, which is what the tests assert against the stored product as oracle. What changes is the cost: three $O(N)$ arrays in place of $O(N^3)$ coefficients (5 KiB against 16.2 MiB at $128^3$, ~10 KiB against 129 MiB at $256^3$), and a pass that reads no field-sized kernel stream — the three $O(N)$ factors are read per mode, so a kernel application drops from three field-sized streams to two.

    Fewer streams did not hand back a free speed-up, and finding out why fixed the implementation. Applying the kernel is bandwidth-bound, so the saved stream is worth having only if evaluating the product costs less than the read it replaces, and how the product is indexed decides that. Handing the lazy product to the linear-indexed fiberwise executor makes every element convert a linear index to a cartesian one — an integer division, which a GPU emulates — and that measured 0.29× a stored kernel at $256^3$. A hand-written three-dimensional kernel removed the conversion but lost as much again to warp fragmentation, because the folded axis is $N/2 + 1$ — 129 at $256^3$ — so its rows do not align to a warp (0.55× on a scalar field, worse than doing nothing). What works is the plainest form: one flat broadcast over the whole section, the per-axis factors carrying singleton axes so they extend along the remaining mode axes and along the fiber. Broadcasting indexes them cartesian and keeps the launch flat and coalesced. Measured against the stored kernel at $256^3$ on an RTX 5070 Ti: 1.37× (scalar) and 1.00× (vector) in Float32, and 0.96× / 0.77× in Float64 — the Float64 shortfall being the extra complex multiplies against a consumer card's 1/64-rate FP64, which full-rate production hardware does not pay. The lesson is the general one for this package: reach for the broadcast route before a hand-written kernel, and measure the launch shape, not only the arithmetic. Decision 28's Nyquist treatment survives unchanged and unexamined, because the per-axis factors are produced by exactly the code that was already correct.

    The mechanism is that a kernel's contribution to a fused pass is fetched through _kernel_data, which yields a stored array for a kernel held as a field and an instantiated Broadcasted for a separable one. Both the broadcast route and the linear-indexed fiberwise executor consume that lazy product exactly as they consume an array — verified including inside the KernelAbstractions device kernel — so no operator body changed to accept one; the kernel arguments merely widened to a union of the two storage classes. That widening initially reached only within Convolution — every operator arity outside it stayed hard-typed to a stored kernel, so a SeparableKernel threw a MethodError at helmholtz_projection!, divergence! and weighted_inner. The module-placement panel of 2026-07-28 caught the overclaim and the gap: the kernel protocol (AbstractSpectralKernel, the _kernel_data and _check_kernel_compatibility generics, the SpectralKernel union) now lives in Fields, the one module every kernel-applying module can see, and the arities are genuinely widened package-wide — pinned by a test that walks every kernel-taking entry point and requires the two storage classes to agree bitwise.

    Two operations come with the type. Composition stays inside the family (the factors multiply axis by axis) — but composing two translation kernels is deliberately not the same operator as one kernel for the summed displacement, because at an even axis's Nyquist the real factor $\cos(\pi h_a/\Delta x_a)$ obeys $\cos a\cos b \ne \cos(a+b)$; displacements compose by addition, which is the more correct route as well as the cheaper one. And inv gives the reciprocal kernel, separable because $1/\prod_a g_a = \prod_a (1/g_a)$ — the deconvolution an assignment window exists for, which retires the one place the point-density recipe reached into kernel storage.

    Deliberately not done here: a general SeparableSymbol(σ₁, σ₂, σ₃) closure. Per-axis factors supplied as functions of $k_a$ would be silently ambiguous about which wavenumber family they are handed — the true $|k_a|$ that keeps its Nyquist value, or the derivative $k_a$ that is zeroed there (invariant 5) — which is precisely the trap decision 28 documents. Storing the factors, built by the package, keeps that choice where it was already made correctly. The radial fragment $\sigma(|k|^2)$ is a separate closure-shaped escalation and is not this decision.

  51. The radial kernel: a symbol of $|k|^2$, evaluated per mode (2026-07-29). The escalation decision 35 recorded and the consuming solver GBGHomogeneousTurbulence's lazy-per-mode-symbols.md requested. The builders spectral_cutoff and spectral_band no longer build a field; they return a RadialKernel holding the symbol $\sigma$ itself, and radial_kernel(σ, multipliers) builds one for any other symbol — a smooth filter envelope, a hyperviscous $\exp(-\nu(|k|^2)^p\tau)$, an integrating factor. The kernel costs a closure and three references against $O(N^3)$ coefficients, and the builders' signatures are unchanged, so no call site moves.

    Why the family is closed with no case analysis: $|k|^2$ is real and even in every component, so any real $\sigma$ of it is real and even too, hence Hermitian-symmetric — the coefficients are always some real field's. It also carries the true squared wavenumber at an even axis's Nyquist (invariant 5), which is the value a magnitude symbol wants there. This is exactly the argument decision 50 found unavailable for a separable closure, where per-axis factors are ambiguous between the two wavenumber families; the radial case has one family, so the argument that killed that closure does not carry over.

    Storage is a symbol, not a table, because a radial kernel has no cheap middle. An axis-factored kernel wins by evaluating its transcendentals $3N$ times and multiplying per mode; $|k|^2$ is a function of the sum of the squared axis wavenumbers, so a general $\sigma$ of it does not factor and there is no per-axis table to precompute (the exponential does factor, and is why exponential_convolve! is its own operation rather than a radial_kernel call) — only the closure ($\sigma$ evaluated per mode, on a $|k|^2$ that is itself already lazy over three stored $O(N)$ axis arrays) or a full field. Which wins follows the crossover of PERFORMANCE.md finding (o) plus the symbol's own cost: a cheap $\sigma$ (a mask is one comparison) or one whose parameters change per call — the integrating factor, whose substep varies every stage — favours the closure decisively, since a stored kernel must be rebuilt and re-read; a fixed expensive $\sigma$ applied many times can favour materializing, which materialize_kernel provides.

    Composition follows the families. Two radial symbols multiply into one radial symbol (refined by decision 61: unconditionally so only when both operands carry canonical multipliers of the same concrete type; any other radial pairing keeps both operands); a radial and an axis-factored kernel do not collapse, so convolve returns a ProductKernel holding both and multiplying their per-mode values — the cross-family composition that lets a mask and a displacement apply in one pass. Only a compressed kernel may lead: convolve(stored, compressed) stays "apply a kernel to this scalar field", since a ScalarFieldSpectral is also a field. (Superseded by decision 60: a field is no longer a kernel, so the lead-argument rule is gone — both orders apply and agree bitwise, and composing two kernels is unambiguous by type.) weighted_inner accepted a stored or radial kernel as a weight and deliberately not a separable one — superseded by decision 62, which found that restriction to be no constraint at all and widened the weight to every class. The reasoning recorded here was already the third attempt at the question and still short of the mark: it correctly called the exclusion scoping, not validity (a real separable $w$ gives a well-defined $\langle f, w g\rangle$, so invariant 1 never reached it) but still supposed each admitted class would cost its own fused reduction path, which measurement disproved — every class already agreed bitwise through the one pass. The two rationales it was correcting were wrong outright: that separable factors are not real in general, contradicted by assignment_window's $\operatorname{sinc}^2$; and that a magnitude-dependent weight does not factor per axis, which argues about the radial class that was accepted rather than the separable one that was not.

    One implementation trap, found on the device and worth the sentence: a symbol closure must capture values, never a type. The spectral_band builder first captured T to write one(T)/zero(T) in the body, which makes the closure hold a Type{Float64} — not a bitstype, so a GPU launch rejects it outright. Capturing the two values instead fixes it, and the radial_kernel docstring states the rule.

No guard is still the right answer, for decision 59's different reason. Marked here because this belief deferred the defect for a day.) Rather than ship a check that might break a measured, load-bearing property, the contract is now stated precisely in the docstring and the failing case is recorded here.

    A crash the audit did not find turned up while testing its alias claim. eachindex of a non-contiguous view is CartesianIndices, not a range, and the fused executor's partitioner had a method only for ranges — so an operation on a view-backed destination threw from inside the scheduler at eight threads and succeeded at one. Storage is a generic AbstractArray by invariant 6, so that is a legitimate operand, and thread-count dependence is precisely what test/threading.jl exists to catch. It was invisible because every fixture in that file is backed by a plain Array — the fixture-blindness of decision 52, in a new place.

    The generalizable lesson is about the denominator again. The operation materialize_kernel was missing for an exported kernel class while every completeness ledger read it as covered, because the ledgers count operation NAMES and the generic had methods. The fix that matters is not the method; it is that the sweep now takes the subtype set, derived from the module namespace, so a new class fails until it is given a specimen.

  57. StoredKernel — the kernel role, made explicit where it matters (2026-07-30, user-decided). A ScalarFieldSpectral serves two roles: a field in spectral representation, and the storage class for a kernel held as one coefficient per mode. That doubling is what the SpectralKernel union exists to express, and for applying a kernel it is exactly right — the coefficients are the coefficients, whatever the object means.

    It stops being right for operations whose meaning depends on the role. Decision 56 added inv on a stored kernel so a mixed compressed/stored product could be inverted, and in doing so defined the coefficientwise reciprocal of a field in spectral representation — which is a nonlinear operation on a field, exactly the class invariant 1 excludes there. The value returned is not wrong; the method's existence is, because a user writing inv(f) for a field gets an operation the spec says the package does not have.

    The fix is a marker rather than a restriction, because the ambiguity has a natural resolution: passing a field as a kernel argument already declares the role. So StoredKernel(f) states it where a caller means it, inv is defined on the marker instead of on the field type, and composition wraps automatically — convolve(compressed, f) yields a product whose stored factor is a StoredKernel, so a mixed product still inverts with nothing asked of the caller. Application is untouched: the union still accepts a bare field everywhere a kernel is taken, so nothing that worked before needs a wrapper. (Superseded by decision 60, which is precisely where that stopped being true: a bare ScalarFieldSpectral no longer dispatches as a kernel, so code that passed one now does need StoredKernel — the compatibility this entry preserved was given up deliberately, for the type to carry the role.)

    Two options were weighed and rejected. Restricting inv to the compressed classes was cheapest — the only consumer of the stored method is the mixed-product path — but it leaves a kernel that exists only as coefficients, read from HDF5 or measured, with no inverse at all. Documenting the role and keeping the method was cheaper still, and the reason it loses is worth stating: the risk there is not a user harmed but the invariant eroded, and "the public method set is the spec" is only true while the method set is kept honest.

    The marker is also the shape the kernel protocol wants long-term. With a stored class that is a real type, the SpectralKernel union could collapse to the abstract supertype and every kernel-taking entry point would take one type. That is a breaking change and is not made here; it is now a one-step move rather than a redesign. (Superseded by decision 60, which made exactly that move: SpectralKernel is now an abstract supertype and every entry point names the one type, so the union this entry reasons about no longer exists.)

    The subtype sweep of decision 56 earned itself immediately: adding the class failed that testset until it was given a specimen, which is precisely the moment to notice a new class needs the class-wide methods.

  58. The context-mismatch matrix — a derived denominator for the negative surface (2026-07-30, user-directed). The suite already held ~94 negative assertions before the audit, so the gap was never "no negative tests". Every one of them was written by hand, which makes a negative surface complete only until someone adds an argument slot — and the audit's source findings were almost all SEMANTIC mismatches, structurally indistinguishable from valid input, which is exactly the class nobody thinks to write a case for.

    Decision 56 closed those findings but closed them the same way: contracts.jl is 72 assertions and two loops, a hand-picked table. The instruction here was to generate all the combinations rather than choose representatives, and the design that achieves it is two tiers, each with a denominator read out of the code.

    Tier 1, the protocol product. The participant set is methods(_context_grid), and every ordered pair is checked against every mismatch axis in both argument orders. Adding a participant adds a row and a column; adding one without a builder fails a completeness assertion rather than silently sitting out. Tier 2, the entry-point product. Every method the package owns is scanned for a context-carrying parameter, giving (operation, carrier-kind) pairs — the right unit, since α/β variants of one call reach one check. Each pair must be invoked or exempted with a reason.

    The two are not redundant, and the mutations prove which is which. Neutering the shared predicate fails Tier 1. Removing one entry point's check while the predicate stands leaves Tier 1 entirely green and fails Tier 2. A predicate nobody calls and a caller with no predicate are different defects, and one tier sees each.

    The derivation caught seven combinations the hand-written table had missed, which is the whole argument for deriving it. Five were kernel arities — convolve!, divergence!, helmholtz_projection!, combine!, weighted_inner — invisible because a slot declared as the SpectralKernel UNION is a subtype of neither of its halves, so a plain <: test walks past it. Two more were the allocating kernel forms, invisible one level further up: SpectralKernel{T} is a UnionAll wrapping that union, so it is not even isa Union. (Superseded by decision 60: the union became an abstract supertype, so a plain <: test now finds those slots — the derived denominator this entry builds is unchanged, but the invisibility it works around is gone.) Both misses were in the detector, found only because the completeness assertion compares what it derived against what was written by hand and complains in both directions. A one-directional check would have reported success.

    The axis worth naming is domain: two grids of identical shape and different extent. Every array is the same size, every structural check passes, and only a context comparison can tell them apart — which is why the operations that lacked one returned plausible wrong answers for months rather than failing.

  59. A section owns its storage, which is what makes invariant 9 true (2026-07-30, user-decided). The exhaustive suite's first lane crossed a shared storage pool over every in-place operation and found fourteen that returned a wrong answer, silently, across three execution routes. The cause is one sentence: a fused pass reads element $n$ and writes element $n$, which is safe when the destination IS an input and unsafe when one section's element $n$ is another's element $n + m$. Two overlapping views at different base offsets are exactly that.

    Three fixes were available and two were wrong. A mightalias guard on every pass would work but puts a per-call test on paths decision 39 measured as guard-free, and leaves invariant 9 needing a caveat forever. A contiguity requirement looks right and is not: reshape(view(flat, 2:end), …) is densely strided, overlaps another dense view at an offset, and still corrupts — verified, not reasoned. Contiguity is the wrong property; ownership is the right one.

    So the constructors reject storage with a SubArray anywhere in its parent chain. Two sections can then share memory only by being backed by the same array, which is the case the passes already handle, so the class is not guarded against — it is unconstructible. The check is on ownership rather than layout, so Array, CuArray, ROCArray and a reshape of any of them all pass and invariant 6 is untouched. Note that isa DenseArray would NOT have served: it is false for a contiguous host view and true for the identical CUDA construction, so it answers differently per backend.

    The cost is one foreclosed pattern: a consumer keeping solver stages in one buffer and slicing view(stages, :, :, :, :, k). Those slices are contiguous and disjoint, hence safe, and they are now refused. Accepted deliberately, on the principle that relaxing a restriction later is safe and tightening one is breaking — if a consumer needs it, permitting views that are full in the three base dimensions is a small non-breaking follow-up, and that class provably cannot produce a base-index offset.

    Two claims made while investigating this were wrong and are recorded so they are not repeated. "A view-backed field cannot be transformed" — false; only the allocating to_spectral rejects one, while to_spectral! accepts it, which is why views sat in the worst possible state: working for the algebra, working for one transform, failing for the other, and corrupting under overlap. And "a mightalias guard would reject sym!(A, A)" — false; the component views for target $(i,j)$ and source $(j,i)$ select disjoint memory, so mightalias is false on them. That mistaken belief is why the defect was deferred for a day.

    The invariant now says what is true rather than what was hoped: aliasing is safe by construction because a section owns its storage. The survey in test/exhaustive/ checks both halves — that no assignment changes an answer, and that the refusal has not reopened — and the second is mutation-verified: removing one constructor's check makes the lane name every reopened case and turn its exit from 0 to 2.

  60. A field in spectral representation is not a kernel (2026-08-03, user-decided). SpectralKernel was a union of ScalarFieldSpectral — a scalar field carrying one coefficient per mode — and an abstract AbstractSpectralKernel covering the compressed classes. Three things were wrong with that. The abstract type read broader than the union while being strictly narrower, so its name misled at every signature. A StoredKernel marker had to exist purely so inv could tell a kernel from a field, since the two hold identical data. And convolve became asymmetric in a way commutativity forbids: convolve(field, kernel) applied the kernel while convolve(kernel, field) composed, because argument position was the only thing left carrying the role.

    The union is gone. SpectralKernel{T} is now the abstract supertype of four concrete classes — StoredKernel (one coefficient per mode), SeparableKernel (per-axis factors), RadialKernel (the real function $\sigma$ of $\lvert k\rvert^2$, held as a closure), and ProductKernel (two factors held unmultiplied). Every signature naming kernel::SpectralKernel{T} is unchanged, materialize_kernel returns a StoredKernel, and both argument orders of convolve apply and agree bitwise at every tensor order. Composing two convolution operators is convolving their kernels, so composition keeps the same verb and is now unambiguous by type rather than by position.

    A field of coefficients is wrapped once, where the kernel is built. Measured at $64^3$, the wrapper costs 144 bytes at construction and zero per call, with _kernel_data inlining to a getfield — so the cost objection to requiring the wrapper does not exist. Two package methods went (Fields 105 → 103, refrozen with the reason), and the materializing squared_wavenumber_field was removed: $\lvert k\rvert^2$ is a radial function, so radial_kernel(identity, multipliers) is the same object at a closure's cost rather than $O(N^3)$ coefficients.

    The rename's one hazard was caught by the pass audit and by no other layer. A guard reading kernel isa AbstractSpectralKernel MEANT "compressed"; renaming made it true for the stored class too, routing it off the fused pass — vector traffic 3M reads against a frozen ideal of M. Every correctness oracle passed throughout, because the answer stayed right and only the traffic changed. That is exactly the hole decision 36 built the element-traffic ledger to cover.

  61. A radial kernel's coordinates are part of its type (2026-08-04, user-directed). The product of two radial symbols is a radial symbol — always, mathematically. Yet convolve(::RadialKernel, ::RadialKernel) returned RadialKernel or ProductKernel depending on an === test of the two operands' multiplier objects, so inference saw a union and the result type turned on object identity, which is invisible at a call site. The exhaustive survey reported it as a type-stability finding in every configuration it built.

    The conservatism was not wrong. A composite keeps ONE multiplier set, so collapsing two kernels that evaluate their symbols against different wavenumbers would silently apply the second symbol at the first's $\lvert k\rvert^2$. Equal grid and layout do not rule that out, because the six-array FieldSpectralMultipliers constructor validates the arrays' LENGTHS and not their values — the escape hatch a sheared, time-dependent (Rogallo) coordinate set would use, which §12 keeps consumer-side. So the type RadialKernel did not carry the guarantee its name implies, and the runtime check was compensating for that at every composition.

    The fix puts the guarantee in the type. FieldSpectralMultipliers gains a final Canonical type parameter recording whether its arrays were derived from the grid (the convenience constructor) or supplied by a caller (the six-array form). Two canonical sets of the same concrete type, on a matching grid and layout, hold identical values by construction — same derivation, same inputs — so the collapse is unconditional there and is selected by dispatch. Every other pairing, including a custom set on either side and two canonical sets of different array types (different backends), keeps both operands. All four cases now infer a concrete type, and the parameter goes last so that every bare ::FieldSpectralMultipliers annotation and the one {T} accessor are untouched.

    One behaviour change, and it is the one the mathematics asks for: two separately-built canonical multiplier sets used to give a ProductKernel and now collapse to a RadialKernel. They hold equal values, so the collapsed form is correct; the old answer was merely conservative. The lane in test/contracts.jl asserted the conservative behaviour and now asserts the collapse, alongside a custom set reaching the retained form — the canonical-versus-custom axis, added to docs/TESTING.md §4 in the same change.

    The type stability is the smaller half of this. A composition happens once, outside any loop, and a two-wide union costs nothing measurable there. What the change buys is that RadialKernel now means what it says.

  62. Any kernel class may weight a reduction, and a complex weight is refused (2026-08-05, user-directed). The reduction weighted_inner(f, g, w) — the multiplier-weighted inner product $\langle f, w g\rangle$ of decision 27 — took its weight as Union{StoredKernel, RadialKernel}, two of the four storage classes a kernel comes in (decision 60). It now takes any SpectralKernel.

    The restriction was never a constraint. Every class implements the _kernel_data protocol as an instantiated broadcastable — coefficients for the stored class, a lazy per-axis product for the separable one, a lazy symbol evaluation for the radial one, a lazy product of two for the composite — and the fused reduction never asks which class it holds. Running its body unchanged against the two excluded classes gave results bitwise identical to the accepted route, before any code was changed. The Union was a signature written narrowly and never revisited.

    Two rationales for it were recorded over time, and both were wrong. The first said a separable kernel's factors are not real in general — contradicted by assignment_window, whose $\operatorname{sinc}^2$ factors are real. The second said a magnitude-dependent weight does not factor per axis, which is a true statement about the radial class that was accepted and says nothing about the separable one that was not. Both are now recorded beside the code as dead, because the failure mode here is inventing a third: when a restriction has no reason, each attempt to state one produces a sentence that is locally plausible and describes something else.

    A complex weight now throws. $\langle f, w g\rangle$ is an inner product only for a real $w$, and the previous code took real(w) elementwise — so translation_kernel, the one complex builder, would have been silently accepted with its phase read as a cosine attenuation. The rejection is made where the type decides it, through Base.Broadcast.combine_eltypes on the kernel's data (plain eltype of an unmaterialized Broadcasted is Any and does not infer). The branch is on a compile-time-known element type and folds away: measured at $128^3$, old-versus-new ratios are 1.001 (radial), 0.997 (separable) and 1.000 (stored) — free, which was the condition set for adding it at all.

    StoredKernel is the one exemption, and it is a genuine one. Its storage is Complex{T} by construction, a spectral field's being complex, so its type cannot distinguish a real multiplier from a complex one — a materialized spectral_cutoff and a materialized translation_kernel have the same type. Separating them needs a full extra pass over the coefficients, which this reduction's single-pass contract does not permit, so that class keeps the documented real-part behaviour. The rule is therefore the check is made wherever the type can decide it, which is a statement about what is knowable for free rather than an inconsistency to tidy away later. Settled by the user (2026-08-05): the exemption stands and the $O(N)$ scan is declined. Reaching that path takes a deliberate act — hand-building a stored kernel's coefficients, or explicitly materializing a complex one — and a consumer who does either knows what they are doing; paying a full coefficient scan on every call to protect a deliberate act is the wrong trade.

    Covered in test/reductions.jl (each class bitwise-equal to its own materialized form over $R = 0, 1, 2$ in the tensor order $R$; the complex refusal on the separable class, on a composition carrying it, and the stored exemption), pinned allocation-free for all three newly admitted classes in test/allocations.jl — the assertion that would catch a widening which quietly reached for materialize_kernel — and given device parity in test/gpu/suite.jl, where the composite class nests two lazy Broadcasteds and is the case that would resurface the instantiate this reduction already needed for AMDGPU's mapreducedim!. Both tests were verified by mutation: disabling the realness check, and dropping a factor from the separable kernel's data, each fail the suite. The Reductions module baseline moves 119 → 123 — the widening itself is a signature change rather than new methods, and the four are the two internal helpers it needed, at two methods each.

  63. Storage families are crossed by adapt, the ecosystem's verb, and precision is not crossed at all (2026-08-10, user-directed). Invariant 14 makes every operation refuse a pair of sections that disagree in storage family, rather than promoting or transferring one to meet the other. That refusal was always half a design: it names what the package will not do implicitly without offering the explicit operation that does it. The complement has now shipped, and it is not a new verb.

    The interface is Adapt.jl's adapt. Adapt exists for exactly this — take a struct holding arrays, return the same struct holding another array type — and CUDA, AMDGPU, oneAPI, Metal, GPUArrays and KernelAbstractions all speak it. The package defines adapt_structure for the six field types, the three point-value types, PointSet, FieldSpectralMultipliers, the four spectral kernel classes and FieldTransformPlans, and re-exports adapt so using GBGPeriodicFields supplies it. Every method rebuilds through the same allocator the rest of the package uses, so the grid, the Fourier layout, the origin and the tensor order propagate by construction rather than by being remembered — the defect that made map drop the layout on every permuted fold (decision 48).

    A package-owned verb was considered and declined. It would have been a pure synonym: with precision excluded, adapt(CuArray, u) takes exactly one parameter, and a second spelling is a second thing to keep consistent. The decisive argument is a dependency one. Because adapt_structure dispatches on a generic target, CUDA supplies the CuArray storage rule and AMDGPU the ROCArray one, so the package gains both vendors without depending on either — preserving the rule that vendor stacks stay test-only. A hand-rolled convert_storage would need that same generic dispatch and would be adapt under another name. Adapt itself is a dependency-free package already reachable through KernelAbstractions, and pure Julia, so it is safe for the aarch64 target of decision 37.

    Precision is deliberately excluded, and the reason is structural as well as prudential. The prudential half is cu, which silently demotes Float64 to Float32: a transfer that changes what a solver computes is the anti-pattern invariant 14 was written against. The structural half is that precision is not the same kind of operation at all — Grid3D{T} stores the domain size, spacing and origin as NTuple{3, T} and a field demands its grid share its element type, so changing precision must rebuild the grid, where changing storage touches only the array. It remains recorded future work.

    Transform plans are rebuilt, and they need nothing from the caller. An FFT plan is bound to its backend and cannot be moved. It was first thought that adapting one would have to refuse and send the user to the constructor — wrong, and the question that exposed it was whether a plan requires both representations. It does not: the constructor reads no data, only shape, element type and family, and a plan outlives the arrays it was planned against (verified on FFTW and cuFFT). A FieldTransformPlans stores its own grid and layout and carries T (its element type) as a type parameter, so it can allocate its own witnesses, plan, and release them. (It also carried R, its tensor order, when this was written; decision 76 removed it — a transform is a property of the base, so one plan pair now serves every rank and the rebuild uses order-0 witnesses.) The struct now also records the planner request it was built with, because a rebuild would otherwise fall back to ESTIMATE — which finding (h) of docs/PERFORMANCE.md records as capable of badly mis-planning a single-precision transform. Planner flags are an FFTW concept and a device rebuild ignores them (cuFFT rejects them with an unrelated-looking pointer error) while retaining them, so a later move back to the host restores the original intent.

    Adapting to the family one is already in is the identity, following Adapt and convert: it returns the same object, sharing storage. That is why the gate drives the metadata assertions with a custom adaptor mapping a host array to a distinct host array — adapting to Array would exercise nothing. The GPU suite's three hand-written device-twin helpers were deleted in favour of the shipped interface, so the suite can no longer pass by testing a private imitation of it.

    The derived per-module baselines move (decision 53's protocol, refrozen with the reason in the same commit): Fields 103 → 115 — four adapt_structure methods and the eight internals they need (_storage_prototype, the two three-method order-dispatch families, and _is_canonical) — Convolution 79 → 83 for one method per kernel class, and Points 207 → 209 for the point-value and PointSet methods.

    A pre-existing limitation surfaced on the way, unrelated to this change: cuFFT rejects any non-strictly-increasing FFT region, so a permuted RealFourierLayout (decision 41) cannot be planned on CUDA at all — only the default $(1, 2, 3)$ fold works there. It is recorded in docs/TESTING.md §4 rather than fixed, since it is a vendor constraint and the permuted folds are a CPU-side generality.

  64. The preserving scratch arity of to_physical! (2026-08-13, requested by the consuming solver GBGHomogeneousTurbulence ("IT") in its third request round). The inverse real transform may consume its input as scratch (the transform input contract above: FFTW destroys it, some device backends happen not to), so the package offered two contracts — the destructive in-place to_physical!(physical, spectral, plans) and the preserving-but- allocating to_physical(spectral, plans) — and the preserving allocation-free member was missing. Every consumer whose spectral coefficients are a state it keeps (a spectral solver's resolved fields, or any field recovered to physical space more than once) was hand-composing copyto! plus the destructive form; the requesting solver did exactly that in two wrappers and a phase-shift chain that recovers twice from one source.

    The new member is a fourth-argument arity of the existing generic, not a new verb: to_physical!(physical, spectral, plans, spectral_scratch) copies the coefficients into the caller-supplied scratch, transforms from there, and returns with spectral bitwise-unchanged and nothing allocated; the scratch's coefficients are undefined on return, exactly as the three-argument form leaves its source — stated that way deliberately, because the destruction itself is backend-divergent and the contract must not be. The arity form follows the house convention for "same operation, more contract" (the kernel-composed operator arities of decision 32, the α/β accumulation forms): the operation is still the transform, and the trailing argument buys the preservation contract, checked by dispatch — the scratch must be a spectral field of the same element type and tensor order, which a keyword could only check at runtime.

    Aliasing is refused, not defined: spectral_scratch sharing storage with spectral would make the copy a no-op and the transform would then consume the very source the argument exists to preserve — a silent contract violation, the worst kind. Same-array is the only sharing the ownership rule of decision 59 leaves constructible, so the one cheap === check on the storage is exhaustive. The copy is a plain copyto! over the storage, deliberately: the price of preservation is exactly one extra read and write of the coefficients, and nothing else — a benchmark row prices it against the destructive form directly.

    The to_spectral! twin is deliberately absent. Not because the forward transform is safe — the input contract records it as backend-divergent too — but because the requesting consumer established that every forward transform's source in its loops is consumable by design, so the twin would be speculative structure — the §2 rule, not invariant 1: as decision 32 says of the identical shape, an invariant-1 absence is a mathematical statement a user can rely on, while this one is merely unbuilt. It becomes a one-method addition the day a consumer needs it. Tested by a bitwise preservation check against the three-argument form's contract, bitwise equality with the allocating form (whose own oracle is the independent DFT sum, terminating the chain independently), the aliasing and context refusals, a zero-allocation pin, and GPU parity; the Fields baseline moves 115 → 116.

  65. SpectralKernel is exported (2026-08-15, from the round-6 documentation panel). The four concrete kernel storage classes — StoredKernel, SeparableKernel, RadialKernel and ProductKernel — were exported while the supertype they share was not, so a user who wanted to write a method or a type annotation against the family had to name GBGPeriodicFields.Fields.SpectralKernel explicitly. That is the type appearing in the public signature of convolve!, of the kernel-composed divergence! and helmholtz_projection!, and of weighted_inner, so the export makes the type public exactly where its methods already are.

    The panel's larger finding was declined, and the reason is worth recording. A hat proposed moving the protocol — the abstract type and the two generics _kernel_data and _check_kernel_compatibility — out of Fields, on the argument that the package had met the same structural problem before and solved it by extracting FiberSections. Three placements were considered against the alternative of leaving it. FiberSections is refused on the architecture's own mathematics: its defining property is base-agnosticism, and a spectral kernel is one multiplier per Fourier mode of a specific grid under a specific layout — base-bound and representation-specific, the horizontal side of the §2 split rather than the vertical one. A dedicated module is refused as disproportionate: the protocol is three declarations with no dependencies and no bodies, where FiberSections was extracted for shared content — the fiber types, _assign!, the whole vertical-algebra codegen. And moving the protocol down into Convolution — which the include order permits, since Convolution and DifferentialOperators have no dependency edge in either direction — is refused on dependency direction: it would make DifferentialOperators and Reductions depend on a heavyweight peer to obtain an abstract type, where today all three depend only on the foundation. A protocol in the foundation is what keeps the consumers independent of one another.

    The placement is also not an accident but the second instance of a recorded convention: FieldSpectralMultipliers was moved into Fields from DifferentialOperators for the identical reason. The shared context object and its compatibility protocol live in Fields; the module that owns the mathematics owns the concrete types and the builders. No method is added, so every parity baseline is unchanged.

  66. The kernel builders drop their reference field (2026-08-15, from the same panel round). The five builders took a reference field in spectral representation and a FieldSpectralMultipliers, where the multipliers already carry everything the reference supplied. Reading the bodies settles it: each used the reference for exactly field_scalar_type, field_grid, field_layout and the compatibility check, and a FieldSpectralMultipliers stores grid::Grid3D{T} and fourier_layout — so the grid, the layout and the element type are all in it. Even the array backend came from the multipliers rather than the reference: the per-axis factors are built by broadcasting over multipliers.squared_wavenumber_*, so the result already lived in the multipliers' storage family.

    The build-time check was itself the proof. _check_multipliers_compatibility compares the scalar type, the grid and layout, and the storage family — which is to say it refused every pair in which the reference could have told us anything the multipliers did not. An argument required to agree with another argument in every observable respect carries no information.

The in-place translation_kernel! had already been written in the target shape, taking the multipliers alone.

Each builder now takes exactly one context-carrying argument, so there is no second context to disagree with at build time and the five move from invocations to exemptions in test/context_entrypoints.jl. The gate moves 6541 → 6523: twenty assertions retire with the five pairs (a matching case and three foreign ones each) and three replace one, the retired call now being asserted to fail loudly rather than resolve.   67. Base.similar is declined for the section allocators (2026-08-15, user-decided, from the same panel round). A hat observed that similar_field and similar_point_values do what Base.similar exists to do for an array-like wrapper — build compatible uninitialized storage from a reference — and that no rationale for declining it was recorded anywhere, unlike the explicit "we own the types, so extending is not piracy" note that justifies extending dot, cross and mean. The observation is fair; the conclusion does not follow, and the names stay.

    The second argument is the problem. Base.similar's four canonical forms are similar(A), similar(A, ::Type{T}), similar(A, dims) and similar(A, ::Type{T}, dims): that positional slot means an element type or a set of dimensions. Here it means the tensor order, carried as a Val{R}, which is neither. Borrowing a name while breaking the convention that gives the name its meaning is worse than a distinct name, which promises nothing.

    And its most idiomatic spelling would be a trap. similar(A, T) is the way a Julia programmer changes element type, while changing element type is exactly what this package must never do implicitly — invariant 14 refuses a mixed-precision pair rather than promoting it, and decision 63 excluded precision from adapt for the same reason. So similar(u, Float32) would read as a request for single precision, which is the one operation the design refuses to perform quietly, and the better the reader knows Julia the more likely they are to write it. It is not even a storage-level operation: Grid3D{T} holds the domain size, spacing and origin at the element type, so a precision change rebuilds the grid.

    Two smaller reasons complete it.   71. reality_residual — the contract the half-spectrum cannot enforce (2026-08-23, requested by the consuming solver at low priority). A real field's spectrum satisfies $\hat f(-k) = \overline{\hat f(k)}$, and the real-to-complex layout exploits that by storing only half the modes — so for most modes the redundant partner is not stored and cannot disagree. Two planes are the exception: the folded axis's zero plane, and its Nyquist plane when that axis has even length, are each their own mirror, so within them both partners are stored and the constraint becomes a relation a caller can break. The reality_residual diagnostic returns the largest discrepancy $\lvert \hat f(k) - \overline{\hat f(-k)} \rvert$ over those planes, zero exactly when the coefficients are a real field's.

    The gap it closes is a promise the type makes but does not guarantee. By invariant 2 a field is a real physical object and the complex array is storage detail, so ScalarFieldSpectral means "the coefficients of a real field" — and nothing enforced the symmetry that makes it true. Every operation downstream is entitled to assume it and all of them keep working when it is false, which is why the failure shows up as spectral diagnostics quietly disagreeing with physical images. The consuming solver had documented the contract as far as it could: "Coefficients written by hand must respect the real-field layout's conjugate symmetry — it is not checked." This is what lets that sentence change.

    It reports rather than repairs, and the repair already exists. A physical round trip is the projection onto a real field's spectrum, since the inverse real transform is exactly that projector — measured: a residual of 0.728 returns to 0.0. So a repairing verb would be an optimization of something reachable today, not a capability, and it would silently rewrite a caller's data at the one boundary where the responsibility is explicitly theirs. The check is $O(N^2)$ — one or two planes — against the round trip's $O(N^3 \log N)$. The table shows that shape rather than asserting it: on the CPU it runs 92 µs → 367 µs → 1.478 ms across $64^3$/$128^3$/$256^3$, about fourfold per doubling, which is a plane's area; on the GPU it is flat at 99–104 µs over the same range, a plane being too small to be anything but launch-bound. The $O(N^3)$ inner beside it goes 85 µs → 204 µs → 1.115 ms.

    A second vendor and a second CPU confirm both halves of that shape (measured 2026-08-23). The RX 7900 XTX is flat at 238–244 µs across $64^3$/$128^3$/$256^3$, the same launch-bound plateau at a different height, and the i7-12700K grows 80 µs → 362 µs → 1.586 ms → 13.258 ms out to $512^3$, fourfold per doubling as the area argument requires. The two profiles cross: at $64^3$ the CPU is about three times faster than the AMD card, and by $256^3$ the card is about six times faster than the CPU, which is what a launch-bound cost meeting an area-bound one looks like. A caller choosing where to run this should read the crossover rather than the peak.

    It does not thread, and threading is not what it would need. The i7-12700K reads 80.2 µs at one thread and 80.6 at eight, 1.586 ms against 1.588 at $256^3$, and at $512^3$ eight threads is 9.7% slower; the 9800X3D is likewise flat — zero scaling on both CPUs at every grid. The pass is written as whole-array reverse, circshift and maximum over one or two planes, so it runs through Base rather than through the package's threaded assignment primitive, and it structurally cannot reach the fixed reduction tree, which lives in a module included after this one. But the binding constraint is that the pass is allocation-bound: it allocates $\approx 6\times$ the plane bytes it reads — three temporaries over each stored plane, from the reversal, the shift, and materializing the difference as the reduction's argument — so a threaded reduce alone would leave the serial allocation in place, which is the likeliest reading of the $512^3$ inversion. The fix that would work is to fuse the mirrored index into one pass, after which threading follows for free. That is worth doing only if the fused form is at least as fast serially, since this is an $O(N^2)$ diagnostic beside the $O(N^3 \log N)$ round trip it exists to let a caller avoid and the GPU, being launch-bound, gains nothing either way. Recorded as a roadmap item rather than settled here.

    Two naming decisions. It is not called symmetrize!, though that is the requester's word and TurTLE's precedent: the package already exports sym, sym!, symmetric_gradient and symmetric_gradient! for the symmetric part of a tensor's fiber, and reusing the word for a relation among spectral coefficients would put one name on two operations acting on different structures. It is named for the reality condition instead, which is the vocabulary convolve!'s docstring and decision 28 already use. And it does not zero the Nyquist planes as TurTLE's symmetrize() does: invariant 5 keeps Nyquist content deliberately, so a violation there is named like any other.

    Verification.   73. Base-space motion: displace! and wrap! — the exponential map of the flat torus, and the canonical representative (2026-08-24, requested by the consuming solver). The solver's Lagrangian layer routed every field sampling through the point layer but performed every trajectory update by unwrapping storage and broadcasting on raw arrays — the only raw-array arithmetic on point-typed data in its tree — because moving the base is not expressible in the fiberwise algebra, which by design maps sections to sections and never moves a point. The gap is real and the operation is canonical: on a flat periodic base whose fiber carries one global orthonormal frame, the tangent space at every base point is canonically the Cartesian $\mathbb R^3$ of the fiber, so displacing a point $x_p$ along vector values $v_j(p)$ held over it, each section scaled by one real number $c_j$, is exactly $x_p \leftarrow x_p + \sum_j c_j v_j(p)$ — the exponential map of the flat metric, which carries a point along a vector and on a flat torus is plain addition, needing no connection choices. The design facts, each argued from that geometry:

    • A named verb, never +. Positions are points of the domain manifold, not vectors; an algebra on PointSet would assert a linear structure the base does not have. The verb displace! is the one operation that legitimately couples base and fiber, and it crosses the position-versus-value role split by name rather than dissolving it.
    • The grid argument selects cover or torus, and the type structure forces that split. A PointSet carries no grid (a deliberate absence recorded on its docstring), so the grid-free arity cannot wrap: it is displacement in the universal cover — the unwrapped torus, where a coordinate keeps growing instead of folding back — which preserves winding — a mean-square-displacement diagnostic needs exactly that. The arity taking a Grid3D is displacement on the torus proper: the reduction to the fundamental domain is fused into the same pass, not a second traversal. Only domain_size and origin enter; the mesh plays no role.
    • wrap! owns the periodic-box subtleties once. The canonical representative lands in the half-open $[o_a, o_a + L_a)$ per axis — origin-aware, which the consumer's own four-line wrap was not (mod(X, L) assumes a zero origin) — with the excluded endpoint folded explicitly (floating-point mod can return the period itself: mod(-1e-20, 2π) === 2π, a pinned test), and the fold testing equality rather than order so a non-finite coordinate passes through non-finite — an x < L test is false for NaN and would silently rewrite a diverged trajectory into a tidy in-box position. The mod function maps an infinity to NaN, so divergence survives as non-finiteness, though not as the same non-finite value.
    • Sampling needs no wrap, and that is now a documented contract with an honest limit. The transfer kernels wrap their stencil indices, so interpolation is invariant under shifting a coordinate by whole periods (tested, at tolerance — the invariance is exact in exact arithmetic and floating-close in practice). It is not "any finite coordinate": at extreme magnitudes the cell-index arithmetic leaves its exact range, and absolute floating-point resolution degrades as coordinates grow — single precision especially — which is why regular canonicalization is load-bearing on a long run rather than presentation-layer hygiene.
    • The coefficients are the operation's definition, not α/β. As with combine! (the recorded invariant-10 exception class), the $c_j$ scale the operands of what the operation is; no trailing α/β pair exists or may be added (the glyph-collision rule).
    • In-place only, one fused traversal. There is no allocating sibling — displacement is motion of an existing base, and a fresh-PointSet variant has no consumer (invariant 1). The $n$-term form reads the positions once, each section once, and writes once, collapsing a $K$-term Adams–Bashforth combination from $K$ broadcast passes to one; the destination may be the source (the fused pass reads an element's inputs before writing its outputs, invariant 9), and a separate destination form serves the predictor step that writes $X^\ast$ beside $X$.
    • Implementation shape. The pass is _assign!-routed (threaded on CPU, materialize! on device, bitwise thread-count-invariant), built by recursion over the term tuple rather than by closures — a closure capturing the element type heap-allocates itself, and an unbounded forwarded Vararg is not specialized, both measured before the recursive spelling brought every arity to zero bytes. The torus arity runs column-wise so each axis sees its extent and origin as plain scalars; the columns are contiguous (SoA), so traffic is still one traversal.
    • Refusals are the package's decision (invariant 14): point count, scalar type and storage family are checked explicitly against the destination — the torus path bypasses the broadcast machinery's own enforcement, and a Grid3D is checked as the non-section partner it is.
    • Verification. An independent raw-array oracle over both element types; the fused torus arity bitwise-equal to cover-then-wrap!; the endpoint, non-finite and origin contracts each pinned by name; GPU parity exact on both arities plus the mixed-family refusals; zero-byte allocation rows for every arity; exact traffic rows (the torus row's counts identical to the cover row's — the fused wrap measured free); four benchmark rows in the transfer class. Points 211 → 226 (five public methods, ten internal), the field surface untouched.

    • Reading revalidates rather than trusts. The parameters go back through the ordinary constructor, so a group asserting an impossible combination ($m > 2n$) throws there instead of yielding a malformed scheme, and the group's schema version, type tag, and required attributes are each checked.
    • An adjacent defect, found and fixed in the same change. The extension's header has always claimed its self-describing attributes are "CHECKED, not merely written", and for read_field they were — but read_points never checked its schema version, so a point group written by a newer version was silently accepted. The version gate is now one shared helper applied by all three read verbs, and the header's claim is true for the first time.
    • Definition of done, with two reasoned exemptions. No GPU parity lane: a scheme is a host-side parameter record, and the persistence layer returns host objects by construction, so there is nothing for a device to disagree about. No benchmark row: the operation writes four attributes, and there is no HDF5 benchmark class to put it in — inventing one to time a constant would measure the library, not the package.
    • Verification. Round trips over nine schemes — both spline families across the parameter region including the $m = 2n$ boundary and the $n = m = 0$ corner, both element types, the CatmullRom alias (which needs no identity of its own: it is LalescuSpline(1, 1) and returns egal to it), DirectDFT, and the parent[name] = scheme sugar — asserted with ===, the strongest available equality, and reinforced by a behavioural check that a read scheme interpolates bitwise identically, so the test still bites if a scheme ever gains state that egality sees and the stored parameters do not determine.   76. The transform plans carry no tensor order (2026-08-26, prompted by a residency request from the consuming solver GBGHomogeneousTurbulence ("IT")). FieldTransformPlans was parameterized on the tensor order $R$, and built one plan pair per rank: a scalar field, a vector field and a second-order tensor field each needed their own. That was never a recorded decision — it was the shape the original construction happened to take — and it is a category error in the terms §2 already sets out. A real Fourier transform on a periodic box is defined on the box; a rank-$R$ field is that transform applied along a fiber. The transform is therefore horizontal, a property of the base, and $R$ is vertical, a property of the fiber. Encoding the second inside the first is the same defect the documentation rules name elsewhere as "a vector space equated with an integer".

    What it cost, in a currency the package does not itself pay. On a GPU backend the wrapper behind plan_rfft allocates a device array per plan, sized to the coefficients that plan transforms. Per-rank plans therefore multiply that array by the rank: IT measured 516 MiB of plan residency against 1158 MiB of working arrays at $256^3$ in single precision, with a further 1161 MiB once a nine-component diagnostic is enabled. The package holds no plans of its own, so the saving accrues entirely to consumers — but the arrangement that causes it is the package's, and so is the fix.

    One pair now serves every rank. The plans are built for a single component (the tensor-order zero shapes), and each transform applies them once per component over contiguous component views, which the fiber-last layout of invariant 3 makes possible: a component of a rank-$R$ field is a contiguous block, exposed by one reshape. FieldTransformPlans{T, PF, PI, G, L, S} no longer names $R$, the transform generics no longer match on it, and the compatibility check was already rank-free — it compares grid and layout, which is exactly what a base-level object should match on (decision 56's context protocol).

    The measured cost is nil or negative ON THE HOST, which is why this could be taken at all — and the device was not measured then. PERFORMANCE.md findings (aj) and (al) later measured it on both vendors: the per-component loop costs $1.11$–$1.61\times$ on a device vector transform at $64^3$ and is flat or faster at every larger grid, so the host verdict below stands and is not a claim about a GPU. Holding planner effort fixed — and that qualifier is load-bearing, see finding (z) and below — a single-component plan runs at $0.98$–$1.04$ of the batched plan at one thread and $0.72$–$1.13$ at eight, i.e. free at $t1$ and a 10–28% win at $256^3$ on eight threads, which is the regime decision 37 names as the CPU tuning target. IT reports the same direction on its own harness.

    The obstacle, and why it does not become a restriction. A component of a rank-$R$ field begins at element offset $(c-1) \prod N_a$, and a transform library may refuse a pointer whose alignment differs from the one it planned for. The two backends ask different questions, so the package answers them differently and _component_offsets_align reports both. FFTW records an alignment class for the real and the complex array alike; the answer there is to plan with FFTW.UNALIGNED, which costs no memory and, measured, 2–65% of throughput — paid only by grids that need it, and no production grid does. The cuFFT library needs only the real pointer aligned to its complex element, which reduces to the real component stride being even, i.e. to the grid not being odd on all three axes; a device has no UNALIGNED flag, so such a grid transforms through a component-sized buffer instead. Both answers are chosen once, at construction, from the grid alone. No grid is refused, which was the alternative considered and declined: every other absence in the package encodes mathematics (invariant 1), whereas "no grid with three odd axes" would have encoded a pointer-alignment rule, and the $\mathrm{odd}$ axes are what invariant 5's even/odd Nyquist split is tested against.

    A measurement trap that nearly reversed the conclusion, recorded because it is cheap to repeat. FFTW_MEASURE is 0, so passing flags = FFTW.UNALIGNED replaces the default rather than adding to it and silently selects MEASURE. A first pass read UNALIGNED as up to $4.3\times$ faster; it was comparing planner efforts, not plan shapes. The constructor therefore composes the flag onto the caller's request (requested | FFTW.UNALIGNED) rather than replacing it. A second pass had one plan of six left on ESTIMATE by an incomplete edit, which read as a $4.6\times$ penalty for the very variant under test. Vary one thing at a time; this is the third and fourth instance the performance notes record.

    A finding that fell out of it and is worth more than this decision. Under ESTIMATE, which is what the package plans with by default, a $256^3$ Float32 forward transform measures 513 ms against Float64's 496 ms — single precision should be roughly twice as fast, not equal. Under MEASURE the same two cells read 108 ms and 180 ms. That is a factor of $4.7$ left on the floor by the default planner effort, it is independent of everything above, and it belongs to PERFORMANCE.md finding (h)'s territory rather than to this entry.

  77. The inverse curl — curl inverted on the solenoidal subspace (2026-08-28, user-requested). inverse_curl!/inverse_curl recover the divergence-free vector field $u$ whose curl is a given $w$, as one coefficientwise pass $\hat u = \mathrm{i} a\times\hat w/\lvert a\rvert^{2}$ with $a$ the derivative wavenumbers of invariant 5 and the $k = 0$ mode written zero.

    • Why it is well posed, and what the choice costs. $\nabla\times u = w$ fixes $u$ only up to a gradient, since $\nabla\times\nabla\phi = 0$; this returns the representative that is itself divergence-free, which is the gauge the operator picks and the one a velocity field satisfies. It also needs a solvable right-hand side — a curl is divergence-free, so $\nabla\cdot w = 0$ is a condition on the input, and a violating $w$ has its longitudinal part annihilated rather than reported. That mirrors inverse_laplacian, which likewise projects a non-mean-free input onto the compatible part instead of refusing it.
    • Both wavenumber families would give the same answer sub-Nyquist; only one is exact. The numerator and the denominator are built from the SAME derivative family, so $(\nabla\times)^{-1}(\nabla\times)$ reproduces a solenoidal field exactly at every mode rather than only on the resolved band — decision 42's argument, one operator over.
    • Route. A one-pass KernelAbstractions kernel with a gated CPU twin, which is what §9's execution-route policy prescribes for a cartesian-coefficient operator reading three components of a mode and writing three. It is therefore alias-safe by construction (all of a mode's reads precede its writes) and needs no guard temporary. The decision-47 dispatch-pair guard counted the new pair and the reachability probe covers it, so a stale CPU-twin arity fails at test time.
    • Deliberate absences. No α/β arity and no kernel-composed arity, matching curl! rather than helmholtz_projection!: there is no consumer for either, and invariant 1 says a combination is added when something needs it, not for symmetry with a neighbour.

  78. The transfer traversal order — locality as an option, and the wrap as a walk (2026-08-30, user-directed). The grid↔point transfer is dominated by where its points read and write, not by the arithmetic on them, and two separate things were costing it.

    • The problem, measured before anything was built. A consumer holds points in the order it seeded them, which bears no relation to the grid, so consecutive work-items touch unrelated parts of the field and every stencil is a fresh set of cache misses. At a $256^3$ grid with $10^6$ points — the regime the consumer runs — visiting the same points in grid order instead was worth $3.1\times$ on the device gather and $3.8\times$ on the scatter. The signature was visible in the baseline before the cause was: the Float64 gather cost $2.0\times$ its Float32 twin at $256^3$ but only $1.26\times$ at $128^3$, which is a field outgrowing cache rather than arithmetic getting harder.

    • An order is a TRAVERSAL ORDER, not a reordering, and that is the whole design. order[i] names the point work-item $i$ handles. Nothing is moved, and each point's result still lands in its own slot. The alternative — permuting the PointSet — was rejected because a consumer holds PointValues indexed alongside its points, and a permutation behind its back desynchronises them silently. It also makes the option purely additive: a keyword on the four point-indexed verbs, no signature changed, and a consumer that ignores it sees nothing. GBGHomogeneousTurbulence is in final development, which made that a requirement rather than a preference.

    • The gather is bitwise invariant under any order, because each output element is an independent sum; the tests assert ==, not isapprox, and a reversed order beside the built one, so the invariance is of the order and not of one order. The scatter's atomic summation order changes, which it already does between runs (§9), so it is compared to tolerance with conservation asserted beside it.

    • Validity is carried by a TYPE, after an adversarial review showed it was only asserted. The first version took a bare index vector and checked its length and storage family. A vector of the right length that was not a permutation was accepted, and every failure it caused was silent: a gather left slots unwritten, so the allocating form returned uninitialised memory as point values; a scatter lost part of the deposited total; an out-of-range entry read and wrote past the end of an array inside @inbounds, segfaulting on CPU and corrupting quietly on device. The check's own error message already claimed the bijection was enforced. PointTraversalOrder now validates once, where the order is built — two reductions and a marking pass, the same work a per-transfer check would do but paid once — so the transfers read a field they can trust. Same shape as decision 59: make the property structural rather than guarded.

    • The order is built by a counting pass, not a sort. Three portable KernelAbstractions/Atomix kernels — key, histogram, place — over blocks of cells, adding no dependency and needing no vendor sort. It costs 0.24–0.27 ms on device at $256^3$ — the figure for the code that ships, the build itself being 0.098–0.13 ms before the permutation validation the adversarial review added — so it repays inside a single transfer; the recorded estimate it replaces guessed 12–24 transfers, having assumed a vendor sort, and the vendor sortperm over the same keys does cost $25\times$ more.

    • Morton was the recorded plan and it LOSES. Plain lexicographic block order beat Z-order on every measured cell, and the literature agrees for this access pattern: Domínguez et al. (2011) report the same result in SPH and name the discriminator — Morton wins where cell access is random, lexicographic where it is a sequential sweep, which a sorted stencil transfer is. Two further candidates were measured and dropped: stability within a bucket changes nothing, so the atomic placement stands, and one bucket per cell is no better while costing $14\times$ more to build.

    • The periodic wrap became a walk. The stencil visits consecutive nodes, so each index is its predecessor plus one — yet every node index came from mod, which LLVM shared down to 32 integer divisions per point per fibre. _wrap_step replaces the division with a conditional zero, keeping three mods per point for the starts. It is bitwise identical to the previous kernel, verified over 48 configurations including a stencil wider than the grid, where the walk wraps repeatedly; only the index arithmetic changed.

    • The walk has one bad corner, and this entry has now described it wrongly twice. It is worth $1.3$–$1.9\times$ on the CPU gather up to six nodes per axis, and on the device it wins on CUDA (24 of 24 cells at or below $1.002\times$) while LOSING $1.74\times$ on the RX 7900 XTX CatmullRom gather at $256^3$ Float64 — the "1.03–1.28x on the device throughout" this entry used to carry is RETRACTED by PERFORMANCE.md findings (aj) and (al). At eight nodes per axis it can also lose on CPU: 4–14% depending on the scheme, only from about four threads, and at $R = 1$ rather than at every rank — $R = 2$ is less affected, and at one thread the walk is 17–22% FASTER. The mechanism recorded here was backwards: mod is the form that VECTORIZES (983–1588 vector instructions against the walk's 133–138) and neither variant spills on the host, so the CPU loss is the walk forfeiting the memory-level parallelism that independent indices keep. On gfx1100 the walk DOES spill for CatmullRom (192 VGPRs, occupancy halved 16 → 8), the device regression's mechanism — finding (aq). The corner is stated rather than fixed, decision 47 forbidding the conditional second body; finding (ad) carries the tables.

    • What the two wrong versions had in common is worth more than either correction: each was measured at one point of a space and written as though it held across it — first at one scheme, then at one thread count. The rule the package already applies to tests, that a fixture must be able to falsify the claim its name makes, applies to benchmarks in exactly the same way.

    • Where it does not pay, which is why it is an option and not a default. At $64^3$ with $10^5$ points everything is launch-bound and building an order for a single transfer loses. On a CPU whose cache holds the whole field there is little locality to win, and the order's own scattered reads of the coordinates cost more than they save. The win grows with tensor order, because a vector field reads three components per point against one coordinate triple.

    • It also grows with the STENCIL, which is the axis the first sweep missed. Across schemes of 2, 4, 6 and 8 nodes per axis the device gather reads $1.86$, $3.39$, $3.84$, $3.69\times$ and the CPU rises to $6.95\times$ at the widest — a wider stencil reads more nodes per point, so there is more traffic for locality to save. Past $256^3$ the schemes separate sharply: at $1024^3$ the default gets $1.21\times$ where an eight-node stencil gets $4.29\times$. The option therefore matters most exactly where a run is most expensive. The measured tables are docs/PERFORMANCE.md finding (ad).

    • The speedups are a property of the INPUT order. Measured across seedings at $256^3$ with $10^6$ points, the same operation reads $12\times$ for uniformly random positions and $0.93\times$ for a lattice in sweep order, with the ordered arm nearly constant throughout — it normalises the access pattern, and what varies is how bad the baseline was. The option repairs a bad order rather than improving a good one, which is the honest statement of when a consumer should reach for it.

    • The signature leads with the grid (point_traversal_order(grid, points)), corrected 2026-08-30 from a points-first first version: the function is TRANSFER infrastructure, so it takes the transfer verbs' argument order (anterpolate(grid, points, …)), not the motion verbs' trailing-grid one (wrap!(points, grid)) it briefly borrowed. The tile_size option was questioned the same day and KEPT on a measurement: the optimal tile tracks the backend, not the interpolation scheme (flat in tile on the device at stencil widths 4 and 8 alike; the CPU prefers coarser blocks for both), and one order serves any scheme and both directions, so a scheme argument would misstate both what the option controls and what the order is valid for.

    • No cadence, per invariant 12. An order describes where the points were when it was built. A consumer whose points move decides when to rebuild it; the package holds no notion of time, and the measured build cost is small enough that "every step" is a defensible default for a consumer to choose.

The exponential kernel $\exp(c |k|^2 [+ s \hat w])$ depends on the mode alone, yet the broadcast route evaluated it per output element — three times per mode for a vector field, nine for a tensor, identical arguments each time. The consumer's codegen report measured that at $\approx 3.5\times$ the shape-identical combine! single-threaded, and it was the pass audit's standing ŵ-extrusion holder.

The user's condition was no regression of any kind, so every variant was verified bit-identical to the committed broadcast over 126 cells — seven arities including the aliased filtered accumulate, three tensor orders, both element types, odd folds — before any performance was even measured.   80. A traversal order is rebuilt in place, so a moving point set stops growing the device pool (2026-09-03, user-directed). Decision 78 shipped point_traversal_order as an allocating builder, and its own docstring closed with the sentence "There is no in-place form yet". Nothing tracked that "yet": a consumer whose points move rebuilds, and each rebuild allocated four arrays — the Int32 result, the counting sort's Int64 bucket keys, the histogram with its exclusive prefix sum, and the Bool marks the permutation check writes — about 15 MiB at $256^3$ with $10^6$ points. On a device those are freed by a collector that runs on host pressure and cannot see them, which is the failure mode invariant 13 exists to prevent, reached through a builder rather than through an operator.

    • The scratch is the caller's, not the order's. point_traversal_order!(order, grid, points, scratch) takes a PointTraversalOrderScratch, built once for a grid, a point count and a tile_size. Two other shapes were considered and rejected. Letting the allocating builder RETAIN the arrays it already allocates would have given the simpler call point_traversal_order!(order, grid, points) at no extra peak memory — but it triples what a build-once consumer holds, and it makes an operation's allocation depend on how its argument was constructed, since PointTraversalOrder(indices) accepts a user-supplied permutation and has no scratch to carry. Sizing the scratch inside the call would have defeated the purpose. The explicit argument follows decision 64's four-argument to_physical!, which is this package's recorded answer to the same question: when an in-place form needs working storage, the caller supplies it and the cost stays visible at the call site.

    • The block layout travels with the scratch, not as a keyword. point_traversal_order! takes no tile_size; it reads the block counts from the scratch it was given. A keyword would have let an order and its histogram disagree about how many buckets exist, which is an out-of-range atomic write rather than a wrong answer — the same class as the Int32 key overflow decision 78 records fixing.

The check is not traded away for the allocation contract: _validate_traversal gained a method taking the caller's marks, so the in-place path runs the identical check against scratch storage.

    • It is not zero, and the docstring does not claim zero. Three KernelAbstractions launches and the validation's own leave about 1.2 KiB of host-side launch bookkeeping — the same class as the FFTW callback of PERFORMANCE.md §4(t). Nothing per-rebuild scales, and nothing per-rebuild is on a device.

    • Renamed to PointTraversalOrder in the same round (user-directed). TransferOrder was the minority spelling of its own concept: the prose said "visit order" 28 times and "transfer order" 7, and the API page section was already titled for the former, so the code name and the documentation disagreed 4:1. Worse, order means tensor order 249 times in this package, and the same source tree held both order = field_tensor_order(...) and order = transfer_order(...) — the keyword read, at the call site, as if it took a rank. And in plain English "transfer order" parses as an instruction to transfer, which is the wrong first reading for this package's audience. Point matches the layer's own prefix family (PointSet, point_positions, point_value_tensor_order), and "traversal" already carries the sense "one pass over the points" here — displace! documents "one fused traversal" — and is the word the cited reference uses, Domínguez et al. (2011) being described in §References as a cell traversal order for neighbour search. The keyword moved with the type, order to traversal, since leaving it would have preserved the exact ambiguity the rename removes. The prose moved too: "visit order" is retired so the concept keeps one name.

    • An adversarial review (2026-09-03) found two claims the code did not keep, and both are now enforced rather than reworded. _check_point_traversal_order refused a foreign-family order and told the caller to "move it with adapt", while no adapt_structure method existed — so Adapt's identity default handed the same order back and the caller met the identical refusal. And the refill's grid check tested that the scratch's block layout FITS inside the grid rather than agreeing with it, so a scratch built on a $16^3$ grid was accepted for a rebuild on a $32^3$ one, silently doubling the block edge while the docstring claimed the layout was checked. The scratch now stores its grid and the refill compares it, which also catches an extent or origin change that a size test never could. Both are pinned in test/point_traversal_order.jl, the adapt one through a probe adaptor, since adapting to the family an object is already in is the identity by contract and cannot distinguish a working method from a missing one.

    • The memory figures moved when this decision landed, and the docstring had kept the old ones. cumsum on an Int32 count array widens to Int64, so the pre-decision path held the running total at twice the counts' width; the scratch allocates it with similar, at the same width. The grid-scaled storage is therefore 2 MiB rather than 3 at the default tile_size on a $256^3$ grid, and 128 MiB rather than 192 at tile_size = 1. The narrowing is safe for the same reason the order itself is Int32: the running total's largest value is the point count, which must already fit an Int32 index.

    • Both forms share one build. _fill_traversal! and _traversal_tiles are called by the allocating builder and the in-place one alike, so the two cannot drift into computing different orders — the failure decision 47 records for a device kernel and its gated CPU twin, in a different place. test/point_traversal_order.jl asserts the agreement block-wise rather than index-wise, because the scatter's atomics leave the order within a block unspecified in both forms; an index-wise comparison would be testing the atomics, not the operation.

  81. The traversal order's running total is threaded on the host (2026-09-04, user-directed). Building a traversal order runs three KernelAbstractions kernels, which thread by KA's own partitioning, and one running total over the block counts, which did not thread at all: it was cumsum!, a serial loop on a host array. Its cost is set by the number of BLOCKS — that is, by the grid and tile_size — and not by the point count, so it does not shrink as the rest of the build is parallelised. Measured on a 9800X3D at $10^6$ points with the default tile_size: at $256^3$ it was 4% of the build at eight threads, and at $512^3$, the grid decision 37 names as the CPU tuning target, 25%.

    • _scan_offsets! replaces the call, and only the host method threads. The generic method is cumsum!, which is already a parallel scan on a device; the host method is a two-pass form — each chunk sums its own slice, the chunk totals are scanned serially, each chunk then writes its slice offset by the total before it. Below a chunk of 4096 blocks, or with threading turned off, it falls back to cumsum!. Measured end-to-end at $512^3$: the build goes 3.41 → 2.91 ms at four threads and 2.85 → 2.26 ms at eight, so $1.17$–$1.26\times$; the scan alone is $2.6$–$7.3\times$.

    • Bitwise equality is a property here, not a tolerance. Integer addition is exact and associative, so regrouping the sums cannot change them and the threaded result equals cumsum! bit for bit — no fixed reduction tree of the kind decision 40 needed for floats. Pinned over sizes bracketing the cutoff and four distributions.

    • The overflow contract had to be carried across deliberately. cumsum! into a narrow integer RAISES on overflow; a scan accumulating in that same width wraps silently, and a wrapped total is a negative offset that the placement kernel would use as an index inside @inbounds — the class decision 78 records fixing. The chunk totals are therefore accumulated wide and the grand total is checked against typemax before the second pass. Both forms are asserted to throw.

    • A data race in the first draft is worth recording, because reading did not catch it. Pass two wrote acc = carry[c] where acc was already bound in the enclosing function scope, so the assignment targeted the OUTER local and every task shared one accumulator. It produced wrong answers and ran 50× slower than the serial loop. It surfaced only because the correctness harness crashed on its first mismatch; the fix is local run = …, and the hazard is that a name reused from an enclosing scope inside Threads.@threads is shared rather than shadowed.

    • What the body carries is decided by what the package REFUSES on, not by what is interesting. _check_field_domain_compatibility rejects a pair differing in scalar type, grid size, extent, origin, layout or storage family. A display omitting those printed two byte-identical strings for the two operands of the very error it existed to explain — measured on four mutually incompatible fields, which rendered as one string. Extent, origin and the fold are therefore shown whenever they differ from the ordinary isotropic $2\pi$ box at the origin in axis order, which keeps the common line near fifty characters while making that collision impossible. The property is asserted directly, over every pair of a family of refused fields.

      The rule binds every base-bound object, not only the fields: an object shows what the check that refuses it compares. A NUFFT keeps its reference grid and layout precisely because a plan handed a same-order field on another domain returns a silently wrong answer; a traversal scratch is refused a rebuild against another grid; a kernel reaches the same domain check through _check_kernel_compatibility.

      All three showed their grid size and nothing else, so all three collided on exactly the pairs they are refused on — found by re-reading rather than by the testset, which covered fields alone when it was written.

    • Coordinates print at full precision, and the predicate that suppresses them is EXACT. Both halves were wrong in a first draft and the second is the instructive one. Rounding to four significant digits reintroduced the collision one axis-scale down, since the grid check is exact equality and boxes differing in the sixth digit are refused. Fixing that alone was not enough: the test asking whether the box was ordinary was ≈(2π), so a box of $2\pi + 10^{-9}$ fell inside the default tolerance and its extent was suppressed before precision could matter. A loose predicate deciding whether to print defeats any precision in the printing. The regression test carries that case.

    • Nothing may read an element. Every quantity is host-side metadata, so a display is safe under allowscalar(false) and forces no synchronisation. A future addition printing a norm, a range or a sample value would break that on the backend where it matters most, so it is prohibited rather than discouraged.

    • A derived quantity is not shown in place of what it derives from. A spline scheme's weight matrix comes from $(n, m)$ — the property decision 75 persists it by — and the default printed 747 characters of matrix and neither number. The constructor call is the honest form and it round-trips, with Julia's parser as an independent oracle. Grid3D was the same case, printing the grid_spacing that decision 45 requires to equal domain_size ./ grid_size.

      The element type appears as the keyword the constructor takes, and only when it is not the default, so both forms round-trip while a Float32 and a Float64 scheme — which invariant 14 refuses against each other — cannot print alike.

    • The gate is DERIVED, in the allocation-audit shape. test/display.jl enumerates every concrete struct type the package defines and requires each to carry a package-owned show or a cited exemption, with completeness checked both ways so a stale exemption fails too. A listed set of types would go stale exactly when a type is added, which is when the check has something to say. Falsified by perturbation before being kept: a planted struct fails it, and so does an exemption naming a type that does not exist.

      Six types are exempt — RealFourierLayout, whose default is already short, unambiguous and round-tripping, and five pass-local callable adapters that are never returned, never stored on a user-visible object, and never interpolated into an error.

    • The work surfaced a defect it was not looking for: three spellings of the storage family. Fields._storage_family took a section and called parent once, Points._motion_storage_family duplicated it for displace!'s refusal, and the display added a third that recursed.

      Two of the three were wrong. The constructors reject only a SubArray, so a reshape of a PermutedDimsArray of a device array is constructible, and one parent call answers ReshapedArray for it — not a storage family at all. So _same_backend could refuse two differently-wrapped views of the same device memory. That is invariant 14's predicate, so unifying them on the recursing form makes a check stricter as well as removing a divergence between a refusal and the display beside it.

  83. reality_projection! — the projector onto real-field spectra, because decision 71's premise holds only to the ulp (2026-09-05, requested by the consuming solver with a measured failure; owner-decided). Decision 71 declined a repairing verb on three grounds: everything the package produces satisfies the reality condition by construction; the repair already exists as a physical round trip; and a repairing verb would silently rewrite a caller's data. The first two are now measured false, and the third is honoured by the design rather than overturned.

    The premise, measured on this fleet's AMD host. The reality_residual of a fresh to_spectral of a random real field is exactly zero for FFTW at $32^3$, $64^3$ and $128^3$ and nonzero at $96^3$ and $256^3$ ($1.3\times10^{-4}$ and $7.4\times10^{-4}$ in Float32 coefficient units), and nonzero for rocFFT at every size ($2\times10^{-5}$ to $8\times10^{-4}$); cuFFT behaves like rocFFT on the consumer's host. So "by construction" is true only for FFTW at some power-of-two sizes, and elsewhere a spectrum leaves the transform carrying an anti-Hermitian part at the round-off floor.

    Why a floor becomes a field. On the folded axis's zero plane both members of a conjugate pair are stored, and their anti-Hermitian combination is invisible to the inverse real transform — so the nonlinear term, formed from physical fields, neither sees it nor feeds it — and is damped only by $\nu\kappa^2$ at $\kappa \approx 1$. Any forcing proportional to the field, $\hat N \mathrel{+}= A \mathbf{1}_{\text{band}} \hat u$, amplifies it with the same coefficient $A$ as the physical modes, which $A$ must hold positive on average to balance the dissipation.

    The consumer measured the net growth at $A - \nu\kappa^2 \approx 0.26$ per time unit: from the Float32 floor the parasitic mode reaches order one at $t \approx 60$, and six seeds died at $t = 66$–$71$ for RK3 and RK4 alike and for both dealiasing rules, Float64 at $t = 142$ — $\ln(10^9)/0.26 \approx 80$ time units later, as the rate predicts. Spectral energy held its target throughout while the physical field's energy, dissipation and maximum speed collapsed; the residual went $4\times10^{-2} \to 1.3\times10^{6}$. Same shape as Wang & Rosa (2009) on the divergence-free constraint: a linear constraint the discretisation does not enforce, a round-off seed, a linear amplifier, and re-projection per step as the remedy.

    What ships. reality_projection!(destination, source) and the in-place reality_projection!(field): on the stored self-conjugate planes — the folded axis's zero plane, and its Nyquist plane when the full axis is even — each pair is replaced by $\tfrac{1}{2}(\hat f(k) + \overline{\hat f(-k)})$ and its conjugate, a self-paired corner by its real part from the same formula, and nothing else is touched. This is the orthogonal projector onto real-field spectra in the Parseval inner product: $\mathbb R$-linear, idempotent — a projected pair is bit-identical under a second application — and commuting with every per-mode multiplier $m$ with $m(-k) = \overline{m(k)}$. FORMALIZATION carries the typed statement.

    Semantics decided with the request: projection only — no Nyquist zeroing, which is a dealiasing policy and the consumer's truncation keeps those planes empty anyway; a mutator returning its argument, no residual returned, so reality_residual stays the checker and the report/repair split of decision 71 stands; and nothing folded into to_spectral!, since a fresh transform of real data satisfies the condition to round-off and the defect lives in state accumulated in place. The two-argument form exists so the twins read alike and costs a field copy before the $O(N^2)$ projection; the in-place form is the cheap one and the one the consumer calls, once per step beside helmholtz_projection! at the step's closing deposit.

    Why the round trip is not the same repair. It costs $O(N^3 \log N)$ — six transforms per step on a vector field — and, measured by the consumer, it resets the residual only to the transform's floor, since the forward transform reseeds it; the projection zeroes it exactly and perturbs no other mode, where the round trip perturbs every mode by round-off. Decision 71's "the repair already exists" named a worse operation for this purpose, not the same one.

    Naming. reality_projection!, pairing with reality_residual: the two verbs act on one condition and share its stem, and "the reality condition" is the term decision 28 and convolve! already use. Declined: real_projection! — the request's word — because it is accurate only at the four self-paired corners, where the result is literally $\operatorname{Re}\hat f$, and reads as real(z) everywhere else; hermitian_projection!, because LinearAlgebra.hermitianpart is the same projection on MATRICES and the package's fibers are matrices, so the name would read as a fiber operation — the collision decision 71 recorded for symmetrize!, TurTLE's word; and real_field_projection!, precise but a rename of the shipped verb for precision it already has.

    Implementation. A decision-38 kernel pair in Fields, beside the residual (one concept, one home): a KernelAbstractions kernel on a device and a gated slab-threaded loop on the CPU, both calling one inlined _project_pair! so they agree bitwise. Each pair is owned by its lexicographically smaller member, so it is touched by exactly one work item and no two items write one element — the pass audit's ideal, every stored plane element read once and written once, holds exactly.

    Indices are mirrored modularly and placed by the layout's folded axis through a Val dispatch, so a permuted fold projects whichever axis is folded and there are no reverse/circshift temporaries — the traversal the roadmap prescribes for fusing reality_residual, now written. Fields joined the decision-47 dispatch guard's scanned modules with its first kernel pair (17 → 18), and its method count refroze 141 → 157 with all sixteen named (nine hand-written, seven generated by @kernel).

    Verification. test/fields.jl: 196 cells per element type — the identity on an exact spectrum, an elementwise pair-mean oracle over the ORIGINAL coefficients, off-plane coefficients bitwise untouched, exact idempotence, agreement with the round trip to $64\varepsilon$, linearity, commutation with the Laplacian symbol, the two-argument form, the domain refusal and the physical absence, over tensor orders 0/1/2, permuted folds and an odd folded axis. GPU parity on ROCm: 491 → 494, the device result exactly Hermitian and bit-equal to the host's. The allocation audit pins both arities, the pass audit both rows at the ideal, and the benchmark gates run the four new rows (192 → 196 CPU, 206 → 210 ROCm).

    CUDA parity is the office host's to run; its count moves too.

    Measured (2026-09-05, idle box; the other 80 operators rows reproduced the day-old archives at medians $0.998$–$1.001$). Scalar Float64 at one thread: 20, 102, 449 and 3140 us across $64^3$–$512^3$, against reality_residual's 80, 362, 1586 and 13258 on the same host — $4.2\times$ faster at $512^3$ while also writing, the fused traversal having no reverse/circshift temporaries. Eight threads bring $512^3$ to 331 us, $9.5\times$. The vector rows run three times the scalar ones at small grids and 14.7 ms at $512^3$ Float64 one-threaded, 4.5 ms at eight. On the RX 7900 XTX every $64^3$–$256^3$ cell sits at 27–48 us, launch-bound, where the residual sits at 238–244; $512^3$ Float32 vector reads 302 us.

    The pass is strided by the layout's nature. The folded axis is the contiguous one, so its zero plane is one element per cache line: $512^3$ scalar moves 16.8 MB in 3.1 ms at one thread, about 5 GB/s, and threading is what recovers it. One observation recorded without a cause: at $512^3$ the Float32 scalar row is $1.04$–$1.29\times$ SLOWER than Float64 on every CPU lane. The archives were not spliced — src/ moved with this decision, which is the case the splice tool refuses — so the rows enter RESULTS.md at the next sweep.

    What decision 71 keeps. The check reports; the projection is an explicit verb the caller invokes; no transform enforces the symmetry — per-operation enforcement would tax every transform for a problem only a multiplicative forcing creates, and a decaying run never needs it. What 71 loses is one sentence, "everything the package produces satisfies the constraint by construction", which was a claim about FFTW at power-of-two sizes and is now stated as such wherever it appeared.

  1. CUDA unified memory is a supported configuration, chosen by CUDA.jl's preference and not by the package (2026-09-17, owner-decided). A consumer runs out of memory on a 96 GiB GH200 at $768^3$ by a few GiB, and CUDA.jl can place every array in unified memory, which the driver pages between the GPU and host RAM. The package needs no code for it: it never names a memory kind, and a CuArray allocated without one takes CUDA.jl's default_memory preference.

    So every allocating route — the family constructors, adapt, similar_field, the multipliers, the kernel builders, the traversal order, the allocating operations — follows the setting, while cuFFT's own workspace, which it allocates itself, stays in device memory. The how-to is one page, Running on a GPU, which covers ROCm too, where AMDGPU has no unified memory at all.

    • Invariant 14 is unchanged. A unified CuArray and a device CuArray are one storage family, because the family is the kind of array and not the memory holding it, and they combine rather than being refused. The definitions that said "host memory, or one vendor's device memory" were wrong under this configuration and now say "the kind of array".

    • The whole process, not per field. Unified memory for some fields and device memory for others is not offered. The family constructors read only a family's element type, so CuArray{Float64,3,UnifiedMemory} yields device memory, and their docstrings now say so; and in a mixed pair the field broadcast takes the result's memory from its first operand, where CUDA.jl's own array broadcast promotes to unified either way. Per-field placement would need both settled, and it is deferred until a measurement on GH200 shows all-unified is too slow to use.

    • What the configuration cannot check. CUDA.jl serves element-by-element access to a unified array through the host and does not honour allowscalar(false) for it, so a unified run is blind to invariant 6. The unified test lane, test/gpu/cuda-unified, therefore adds to the device lane rather than replacing it; it refuses to run when the preference has not taken effect, asserts that every allocating route is unified, and runs a working set larger than the card.

    • What it cannot measure. The benchmark harness decides its per-row GC from CUDA.used_memory(), which does not count unified allocations, so it refuses the configuration outright rather than report timings of the wrong thing. The one measurement taken is a bespoke script on an x86 card (Performance finding (as)); GH200 is unmeasured (Performance §7 gap 9).

    • The preference itself is the fragile part. CUDA.jl 6 reads it inside its CUDACore subpackage, which the running project must name in [extras], and CUDA.jl's own template still shows the section as [CUDA] — silently ignored. The documentation build rejects that spelling, and the test lane's first act is a residency check, because the failure mode is a green run that proves nothing.

  2. Two grids over one box share their low modes, and two verbs move them (2026-09-18, user-directed, after an adversarial naming panel). A consumer forcing a turbulent flow needs content computed on a small grid — a stochastic process on a few dozen modes, or the nonlinear term of a low-wavenumber block obeying its own equations — to reach a large field every stage, and the reverse for the block's input. Nothing in the method set did that: the algebra needs operands of one shape, a kernel multiplies rather than adds, and the point transfer would sum over points at every mode.

    A grid of $N$ points over a domain of length $L$ resolves the wavenumbers $2\pi/L$ times the integers it stores, so two grids over the SAME box name the same physical modes where they overlap, whatever their counts. The verb embed_modes! writes a coarser field's modes into a finer one and extract_modes! reads them back, each leaving every other mode of its destination untouched, at a cost set by the coarse field.

    • Why two verbs for one index map. The directions are different operations: embedding is injective and lossless, extraction discards everything the coarse grid cannot hold, and their scale factors are reciprocal. The deciding argument is a failure mode rather than taxonomy. The motivating consumer calls both directions on the same pair of fields a few lines apart, so under a single verb taking its direction from which argument is coarser, a transposed call would be legal by definition — no assertion could be written against it — and would leave a run silently unforced. With two verbs each wrong argument order is refused from grid metadata before a coefficient is read.

    • Amplitudes are physical. The stored coefficient of a mode is prod(grid_size) times its amplitude (invariant 4), so a transfer between resolutions carries the ratio of the two counts, and the operations apply it. That is what makes the round trip an identity on the shared modes, keeps a grid-size ratio out of the middle of a consumer's equation of motion, and makes the two verbs exact adjoints under inner, with no measure factor — unlike the point transfer's W/Wᵀ, which carries N/M. Raw coefficient copying would be adjoint only up to $(N_c/N_f)^2$.

    • The coarse grid's Nyquist shell is excluded, and that exclusion is what makes the shared set a subspace. A coarse mode at $|k| = N_c/2$ on an even axis describes one sampled wave that the fine grid splits into two modes, so the single coarse coefficient does not determine them. Transferring it would have to choose, and either choice breaks the reality condition or the adjointness. Excluding it costs nothing a de-aliased solver relies on.

    • Refusals are structural, never data-dependent: same domain size, origin, Fourier layout, scalar type, tensor order and storage family, and a coarse grid no larger on any axis and smaller on at least one. Equal grids are refused, because the Nyquist exclusion would make such a call a copy that silently leaves those planes stale, and two fields on one grid combine with add! or combine!.

    • Neither verb has an allocating sibling. The result depends on the destination's prior contents — the modes outside the shared set survive — so an allocating form would have to invent them. The same reasoning as combine! and displace!.

    • The NUFFT was refactored onto these verbs, as the strongest available test of them. Its oversampled work grid covers the same box as the reference with more points, so the reference's modes are a subset of it and the scheme's own _nufft_embed_kernel!/_nufft_extract_kernel! were this operation in private form. They are gone, with _nufft_index_map and the three embed_map_* fields and their type parameter: 57 lines, three device arrays per plan, and a piece of index arithmetic that had to be kept in step with the layout by hand. The scheme is verified exact against DirectDFT, and that equality still holds — which is a test no fixture of this operation's own could give it.

      Two differences had to be paid rather than assumed. The old map carried the reference's Nyquist shell to -N/2 of the oversampled grid where the shared set excludes it, which is consistent with the scheme's documented band but means its scratch must now be ZEROED, since the operation writes only shared modes and similar returns whatever was in memory — the first run after the refactor produced NaN for exactly that reason. And the scale factor moved: the embed site dropped its hand-applied total_oversampling because the operation carries that ratio, while the extract site multiplies by it, the operation dividing where the old kernel did nothing. The NUFFT had been applying the same physical-amplitude convention all along.

    • The names were chosen adversarially, against the alternatives truncate, project, inject, restrict, prolong, pad and a single copy_shared_modes!. truncate and chop are exported by Base and truncate already means de-aliasing in the consuming solver; project is spent here on two genuine idempotent projections; inject reverses direction between audiences and means energy injection in the calling module; restrict reads as this package's word for an invariant-1 absence; prolong/restrict are multigrid's PHYSICAL-space transfer operators and would become a lie the day a physical analogue arrives. The _modes suffix is earned by the same rule as the package's other verb-plus-object names: the bare verb is ambiguous, this package embedding points into cells and coarse spectra into oversampled ones elsewhere.

  3. The Lamb vector is $\omega \times u$ (2026-09-24, owner-decided). For a velocity field $u$ with vorticity $\omega = \nabla \times u$, the rotational form of the Navier–Stokes nonlinearity is $u \cdot \nabla u = \omega \times u + \nabla(\tfrac{1}{2}\lvert u\rvert^{2})$, and the package's documentation calls $\omega \times u$ the Lamb vector. That is the literature's convention: Sagaut & Cambon (2018) name "the Lamb vector $\omega \times u$" (their Eq. 12.2 and the text after it), and Pope (2000) writes the opposite product $U \times \omega$ in the same identity but never uses the name. The opposite sign, $u \times \omega$, is in circulation too, as Wikipedia's article shows, so the name without its sign is ambiguous.

    • What it changed. The CPU example advances the Leray projection $\mathbb{P}$ of the transform of $u \times \omega$, and called $u \times \omega$ the Lamb vector. The equation was right and the name was off by a sign; the prose now says the term is minus the Lamb vector. The code is unchanged, since it computes $u \times \omega$, which enters with the sign the equation needs.

    • How it surfaced. The first documentation review's differential-geometry reader flagged it (finding 004 of 2026-09-22), and the wording changed only after the sign was checked in Pope (2000) and Sagaut & Cambon (2018).
