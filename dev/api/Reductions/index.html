Reductions

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions — Module
Reductions

The L² / measure structure on sections — the third operation family alongside the vertical (fiberwise) algebra and the horizontal (differential) operators (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §2, decision 18). A reduction integrates a section over its base, against the measure the base carries: Grid3D's uniform quadrature (cell volume $\prod_i \Delta x_i$) and PointSet's counting measure (unit weight per point — forced, since a PointSet is grid-free). One set of formulas serves fields and point-values, dispatched on AbstractFiberSection.

Defined on physical fields and point-values — representation-free / physical-space quantities, where the base reduction is the naive one. inner/l2norm additionally act on fields in spectral representation via Parseval (equal to the physical inner, no transform); integral/mean/lpnorm/ maximum stay physical-only.

  • integral(f) — the extensive integral $\int_\Omega f \, d\mu$ (the only quantity that scales with the base measure): $\tfrac{|\Omega|}{N}\sum_x f$ on a grid, $\sum_p f$ over points.
  • mean(f), std(f), var(f) — extend Statistics; intensive and fiber-preserving (mean of a vector field is a FiberVector): the arithmetic average over base elements, $\tfrac1N\sum_x f$ on the grid (the domain average, the grid being uniform) and $\tfrac1M\sum_p f$ over points (a plain sample mean).
  • inner(f, g) — the normalized L² inner product $\langle f, g\rangle = \tfrac1N\sum_x f\cdot g$ → scalar (intensive, matching mean); $\mathrm{l2norm}(f) = \sqrt{\langle f, f\rangle}$, and lpnorm(f, p) the $L^p$ norm ($p = 2$ default, $p = \infty$ ⇒ the sup of the fiber magnitude).
  • maximum/minimum/extrema — extend Base for scalar sections.

Physics stays consumer-side: kinetic energy $\tfrac12 \langle u, u\rangle$, enstrophy $\tfrac12 \langle \omega, \omega\rangle$, dissipation $\nu \langle \omega, \omega\rangle$, rms $\sqrt{\langle \cdot, \cdot\rangle}$, intensities from std.

The Reductions module is the L² / measure structure on sections — the third operation family alongside the vertical (fiberwise) algebra of FieldAlgebra and the horizontal (differential) operators of DifferentialOperators. A reduction integrates a section over its base against the measure the base carries (Grid3D's uniform cell volume; PointSet's counting measure), and is defined on physical fields and point-values (representation-free quantities). See docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §2 and decision 18.

integral is the extensive ∫f dμ; inner/l2norm/lpnorm are the normalized L² inner product and norms. The mean/std/var (from Statistics) and maximum/minimum/extrema (from Base) are extended on sections here — reached through using Statistics / Base, not re-exported. Physics stays in the consumer (kinetic energy ½ inner(u,u), enstrophy, dissipation, rms).

A field in spectral representation also admits inner/l2norm via Parseval (equal to the physical inner, no transform), and radial_spectrum(f, mult) — the radial (shell-resolved) power spectrum: inner decomposed into isotropic |k|-shells, returning (shells, spectrum) with the exact refinement sum(spectrum) == inner(f, f) (decision 21). The consumer's energy spectrum is E = ½ .* spectrum.

Public API

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.inner — Function
inner(f, g)

The normalized L² inner product $\langle f, g\rangle = \tfrac1N \sum_x f \cdot g$ of two compatible sections (inner $\equiv \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$; the full fiber contraction $f \cdot g$ summed over the base, divided by the base count) — a scalar, intensive (matching mean). For homogeneous turbulence this is the natural pairing: kinetic energy is $\tfrac12\langle u, u\rangle$.

Defined for physical fields and point-values (the naive sum above), and for two fields in spectral representation via Parseval: $\langle \hat f, \hat g\rangle$ equals the physical inner of the same fields — the inner product without an inverse transform. lpnorm, integral, mean, maximum remain physical-only (only the $p = 2$ inner is representation-invariant).

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.integral — Function
integral(f)

The base integral $\int_\Omega f \, d\mu$ of a section (integral $\equiv \int\,d\mu$) — extensive (scales with the base measure): the uniform quadrature $\tfrac{|\Omega|}{N}\sum_x f$ on a Grid3D, the counting-measure $\sum_p f$ over a PointSet. Fiber-preserving: a scalar for a scalar section, a FiberVector for a vector section, a FiberTensor2 for a tensor section. Physical-space only; spectral integration is future.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.l2norm — Method
l2norm(f)

The normalized L² norm $\lVert f\rVert_2 = \sqrt{\langle f, f\rangle} = \sqrt{\langle |f|^2\rangle}$ of a section (l2norm $\equiv \lVert\cdot\rVert_2$) — the root-mean-square of the fiber magnitude $|f|$. The $p = 2$ case of lpnorm. Defined for physical fields and point-values, and — via the Parseval inner — for a field in spectral representation.

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.lpnorm — Function
lpnorm(f, p = 2)

The normalized Lᵖ norm $\lVert f\rVert_p = \bigl(\langle |f|^p\rangle\bigr)^{1/p}$ of a section (lpnorm $\equiv \lVert\cdot\rVert_p$), where $|f|$ is the pointwise fiber magnitude (the vertical norm). $p = 2$ reduces to l2norm; $p = \infty$ is the sup of the fiber magnitude $\lVert f\rVert_\infty$ (e.g. the maximum speed for a CFL condition). Physical-space only.

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.radial_spectrum — Method
radial_spectrum(f, multipliers)

The radial (shell-binned) power spectrum $E(\kappa)$ of a field f in spectral representation (radial_spectrum $\equiv E(\kappa)$): returns (shells, spectrum), where shells are the bin-center wavenumber magnitudes $\kappa$ and spectrum[i] is the Parseval-weighted $\sum_{|k| \in \text{shell } i} |\hat f(k)|^2$ summed over the fiber (the rfft half-spectrum weighting). It refines inner: $\sum_\kappa E(\kappa) = \langle f, f\rangle$ — the total L² energy split across isotropic $|k|$-shells of unit-fundamental width. The consumer's kinetic-energy spectrum is then $\tfrac12 E(\kappa)$ (so $\sum_\kappa \tfrac12 E(\kappa) = \tfrac12\langle |u|^2\rangle$); the package keeps the neutral name, physics stays consumer-side.

Spectral representation only (a Parseval reduction, like the spectral inner). Shells run from $0$ to the corner wavenumber, so the sum is over all modes (an exact inner refinement); on a de-aliased field the high-$|k|$ shells are empty. For a non-cubic box the shells beyond $\min(N_i)/2$ are incompletely sampled (only corner modes) — the standard isotropic-spectrum caveat — but $\sum_\kappa E(\kappa) = \langle f, f\rangle$ still holds. GPU-portable: the binning is a sequence of masked reductions (no scalar indexing, no histogram kernel), suited to an occasional diagnostic rather than a per-step call.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — the base measure (count and cell measure) and the fiber-preserving reduction helper.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions._fiberwise — Method
_fiberwise(section, whole_reduce, over_base_reduce)

Apply a reduction that keeps the fiber and integrates the base away, returning a fiber value: a scalar for tensor order 0, a FiberVector for 1, a FiberTensor2 for 2. whole_reduce(data) is used for the scalar case (reduce the whole array); over_base_reduce(data, base_dims) reduces only the leading base dimensions for higher orders. The (tiny) reduced array is copied to the host before the SVector/SMatrix build, so no device array is scalar-indexed (invariant 6).
