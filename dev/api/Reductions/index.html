GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions — Module
Reductions

The L² / measure structure on sections — the third operation family alongside the vertical (fiberwise) algebra and the horizontal (differential) operators (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §2, decision 18). A reduction integrates a section over its base, against the measure the base carries: Grid3D's uniform quadrature (cell volume $\prod_i \Delta x_i$) and PointSet's counting measure (unit weight per point — forced, since a PointSet is grid-free). One set of formulas serves fields and point-values, dispatched on AbstractFiberSection.

Defined on physical fields and point-values — representation-free / physical-space quantities, where the base reduction is the naive one. inner/l2norm additionally act on fields in spectral representation via Parseval's theorem (equal to the physical inner, no transform); integral/mean/lpnorm/ maximum stay physical-only.

  • integral(f) — the extensive integral $\int_\Omega f \, d\mu$ (the only quantity that scales with the base measure): $\tfrac{|\Omega|}{N}\sum_x f$ on a grid, $\sum_p f$ over points.
  • mean(f), std(f), var(f) — extend Statistics; intensive and fiber-preserving (mean of a vector field is a FiberVector): the arithmetic average over base elements, $\tfrac1N\sum_x f$ on the grid (the domain average, the grid being uniform) and $\tfrac1M\sum_p f$ over points (a plain sample mean).
  • inner(f, g) — the normalized L² inner product $\langle f, g\rangle = \tfrac1N\sum_x f\cdot g$ → scalar (intensive, matching mean); $\mathrm{l2norm}(f) = \sqrt{\langle f, f\rangle}$, and lpnorm(f, p) the $L^p$ norm ($p = 2$ default, $p = \infty$ ⇒ the sup of the fiber magnitude).
  • maximum/minimum/extrema — extend Base for scalar sections.

Two radial reductions resolve inner over a radial coordinate, a Fourier-dual pair (Wiener–Khinchin): radial_spectrum(f, multipliers) bins $|\hat f|^2$ over $|k|$-shells (the power spectrum, spectral input), and radial_correlation(f, plans) is its physical-space dual, the shell-averaged two-point autocorrelation $C(r) = \mathcal F^{-1}(|\hat f|^2)/N$; both satisfy $\sum E = C(0) = \mathrm{inner}(f, f)$. Each also has a two-field form — the co-spectrum radial_spectrum(f, g, multipliers) (binning $\sum_c \mathrm{Re}(\hat f_c \overline{\hat g_c})$) and the cross-correlation radial_correlation(f, g, plans) ($C_{fg}(r) = \langle f(x)\cdot g(x+r)\rangle$, via $\overline{\hat f}\,\hat g$) — refining the mixed inner(f, g) ($\sum E_{fg} = C_{fg}(0) = \mathrm{inner}(f, g)$); the one-field forms are the $g = f$ case.

Physics stays consumer-side: kinetic energy $\tfrac12 \langle u, u\rangle$, enstrophy $\tfrac12 \langle \omega, \omega\rangle$, dissipation $\nu \langle \omega, \omega\rangle$, rms $\sqrt{\langle \cdot, \cdot\rangle}$, intensities from std.

The Reductions module is the L² / measure structure on sections — the third operation family alongside the vertical (fiberwise) algebra of FieldAlgebra and the horizontal (differential) operators of DifferentialOperators. A reduction integrates a section over its base against the measure the base carries (Grid3D's uniform cell volume; PointSet's counting measure), and is defined on physical fields and point-values (representation-free quantities). See docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §2 and decision 18.

integral is the extensive $\int f \, d\mu$; inner ($\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$), l2norm ($\lVert\cdot\rVert_2$), and lpnorm ($\lVert\cdot\rVert_p$) are the normalized L² inner product and norms. The mean/std/var (from Statistics) and maximum/minimum/extrema (from Base) are extended on sections here — reached through using Statistics / Base, not re-exported. Physics stays in the consumer (kinetic energy $\tfrac12\langle u, u\rangle$, enstrophy, dissipation, rms).

A field in spectral representation also admits inner/l2norm via Parseval's theorem (equal to the physical inner, no transform), and radial_spectrum(f, mult) — the radial (shell-binned) power spectrum $S(\kappa)$ ($\kappa$ the shell-center wavenumber): inner decomposed into isotropic $|k|$-shells ($|k|$ the wavenumber magnitude), returning (shells, spectrum) with the exact refinement $\sum_\kappa S(\kappa) = \langle f, f\rangle$ (decision 21). The consumer's energy spectrum is $E(\kappa) = \tfrac12\,S(\kappa)$.

Its physical-space Wiener–Khinchin dual is radial_correlation(f, plans) (decision 22) — the shell-averaged two-point autocorrelation $C(r) = \mathcal F^{-1}(|\hat f|^2)/N$, binned over minimum-image $|r|$ shells, with $C(0) = \langle |f|^2\rangle = \mathrm{inner}(f, f)$. The two resolve the same inner over the conjugate radial coordinates ($|k|$ and $|r|$).

Each has a two-field co-spectrum form (decision 22): radial_spectrum(f, g) bins the cross-power $\sum_c \mathrm{Re}(\hat f_c \overline{\hat g_c})$ (the sum over fiber components $c$) into per-shell totals $S_{fg}(\kappa)$, and radial_correlation(f, g) is the cross-correlation $C_{fg}(r) = \langle f(x)\cdot g(x+r)\rangle = \mathcal F^{-1}(\overline{\hat f}\,\hat g)/N$, each refining the mixed $\mathrm{inner}(f, g)$ ($\sum_\kappa S_{fg}(\kappa) = C_{fg}(0) = \mathrm{inner}(f, g)$). Both are symmetric in $f, g$, the one-field forms are the $g = f$ case, and — unlike the power spectrum — the co-spectrum may be negative in a shell (anticorrelated modes; e.g. a momentum/scalar flux co-spectrum, where the sign is physical).

Public API

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.inner — Function
inner(f, g)

The normalized L² inner product of two compatible sections (inner $\equiv \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$) — the fiber contraction $f \cdot g$ averaged over the domain; a scalar, intensive (matching mean). It is the volume average of the pointwise dot product over the periodic box $\Omega$ of volume $|\Omega|$, which on the grid is the equal-weight sum over the $N$ cells (each of volume $|\Omega|/N$):

\[\langle f, g\rangle = \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \int_\Omega f(x) \cdot g(x)\, dx = \frac1N \sum_x f(x) \cdot g(x).\]

For homogeneous turbulence this is the natural pairing: kinetic energy is $\tfrac12\langle u, u\rangle$.

For two fields in spectral representation the same value is computed by Parseval's theorem, without an inverse transform. The rfft stores only $k_x \ge 0$, so each mode counts twice except those on the $k_x = 0$ plane (and, for even $N_x$, the Nyquist plane) — the planes the rfft maps to themselves under conjugation, whose partners are already stored, so they count once; the kernel forms a dot over the stored coefficients and subtracts those planes:

\[\langle f, g\rangle = \frac{1}{N^2}\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left( 2 \sum_{k_x \ge 0} \hat f(k)\, \overline{\hat g(k)} \;-\; \sum_{k_x = 0} \hat f\, \overline{\hat g} \;-\; \sum_{k_x = N_x/2} \hat f\, \overline{\hat g} \right),\]

with the last (Nyquist) sum present only for even $N_x$, and $N = \prod_i N_i$. lpnorm, integral, mean, maximum remain physical-only (only the $p = 2$ inner is representation-invariant).

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.integral — Function
integral(f)

The base integral $\int_\Omega f \, d\mu$ of a section (integral $\equiv \int\,d\mu$) — extensive (scales with the base measure). Computed as the measure-weighted sum over base elements (the uniform quadrature on a Grid3D, the counting measure on a PointSet):

\[\int_\Omega f \, d\mu = \frac{|\Omega|}{N} \sum_{x} f(x) \quad (\texttt{Grid3D}), \qquad \sum_{p} f_p \quad (\texttt{PointSet}).\]

Here $N$ is the number of base points (grid cells) and $|\Omega|$ the domain volume. For a band-limited field the grid quadrature is exact (the periodic trapezoidal rule integrates the represented modes exactly). Fiber-preserving: a scalar for a scalar section, a FiberVector for a vector section, a FiberTensor2 for a tensor section. Physical-space only; spectral integration is future.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.l2norm — Method
l2norm(f)

The normalized L² norm of a section (l2norm $\equiv \lVert\cdot\rVert_2$) — the root-mean-square of the fiber magnitude $|f|$ over the periodic box $\Omega$ of volume $|\Omega|$:

\[\lVert f\rVert_2 = \sqrt{\langle f, f\rangle} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\Omega|} \int_\Omega |f(x)|^2\, dx} = \sqrt{\frac1N \sum_x |f(x)|^2}.\]

The $p = 2$ case of lpnorm. Defined for physical fields and point-values, and — via the Parseval inner — for a field in spectral representation.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.lpnorm — Function
lpnorm(f, p = 2)

The normalized Lᵖ norm of a section (lpnorm $\equiv \lVert\cdot\rVert_p$), where $|f|$ is the pointwise fiber magnitude (the vertical norm) — the $L^p$ average over the periodic box $\Omega$ of volume $|\Omega|$, which on the grid is the equal-weight sum over the $N$ cells:

\[\lVert f\rVert_p = \Bigl(\frac{1}{|\Omega|} \int_\Omega |f(x)|^p\, dx\Bigr)^{1/p} = \Bigl(\frac1N \sum_x |f(x)|^p\Bigr)^{1/p}, \qquad \lVert f\rVert_\infty = \sup_{x \in \Omega} |f(x)| = \max_x |f(x)|.\]

$p = 2$ reduces to l2norm; $p = \infty$ is the sup of the fiber magnitude (e.g. the maximum speed for a CFL condition). Physical-space only.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.radial_correlation — Function
radial_correlation(f, plans)
radial_correlation(f, g, plans)

The radial (shell-averaged) two-point correlation of a field — the physical-space Wiener–Khinchin dual of radial_spectrum. The one-argument form is the autocorrelation $C(r)$; the two-argument form is the cross-correlation $C_{fg}(r) = \langle f(x)\cdot g(x+r)\rangle$ of two compatible, equal-order fields. f (and g) are in spectral representation; plans are their transform plans (matching the tensor order). Returns (separations, correlation), where separations are the bin-center magnitudes $r$ and correlation[i] is the value averaged over the minimum-image shell $|r| \approx r$. By the (cross-)correlation theorem the kernel forms the fiber-summed (cross-)power spectrum, inverse-transforms it, and normalizes:

\[C(r) = \frac1N \sum_x f(x)\cdot f(x+r) = \frac1N\,\mathcal F^{-1}\!\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_c |\hat f_c|^2\Bigr)(r), \qquad C_{fg}(r) = \frac1N \sum_x f(x)\cdot g(x+r) = \frac1N\,\mathcal F^{-1}\!\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_c \overline{\hat f_c}\,\hat g_c\Bigr)(r),\]

followed by the shell average $C(r_i) = |\text{shell } i|^{-1} \sum_{|r| \approx r_i} C(r)$. At zero separation each is inner: $C(0) = \langle |f|^2\rangle = \mathrm{inner}(f, f)$ and $C_{fg}(0) = \langle f\cdot g\rangle = \mathrm{inner}(f, g)$ — inner resolved over physical separation exactly as radial_spectrum resolves it over $|k|$ (a Fourier pair: $\mathcal F C = |\hat f|^2$, $\mathcal F C_{fg} = \overline{\hat f}\,\hat g$). The one-argument form is the $g = f$ case; the radial binning symmetrizes the cross-correlation — the $\pm r$ folding of the min-image shells averages $C_{fg}(r)$ with $C_{fg}(-r) = C_{gf}(r)$, so the result equals $\tfrac12(C_{fg} + C_{gf})$ and is symmetric in $f, g$.

Separations run from $0$ to the minimum-image corner $\sqrt{\sum_a (L_a/2)^2}$ ($\sqrt3\,L/2$ for a cubic box) at the finest spacing $\min_a \Delta x_a$, the $\min(i, N_a - i)$ fold making $C$ periodic and even ($C(r) = C(L - r)$). This is the physical-space mirror of the spectrum's corner caveat: only $|r| \le \min_a L_a/2$ — the sphere inscribed in the min-image box — is sampled in all directions; shells from there out to the corner keep only the box-diagonal directions (incompletely sampled). And in a finite periodic box the large-$r$ tail is itself an artifact — there $C$ reflects the field correlating with its own periodic image, not a true infinite-domain correlation — so a consumer reads an integral scale only out to where $C$ has decayed (e.g. the first zero crossing), not the full curve. As with the spectrum the total is unaffected: $C(0) = \mathrm{inner}(f, f)$ exactly.

Spectral input only (it needs the inverse transform); GPU-portable (one inverse rfft + a single scatter-add binning pass, no scalar indexing).

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.radial_spectrum — Function
radial_spectrum(f, multipliers)
radial_spectrum(f, g, multipliers)

The radial (shell-binned) spectrum of a field in spectral representation: the one-argument form is the power spectrum $E(\kappa)$ (radial_spectrum $\equiv E(\kappa)$); the two-argument form is the co-spectrum (cross-power) $E_{fg}(\kappa)$ of two compatible, equal-order fields. Returns (shells, spectrum), where shells are the bin-center wavenumber magnitudes $\kappa$ and spectrum[i] sums the Parseval-weighted fiber (co-)energy over the modes whose rounded magnitude falls in shell $i$:

\[E(\kappa_i) = \frac{1}{N^2} \sum_{\substack{k \,:\, \mathrm{round}(|k|/\Delta k) = i}} \alpha(k) \sum_c |\hat f_c(k)|^2, \qquad E_{fg}(\kappa_i) = \frac{1}{N^2} \sum_{\substack{k \,:\, \mathrm{round}(|k|/\Delta k) = i}} \alpha(k) \sum_c \mathrm{Re}\!\bigl(\hat f_c(k)\,\overline{\hat g_c(k)}\bigr),\]

with $c$ indexing the fiber components, $\alpha(k)$ the rfft half-spectrum Parseval weight ($1$ on the $k_x = 0$ and even-$N_x$ Nyquist planes, $2$ otherwise), and shell width $\Delta k$ (the box fundamental). Each refines inner: $\sum_\kappa E(\kappa) = \langle f, f\rangle$ and $\sum_\kappa E_{fg}(\kappa) = \langle f, g\rangle$ — the L² (co-)energy split across isotropic $|k|$-shells of unit-fundamental width. The one-argument form is the $g = f$ case ($\mathrm{Re}(\hat f\,\overline{\hat f}) = |\hat f|^2$); the co-spectrum is symmetric ($E_{fg} = E_{gf}$, $\mathrm{Re}$ being conjugation-symmetric) and, unlike the power spectrum, may be negative in a shell (anticorrelated modes). The consumer's kinetic-energy spectrum is $\tfrac12 E(\kappa)$; the package keeps the neutral name, physics stays consumer-side.

Spectral representation only (a Parseval reduction, like the spectral inner). Shells run from $0$ to the corner wavenumber, so the sum is over all modes (an exact inner refinement); on a de-aliased field the high-$|k|$ shells are empty. For a non-cubic box the shells beyond $\min(N_i)/2$ are incompletely sampled (only corner modes) — the standard isotropic-spectrum caveat — but the total still equals inner. GPU-portable: the binning is a single scatter-add pass over the modes (one atomic add per mode into the shell bins — no scalar indexing, no per-shell full-grid temporary), suited to an occasional diagnostic rather than a per-step call.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — the base measure (count and cell measure) and the fiber-preserving reduction helper.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions._fiberwise — Method
_fiberwise(section, whole_reduce, over_base_reduce)

Apply a reduction that keeps the fiber and integrates the base away, returning a fiber value: a scalar for tensor order 0, a FiberVector for 1, a FiberTensor2 for 2. whole_reduce(data) is used for the scalar case (reduce the whole array); over_base_reduce(data, base_dims) reduces only the leading base dimensions for higher orders. The (tiny) reduced array is copied to the host before the SVector/SMatrix build, so no device array is scalar-indexed (invariant 6).
