Reductions

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions — Module
Reductions

The L² / measure structure on sections — the third operation family, alongside the fiberwise algebra and the differential operators. A reduction integrates a section over its base against the measure the base carries: a Grid3D uses its uniform quadrature (the cell volume), and a PointSet uses its counting measure (unit weight per point, forced because a PointSet is grid-free). One set of formulas serves both fields and point-values, dispatched on AbstractFiberSection.

The measure reductions act on physical fields and point-values — representation-free, physical-space quantities. Several also act on fields in spectral representation, returning the identical physical value with no inverse transform: inner/l2norm by Parseval's theorem, and integral/mean from the zero (DC) Fourier mode. The remaining reductions (lpnorm, std/var, maximum/minimum/extrema) stay physical-only.

Each family links to the home where its formula lives:

  • integral — the extensive integral, the one quantity that scales with the base measure.
  • mean, std, var — the intensive, fiber-preserving base moments (they extend Statistics; the mean of a vector field is a FiberVector).
  • inner — the normalized L² inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, a scalar that averages the fiber contraction over the $N$ grid cells or the $M$ points. l2norm and lpnorm are the norms derived from it.
  • maximum, minimum, extrema — the $L^\infty$ end, extending Base for scalar sections.
  • radial_spectrum and radial_correlation — a Fourier-dual pair (Wiener–Khinchin) that resolves inner over a radial coordinate, the shell-binned power spectrum over $|k|$ and its physical-space two-point correlation over $|r|$, each also in a two-field co-spectrum form.

Physics stays consumer-side: the consumer builds kinetic energy, enstrophy, dissipation, rms, and turbulence intensities from these reductions.

Integral reductions

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.integral — Function
integral(f)

The base integral $\int_\Omega f \, d\mu$ of a section (integral $\equiv \int\,d\mu$) — the measure-weighted sum of f over its base. It is the one extensive reduction, the only one that scales with the base measure; reach for inner or mean when you want an intensive, size-independent average instead. On a Grid3D the sum uses the uniform cell-volume quadrature, and on a PointSet it uses the unit-weight counting measure:

\[\int_\Omega f \, d\mu = \frac{|\Omega|}{N} \sum_{x} f(x) \quad (\texttt{Grid3D}), \qquad \sum_{p} f_p \quad (\texttt{PointSet}),\]

where $N$ is the number of grid cells and $|\Omega|$ the domain volume. The result is fiber-preserving: a scalar for a scalar section, a FiberVector for a vector section, and a FiberTensor2 for a tensor section. For a band-limited field the grid quadrature is exact, because the periodic trapezoidal rule integrates the represented modes exactly. For a field in spectral representation the same value comes from the zero (DC) Fourier mode with no inverse transform, $\int_\Omega f\,d\mu = \tfrac{|\Omega|}{N}\,\hat f(0)$, since the DC coefficient $\hat f(0) = \sum_x f(x)$ is real for a real field.

Inner-product and norm reductions

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.inner — Function
inner(f, g)

The normalized L² inner product $\langle f, g\rangle$ of two compatible sections (inner $\equiv \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$) — the fiber contraction $f \cdot g$ averaged over the base, returning a scalar. It is intensive (it matches mean), which makes it the natural pairing for homogeneous turbulence, where kinetic energy is $\tfrac12\langle u, u\rangle$. For the norms derived from it use l2norm or lpnorm; to resolve the pairing over a wavenumber or separation shell use radial_spectrum or radial_correlation.

The arguments f and g are two compatible sections of equal fiber order. On a grid the pairing is the equal-weight average over the $N$ cells; on a point-set it is the average over the $M$ points:

\[\begin{aligned} \langle f, g\rangle &= \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \int_\Omega f(x) \cdot g(x)\, dx = \frac1N \sum_x f(x) \cdot g(x) && (\texttt{Grid3D}), \\ \langle f, g\rangle &= \frac1M \sum_p f_p \cdot g_p && (\texttt{PointSet}). \end{aligned}\]

For two fields in spectral representation inner returns the same value by Parseval's theorem with no inverse transform, applying the rfft half-spectrum weighting internally. This $p = 2$ inner and l2norm are representation-invariant by Parseval, and integral/mean by the zero Fourier mode; lpnorm, std/var, and maximum/minimum/extrema remain physical-only.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.l2norm — Function
l2norm(f)

The normalized L² norm $\lVert f\rVert_2$ of a section (l2norm $\equiv \lVert\cdot\rVert_2$) — the root-mean-square of the fiber magnitude $|f|$ over the base, and the $p = 2$ case of lpnorm. It is $\sqrt{\langle f, f\rangle}$ built on inner, so it inherits both bases: the grid cell-volume average and the point-set counting average.

\[\lVert f\rVert_2 = \sqrt{\langle f, f\rangle} = \sqrt{\frac1N \sum_x |f(x)|^2} \quad (\texttt{Grid3D}), \qquad \sqrt{\frac1M \sum_p |f_p|^2} \quad (\texttt{PointSet}).\]

It is defined for physical fields and point-values, and — through the Parseval inner — for a field in spectral representation.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.lpnorm — Function
lpnorm(f, p = 2)

The normalized $L^p$ norm $\lVert f\rVert_p$ of a section (lpnorm $\equiv \lVert\cdot\rVert_p$), where $|f|$ is the pointwise fiber magnitude (the fiberwise norm) — the $L^p$ average of $|f|$ over the base. The exponent p selects the norm: p = 2 (the default) reduces to l2norm, and p = Inf gives the supremum of the fiber magnitude (for example the maximum speed in a CFL condition). On a grid the average is the equal-weight sum over the $N$ cells; on a point-set it is the sum over the $M$ points:

\[\begin{aligned} \lVert f\rVert_p &= \Bigl(\frac{1}{|\Omega|} \int_\Omega |f(x)|^p\, dx\Bigr)^{1/p} = \Bigl(\frac1N \sum_x |f(x)|^p\Bigr)^{1/p} && (\texttt{Grid3D}), \\ \lVert f\rVert_p &= \Bigl(\frac1M \sum_p |f_p|^p\Bigr)^{1/p} && (\texttt{PointSet}), \\ \lVert f\rVert_\infty &= \sup_{x} |f(x)| = \max_x |f(x)|. && \end{aligned}\]

It is physical-space only.

Radial reductions

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.radial_spectrum — Function
radial_spectrum(f, multipliers)
radial_spectrum(f, g, multipliers)

The radial (shell-binned) spectrum resolves a field's L² energy over wavenumber-magnitude shells (radial_spectrum $\equiv E(\kappa)$). The one-argument form is the power spectrum $E(\kappa)$, and the two-argument form is the co-spectrum (cross-power) $E_{fg}(\kappa)$ of two compatible, equal-order fields; f and g are in spectral representation and multipliers supplies the per-axis wavenumbers $k$ (FieldSpectralMultipliers). It returns (shells, spectrum), where shells are the bin-center wavenumber magnitudes $\kappa$ and spectrum[i] sums the Parseval-weighted fiber (co-)energy over the modes whose rounded magnitude falls in shell $i$:

\[\begin{gathered} E(\kappa_i) = \frac{1}{N^2} \sum_{\substack{k \,:\, \mathrm{round}(|k|/\Delta k) = i}} \alpha(k) \sum_c |\hat f_c(k)|^2, \\ E_{fg}(\kappa_i) = \frac{1}{N^2} \sum_{\substack{k \,:\, \mathrm{round}(|k|/\Delta k) = i}} \alpha(k) \sum_c \mathrm{Re}\!\bigl(\hat f_c(k)\,\overline{\hat g_c(k)}\bigr), \end{gathered}\]

with $c$ indexing the fiber components, $\alpha(k)$ the rfft half-spectrum Parseval weight ($1$ on the $k_x = 0$ and even-$N_x$ Nyquist planes, $2$ otherwise), and shell width $\Delta k$ (the box fundamental). Each form refines inner: $\sum_\kappa E(\kappa) = \langle f, f\rangle$ and $\sum_\kappa E_{fg}(\kappa) = \langle f, g\rangle$ — the L² (co-)energy split across isotropic $|k|$-shells of unit-fundamental width. The one-argument form is the $g = f$ case ($\mathrm{Re}(\hat f\,\overline{\hat f}) = |\hat f|^2$); the co-spectrum is symmetric ($E_{fg} = E_{gf}$, $\mathrm{Re}$ being conjugation-symmetric) and, unlike the power spectrum, may be negative in a shell (anticorrelated modes). The consumer's kinetic-energy spectrum is $\tfrac12 E(\kappa)$; the package keeps the neutral name, physics stays consumer-side.

This reduction acts on the spectral representation only, being a Parseval reduction like the spectral inner. Shells run from $0$ to the corner wavenumber, so the sum covers all modes (an exact inner refinement); on a de-aliased field the high-$|k|$ shells are empty. For a non-cubic box the shells beyond $\min(N_i)/2$ are incompletely sampled (only corner modes contribute) — the standard isotropic-spectrum caveat — but the total still equals inner. The reduction is GPU-portable, a single pass over the modes that suits an occasional diagnostic rather than a per-step call.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions.radial_correlation — Function
radial_correlation(f, plans)
radial_correlation(f, g, plans)

The radial (shell-averaged) two-point correlation of a field is the physical-space Wiener–Khinchin dual of radial_spectrum. The one-argument form is the autocorrelation $C(r)$, and the two-argument form is the cross-correlation $C_{fg}(r) = \langle f(x)\cdot g(x+r)\rangle$ of two compatible, equal-order fields. The fields f (and g) are in spectral representation, and plans are their transform plans (matching the tensor order). It returns (separations, correlation), where separations are the bin-center magnitudes $r$ and correlation[i] is the value averaged over the minimum-image shell $|r| \approx r$. By the (cross-)correlation theorem the kernel forms the fiber-summed (cross-)power spectrum, inverse-transforms it, and normalizes:

\[\begin{gathered} C(r) = \frac1N \sum_x f(x)\cdot f(x+r) = \frac1N\,\mathcal F^{-1}\!\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_c |\hat f_c|^2\Bigr)(r), \\ C_{fg}(r) = \frac1N \sum_x f(x)\cdot g(x+r) = \frac1N\,\mathcal F^{-1}\!\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_c \overline{\hat f_c}\,\hat g_c\Bigr)(r), \end{gathered}\]

followed by the shell average $C(r_i) = |\text{shell } i|^{-1} \sum_{|r| \approx r_i} C(r)$. At zero separation each is inner: $C(0) = \langle |f|^2\rangle = \mathrm{inner}(f, f)$ and $C_{fg}(0) = \langle f\cdot g\rangle = \mathrm{inner}(f, g)$ — inner resolved over physical separation exactly as radial_spectrum resolves it over $|k|$ (a Fourier pair: $\mathcal F C = |\hat f|^2$, $\mathcal F C_{fg} = \overline{\hat f}\,\hat g$). The one-argument form is the $g = f$ case; the radial binning symmetrizes the cross-correlation — the $\pm r$ folding of the min-image shells averages $C_{fg}(r)$ with $C_{fg}(-r) = C_{gf}(r)$, so the result equals $\tfrac12(C_{fg} + C_{gf})$ and is symmetric in $f, g$.

Separations run from $0$ to the minimum-image corner $\sqrt{\sum_a (L_a/2)^2}$ ($\sqrt3\,L/2$ for a cubic box) at the finest spacing $\min_a \Delta x_a$, the $\min(i, N_a - i)$ fold making $C$ periodic and even ($C(r) = C(L - r)$). This is the physical-space mirror of the spectrum's corner caveat: only $|r| \le \min_a L_a/2$ — the sphere inscribed in the min-image box — is sampled in all directions; shells from there out to the corner keep only the box-diagonal directions (incompletely sampled). And in a finite periodic box the large-$r$ tail is itself an artifact — there $C$ reflects the field correlating with its own periodic image, not a true infinite-domain correlation — so a consumer extracts an integral scale only out to where $C$ has decayed (e.g. the first zero crossing), not the full curve. As with the spectrum the total is unaffected: $C(0) = \mathrm{inner}(f, f)$ exactly.

It takes spectral input only, since it needs the inverse transform, and it is GPU-portable: one inverse transform plus a single binning pass.

The mean, std, and var moments (extending Statistics) and the maximum, minimum, and extrema order statistics (extending Base) are also defined for sections; they are method extensions of the standard-library generics rather than package exports, and are described in the module summary above.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — the base measure (count and cell measure) and the fiber-preserving reduction helper.

GBGPeriodicFields.Reductions._fiberwise — Method
_fiberwise(section, whole_reduce, over_base_reduce)

Apply a reduction that keeps the fiber and integrates the base away, returning a fiber value: a scalar for tensor order 0, a FiberVector for 1, a FiberTensor2 for 2. whole_reduce(data) is used for the scalar case (reduce the whole array); over_base_reduce(data, base_dims) reduces only the leading base dimensions for higher orders. The (tiny) reduced array is copied to the host before the SVector/SMatrix build, so no device array is scalar-indexed.
