FieldAlgebra

GBGPeriodicFields.FieldAlgebra — Module
FieldAlgebra

Fiberwise and pointwise algebra for the scalar, vector, and second-order tensor fields defined in Fields. Each operation comes as an allocating form — a Base or LinearAlgebra operator where one exists — and an in-place ! form.

Every operation here acts pointwise over the base — the set of locations the field attaches a value to, which for a field is the grid's nodes — applying the same formula to the value at each one and touching no neighbours. That is what the formulas below mean by "pointwise over the base".

Acting pointwise is not what makes an operation available in both representations, and the two must not be confused: det and norm are as pointwise as any operation here and are physical-representation only. What carries an operation across the transform is linearity in the field argument, its partners held fiber-constant — a product of two fields is linear in each factor separately yet not in the pair, and it stays physical-only — since only a linear map commutes with the rfft isomorphism — which is why the linear operations below (the sums, the fiber-constant contractions, the transposes, sym/skew/dev) exist in both representations while every operation nonlinear in a field is defined in physical representation alone.

Two words recur below. A fiber is the value at one base point — a scalar, a FiberVector or a FiberTensor2 — and a section is one fiber at every point of a base, which is what a field and a set of point-values each are. That shared shape is why one set of formulas serves both.

The parts are:

  • Vector-space and scalar-field-scaling operations — add! and subtract! (+, -), the weighted combination combine! ($\alpha a + \beta b$), real scaling, and scalar-field multiplication and division (multiply!, divide!, *, /). Addition, weighted combination, and real scaling are the vector-space structure; scalar-field multiplication is the action that makes fields a module over the ring of scalar fields — the algebraic sense of the word, not a Julia module: scaling a field by another field rather than by a number.
  • Contraction and product operations — dot! computes the vector Euclidean and tensor Frobenius inner products (⋅), cross! the cross product (×), mul! the tensor-on-vector action (left and right) and the tensor-on-tensor product — contracted (matrix) products, not the outer tensor product — with a five-argument α/β accumulation form and an allocating *, tr! the trace, the self-contraction of a tensor's own two indices, and det! the determinant, the tensor's third principal invariant.
  • Transposition and decomposition operations — transpose! transposes the fiber indices, and sym, skew, dev split off the symmetric, skew-symmetric, and deviatoric parts (each with a ! form).
  • Norm operations — norm! computes the pointwise absolute-value, Euclidean, and Frobenius norms.
  • Arbitrary fiber map — map! and map apply any fiber map φ you supply at every base point — a composition like exp ∘ det, a pointwise equation of state, whatever is not one of the named operations above. This is the section-valued escape hatch for un-named maps (and the sibling of the mapped reductions such as mean(det, ·)): φ may change the tensor order, and the result's order is φ's (map(det, A) gives a scalar field). A field must be in physical representation, since an arbitrary φ is nonlinear.

Field-field and field-constant operations preserve the representation of their arguments: they act pointwise on fields in physical representation and coefficient-wise on fields in spectral representation. An operation that is not linear in a field argument — multiplying two fields together, or contracting two fields — is defined only in physical representation, except where one factor is a compile-time constant fiber, which keeps the operation coefficient-wise; the missing combinations are deliberate.

Nonlinear results are spectrally aliased

(Spectral aliasing here — content folding back onto resolved modes — not the storage aliasing of the admonition below, which is about two arguments sharing memory. The two senses are unrelated.) A nonlinear operation — a field-field product (*, mul!, dot, cross, divide!) or a nonlinear fiber invariant (det, norm) — generates content above the grid's Nyquist wavenumber, so its result is not band-limited — it carries wavenumbers the grid cannot represent. The pointwise values at the grid nodes are exact, but transforming the result to spectral representation folds that out-of-band content back onto the resolved modes as aliasing error. Before feeding such a result into a spectral computation — a solver's nonlinear term — de-alias it with the sharp low-pass spectral_cutoff or the phase-shift translation_kernel from Convolution. This is the same fact that makes these operations physical-representation only: a nonlinearity has no finite-band spectral form.

The mul! product follows the LinearAlgebra convention that the destination must not share storage with an input, while the index-shuffling operations (cross, transpose, sym, skew, dev) are alias-safe by construction (each fused pass reads an element's sources before writing any output) and may be called with the destination aliasing an input.

Why an aliased in-place call is safe

destination may be an input — sym!(A, A) is computed directly and is bitwise-identical to the non-aliased call — because a fused pass reads all of an element's sources into registers before writing any of that element's outputs. That argument needs one section's element $n$ to be the other's element $n$, and it is the ownership rule that supplies it: a section's constructor refuses borrowed storage, so two sections can share memory only by being backed by the same array. Two partially-overlapping views at different base indices would break the argument, and they are not merely undetected — they cannot be constructed. The mul! operation is the one exception, keeping LinearAlgebra's contract that its destination must not alias.

Most of this module is mirrored onto point-values (AbstractPointValues) — the same fiberwise algebra applied to field values sampled at an unstructured point set, generated from the same builders so no formula is written twice. The mirror covers the physical-representation surface, not every binding: the weighted combination combine! has no point-value method, for instance. See the Points module for what the point layer provides.

Add fields, or a field and a constant, in place, and return destination.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating + returns a fresh result instead.

The arguments are:

  • destination — the field that receives the sum; it shares the domain and tensor order of the operands.
  • a, b, fields... — two or more fields to add.
  • field, constant — the field-and-constant variants, in either argument order: a Real with a scalar field, a FiberVector with a vector field, a FiberTensor2 with a second-order tensor field.

Field-field addition is storage-wise and representation-preserving: it acts pointwise on fields in physical representation and coefficient-wise on fields in spectral representation.

In physical representation the constant is added at every grid point. In spectral representation only the zero Fourier coefficient changes, scaled by the number of grid points prod(grid_size), so that the physical values shift by exactly the constant.

GBGPeriodicFields.FieldAlgebra.combine! — Function
combine!(destination, α::Real, a, β::Real, b)
combine!(destination, α::Real, a, β::Real, b, kernel::SpectralKernel)

Overwrite destination with the real-weighted linear combination $\alpha a + \beta b$ of two compatible fields, and return destination.

Here α and β weight the two inputs, not the destination

Almost every in-place operation in the package uses trailing α, β that accumulate a result onto destination: $\alpha (\mathrm{result}) + \beta \texttt{destination}$. This operation is one of two exceptions — the other is exponential_convolve!'s two-operand forms, which carry this same combination into a fused convolution — and it is the exception the name announces: it is the weighted combination, so its α and β sit between the operands they weight and there is no accumulate form — pass b === destination when you want one.

Reach for combine! with a preallocated destination when you need a real-weighted combination of two fields in a single allocation-free pass. It fills the gap between add!, which is unit-weight ($a + b$), and multiply!, which scales a single field; the allocating equivalent is the operator expression $\alpha a + \beta b$, which builds scratch fields instead. The combination is linear and representation-preserving, so it is defined for both physical and spectral representation.

The arguments are:

  • destination — the field that receives the result; either input may alias it — the write is coefficientwise, so a, b, or both may be destination itself, with no guard temporary.
  • α, a — the real weight and the first field.
  • β, b — the real weight and the second field.

The aliased call combine!(u, 1, u, β, b) computes the scaled accumulate $u \leftarrow u + \beta b$ in place.

\[\texttt{destination} = \alpha a + \beta b.\]

The kernel-composed form

Passing a trailing kernel — a kernel's transfer function $\hat g$, in any storage class, exactly as convolve! takes it — convolves the combination with the kernel in the same fused pass. As a real physical object the result is the kernel-weighted local average of the combination, $g \star (\alpha a + \beta b)$; in spectral representation it is one coefficientwise product,

\[\widehat{\texttt{destination}}(k) = \hat g(k) \bigl(\alpha \hat a(k) + \beta \hat b(k)\bigr),\]

replacing a combine! followed by a convolve! — one pass instead of two, saving a full read and a full write of destination per call. This form exists only in spectral representation (a kernel is a spectral object); α/β keep their combination-weight roles, and with b === destination it computes the kernel-filtered accumulate in place. The scalar kernel broadcasts over the fiber, so one method serves every tensor order, and the write stays coefficientwise.

The additional argument is:

  • kernel — the transfer function $\hat g$, in any of its storage classes, exactly as convolve! takes it. It may alias destination, as may a and b, the write being coefficientwise.

multiply!(destination, scalar::Real, field)

Multiply a field in place by a real number or by a scalar field, and return destination.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating * returns a fresh result instead.

The arguments are:

  • destination — the field that receives the product.
  • scalar — a Real factor, accepted in either argument order.
  • scalar_field — a scalar field factor, multiplying the other operand pointwise.
  • field, constant — the operand being scaled: a field of any tensor order, or a constant FiberVector or FiberTensor2 paired with a scalar field.

Multiplication by a real number is representation-preserving and is defined for fields in both physical and spectral representation, in either argument order.

Multiplication by a scalar field is pointwise, so when the other factor is a field it is defined only in physical representation; in spectral representation the product of two fields is a convolution, which this module does not provide. The field-field result is not band-limited, so it aliases if transformed back to spectral representation; de-alias (spectral_cutoff) before spectral reuse. When the other factor is a constant FiberVector or FiberTensor2, the operation is linear in the scalar field and stays coefficient-wise, so it is defined in both representations and yields the corresponding vector or tensor field. Either argument order is accepted.

Compute a fiberwise inner product in place, and return destination.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating dot returns a fresh result instead.

The arguments are:

  • destination — the scalar field that receives the contraction.
  • u, v — two vector fields, or A, B two second-order tensor fields.
  • a, B — a constant FiberVector or FiberTensor2 contracted against the field, in either argument order.

For two vector fields this is the Euclidean scalar product; for two second-order tensor fields it is the Frobenius double contraction. Either argument order is accepted.

Contraction of two fields is nonlinear and is defined only in physical representation. In spectral representation only contraction with a constant fiber vector or fiber tensor is provided, which keeps the operation coefficient-wise. The field-field result is not band-limited, so it aliases if transformed back to spectral representation; de-alias (spectral_cutoff) before spectral reuse.

Pointwise over the base, with summation over the repeated fiber indices:

\[(u \cdot v)(x) = u_i(x) v_i(x), \qquad (A : B)(x) = A_{ij}(x) B_{ij}(x).\]

Compute a fiberwise cross product (cross $\equiv \times$) in place, and return destination.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating cross returns a fresh result instead.

The arguments are:

  • destination — the vector field that receives the product.
  • u, v — the two vector fields.
  • a — a constant FiberVector crossed with the field. Both orders have methods, and being a cross product they differ in sign: $a \times u = -(u \times a)$.

\[\begin{gathered} (u \times v)_i = \varepsilon_{ijk} u_j v_k, \\ (u \times v)_1 = u_2 v_3 - u_3 v_2, \quad (u \times v)_2 = u_3 v_1 - u_1 v_3, \quad (u \times v)_3 = u_1 v_2 - u_2 v_1. \end{gathered}\]

LinearAlgebra.mul! — Function
mul!(destination::VectorFieldPhysical, A::TensorFieldPhysical{2},
     u::VectorFieldPhysical[, α, β])
mul!(destination::VectorFieldPhysical, A::TensorFieldPhysical{2},
     a::FiberVector[, α, β])
mul!(destination::VectorFieldSpectral, A::FiberTensor2,
     u::VectorFieldSpectral[, α, β])
mul!(destination::TensorFieldPhysical{2}, A::TensorFieldPhysical{2},
     B::TensorFieldPhysical{2}[, α, β])
mul!(destination::TensorFieldPhysical{2}, A::TensorFieldPhysical{2},
     B::FiberTensor2[, α, β])
mul!(destination::VectorFieldPhysical, u::VectorFieldPhysical,
     A::TensorFieldPhysical{2}[, α, β])
mul!(destination::VectorFieldPhysical, a::FiberVector,
     A::TensorFieldPhysical{2}[, α, β])

Compute a fiberwise tensor (matrix) product in place, and return destination. The three-argument forms compute the tensor-on-vector left and right action, or the tensor-on-tensor product; the five-argument form scales and accumulates as $\texttt{destination} = \alpha (A B) + \beta \texttt{destination}$.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating * returns a fresh result instead.

The arguments are:

  • destination — the field written with the product. It must not share storage with either operand, and that is not checked: mul! keeps LinearAlgebra's contrary contract, where every other in-place operation here is alias-safe by construction.
  • the two operands — given in the order they multiply, so the signatures above cover both the left action $(A u)_i = A_{ij} u_j$ and the right action $(u A)_i = u_j A_{ji}$. Each may be a field or a fiber constant: a second-order tensor field or a FiberTensor2, a vector field or a FiberVector. At least one must be a field, since a product of two constants is not a field operation.
  • α, β — real accumulation weights, converted to the field's scalar type. The three-argument forms overwrite ($\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 0$); the five-argument forms compute $\alpha (A B) + \beta \texttt{destination}$, so $\beta = 0$ overwrites and any other value accumulates — the LinearAlgebra.mul! convention.

A product of two fields is nonlinear and is defined only in physical representation; a spectral-representation product requires one factor to be a constant fiber tensor or fiber vector. The field-field result is not band-limited, so it aliases if transformed back to spectral representation; de-alias (spectral_cutoff) before spectral reuse.

Pointwise over the base, with summation over the repeated fiber indices:

\[(A u)_i = A_{ij} u_j, \qquad (u A)_i = u_j A_{ji}, \qquad (A B)_{ij} = A_{ik} B_{kj}.\]

Arbitrary fiber map

The escape hatch for an arbitrary fiber map that is not one of the named operations above — fields in physical representation here, point-values in Points; see map! for the physical-space-only and aliasing caveats.

Base.map! — Function
map!(φ, destination, sources...)
map!(φ, destination, source, α, β)

Apply an arbitrary fiber map φ at every base point, writing the result into destination — the section-valued companion of the mapped reductions (mean(det, A), maximum(norm, u)): where those reduce φ of the fiber over the base, this writes it as a field. It fuses in one pass, building no intermediate section, and inherits the executor's parallelism (a device kernel on a GPU, a slab-threaded loop on a CPU); being a per-point transform with disjoint writes, it is bitwise thread-count-invariant.

Use this when the operation is not one of the named fiberwise operations (det, norm, the algebra) — an arbitrary pointwise function of the fiber, a composition such as exp ∘ det included. Prefer the named operations where they exist (they carry their validity and their spectral forms); map! is the escape hatch for the unnamed, physical-only maps.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating map returns a fresh result instead.

At every base point x, destination's fiber is set to φ applied to the sources' fibers there; map!(φ, dest, f, g) sets it to φ(f(x), g(x)).

The arguments are:

  • φ — the fiber map, applied to the input fiber value(s) and returning the output fiber value. It must be a pure, GPU-safe function (no captured state, no scalar indexing of device arrays); its output tensor order sets destination's order (det gives a scalar field, an SMatrix-valued φ a tensor field). It must also read only the fiber it is given — see the warning below.
  • destination — the field in physical representation (or point-value section) that receives the result; its tensor order must match φ's output.
  • sources — one or more compatible fields in physical representation (or point-value sections) sharing destination's base; φ receives their fiber values in argument order.
  • α, β — the accumulation of the single-source form, $\texttt{destination} = \alpha \varphi(\texttt{source}) + \beta \texttt{destination}$ ($β = 0$ overwrites, the mul! convention); the variadic form overwrites ($α = 1$, $β = 0$).
A fiber map must not read the destination

The pass writes each base point independently and in an unspecified order, so φ may read only the fiber handed to it. A φ that reads elsewhere in the destination sees storage another thread may already have overwritten, and the result depends on the schedule — it will differ between runs, and between a threaded and a serial one.

The trap is that a field is callable: f(x) interpolates, so passing a section as φ makes it a gather over a neighbourhood rather than an element-local map. That is why the usual aliasing freedom does not apply here. A destination may normally alias an input precisely because a fused pass reads element $n$ to write element $n$; a gathering φ breaks that premise.

A section φ that is the destination is rejected — sections own their storage, so identity is a complete test for that case. A closure that captures the destination cannot be detected, and is the caller's responsibility. Aliasing between destination and the sources remains safe and is unaffected.

Physical-space only, and the result is aliased in spectral representation

An arbitrary φ is nonlinear, so — like the other nonlinear operations of this module — map! is defined for fields in physical representation only, and its result carries content above the grid's Nyquist: it is aliased once transformed to spectral representation. De-alias before spectral reuse (see the module docstring). Point-value sections are representation-free and carry no such restriction.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — two storage helpers the generated operations share: one copies a field's data into a destination for the paths that cannot assume it starts empty, the other reshapes a scalar field so it broadcasts against a fiber.

The code generators themselves are not here. They live in FiberSections, which is what lets FieldAlgebra and Points stamp the same formulas over their different bases, and the per-operation methods @eval produces from them carry no docstrings of their own — the formula is documented on the builder.
