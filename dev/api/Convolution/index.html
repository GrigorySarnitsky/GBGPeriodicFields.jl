Convolution

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution — Module
Convolution

Periodic (circular) convolution of a field with a kernel, computed in spectral representation. This is the kernel-multiplier operation family — a grid→grid multiplier that filters a field against a fixed kernel — the spectral sibling of the differential operators and the same-base counterpart of Interpolation. On the torus the only convolution is the periodic one, and by the convolution theorem it is exactly a coefficientwise product of the transforms, so the operation lives in spectral representation. A kernel is supplied by its transfer function as a scalar field in spectral representation, and a scalar kernel multiplies every fiber component, so one operation serves scalar, vector, and tensor fields. The operation is named convolve (allocating infix ⋆) rather than *, because the coefficientwise product would read as a pointwise multiplication, which it is not.

Physics stays consumer-side: an LES filter, a viscous integrating factor, or a de-aliasing mask is a scalar field in spectral representation that the consumer builds from FieldSpectralMultipliers and convolves with — the package supplies the operation, not the physical meaning.

  • convolve / convolve! — convolve a field with a kernel; the in-place form adds $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulation, and convolve(g₁, g₂) composes two kernels into one.
  • spectral_cutoff / spectral_band — build the isotropic sharp low-pass kernel and its annular band-pass generalization.
  • squared_wavenumber_field — the squared-wavenumber radial coordinate of spectral space, as a scalar field in spectral representation, from which a consumer builds a Fourier symbol.
  • assignment_window — the linear (cloud-in-cell) mass-assignment window, for deconvolving a gridded point density.

Convolution operations

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.convolve! — Function
convolve!(destination, field, kernel::ScalarFieldSpectral)
convolve!(destination, field, kernel::ScalarFieldSpectral, α, β)

Convolve field with the kernel whose transfer function is kernel, writing the result into destination in place; both field and destination are in spectral representation.

As a real physical object the periodic convolution is the kernel-weighted local average of the field $f$ about each point $x$ — a low-pass filter when the kernel $g$ is a smoothing bump:

\[(f \star g)(x) = \int_\Omega f(y)\, g(x - y)\, dy ,\]

with $\Omega$ the periodic box and the displacement $x - y$ reduced onto the torus; on the grid this is the circular sum $(f \star g)[n] = \sum_m f[m]\, g[n - m]$ with indices modulo the grid size. By the convolution theorem (the forward transform unnormalized) it is exactly the coefficientwise product of the transforms, which is what this operation computes — $\hat g$ = kernel, broadcast over every fiber component of $\hat f$, with the $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulate:

\[\widehat{\mathrm{destination}}(k) = \alpha\, \hat f(k)\, \hat g(k) + \beta\, \widehat{\mathrm{destination}}(k).\]

  • field — the field to filter, in spectral representation.
  • kernel — the kernel's transfer function $\hat g$, a scalar field in spectral representation; one scalar kernel serves scalar, vector, and tensor fields.
  • destination — the field written with the result; it may alias field or kernel, since the product is coefficientwise.
  • α, β — real accumulation weights, converted to the field's scalar type; the three-argument form overwrites ($\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 0$), the five-argument form computes $\alpha\,(f \star g) + \beta\,\mathrm{destination}$.

Spectral representation only: on the torus convolution is the coefficientwise spectral product. Returns the modified destination.

Kernel-multiplier builders

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.spectral_cutoff — Function
spectral_cutoff(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral,
                multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers, wavenumber_cutoff)

Build an isotropic sharp low-pass kernel as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend: the transfer function is $1$ for $|k| \le k_c$ and $0$ above it.

Convolve a field with it for the ideal low-pass projection, the basis of spectral truncation and de-aliasing. It is idempotent ($\hat g^2 = \hat g$), hence a projection, and it is the low-pass special case of spectral_band with $k_\mathrm{lo} = 0$.

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the kernel matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked against reference.
  • wavenumber_cutoff — the cutoff wavenumber $k_c$; a mode is kept when $|k| \le k_c$.

\[\hat g(k) = \mathbb 1\!\left[\, |k| \le k_c \,\right].\]

The choice of cutoff, such as a $2/3$ de-aliasing rule or an LES filter scale, is the consumer's. The squared wavenumber $|k|^2$ keeps the true value at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention).

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.spectral_band — Function
spectral_band(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral, multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers,
              wavenumber_low, wavenumber_high)

Build an isotropic sharp spectral band-pass kernel as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend: the transfer function is $1$ on the wavenumber shell and $0$ outside it.

Convolve a field with it to keep an isotropic shell of wavenumbers — a de-aliasing band, a forcing band, or any shell selector. It is idempotent ($\hat g^2 = \hat g$), hence a projection, and it is the annular generalization of spectral_cutoff, which is the low-pass case $k_\mathrm{lo} = 0$.

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the kernel matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked against reference.
  • wavenumber_low, wavenumber_high — the inner and outer edges $k_\mathrm{lo}$, $k_\mathrm{hi}$ of the retained shell.

\[\hat g(k) = \begin{cases} 1, & k_\mathrm{lo} \le |k| \le k_\mathrm{hi}, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases}\]

with $|k|^2$ keeping the true value at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention). The band edges are the consumer's choice.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.squared_wavenumber_field — Function
squared_wavenumber_field(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral,
                         multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers)

Return the squared wavenumber $|k|^2$ at every mode as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend.

Use it as the neutral radial coordinate of spectral space: a consumer builds a Fourier symbol from it — a viscous integrating factor $\exp(-\nu |k|^2 \Delta t)$, a smooth spectral envelope, or a Germano subgrid filter — by broadcasting over its field_data and applying it with convolve. The package supplies the coordinate, while $\nu$, $\Delta t$, and any filter width stay consumer-side.

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the result matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked for compatibility with reference.

\[|k|^2 = k_1^2 + k_2^2 + k_3^2 ,\]

summed with the true value kept at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention). The value is real but is stored in the Complex{T} coefficient array of a scalar field in spectral representation, its imaginary part zero, exactly as spectral_cutoff stores its $0/1$ mask.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.assignment_window — Function
assignment_window(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral, multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers)

Return the linear (cloud-in-cell) mass-assignment window as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend, for deconvolving a gridded point density.

Use it in the pair-correlation route. Depositing $M$ points on the grid with the positive linear assignment (LalescuSpline(0, 0), the 2-point linear kernel used for a density) spreads each point's mass over a tent, so the gridded density is the true density $\rho$ convolved with this window, $\rho_g = \rho \star W$. Dividing the transform by $\hat W$ — a convolve by $1/\hat W$ — undoes that smearing before you estimate the radial distribution function.

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the window matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked against reference.

As a real physical object $W$ is the linear B-spline ("tent") cloud, the tensor product of per-axis tents:

\[W(x) = \prod_{a=1}^{3} \frac{1}{\Delta x_a}\, \max\!\Bigl(0,\; 1 - \frac{|x_a|}{\Delta x_a}\Bigr),\]

each a tent of half-width one cell $\Delta x_a$ along axis $a$, of unit integral and peaked at the origin (a cell-wide top-hat convolved with itself, hence the squared sinc). The function returns its transfer function $\hat W(k)$, the squared sinc

\[\begin{gathered} \hat W(k) = \prod_{a=1}^{3} \operatorname{sinc}^2\!\Bigl(\tfrac{k_a \Delta x_a}{2}\Bigr), \\ \operatorname{sinc}(y) = \frac{\sin y}{y}, \end{gathered}\]

computed from the per-axis wavenumber $k_a$ (kept at the Nyquist frequency) and the grid spacing $\Delta x_a$, so $\hat W(0) = 1$ and it decays toward the grid scale. Because the squared sinc does not vanish anywhere in the resolved band, dividing by it is stable. The package supplies the window; the density estimate and the correlation normalization stay consumer-side.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — the coefficientwise convolution kernel.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution._apply_convolution! — Method
_apply_convolution!(destination_data, source_data, kernel_data, alpha, beta)

Apply the coefficientwise spectral product that realizes the periodic convolution in spectral representation, writing $\mathrm{dst} \mathrel{.=} \alpha\,\hat g \cdot \hat f + \beta\,\mathrm{dst}$ ($\mathrm{dst}$ the destination_data, $\hat f$ the source_data, $\hat g$ = kernel_data). The scalar kernel_data, of shape (Nx, Ny, Nz), broadcasts over the fiber components of source_data exactly as the Laplacian's $|k|^2$ does, so one body serves every tensor order. The call branches on iszero(beta) to overwrite or accumulate. The product is coefficientwise, so it is aliasing-safe: destination_data may be source_data or the kernel.
