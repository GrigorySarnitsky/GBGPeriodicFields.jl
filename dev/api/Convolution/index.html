Convolution

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution — Module
Convolution

The kernel-multiplier operation family (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §2, decision 20): periodic convolution of a field with a kernel, in spectral representation. It is the spectral sibling of the differential operators and the same-base counterpart of Interpolation — both realize the "kernel measure" of the measure taxonomy (decision 19); convolution is the grid→grid multiplier, interpolation the grid↔point base-change.

On the torus the only convolution is the periodic (circular) one, and by the convolution theorem it is the coefficientwise spectral product f̂ · ĝ. The kernel g is supplied by its transfer function ĝ as a scalar field in spectral representation (ScalarFieldSpectral); a scalar kernel multiplies every fiber component, so one operation serves scalar, vector, and tensor fields.

This coefficientwise product is exactly the operation invariant 1 names but bans as * (because * would be read as the spectral pointwise product, which it is not). It is offered here under its true name — convolve, never * — and is linear in the field for a fixed kernel. The pointwise spectral product, division, and norms remain absent.

  • convolve(f, g) / convolve! — the convolution f ⋆ g = (f̂ · ĝ)ˇ, with the in-place and α/β-accumulate forms. Because a kernel is itself a scalar field in spectral representation, convolve(g₁, g₂) composes two kernels. Spectral representation only.
  • spectral_cutoff — a neutral kernel builder: the isotropic sharp low-pass (de-aliasing) cutoff.

Physics stays consumer-side: an LES filter, an integrating factor exp(-νk²Δt), or a de-aliasing mask is a ScalarFieldSpectral the consumer builds from FieldSpectralMultipliers and convolves with — the package supplies the operation, not the physical meaning (invariant 12).

See docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §2 and decision 20.

convolve/convolve! apply the kernel (with allocating, in-place, and α/β-accumulate forms); because a kernel is itself a scalar field in spectral representation, convolve(g₁, g₂) composes two kernels. This is the coefficientwise spectral product invariant 1 names but bans as * — offered here under its true name convolve, never as *. Spectral representation only; physics (an LES filter, an integrating factor) stays a consumer-built kernel.

Three neutral builders ship (decisions 20, 21): spectral_cutoff is the isotropic sharp low-pass (de-aliasing) kernel; spectral_band(ref, mult, k_lo, k_hi) is its annular generalization (a band-pass shell selector; the cutoff is the k_lo = 0 case); and squared_wavenumber_field(ref, mult) is the |k|² radial coordinate of spectral space as a ScalarFieldSpectral — the consumer builds a Fourier symbol from it (a viscous integrating factor exp(−ν|k|²Δt), a smooth envelope, a Germano filter) and applies it with convolve.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.convolve — Function
convolve(field, kernel::ScalarFieldSpectral)

Allocate and return the periodic convolution of field with the kernel ĝ = kernel, in spectral representation — f ⋆ g, the coefficientwise product f̂ · ĝ. Representation- and tensor-order-preserving. Because a kernel is itself a scalar field in spectral representation, convolve(g₁, g₂) composes two kernels (the product of their transfer functions). Spectral representation only.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.convolve! — Function
convolve!(destination, field, kernel::ScalarFieldSpectral)
convolve!(destination, field, kernel::ScalarFieldSpectral, α, β)

Convolve field (in spectral representation) with the kernel whose transfer function is kernel, writing into destination in-place: the periodic (circular) convolution f ⋆ g, realized coefficientwise as f̂ · ĝ. kernel is a scalar field in spectral representation holding ĝ; it multiplies every fiber component of field, so one method serves scalar, vector, and tensor fields. Representation-preserving: destination has field's tensor order.

The five-argument form computes destination = α·(f ⋆ g) + β·destination (α, β real numbers, converted to the field's scalar type); the three-argument form overwrites (α = 1, β = 0). Coefficientwise ⇒ aliasing-safe: destination may alias field (or the kernel).

Spectral representation only — there is no physical-space convolve (on the torus, convolution is the coefficientwise spectral product). That product is offered here under its true name, never as * (invariant 1): convolve is convolution, not the spectral pointwise product.

Returns the modified destination.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.spectral_band — Method
spectral_band(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral, multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers, wavenumber_low, wavenumber_high)

Build the isotropic sharp spectral band-pass as a scalar field in spectral representation: the transfer function ĝ(k) = 1 for wavenumber_low ≤ |k| ≤ wavenumber_high and 0 otherwise, on reference's grid and array backend. The annular generalization of spectral_cutoff — which is the low-pass special case wavenumber_low = 0. Convolving with it keeps an isotropic shell of wavenumbers: a de-aliasing band, a forcing band (the energy held in 1 ≤ |k| ≤ k_f), or any shell selector. Idempotent (ĝ² = ĝ), so it is a projection.

A neutral mathematical primitive: the band edges are the consumer's. |k|² keeps the true value at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention, invariant 5).

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.spectral_cutoff — Method
spectral_cutoff(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral, multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers, wavenumber_cutoff)

Build the isotropic sharp spectral cutoff as a scalar field in spectral representation: the transfer function ĝ(k) = 1 for |k| ≤ wavenumber_cutoff and 0 otherwise, on reference's grid and array backend. Convolving with it is the ideal low-pass projection (idempotent — ĝ² = ĝ), the basis of spectral truncation and de-aliasing. The low-pass special case of spectral_band (wavenumber_low = 0).

A neutral mathematical primitive: the choice of cutoff (e.g. a 2/3 de-aliasing rule, or an LES filter scale) is the consumer's. The squared wavenumber |k|² is summed with the true value kept at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention, invariant 5).

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.squared_wavenumber_field — Method
squared_wavenumber_field(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral, multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers)

The squared-wavenumber field |k|² = k₁² + k₂² + k₃² as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend. The neutral isotropic radial coordinate of spectral space: the consumer builds a Fourier symbol from it — a viscous integrating factor exp(−ν|k|²Δt), a smooth spectral envelope exp(−(|k|/k_p)²), a Germano filter 1/(1+Δ²|k|²) — by broadcasting over its field_data, then applies it with convolve. Carrying ν, Δt, and the filter width is the consumer's (invariant 12); the package supplies only the coordinate.

|k|² is summed with the true value kept at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention, invariant 5). The value is real but stored in the Complex{T} coefficient array of a ScalarFieldSpectral (imaginary part zero), exactly as spectral_cutoff stores its 0/1 mask.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution._apply_convolution! — Method
_apply_convolution!(destination_data, source_data, kernel_data, alpha, beta)

Apply the coefficientwise spectral product destination .= α · kernel · source + β · destination — the periodic convolution f ⋆ g in spectral representation, with kernel_data = ĝ. The scalar kernel_data ((Nx, Ny, Nz)) broadcasts over the fiber components of source_data, exactly as the Laplacian's squared wavenumber does, so one body serves every tensor order. Branches on iszero(beta) (overwrite vs. accumulate, invariant 10). Coefficientwise ⇒ aliasing-safe: destination may be source (or the kernel).
