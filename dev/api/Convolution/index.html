Convolution

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution — Module
Convolution

Periodic (circular) convolution of a field with a kernel, computed in spectral representation. This is the kernel-multiplier role of the package's measure structure — a grid→grid multiplier that filters a field against a fixed kernel — the spectral sibling of the differential operators and the same-base counterpart of Interpolation. The reductions, this module, and the grid↔point transfer realize one integration structure in three roles. On the torus the only convolution is the periodic one, and by the convolution theorem it is exactly a coefficientwise product of the transforms, so the operation lives in spectral representation. A kernel is supplied by its transfer function as a scalar field in spectral representation, and a scalar kernel multiplies every fiber component, so one operation serves scalar, vector, and tensor fields. The operation is named convolve (allocating infix ⋆) rather than *, because the coefficientwise product would read as a pointwise multiplication, which it is not.

Physics stays consumer-side: an LES filter, a viscous integrating factor, or a de-aliasing mask is a scalar field in spectral representation that the consumer builds from FieldSpectralMultipliers and convolves with — the package supplies the operation, not the physical meaning.

  • convolve / convolve! — convolve a field with a kernel; the in-place form adds $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulation, and convolve(g₁, g₂) composes two kernels into one.
  • exponential_convolve! / exponential_convolve — convolve with the exponential radial kernel, computed from the wavenumber arrays on the fly instead of stored as a field; the two-operand form filters a weighted combination in the same pass.
  • The kernel-composed combine! method — the weighted combination of two fields convolved with a stored kernel, in one fused pass.
  • spectral_cutoff / spectral_band — build the isotropic sharp low-pass kernel and its annular band-pass generalization.
  • squared_wavenumber_field — the squared-wavenumber radial coordinate of spectral space, as a scalar field in spectral representation, from which a consumer builds a Fourier symbol.
  • assignment_window — the linear (cloud-in-cell) mass-assignment window, for deconvolving a gridded point density.
  • translation_kernel — the kernel that translates a field by a physical displacement, the shift theorem's phase.

Convolution operations

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.convolve! — Function
convolve!(destination, field, kernel::ScalarFieldSpectral)
convolve!(destination, field, kernel::ScalarFieldSpectral, α, β)

Convolve field with the kernel whose transfer function is kernel, writing the result into destination in place and returning destination; both field and destination are in spectral representation.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating convolve returns a fresh result instead.

The arguments are:

  • field — the field to filter, in spectral representation.
  • kernel — the kernel's transfer function $\hat g$, a scalar field in spectral representation; one scalar kernel serves scalar, vector, and tensor fields.
  • destination — the field written with the result; it may alias field or kernel, since the product is coefficientwise.
  • α, β — real accumulation weights, converted to the field's scalar type; the three-argument form overwrites ($\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 0$), the five-argument form computes $\alpha (f \star g) + \beta \texttt{destination}$.

As a real physical object the periodic convolution is the kernel-weighted local average of the field $f$ about each point $x$ — a low-pass filter when the kernel $g$ is a smoothing bump:

\[(f \star g)(x) = \int_\Omega f(y) g(x - y)\,\mathrm{d}y ,\]

with $\Omega$ the periodic box and the displacement $x - y$ reduced onto the torus. On the grid the operation computes the circular sum $(f \star g)[n] = \sum_m f[m] g[n - m]$ (indices modulo the grid size) — the integral's Riemann sum without the cell volume $|\Omega|/N$, the dimensionless convention that makes a kernel act as a per-mode transfer function ($\hat g \equiv 1$ is the identity; a sharp cutoff is a projection); the continuum integral above is $|\Omega|/N$ times it. By the convolution theorem (the forward transform unnormalized) the circular sum is exactly the coefficientwise product of the transforms, which is what this operation computes — $\hat g$ = kernel, broadcast over every fiber component of $\hat f$, with the $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulate:

\[\widehat{\texttt{destination}}(k) = \alpha \hat f(k) \hat g(k) + \beta \widehat{\texttt{destination}}(k).\]

A real field needs a Hermitian kernel

The stored half-spectrum holds only the modes with $k_1 \ge 0$; the rest are implied by the reality of the field, $\hat f(-k) = \overline{\hat f(k)}$. A kernel returns a field that is still real exactly when its own transfer function carries that symmetry, $\hat g(-k) = \overline{\hat g(k)}$ — equivalently, when the physical-space kernel $g$ is real. Every kernel the package builds satisfies it: the real, even ones (spectral_cutoff, spectral_band, squared_wavenumber_field, assignment_window) trivially, and the complex translation_kernel by construction. A consumer-built kernel that breaks the symmetry yields coefficients that are those of no real field, and the inverse transform then silently keeps only their Hermitian part.

Spectral representation only: on the torus convolution is the coefficientwise spectral product.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.exponential_convolve! — Function
exponential_convolve!(destination, field, multipliers, coefficient::Real)
exponential_convolve!(destination, field, multipliers, coefficient::Real, α, β)
exponential_convolve!(destination, field, multipliers, coefficient::Real,
                      offset_scale::Real, offset::ScalarFieldSpectral)
exponential_convolve!(destination, field, multipliers, coefficient::Real,
                      offset_scale::Real, offset::ScalarFieldSpectral, α, β)
exponential_convolve!(destination, α::Real, a, β::Real, b, multipliers,
                      coefficient::Real)
exponential_convolve!(destination, α::Real, a, β::Real, b, multipliers,
                      coefficient::Real, offset_scale::Real,
                      offset::ScalarFieldSpectral)

Convolve a field with the exponential radial kernel $\exp(c |k|^2 + s \hat w)$, evaluated from the wavenumber arrays on the fly — the kernel is never built or stored as a field — writing the result into destination in place and returning destination.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating exponential_convolve returns a fresh result instead.

For a coefficient $c \le 0$ this is, as a real physical object, convolution with the periodic Gaussian (heat) kernel: the operator $e^{-c\Delta}$, which smooths the field at the length scale $\sqrt{-2c}$. In spectral representation it is the coefficientwise product with $\exp(c |k|^2)$, the true squared wavenumber kept at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention), so the kernel is real, even, and positive — a valid kernel for every real field. The optional per-mode term $s \hat w$ generalizes the exponent to a consumer-supplied symbol.

Choose between this and a stored kernel by reuse. When the exponent changes between applications — so a stored kernel would be rebuilt each time — the lazy form wins: it removes the kernel's build pass, its residency, and its per-application field read, reading three one-dimensional wavenumber arrays instead (measured $\approx 1.4\times$ over rebuild-then-convolve! on a GPU at $256^3$). When one fixed kernel is applied many times, a stored kernel with convolve! is the faster route: the per-mode exponential costs more than the field read it saves ($\approx 1.1\times$ on a GPU; $\approx 1.7\times$ on a CPU, where the exponential is expensive and is evaluated once per fiber component). The two-operand forms win on both backends — $\approx 1.5\times$ over the sequential combine!-then-convolve! pair, except the CPU two-operand exponential at $\approx 1.1\times$ — fusing away the combination's destination write and re-read as well.

On a CPU a deeply damped kernel has one further Float32 cost cliff: modes whose factor lands in the subnormal range — exponent arguments $c \lvert k\rvert^2 + s \hat w$ between about $-103$ and $-87$ — are produced through a hardware assist at roughly $16\times$ the usual per-element cost, so a kernel deep enough to cross that band pays a multiple of its whole pass. The Float64 band (arguments below about $-708$) is beyond any physical kernel, and the GPU backends flush subnormals to zero, so both are unaffected; where the band matters, use Float64 or a milder exponent.

The arguments are:

  • destination — the field written with the result; it may alias field (or a, b, offset), since the product is coefficientwise.
  • field — the field to filter, in spectral representation.
  • α, β — in the single-operand forms, where they trail the argument list, the usual accumulation weights: the result is written as $\alpha (\text{filtered } f) + \beta\,\texttt{destination}$.
  • α, a, β, b — in the two-operand forms, where they lead it, the real weights and fields of the combination $\alpha a + \beta b$ the kernel is applied to, exactly as combine! takes them; with b === destination the filtered accumulate runs in place. The two roles never share a signature, which is why one pair of glyphs can serve both.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for the field's grid; the kernel is evaluated from their squared-wavenumber family.
  • coefficient — the real factor $c$ on $|k|^2$ in the exponent, dimensionless as received (the consumer folds its own physical factors before the call).
  • offset_scale, offset — the real scale $s$ and the per-mode field $\hat w$ of the optional exponent term $s \hat w$; offset is a scalar field in spectral representation holding real values (as squared_wavenumber_field does). The pair enters as scale times field so a changed scale costs nothing — the resident $\hat w$ is built once.
The offset must be real-valued

The exponential of a real, even symbol keeps a real field real. A complex-valued offset breaks that Hermitian symmetry exactly as a consumer-built kernel can under convolve!, and the inverse transform then silently keeps only the Hermitian part. There is deliberately no scalar exponent offset: a constant $s_0$ in the exponent is $e^{s_0}$ times the kernel and belongs in $α$.

The single-operand accumulate form computes

\[\widehat{\texttt{destination}}(k) = \alpha \exp\bigl(c |k|^2 + s \hat w(k)\bigr) \hat f(k) + \beta \widehat{\texttt{destination}}(k),\]

Spectral representation only.

The two-operand form

Passing α, a, β, b ahead of the multipliers applies the same kernel to a weighted combination, $\exp(c |k|^2 + s \hat w) \cdot (\alpha \hat a + \beta \hat b)$, in one pass — the lazy-kernel twin of the kernel-composed combine! method, and the same fusion the kernel-composed divergence! and helmholtz_projection! arities perform. With b === destination it is the filtered accumulate, computed in place.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.spectral_cutoff — Function
spectral_cutoff(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral,
                multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers, wavenumber_cutoff)

Build an isotropic sharp low-pass kernel as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend: the transfer function is $1$ for $|k| \le k_c$ and $0$ above it.

Convolve a field with it for the ideal low-pass projection, the basis of spectral truncation and de-aliasing. It is idempotent ($\hat g^2 = \hat g$), hence a projection, and it is the low-pass special case of spectral_band with $k_\mathrm{lo} = 0$.

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the kernel matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked against reference.
  • wavenumber_cutoff — the cutoff wavenumber $k_c$; a mode is kept when $|k| \le k_c$.

\[\hat g(k) = \mathbf{1}\bigl[ |k| \le k_c \bigr].\]

The choice of cutoff, such as a $2/3$ de-aliasing rule or an LES filter scale, is the consumer's. The squared wavenumber $|k|^2$ keeps the true value at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention).

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.spectral_band — Function
spectral_band(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral,
              multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers,
              wavenumber_low, wavenumber_high)

Build an isotropic sharp spectral band-pass kernel as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend: the transfer function is $1$ on the wavenumber shell and $0$ outside it.

Convolve a field with it to keep an isotropic shell of wavenumbers — a de-aliasing band, a forcing band, or any shell selector. It is idempotent ($\hat g^2 = \hat g$), hence a projection, and it is the annular generalization of spectral_cutoff, which is the low-pass case $k_\mathrm{lo} = 0$.

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the kernel matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked against reference.
  • wavenumber_low, wavenumber_high — the inner and outer edges $k_\mathrm{lo}$, $k_\mathrm{hi}$ of the retained shell.

\[\hat g(k) = \begin{cases} 1, & k_\mathrm{lo} \le |k| \le k_\mathrm{hi}, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases}\]

with $|k|^2$ keeping the true value at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention). The band edges are the consumer's choice.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.squared_wavenumber_field — Function
squared_wavenumber_field(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral,
                         multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers)

Return the squared wavenumber $|k|^2$ at every mode as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend.

Use it as the neutral radial coordinate of spectral space: a consumer builds a Fourier symbol from it — a viscous integrating factor $\exp(-\nu |k|^2 \Delta t)$, a smooth spectral envelope, or a subgrid test filter of the dynamic Germano procedure (an LES closure choice) — by broadcasting over its field_data and applying it with convolve. The package supplies the coordinate, while $\nu$ and $\Delta t$ (a viscosity and a step size — physics) and any filter width stay consumer-side. For a symbol of the exponential family specifically, exponential_convolve! applies $\exp(c |k|^2 + s \hat w)$ straight from the wavenumber arrays, with no kernel field built at all.

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the result matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked for compatibility with reference.

\[|k|^2 = k_1^2 + k_2^2 + k_3^2 ,\]

summed with the true value kept at the Nyquist frequency (the Laplacian convention). The value is real but is stored in the Complex{T} coefficient array of a scalar field in spectral representation, its imaginary part zero, exactly as spectral_cutoff stores its $0/1$ mask.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.assignment_window — Function
assignment_window(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral,
                  multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers)

Return the linear (cloud-in-cell) mass-assignment window as a scalar field in spectral representation, on reference's grid and array backend, for deconvolving a gridded point density.

Use it when a pair correlation is estimated from points deposited on a grid rather than counted directly. Depositing $M$ points on the grid with the positive linear assignment (LalescuSpline(0, 0), the 2-point linear kernel used for a density) spreads each point's mass over a tent, so the gridded density is the true density $\rho$ convolved with this window, $\rho_g = \rho \star W$. Dividing the transform by $\hat W$ — a convolve by $1/\hat W$ — undoes that smearing before you estimate the radial distribution function $g(r)$ (the pair density at separation $r$ relative to an uncorrelated uniform gas).

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the window matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked against reference.

As a real physical object $W$ is the linear B-spline ("tent") cloud, the tensor product of per-axis tents:

\[W(x) = \prod_{a=1}^{3} \frac{1}{\Delta x_a} \max\Bigl(0, 1 - \frac{|x_a|}{\Delta x_a}\Bigr),\]

each a tent of half-width one cell $\Delta x_a$ along axis $a$, of unit integral and peaked at the origin (a cell-wide top-hat convolved with itself, hence the squared sinc). The function returns its transfer function $\hat W(k)$, the squared sinc

\[\begin{gathered} \hat W(k) = \prod_{a=1}^{3} \operatorname{sinc}^2\Bigl(\tfrac{k_a \Delta x_a}{2}\Bigr), \\ \operatorname{sinc}(y) = \frac{\sin y}{y}, \end{gathered}\]

computed from the per-axis wavenumber $k_a$ (kept at the Nyquist frequency) and the grid spacing $\Delta x_a$, so $\hat W(0) = 1$ and it decays toward the grid scale. Because the squared sinc does not vanish anywhere in the resolved band, dividing by it is stable. The package supplies the window; the density estimate and the correlation normalization stay consumer-side.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution.translation_kernel — Function
translation_kernel(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral,
                   multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers,
                   offset::NTuple{3, Real})

Build the kernel that translates a field by the physical displacement $h$ = offset, as a scalar field in spectral representation on reference's grid and array backend: convolving a field $f$ with it gives the displaced field $f(x - h)$, the position $x$ reduced onto the periodic box.

Use it to move a field onto a displaced grid without re-gridding — a shift-and-average filter, the relocation of a probe, or the displaced evaluation of a phase-shift de-aliasing scheme. Apply it with convolve / convolve!, which is allocation-free in place; because a scalar kernel multiplies every fiber component, one call translates a whole vector or tensor field, and because the kernel depends on nothing but the displacement, one kernel serves every field it is applied to (its own cost is a single pass over the coefficients). To sample the field at the displaced positions $x + h$ instead, pass the negated offset — the two are inverse translations. This is the package's one complex kernel: on every resolved mode it is a pure phase of unit modulus, so it moves the field without touching any amplitude, unlike the real and even spectral_cutoff or assignment_window.

  • reference — a field in spectral representation whose grid and backend the kernel matches.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber arrays for that grid, checked against reference.
  • offset — the displacement $h = (h_1, h_2, h_3)$, a physical length per axis in the units of the box rather than a count of cells; a half-cell displacement is 0.5 .* grid.grid_spacing on the Grid3D the field lives on.

As a real physical object the translation is the convolution with a Dirac delta displaced to $h$ and periodized on the box, $g = \delta(x - h)$, whose sifting property (integrating against a delta evaluates the integrand at the delta's location) gives $(f \star g)(x) = f(x - h)$ exactly. On the grid that $g$ is the band-limited (periodic-sinc) interpolation stencil centred at $h$, so the convolution re-samples the trigonometric interpolant of $f$ at the displaced nodes $x_n - h$, and collapses to a plain index shift when $h$ is a whole number of cells. Its transfer function $\hat g$ at wavenumber $k$ is the shift theorem's phase, assembled per axis from that wavenumber's component $k_a$ and the displacement $h_a$ along the axis:

\[\hat g(k) = e^{-\mathrm{i} k \cdot h} = \prod_{a=1}^{3} \bigl[ \cos(k_a h_a) - \mathrm{i} \sin(k_a h_a) \bigr].\]

The two factors draw on the package's two wavenumber families: the even cosine takes the true wavenumber $k_a$ and the odd sine the Nyquist-zeroed derivative wavenumber. These coincide on every resolved mode — so the factor there is the pure phase $e^{-\mathrm{i} k_a h_a}$ above — and part only at an even axis's Nyquist frequency, where the warning below applies.

At the Nyquist frequency the phase becomes a real attenuation

On an even-length axis the Nyquist mode of a real field carries a single real amplitude that stands for both of the wavenumbers $\pm\pi/\Delta x_a$ (with $\Delta x_a$ the grid spacing along axis $a$), so its band-limited form is the cosine $\cos(\pi x_a/\Delta x_a)$, and displacing that cosine re-samples on the grid to $\cos(\pi h_a/\Delta x_a)$ times itself. At that one frequency the kernel therefore carries this real factor rather than a complex phase — and it has to be real: the Nyquist mode is its own conjugate partner, so a complex factor there would leave coefficients that are those of no real field at all. A displaced Nyquist mode is thus attenuated rather than moved: it vanishes for a half-cell displacement and changes sign for an odd whole-cell one. Two consequences follow, both confined to that frequency: the translation is not invertible there (displacing by $h$ and back scales the mode by $\cos^2$), and two translations compose exactly on every resolved mode but not at Nyquist. Whole-cell displacements are exact everywhere, and a field with no Nyquist content — one truncated by a de-aliasing cutoff — never meets the caveat at all.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — the coefficientwise convolution, combination, and exponential-kernel bodies.

GBGPeriodicFields.Convolution._apply_convolution! — Method
_apply_convolution!(destination_data, source_data, kernel_data, alpha, beta)

Apply the coefficientwise spectral product that realizes the periodic convolution in spectral representation, writing $\texttt{dst} \mathrel{.=} \alpha \hat g \cdot \hat f + \beta \texttt{dst}$ ($\texttt{dst}$ the destination_data, $\hat f$ the source_data, $\hat g$ = kernel_data). The scalar kernel_data, of shape (Nx, Ny, Nz), broadcasts over the fiber components of source_data exactly as the Laplacian's $|k|^2$ does, so one body serves every tensor order. The call branches on iszero(beta) to overwrite or accumulate. The product is coefficientwise, so it is aliasing-safe: destination_data may be source_data or the kernel.
