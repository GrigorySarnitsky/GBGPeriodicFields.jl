Interpolation

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation — Module
Interpolation

Transfer between the grid sections of Fields and the point sections of Points: evaluating a field at off-grid positions, and depositing per-point quantities back onto the grid. The two operations are formal adjoints of each other. The transfer uses the local spline interpolation of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) as portable gather/scatter kernels (CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU, via KernelAbstractions; the scatter accumulates atomically); the method, terminology, and GPU rationale are in docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §10, and the point-value data types and their fiberwise algebra live in the Points module.

API

  • Interpolation (grid to point, gather): interpolate(f, xs[, scheme]) / in-place interpolate!(out, f, xs[, scheme]), plus the callable f(x) (and its broadcast f.(xs)) as the ergonomic face of interpolate.
  • Anterpolation (point to grid, scatter): anterpolate(grid, xs, qs[, scheme]) / in-place anterpolate!(field, xs, qs[, scheme]), with a five-argument α/β form (β = 0 overwrite, β ≠ 0 accumulate, the mul! convention; accumulation is the common case for the scatter).

interpolate / anterpolate! are the symmetric named pair (the operators 𝓘 = W / 𝓘* = Wᵀ); only interpolation also carries the pointwise callable f(x), because evaluation is pointwise while its adjoint is not. Both directions share one scheme (the same weights) — which is what makes them an adjoint pair.

Why interpolation and anterpolation are adjoint: the function–measure duality

𝓘 : G → P and 𝓘* : P → G satisfy ⟨𝓘 f, q⟩_P = ⟨f, 𝓘* q⟩_G, a duality between functions (which you evaluate) and measures (which you integrate against):

  • evaluation is pairing with a Dirac mass, f(x) = ⟨δₓ, f⟩, so (𝓘 f)ₚ = ⟨δ_{xₚ}, f⟩ = f(xₚ);
  • its adjoint cannot be pointwise — it returns a measure, a sum of point masses 𝓘* q = Σₚ qₚ δ_{xₚ}, regularized by the stencil kernel to Σₚ qₚ β(·−xₚ), a point source density.

Hence the API asymmetry: you evaluate functions (f(x) returns a value) but accumulate measures (anterpolate! adds a source density onto a field); there is no pointwise f(x) = q. Discretely, with G = ℝᴺ (N grid nodes) and P = ℝᴹ (M points), interpolation is the matrix 𝓘 = W (Wₚₙ = βₙ(xₚ)) and anterpolation its transpose 𝓘* = Wᵀ. With shared partition-of-unity weights (𝓘 𝟙 = 𝟙, i.e. Σₙ Wₚₙ = 1), anterpolation is the exact transpose of interpolation and the total deposited quantity is conserved,

Σₙ (𝓘* q)ₙ = ⟨𝟙, 𝓘* q⟩ = ⟨𝓘 𝟙, q⟩ = ⟨𝟙, q⟩ = Σₚ qₚ,

which is mandatory for the two-way-coupled parcel application (total water mass and latent heat conserved). This holds only when both directions use the same weights, so the design fixes anterpolation as the transpose/adjoint of whatever interpolation scheme is in use (mismatched weights inject a spurious source — Sundaram & Collins 1996). The inner products are a modelling choice: with the Euclidean (counting) products the adjoint is the bare transpose Wᵀ; with L²/volume-weighted products it is M_G⁻¹ Wᵀ M_P (the immersed-boundary spreading/interpolation pair of Peskin), conserving an energy pairing rather than the plain sum.

source

The Interpolation module is the grid↔point transfer layer: interpolation (grid → point, the gather 𝓘 = W) evaluates a field at off-grid positions, and its adjoint anterpolation (point → grid, the scatter 𝓘* = Wᵀ) deposits per-point quantities back onto the grid. Both use the local high-order splines of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) — the smooth LalescuSpline (two-point Hermite, default CatmullRom, pinned bit-for-bit to TurTLE) and the C⁰ LagrangeSpline, under a shared AbstractSplineScheme — so interpolation and anterpolation are exact transposes and conserve the deposited total (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §10). The kernels are portable across CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU (KernelAbstractions); the point-data types live in the Points module.

Types

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LagrangeSpline — Type
LagrangeSpline(n; T = Float64)

The C⁰ Lagrange hierarchy of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) (TurTLE's Lagrange_polys): the unique degree-2n+1 polynomial interpolating all 2n+2 stencil nodes at offsets {−n,…,n+1} (n ≥ 0). The per-axis weights are the Lagrange cardinal polynomials, stored in the (2n+2)×(2n+2) SMatrix coefficients. Reproduces polynomials up to degree 2n+1 exactly, but is only C⁰ (kinks at the nodes); for smooth derivatives use LalescuSpline instead. n = 0 is linear interpolation.

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LalescuSpline — Type
LalescuSpline(n, m = 1; T = Float64)

Smooth (Cᵐ) local spline interpolation of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010), pinned bit-for-bit to TurTLE's beta_n{n}_m{m} (TurTLE's interp_neighbours = n, smoothness = m). Over a compact stencil of 2n+2 nodes at offsets {−n,…,n+1} (n ≥ 0, the finite-difference half-width), the per-axis weights βq(t) for fractional cell position t ∈ [0,1) are the coefficients of the two-point Hermite interpolant of degree 2m+1: the polynomial that reproduces the field value and the derivatives of order 1…m at the two bracketing nodes (offsets 0 and 1), each derivative estimated by a centred finite difference over the 2n+2-node stencil (Fornberg weights). Smoothness is Cᵐ with 0 ≤ m ≤ 2n; the weights are stored in the (2n+2)×(2m+2) SMatrix coefficients (βq(t) = Σₚ coefficients[q, p+1] tᵖ).

It reproduces polynomials up to degree min(2m+1, 2n) (for n ≥ 1). Special cases: m = 0 is linear interpolation over the two central nodes (independent of n); n = 1, m = 1 is the Catmull–Rom spline (the default CatmullRom, quadratic precision). For the C⁰ Lagrange hierarchy (degree 2n+1, all 2n+2 nodes interpolated) use LagrangeSpline. Used by interpolate (grid → point) and anterpolate (point → grid).

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.CatmullRom — Constant
CatmullRom

The default interpolation scheme: the cubic n = 1, m = 1 LalescuSpline, a four-point C¹ stencil that reproduces the Catmull–Rom spline (and quadratic polynomials exactly).

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.anterpolate! — Function
anterpolate!(field::AbstractFieldPhysical, points::PointSet, quantities::AbstractPointValues,
             scheme = CatmullRom, α = true, β = false)

Deposit the per-point quantities onto field in-place — the adjoint (transpose) of interpolate! under the same scheme — computing field = α·(Wᵀ quantities) + β·field; returns field. With β = 0 (default) the field is overwritten; with β ≠ 0 the deposit is accumulated (the common case for the two-way-coupling scatter). field and quantities must share tensor order and points must cover the same number of points. Runs as a portable atomic-scatter kernel on the field's array backend.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.interpolate! — Function
interpolate!(out::AbstractPointValues, field::AbstractFieldPhysical, points::PointSet, scheme = CatmullRom)

Interpolate field (physical-space representation) to the off-grid points, writing into out; returns out.

out must match field in tensor order and cover the same number of points as points. The transfer runs as a portable gather kernel on the field's array backend (CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU). Interpolation is defined for physical-space representation only; transform a spectral field to physical space first.

GBGPeriodicFields.Fields.AbstractFieldPhysical — Method
(field::AbstractFieldPhysical)(x; scheme = CatmullRom)

Evaluate field at a single off-grid position x (a length-3 indexable), returning a scalar, an SVector{3}, or an SMatrix{3,3} for scalar/vector/second-order-tensor fields. A convenience wrapper over interpolate (it builds a one-point set and runs the gather kernel); for many points, call interpolate with a PointSet instead.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractSplineScheme — Type
AbstractSplineScheme{N2, M2, T, L}

Supertype of the local interpolation schemes — the two hierarchies of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010): the smooth LalescuSpline and the C⁰ LagrangeSpline. A scheme is a tensor product of 1D weight polynomials over a compact stencil of N2 nodes, each weight a degree-(M2−1) polynomial in the fractional cell position t ∈ [0,1). The N2 × M2 coefficient matrix ([node, power]) is precomputed once into an SMatrix so it passes into the gather/scatter kernels by value (no device array).

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._axis_weights — Method
_axis_weights(coefficients, t)

Evaluate the per-axis spline weights βq(t) for all N2 stencil nodes at fractional position t, returning an SVector{N2}. Horner over the M2 polynomial-coefficient columns of coefficients (M2 = degree + 1); pure and allocation-free, so it runs inside the gather/scatter kernels on CPU and GPU.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._fornberg — Method
_fornberg(evaluation_point, nodes, max_order)

Fornberg (1988) finite-difference weights: weights[i, k+1] is the weight of nodes[i] in the order-k derivative (k = 0…max_order) of a function sampled at nodes, evaluated at evaluation_point. Host-side helper for the spline-weight construction.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._hermite_weight_matrix — Method
_hermite_weight_matrix(n, m)

Build the (2n+2)×(2m+2) matrix of smooth-spline weight-polynomial coefficients (row q is the weight polynomial for stencil offset q − 1 − n ∈ {−n,…,n+1}; column p+1 is the tᵖ coefficient). Each weight has degree 2m+1; together they form the two-point Hermite interpolant reproducing the value and the order-1…m derivatives at the two bracketing nodes (offsets 0 and 1), the derivatives taken from centred finite differences over the 2n+2-node stencil. See LalescuSpline; pinned bit-for-bit against TurTLE's beta_n{n}_m{m}.

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._lagrange_weight_matrix — Method
_lagrange_weight_matrix(n)

Build the (2n+2)×(2n+2) matrix of Lagrange cardinal-polynomial coefficients over the stencil offsets {−n,…,n+1} (row q is the cardinal polynomial of node q; column p+1 is the tᵖ coefficient). Each is the degree-2n+1 polynomial that is one at its own node and zero at the others (the inverse of the offsets' Vandermonde matrix). See LagrangeSpline.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._locate — Method
_locate(coordinate, origin, spacing, number_of_grid_points)

Return (base_cell, t) for one axis: the zero-based index base_cell of the left node of the cell containing coordinate, and the fractional position t ∈ [0,1) within it. Periodic wrapping of the stencil is applied later via mod. Pure scalar arithmetic, kernel-safe.
