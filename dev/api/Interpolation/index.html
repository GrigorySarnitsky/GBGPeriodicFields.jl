Interpolation

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation — Module
Interpolation

Transfer between the grid sections of Fields and the point sections of Points: interpolation evaluates a field at off-grid positions, and its adjoint anterpolation deposits per-point quantities back onto the grid. A PointSet holds the positions and an AbstractPointValues the per-point values.

Interpolation (interpolate) is the gather $W$, the linear map that evaluates a field $f$ at the points: its value $(Wf)_p = f(x_p)$ at the $p$-th point is $f$ at that point's position $x_p$. Anterpolation (anterpolate) is the scatter $W^\top$, the adjoint (transpose) of $W$ and likewise linear: it spreads per-point quantities $q$ (with $q_p$ the value carried at $x_p$) back onto the surrounding grid nodes with stencil weights, returning a whole field whose value $(W^\top q)(x)$ is what it deposits at grid position $x$. The two are exact transposes — pairing the gathered values against $q$ over the $M$ points equals pairing $f$ against the scattered field over the $N$ grid cells. Written with the normalized inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ (inner, the fiber contraction averaged over its base), the factors $M$ and $N$ undo its $1/M$ and $1/N$:

\[M \langle Wf, q \rangle_{\text{pts}} = \sum_p (Wf)_p \cdot q_p = \sum_x f(x) \cdot (W^\top q)(x) = N \langle f, W^\top q \rangle_{\text{grid}}.\]

This holds because $W$ and $W^\top$ share the same stencil weights. Because those weights sum to one (a partition of unity), anterpolation conserves the total it deposits — the property that coupling a quantity back onto the flow needs. Conservation holds only when both directions use the same weights; mismatched weights inject a spurious source (Sundaram & Collins 1996). Interpolation yields a value while its adjoint returns a field, the one asymmetry in the API. Each scheme's own docstring gives the gather and scatter weights it applies.

A transfer scheme (an AbstractTransferScheme) fixes the weights and the representation it acts on. Two families ship: the physical-domain local splines of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) (portable KernelAbstractions gather/scatter kernels on CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU, with an atomic scatter; the default CatmullRom), and the spectral-domain exact DirectDFT / fast NUFFT that act on a field in spectral representation. The point-value data types and their fiberwise algebra live in the Points module.

API

  • Interpolation (grid → point, gather; interpolate $\equiv W$): interpolate and the in-place interpolate!, plus the callable f(x) (and its broadcast f.(xs)) as the ergonomic face of a single-point gather.
  • Anterpolation (point → grid, scatter; anterpolate $\equiv W^\top$): anterpolate and the in-place anterpolate!, the latter with an $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulate form ($\beta = 0$ overwrite, $\beta \ne 0$ accumulate, the mul! convention; accumulation is the common case for the scatter).

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.
  • S. Sundaram, L. R. Collins, Numerical considerations in simulating a turbulent suspension of finite-volume particles, J. Comput. Phys. 124(2) (1996) 337–350.

Transfer operations

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.interpolate! — Function
interpolate!(out, field::AbstractFieldPhysical, points, scheme = CatmullRom)
interpolate!(out, field::AbstractFieldSpectral, points, scheme::DirectDFT)
interpolate!(out, field::AbstractFieldSpectral, points, scheme::NUFFT)

Interpolate field to the off-grid points, writing the gathered values into out; returns out. This is the in-place home of the gather $W$; for a freshly allocated result use interpolate, and for a single position the callable field(x).

The scheme (an AbstractTransferScheme) selects the transfer kernel and the representation it acts on. The physical-domain local splines (CatmullRom and the other LalescuSpline / LagrangeSpline) gather the nodal values of a physical-space field over a compact stencil, while DirectDFT and NUFFT evaluate a field in spectral representation in the Fourier basis; each scheme's own docstring gives the weights it applies.

out must match field in tensor order (scalar, vector, or second-order tensor) and cover the same number of points as points. The transfer runs as a portable gather kernel on the field's array backend (CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU). The spline schemes are exact at grid nodes and for polynomials up to the scheme's reproduction degree and approximate otherwise; the spectral schemes reconstruct the band-limited field exactly (DirectDFT) or to a set tolerance (NUFFT).

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.anterpolate! — Function
anterpolate!(field, points, quantities, scheme = CatmullRom, α = true, β = false)

Deposit (scatter) the per-point quantities onto field in place — the adjoint (transpose) of interpolate! under the same scheme — computing $\mathrm{field} = \alpha (W^\top q) + \beta \mathrm{field}$; returns field. This is the in-place home of the scatter $W^\top$; for a freshly allocated result use anterpolate.

Because it applies the same weights as the matching gather, anterpolation is the exact transpose, and with the weights' partition of unity the total deposited quantity is conserved. Other communities name this operation spreading (immersed-boundary methods), deposition or charge assignment (particle-in-cell), projection (particle-source-in-cell), or gridding (nonuniform FFTs); anterpolation — the adjoint of interpolation — follows Brandt & Lubrecht (1990). The scheme (an AbstractTransferScheme) selects the transfer kernel and the representation: the physical-domain local splines (CatmullRom and the other LalescuSpline / LagrangeSpline) scatter onto a physical-space field, while DirectDFT and NUFFT deposit into a field in spectral representation; each scheme's own docstring gives the weights.

  • α scales the deposited contribution.
  • β sets the accumulation: $\beta = 0$ (default) overwrites field, $\beta \ne 0$ accumulates onto it (the mul! convention; accumulation is the common case for a two-way-coupling scatter).

field and quantities must share tensor order (scalar, vector, or second-order tensor), and points must cover the same number of points. The transfer runs as a portable scatter kernel on the field's array backend (CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU).

Reference: A. Brandt, A. A. Lubrecht, Multilevel matrix multiplication and fast solution of integral equations, J. Comput. Phys. 90(2) (1990) 348–370 (an early use of "anterpolation" for the adjoint of interpolation).

Transfer-scheme types

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractTransferScheme — Type
AbstractTransferScheme

Supertype of all grid↔point transfer schemes — the parameter that fixes how the adjoint pair interpolate $= W$ / anterpolate $= W^\top$ is realized. A scheme is a choice of kernel and representation: the physical-domain local splines (AbstractSplineScheme — gather/scatter on the nodal values) and the spectral-domain exact transfers (DirectDFT and NUFFT — evaluate/deposit in the Fourier basis, acting on a field in spectral representation). Same verbs, one adjointness contract; the schemes differ only in (kernel, representation).

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LalescuSpline — Type
LalescuSpline(n, m = 1; T = Float64)

Smooth ($C^m$) local spline interpolation of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010), pinned bit-for-bit to TurTLE's beta_n{n}_m{m} (TurTLE's interp_neighbours = n, smoothness = m). Over a compact stencil of $2n{+}2$ nodes at offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$ ($n \ge 0$, the finite-difference half-width), the per-axis weights $w_q(t)$ for fractional cell position $t \in [0, 1)$ are the coefficients of the two-point Hermite interpolant of degree $2m{+}1$: the polynomial that reproduces the field value and the derivatives of order $1, \dots, m$ at the two bracketing nodes (offsets $0$ and $1$), each derivative estimated by a centred finite difference over a $2n{+}1$-node stencil about that node (Fornberg weights). Smoothness is $C^m$ with $0 \le m \le 2n$; the weights are stored in the $(2n{+}2) \times (2m{+}2)$ SMatrix coefficients ($w_q(t) = \sum_p \mathrm{coefficients}[q, p{+}1] t^p$).

It reproduces polynomials up to degree $\min(2m{+}1, 2n)$ (for $n \ge 1$). Special cases: $m = 0$ is linear interpolation over the two central nodes (independent of $n$); $n = 1, m = 1$ is the Catmull–Rom spline (the default CatmullRom, quadratic precision). For the $C^0$ Lagrange hierarchy (degree $2n{+}1$, all $2n{+}2$ nodes interpolated) use LagrangeSpline. Used by interpolate (grid → point) and anterpolate (point → grid).

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.
  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Bramas, M. Rampp, M. Wilczek, An efficient particle tracking algorithm for large-scale parallel pseudo-spectral simulations of turbulence (TurTLE), Comput. Phys. Commun. 278 (2022) 108406; arXiv:2107.01104. Code: gitlab.mpcdf.mpg.de/TurTLE/turtle.
  • B. Fornberg, Generation of finite difference formulas on arbitrarily spaced grids, Math. Comp. 51(184) (1988) 699–706.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.CatmullRom — Constant
CatmullRom

The default interpolation scheme: the cubic $n = 1, m = 1$ LalescuSpline, a four-point $C^1$ stencil that reproduces the Catmull–Rom spline (and quadratic polynomials exactly).

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LagrangeSpline — Type
LagrangeSpline(n; T = Float64)

Local, $C^0$, Lagrange interpolation: the unique degree-$2n{+}1$ polynomial through the $2n{+}2$ nearest grid nodes (the stencil offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$, $n \ge 0$). The per-axis weights are the Lagrange cardinal polynomials, stored in the $(2n{+}2) \times (2n{+}2)$ SMatrix coefficients. It reproduces polynomials up to degree $2n{+}1$ exactly, but is only $C^0$ (the interpolant kinks at the nodes); for smooth derivatives use LalescuSpline instead. $n = 0$ is linear interpolation over the two bracketing nodes. Matches TurTLE's Lagrange_polys.

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Bramas, M. Rampp, M. Wilczek, An efficient particle tracking algorithm for large-scale parallel pseudo-spectral simulations of turbulence (TurTLE), Comput. Phys. Commun. 278 (2022) 108406; arXiv:2107.01104. Code: gitlab.mpcdf.mpg.de/TurTLE/turtle.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.DirectDFT — Type
DirectDFT()

The exact spectral-domain transfer scheme: it interpolates and anterpolates by the literal $O(NM)$ discrete Fourier sum over the field's stored Fourier modes $k$ and the $M$ points ($x_p$ the $p$-th point position, $q_p$ the value carried there), with $N = N_x N_y N_z$ the grid-point count — no window, no oversampling, no FFT. It is the definition of the spectral-domain adjoint pair, so it serves both as a transparent exact baseline and as the oracle that validates the fast NUFFT; for many points or modes, prefer NUFFT. It acts on a field in spectral representation:

\[\begin{aligned} f(x_p) &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k} \hat f(k) e^{\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{\text{stored }k} \alpha(k) \mathrm{Re}\!\bigl(\hat f(k) e^{\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)}\bigr), \\ (W^\top q)(k) &= \sum_p q_p e^{-\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)}, \end{aligned}\]

reconstructing the real field ($o$ the grid origin) from the stored rfft half-spectrum with the Parseval weight $\alpha(k) = 1$ on the self-conjugate $k_x = 0$ plane (and the even-$N_x$ Nyquist plane) and $2$ elsewhere. Interpolation reproduces the band-limited field exactly, so it is pointwise divergence-free (unlike the local splines) and matches to_physical at the grid nodes; anterpolation matches to_spectral from grid-node samples. It is stateless — build it as DirectDFT() and pass it as the scheme.

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.NUFFT — Type
NUFFT(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral; tol = 1e-10, oversampling = 2, width = 0, beta = 0)

The fast, approximate spectral-domain transfer scheme: the $O(N\log N + M)$ windowed-gridding (nonuniform FFT) counterpart of DirectDFT for evaluating a field in spectral representation at scattered points and depositing point values back, agreeing with DirectDFT to a tolerance tol. It spreads the transfer onto an oversampled grid through a smooth compact window and takes a single FFT, so it is far cheaper than the exact $O(NM)$ sum once the point and mode counts grow; for a few points or modes, DirectDFT is simpler and exact.

Build a plan once from a reference spectral field, which fixes the grid, layout, element type, and tensor order, then pass it as the scheme to interpolate / anterpolate on fields like the reference. The options:

  • tol — the target accuracy; the window width and shape default to the values that reach it.
  • oversampling — the linear oversampling factor of the work grid (default 2).
  • width — the gridding stencil width in nodes; 0 (default) picks it from tol.
  • beta — the window shape parameter; 0 (default) picks it from the width.

It agrees with DirectDFT (hence to_physical / to_spectral at the grid nodes) to tol on the resolved sub-Nyquist band (the rfft Nyquist plane is projected out), shares its adjoint relation, and like it reconstructs the band-limited field (divergence-free).

Warning

A NUFFT plan holds an oversampled work grid of about $\sigma^3$ times the field's memory (eight times at the default oversampling $\sigma = 2$) for its whole lifetime, which matters on a memory-tight GPU.

References

  • A. H. Barnett, J. Magland, L. af Klinteberg, A parallel nonuniform fast Fourier transform library based on an exponential of semicircle kernel, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 41(5) (2019) C479–C504.

Internal API

The scheme supertype AbstractSplineScheme, the spline-weight generators (Fornberg finite differences + a small linear solve), the periodic cell-finding, and the in-kernel helpers — documented for contributors.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractSplineScheme — Type
AbstractSplineScheme{N2, M2, T, L}

Supertype of the physical-domain local interpolation schemes — the two hierarchies of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010): the smooth LalescuSpline and the $C^0$ LagrangeSpline. Each acts on a field in physical-space representation, gathering and scattering its nodal values over a compact $(2n{+}2)^3$ stencil around the cell $c$ that contains a point $x_p$. With $t_a$ the point's fractional position along axis $a$ and $w$ the scheme's per-axis weight polynomials, interpolation is the tensor-product gather

\[f(x_p) = \sum_{i_1, i_2, i_3} w_{i_1}(t_1) w_{i_2}(t_2) w_{i_3}(t_3)\; f\bigl(x_{c + (i_1, i_2, i_3)}\bigr),\]

and anterpolation is its transpose, adding $w_{i_1}(t_1) w_{i_2}(t_2) w_{i_3}(t_3) q_p$ (with $q_p$ the value carried at $x_p$) onto each of those same nodes. Concretely a scheme is a tensor product of 1D weight polynomials: a compact stencil of $N_2 = 2n{+}2$ nodes, each weight a degree-$(M_2-1)$ polynomial in the fractional cell position $t \in [0, 1)$. The $N_2 \times M_2$ coefficient matrix ([node, power]) is precomputed once into an SMatrix, so it passes into the gather/scatter kernels by value (no device array). One of the two families of AbstractTransferScheme; the spectral-domain DirectDFT / NUFFT are the other.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._axis_weights — Method
_axis_weights(coefficients, t)

Evaluate the per-axis spline weights $w_q(t)$ for all N2 stencil nodes at fractional position $t$, returning an SVector{N2}. Horner over the M2 polynomial-coefficient columns of coefficients ($M_2 = \deg + 1$); pure and allocation-free, so it runs inside the gather/scatter kernels on CPU and GPU.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._fornberg — Method
_fornberg(evaluation_point, nodes, max_order)

Fornberg (1988) finite-difference weights: weights[i, k+1] is the weight of nodes[i] in the order-$k$ derivative ($k = 0, 1, \dots$ up to max_order) of a function sampled at nodes, evaluated at evaluation_point. Host-side helper for the spline-weight construction.

Reference: B. Fornberg, Generation of finite difference formulas on arbitrarily spaced grids, Math. Comp. 51(184) (1988) 699–706.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._hermite_weight_matrix — Method
_hermite_weight_matrix(n, m)

Build the $(2n{+}2) \times (2m{+}2)$ matrix of smooth-spline weight-polynomial coefficients (row q is the weight polynomial for stencil offset $q - 1 - n \in \{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$; column p+1 is the $t^p$ coefficient). Each weight has degree $2m{+}1$; together they form the two-point Hermite interpolant reproducing the value and the order-$1, \dots, m$ derivatives at the two bracketing nodes (offsets $0$ and $1$), the derivatives taken from centred finite differences over a $2n{+}1$-node stencil about each bracketing node. See LalescuSpline; pinned bit-for-bit against TurTLE's beta_n{n}_m{m}.

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._lagrange_weight_matrix — Method
_lagrange_weight_matrix(n)

Build the $(2n{+}2) \times (2n{+}2)$ matrix of Lagrange cardinal-polynomial coefficients over the stencil offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$ (row q is the cardinal polynomial of node q; column p+1 is the $t^p$ coefficient). Each is the degree-$2n{+}1$ polynomial that is one at its own node and zero at the others (the inverse of the offsets' Vandermonde matrix). See LagrangeSpline.
