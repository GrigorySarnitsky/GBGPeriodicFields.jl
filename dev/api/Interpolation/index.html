Interpolation

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation — Module
Interpolation

Transfer between the grid sections of Fields and the point sections of Points: interpolation evaluates a field at off-grid positions, and its adjoint anterpolation deposits per-point quantities back onto the grid. A PointSet holds the positions and an AbstractPointValues the per-point values.

Interpolation (interpolate) is the gather $W$, the linear map that evaluates a field $f$ at the points: its value $(Wf)_p = f(x_p)$ at the $p$-th point is $f$ at that point's position $x_p$. Anterpolation (anterpolate) is the scatter $W^\top$, the adjoint (transpose) of $W$ and likewise linear: it spreads per-point quantities $q$ (with $q_p$ the value carried at $x_p$) back onto the surrounding grid nodes with stencil weights, returning a whole field whose value $(W^\top q)(x)$ is what it deposits at grid position $x$. The two are exact transposes — pairing the gathered values against $q$ over the $M$ points equals pairing $f$ against the scattered field over the $N$ grid cells. Written with the normalized inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ (inner, the fiber contraction averaged over its base), the factors $M$ and $N$ undo its $1/M$ and $1/N$:

\[\begin{aligned} M \langle Wf, q \rangle_{\mathrm{pts}} &= \sum_p (Wf)_p \cdot q_p \\ &= \sum_x f(x) \cdot (W^\top q)(x) = N \langle f, W^\top q \rangle_{\mathrm{grid}}. \end{aligned}\]

Here the subscripts name the two mean-normalized inner products — over the $M$ sample points and over the $N$ grid nodes. This holds because each scheme's scatter applies its own gather's transpose — the same stencil weights for the local splines, the conjugate Fourier sum for the spectral pair. Because those weights sum to one (a partition of unity), anterpolation conserves the total it deposits — the property that coupling a quantity back onto the flow needs. That is a property of the local-spline schemes, whose weights sum to one at every position, and it holds for DirectDFT too, by a different route: a constant is band-limited, so the exact spectral gather reproduces it exactly, which makes the equivalent nodal weights sum to one; and the deposited zero mode is exactly $\sum_p q_p$, which is the conservation statement. Only NUFFT falls short, and only by its tolerance. Conservation holds only when both directions use the same weights; mismatched weights inject a spurious source (Sundaram & Collins 1996). The pair is symmetric in name and in adjointness, and asymmetric in three places, listed together because each is easy to meet as a surprise: a gather yields point values while its adjoint returns a field; the $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulation weights are taken by the scatter alone; and only the gather carries the callable sugar f(x), evaluation being pointwise where its adjoint is not. Each scheme's own docstring gives the gather and scatter weights it applies.

A transfer scheme (an AbstractTransferScheme) fixes the weights and the representation it acts on. Two families ship: the physical-domain local splines of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) (the default CatmullRom), which act on a field in physical representation, and the spectral-domain exact DirectDFT / fast NUFFT that act on a field in spectral representation. The point-value data types and their fiberwise algebra live in the Points module.

The parts are:

  • Interpolation (grid → point, gather; interpolate $\equiv W$): interpolate and the in-place interpolate!, plus the callable f(x) (and its broadcast f.(xs)) as the ergonomic face of a single-point gather, which the physical-domain spline schemes carry and the spectral ones do not.
  • Anterpolation (point → grid, scatter; anterpolate $\equiv W^\top$): anterpolate and the in-place anterpolate!, the latter with an $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulate form ($\beta = 0$ overwrite, $\beta \ne 0$ accumulate, the mul! convention; accumulation is the common case for the scatter).
  • Scheme persistence. write_scheme and read_scheme map a scheme to and from one self-describing HDF5 group, so a checkpoint restores not only where a point set samples but how (methods load with the HDF5 package, as a weak-dependency extension). A scheme is a parameter record rather than a section, so the group carries attributes and no dataset: the weights are derived and recomputed on read. NUFFT is excluded by design — it owns plans built against a reference field, so it is rebuilt rather than stored.

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.
  • S. Sundaram, L. R. Collins, Numerical considerations in simulating a turbulent suspension of finite-volume particles, J. Comput. Phys. 124(2) (1996) 337–350.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.interpolate! — Function
interpolate!(out::AbstractPointValues, field::AbstractFieldPhysical,
             points::PointSet, scheme::AbstractSplineScheme = CatmullRom)
interpolate!(out::AbstractPointValues, field::AbstractFieldSpectral,
             points::PointSet, scheme::DirectDFT)
interpolate!(out::AbstractPointValues, field::AbstractFieldSpectral,
             points::PointSet, scheme::NUFFT)

Interpolate field to the off-grid points, writing the gathered values into out; returns out. This is the in-place home of the gather $W$, and for a single position there is the callable field(x).

Use this in-place form when out is already allocated; the allocating interpolate returns a fresh result instead.

The arguments are:

  • out — the point-value section written with the gathered values. It must match field in tensor order and cover the same number of points as points. The local-spline transfer is one of the few layers that serves tensor orders above the usual ceiling: it carries any order the field types can hold, third-order tensors included.
  • field — the field to sample, in the representation the scheme acts on.
  • points — the PointSet of positions to evaluate at.
  • scheme — an AbstractTransferScheme, selecting the transfer kernel and the representation it acts on. The physical-domain local splines (CatmullRom and the other LalescuSpline / LagrangeSpline) gather the nodal values of a physical-space field over a compact stencil, while DirectDFT and NUFFT evaluate a field in spectral representation in the Fourier basis; each scheme's own docstring gives its weights.
  • order — an optional visit order from transfer_order: a permutation of the point indices saying which point each work item handles. It changes only the sequence in which points are visited, never the result — the gather is bitwise unchanged — and is worth several times the throughput when the points are in an order unrelated to the grid. It must name every point exactly once and live in the same storage family as the field; nothing, the default, visits them in index order.

The spline schemes are exact at grid nodes and for polynomials up to the scheme's reproduction degree and approximate otherwise; the spectral schemes reconstruct the band-limited field exactly (DirectDFT) or to a set tolerance (NUFFT).

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.anterpolate! — Function
anterpolate!(field::AbstractFieldPhysical, points::PointSet,
             quantities::AbstractPointValues,
             scheme::AbstractSplineScheme = CatmullRom,
             α::Real = true, β::Real = false)
anterpolate!(field::AbstractFieldSpectral, points::PointSet,
             quantities::AbstractPointValues, scheme::DirectDFT,
             α::Real = true, β::Real = false)
anterpolate!(field::AbstractFieldSpectral, points::PointSet,
             quantities::AbstractPointValues, scheme::NUFFT,
             α::Real = true, β::Real = false)

Deposit (scatter) the per-point quantities onto field in place — the adjoint (transpose) of interpolate! under the same scheme — computing $\texttt{field} = \alpha (W^\top q) + \beta \texttt{field}$; returns field. This is the in-place home of the scatter $W^\top$.

Use this in-place form when field is already allocated; the allocating anterpolate returns a fresh result instead.

Because it applies the same weights as the matching gather, anterpolation is the exact transpose. For a local-spline scheme the weights are also a partition of unity — at any position they sum to exactly one — and then the total deposited quantity is conserved. Other communities name this operation spreading (immersed-boundary methods), deposition or charge assignment (particle-in-cell), projection (PSI-Cell, particle-source-in-cell), or gridding (nonuniform FFTs); anterpolation — the adjoint of interpolation — follows Brandt & Lubrecht (1990).

The arguments are:

  • field — the field deposited onto, in the representation the scheme acts on. It shares its tensor order with quantities.
  • points — the PointSet the quantities sit at, covering as many points as quantities.
  • quantities — the per-point values to deposit.
  • scheme — an AbstractTransferScheme, selecting the transfer kernel and the representation: the physical-domain local splines (CatmullRom and the other LalescuSpline / LagrangeSpline) scatter onto a physical-space field, while DirectDFT and NUFFT deposit into a field in spectral representation; each scheme's own docstring gives the weights.
  • α — scales the deposited contribution.
  • β — sets the accumulation: $\beta = 0$ (default) overwrites field, $\beta \ne 0$ accumulates onto it (the mul! convention; accumulation is the common case for a two-way-coupling scatter — a deposit that accumulates onto a field the same points also sample).
  • order — an optional visit order from transfer_order: a permutation of the point indices saying which point each work item handles. It changes only the sequence in which points are deposited, and since the accumulation is atomic its summation order was never fixed to begin with, so the result moves by no more than it already does between runs. It must name every point exactly once and live in the same storage family as the field; nothing, the default, visits them in index order.

Reference: A. Brandt, A. A. Lubrecht, Multilevel matrix multiplication and fast solution of integral equations, J. Comput. Phys. 90(2) (1990) 348–370 (an early use of "anterpolation" for the adjoint of interpolation).

Visit order

An order is an option on the transfer operations above, not an operation on the sections themselves: it changes the sequence in which the points are visited and never the result.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.transfer_order — Function
transfer_order(grid::Grid3D, points::PointSet; tile_size = 4)

Build a visit order for points on grid: a permutation of 1:M that groups points falling in the same block of grid cells, so a transfer that follows it reads and writes with spatial locality.

Pass it as the order keyword of interpolate!, anterpolate! or their allocating siblings. It is an option — the same call without it is unchanged — and moves no data: order[i] names the point that work-item i handles, so points and any PointValues indexed alongside them keep their own indexing, and every result lands in the slot it would have anyway.

Use it when the points are in an order unrelated to the grid — a set that has been advected long enough to decorrelate index from position is the case it is for. It is not free, and it is not always a gain. Points still in a lattice or sweep order are already local, and ordering them costs a few percent on top of a build that never repays; the measured span across seedings runs from $0.93 imes$ to $12 imes$, so what the option repairs is a BAD order rather than improving a good one. Measure your own case if the points are not well mixed.

The gather is bitwise unchanged by the order; the scatter's atomic summation order changes, as it already does between runs.

The arguments are:

  • grid: the grid whose cells define the blocks, and whose origin the points are measured from. It leads, as it does in anterpolate: transfer_order is transfer infrastructure, so it follows the transfer verbs' argument order, not the motion verbs' trailing-grid one.
  • points: the point set to order. Its storage family decides where the ordering is built and returned — a device point set gives a device vector, built by device kernels.
  • tile_size: the target block edge in grid cells (default 4). Each axis is divided into $\lceil N / \texttt{tile\_size} \rceil$ blocks, so the block is exactly tile_size cells wide only when it divides the grid, and slightly narrower otherwise. The bucket count falls as the cube of the edge, and with it both the build time and the working memory: at $256^3$ the default builds in $\approx 0.1$ ms against 3 MiB of scratch, while tile_size = 1 — one bucket per cell — takes $14\times$ the time and 192 MiB, for no better locality. The optimum tracks the BACKEND, not the interpolation scheme — measured across stencil widths 4 and 8, a device is flat in the tile while a CPU gathers faster with coarser blocks (up to about 16) — which is why this option is a block size and not a scheme: one order serves any scheme and both transfer directions, and the scheme does not determine the best tile.

Points outside the grid are clamped into the edge blocks rather than refused, since an order is a hint and never changes a result.

An order goes stale as points move

The order describes where the points were when it was built. A consumer whose points move must decide how often to rebuild it — this package holds no notion of time (see the manual), so the cadence is the consumer's. A stale order is never wrong, only progressively less useful.

Cost is one pass over the points, a cumulative sum over the buckets, and the validation above — $\approx 0.27$ ms against an ordered gather of 0.71 at $256^3$ with $10^6$ points, so roughly a third of one transfer, and well inside what a single ordered transfer saves. It allocates its result and three working arrays, and two of those scale with the GRID rather than the points — the bucket histogram and its cumulative sum, the latter at twice the width. That is what tile_size prices: 3 MiB of scratch at the default on a $256^3$ grid, 192 MiB at tile_size = 1.

Rebuilding this on a device grows the pool

Every call allocates about 15 MiB at $256^3$ with $10^6$ points, and on a device those allocations are freed by Julia's collector, which runs on host pressure and cannot see them — a hundred rebuilds leave roughly 1.5 GiB resident. A consumer rebuilding the order as its points move should either rebuild it rarely, since a stale order stays valid, or reclaim between rebuilds. There is no in-place form yet.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.TransferOrder — Type
TransferOrder(indices)

A validated visit order: a permutation of 1:M saying which point each work item of a transfer handles. Build one with transfer_order, or wrap your own permutation by calling this constructor, which checks it.

The type exists because the guarantee has to be carried rather than asserted. An order that is the right length but not a permutation is not a detectable mistake at the point of use: it silently leaves some points unvisited and others visited twice, so a gather returns whatever was in the unwritten slots and a scatter quietly loses part of the total, while an out-of-range entry indexes past the end of an array inside @inbounds.

Checking that on every transfer would cost a pass and an allocation on the hot path. Checking it once, here, costs the same work where the order is built and leaves the transfers nothing to do but read a field they can trust — validity as a property of the type, in the way that a section owning its storage is what makes aliasing safe by construction.

The constructor throws an ArgumentError unless indices names every point in 1:M exactly once. It accepts any integer vector, including a UnitRange such as 1:M, and keeps the storage family it is given — build a device order from device points, since a transfer will not mix families.

Transfer-scheme types

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractTransferScheme — Type
AbstractTransferScheme

Supertype of all grid↔point transfer schemes — the parameter that fixes how the adjoint pair interpolate $= W$ (the gather that evaluates a field at each point) / anterpolate $= W^\top$ (its transpose, the scatter that deposits per-point values onto the grid) is realized. A scheme is a choice of kernel and representation: the physical-domain local splines (AbstractSplineScheme — gather/scatter on the nodal values) and the spectral-domain transfers (the exact DirectDFT and the fast, approximate NUFFT — evaluate/deposit in the Fourier basis, acting on a field in spectral representation). Same verbs, one adjointness contract, with the one non-uniformity documented below; the schemes differ only in (kernel, representation).

One place that uniformity does not reach is the allocating anterpolate's leading argument, which names what the fresh destination is built from: a Grid3D suffices for the spline schemes, whose result is a field in physical representation, while the spectral schemes take a whole field in spectral representation, their destination needing that representation's layout, backend and element type too. Code polymorphic over the allocating form therefore has to branch; the in-place anterpolate! forms, where the caller owns the destination, are uniform.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LalescuSpline — Type
LalescuSpline(n, m = 1; T = Float64)

Smooth ($C^m$) local spline interpolation of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010), pinned bit-for-bit to the beta_n{n}_m{m} weights of TurTLE, the reference pseudospectral turbulence solver with Lagrangian particle tracking (its interp_neighbours = n, smoothness = m). Over a compact stencil of $2n{+}2$ nodes at offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$ ($n \ge 0$, the first argument — the finite-difference half-width), the per-axis weights $w_q(t)$ for fractional cell position $t \in [0, 1)$ are the coefficients of the two-point Hermite interpolant of degree $2m{+}1$: the polynomial that reproduces the field value and the derivatives of order $1, \dots, m$ at the two bracketing nodes (offsets $0$ and $1$), each derivative estimated by a centred finite difference over a $2n{+}1$-node stencil about that node (Fornberg weights). The second argument m sets the smoothness, $C^m$ with $0 \le m \le 2n$. That bound makes the default $m = 1$ valid only for $n \ge 1$, so the one-argument LalescuSpline(0) is rejected rather than silently repaired: the smallest stencil admits $m = 0$ alone, spelled LalescuSpline(0, 0) (the linear scheme). The weights are stored in the $(2n{+}2) \times (2m{+}2)$ SMatrix coefficients ($w_q(t) = \sum_r \texttt{coefficients}[q, r{+}1] t^r$, the exponent $r$ running over the polynomial degrees).

The T keyword sets the element type the weight coefficients are stored in (Float64 by default).

It reproduces polynomials up to degree $\min(2m{+}1, 2n)$ (for $n \ge 1$) wherever the whole stencil samples one branch of that polynomial — a periodic base wraps the stencil at the boundary, and the wrapped samples come from the opposite edge, which no non-periodic polynomial continues. Special cases: $m = 0$ is linear interpolation over the two central nodes (independent of $n$); $n = 1, m = 1$ is the Catmull–Rom spline (the default CatmullRom, quadratic precision). For the $C^0$ Lagrange hierarchy (degree $2n{+}1$, all $2n{+}2$ nodes interpolated) use LagrangeSpline. Used by interpolate (grid → point) and anterpolate (point → grid).

References

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LagrangeSpline — Type
LagrangeSpline(n; T = Float64)

Local, $C^0$, Lagrange interpolation: the unique degree-$2n{+}1$ polynomial through the $2n{+}2$ nearest grid nodes (the stencil offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$, $n \ge 0$). The per-axis weights are the Lagrange cardinal polynomials, stored in the $(2n{+}2) \times (2n{+}2)$ SMatrix coefficients. The n argument is the stencil half-width: it fixes both the stencil, which reaches n nodes below a point and n + 1 above it, and the degree, so a larger n buys reproduction of a higher-degree polynomial at the cost of a wider gather. It reproduces polynomials up to degree $2n{+}1$ exactly wherever the whole stencil samples one branch of that polynomial (a wrapped stencil draws from the opposite edge, which no non-periodic polynomial continues), but is only $C^0$ (the interpolant kinks at the nodes); for smooth derivatives use LalescuSpline instead. The case $n = 0$ is linear interpolation over the two bracketing nodes. Matches Lagrange_polys of TurTLE, the reference pseudospectral turbulence solver with Lagrangian particle tracking. The T keyword sets the element type the weight coefficients are stored in (Float64 by default).

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.DirectDFT — Type
DirectDFT()

The exact spectral-domain transfer scheme: it interpolates and anterpolates by the literal $O(NM)$ discrete Fourier sum over the field's stored Fourier modes $k$ and the $M$ points ($x_p$ the $p$-th point position, $q_p$ the value carried there), with $N = N_x N_y N_z$ the grid-point count — none of the fast scheme's apparatus (no spreading window, no oversampled grid, no FFT — the machinery of NUFFT, which it validates as the oracle). It is the definition of the spectral-domain adjoint pair and the transparent exact baseline; for many points or modes, prefer NUFFT. It acts on a field in spectral representation:

\[\begin{aligned} f(x_p) &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k} \hat f(k) e^{\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{\text{stored }k} \alpha(k) \mathrm{Re}\bigl(\hat f(k) e^{\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)}\bigr), \\ (W^\top q)(k) &= \sum_p q_p e^{-\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)}, \end{aligned}\]

The second form reconstructs the real field ($o$ the grid origin) from the stored rfft half-spectrum with the Parseval weight $\alpha(k) = 1$ on the self-conjugate planes — the zero plane of the folded rfft axis and, on an even-length folded axis, its Nyquist plane — and $2$ elsewhere. The first equality reads an even axis's Nyquist amplitude split across $\pm k$ — its band-limited cosine form, which is what the stored-$k$ sum with the weight $\alpha(k)$ implements; sub-Nyquist, and at the grid nodes, the two sides agree with no convention needed.

Interpolation reproduces the band-limited field exactly, so it is pointwise divergence-free on the resolved sub-Nyquist band — the frequencies this grid represents, each even axis's Nyquist plane excluded — where the local splines are not, and matches to_physical at the grid nodes; anterpolation matches to_spectral from grid-node samples. It is stateless — build it as DirectDFT() and pass it as the scheme.

Anterpolating off-node points does not give a real field's spectrum at an even axis's Nyquist

The deposit $(W^\top q)(k)$ is evaluated at every stored mode, the Nyquist planes included, and it is the exact adjoint of the gather there. But an even-length axis identifies its Nyquist mode with its own negative, so a real field's coefficients must satisfy $\hat g(-k) = \overline{\hat g(k)}$ within that plane, and the sum above meets that condition only when every point sits on a grid node of that axis: off-node, the two sides differ by $e^{-2\mathrm{i} k_a x_{p,a}} \ne 1$. The deposited array is then the half-spectrum of no real field, and to_physical — which is the Hermitian projector — silently keeps only its Hermitian part, so a transform round trip does not return it unchanged. This is the same hazard translation_kernel documents for its Nyquist factor, and it is why NUFFT projects that plane out; DirectDFT deliberately does not, keeping the literal adjoint instead. The resolved sub-Nyquist band is unaffected, which is the band a de-aliased spectral computation uses.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.NUFFT — Type
NUFFT(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral;
      tol = 1e-10, oversampling = 2, width = 0, beta = 0)

The fast, approximate spectral-domain transfer scheme: the $O(N\log N + M)$ windowed-gridding (nonuniform FFT) counterpart of DirectDFT for evaluating a field in spectral representation at scattered points and depositing point values back, agreeing with DirectDFT to a tolerance tol. It runs the transfer through an oversampled work grid and a single FFT — spreading through a smooth compact window then transforming for the deposit, and the reverse for the evaluation, deconvolving the coefficients before the transform and gathering through the window after it — so it is far cheaper than the exact $O(NM)$ sum once the point and mode counts grow; for a few points or modes, DirectDFT is simpler and exact.

Build a plan once from a reference field in spectral representation, which fixes the grid, layout, element type, and tensor order, then pass it as the scheme to interpolate / anterpolate on fields like the reference. The arguments are:

  • reference — a field in spectral representation, fixing the grid, layout and element type the plan is built for.
  • tol — the target accuracy; the window width and shape default to the values that reach it. It is a target, not a guaranteed bound: it must be finite and positive, and a value finer than the element type can resolve widens the window as far as the formula goes and then saturates at that resolution rather than erroring.
  • oversampling — the linear oversampling factor $\sigma$ of the work grid (default 2); must be at least 2. That is an implementation bound rather than a theoretical one — lower upsampling is standard with a correspondingly wider window — because the width and shape formulas here are fitted for $\sigma \ge 2$; a value of 1 leaves no spectral guard band, so the gridding breaks down.
  • width — the gridding stencil width in nodes; 0 (default) picks it from tol.
  • beta — the window shape parameter, unrelated to the accumulation weight $\beta$ the transfer verbs take; 0 (default) picks it from the width.

It agrees with DirectDFT (hence to_physical / to_spectral at the grid nodes) to tol on the resolved sub-Nyquist band (the rfft Nyquist plane is projected out), shares its adjoint relation, and like it reconstructs the band-limited field (divergence-free on the resolved sub-Nyquist band — the package's derivative wavenumbers are zeroed at an even axis's Nyquist frequency while this scheme evaluates the true phase there, so a discretely divergence-free field carrying Nyquist content is not pointwise divergence-free in the continuum).

A plan costs about eight times the field in memory, for its whole lifetime

A NUFFT plan holds an oversampled work grid of about $\sigma^3$ times the field's memory (eight times at the default oversampling $\sigma = 2$) for its whole lifetime, which matters on a memory-tight GPU.

Reference: A. H. Barnett, J. Magland, L. af Klinteberg, A parallel nonuniform fast Fourier transform library based on an exponential of semicircle kernel, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 41(5) (2019) C479–C504.

Scheme persistence

Serialize a transfer scheme to, and reconstruct it from, an HDF5 group, so a checkpoint restores not only where a point set samples but how. Provided by a package extension, available only when the HDF5 package is loaded; the core carries no HDF5 dependency. A scheme is a parameter record rather than a section, so its group holds attributes and no dataset: the weight coefficients are derived from the parameters and recomputed on read. NUFFT is deliberately excluded — it owns FFT plans built against a reference field, so it is rebuilt rather than stored, and passing one throws.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.write_scheme — Function
write_scheme(parent::HDF5.H5DataStore, name::AbstractString,
             scheme::AbstractTransferScheme)

Persist a grid↔point transfer scheme — the choice of kernel and representation that fixes how interpolate and anterpolate are realized — as a new HDF5 group name under parent, returning the group it created. Use it when a checkpoint must restore not only the positions and values of a point set but also how that set samples a field, so a restarted run transfers identically rather than falling back to a default. Reload with read_scheme.

The group is self-describing and holds no dataset: a scheme's weight coefficients are derived from its parameters, so the group carries a type tag, a schema version, the weight element type, and the defining parameters — the neighbourhood $n$ and, for LalescuSpline, the smoothness $m$. Reading reconstructs through the ordinary constructor, which recomputes the weights and revalidates the parameters, so a group asserting an impossible combination is refused rather than producing a malformed scheme. The sugar parent[name] = scheme is equivalent. CatmullRom needs no identity of its own: it is LalescuSpline(1, 1) and round-trips as one, comparing equal to the alias.

Because parent is a handle you opened and own, this never opens or closes a file, and the package attaches no notion of time — the consumer bundles a snapshot and owns any time / iteration attributes.

The arguments are:

  • parent — an open HDF5 File or Group to create the new subgroup under.
  • name — the name of the group to create.
  • scheme — the scheme to write: a LalescuSpline, a LagrangeSpline, or DirectDFT.
`NUFFT` is deliberately not persistable

Every other scheme is its parameters, which is what makes a few attributes a faithful record. A NUFFT is not: it owns oversampled FFT plans and a deconvolution window built against a particular reference field, so a stored copy would be either a large duplicate of derived data or a set of parameters pretending to be the object. It is the same class as FieldTransformPlans, which is likewise rebuilt rather than moved when storage families change. Rebuild it from the reference field the consumer restores anyway — NUFFT(reference; tol) — and write the tol in the consumer's own snapshot if it varies. Passing one here throws an ArgumentError naming this.

Provided by a package extension, so it is available only when HDF5 is loaded.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.read_scheme — Function
read_scheme(parent::HDF5.H5DataStore, name::AbstractString)

Reconstruct the transfer scheme written by write_scheme from the HDF5 group name under parent, dispatching on the stored type attribute to return it in the matching type. The reload is exact: the reconstructed scheme compares equal (==, and in fact ===) to the one written, because a scheme is fully determined by the parameters the group stores and the constructor recomputes the weights identically.

Here parent is an open HDF5 File or Group holding the group, and name is the name (or path) of that group under parent. The group's schema is checked, not merely read: a file from a newer schema version, a group missing a parameter its type needs, an unrecognized type tag, or a parameter combination the scheme's own constructor rejects all raise an ArgumentError rather than producing a scheme that is not what the file claims.

Provided by a package extension, so it is available only when HDF5 is loaded.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — the scheme supertype AbstractSplineScheme, the spline-weight generators (Fornberg finite differences plus a small linear solve), the periodic cell-finding, and the compatibility checks the transfer verbs route through.

Types

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractSplineScheme — Type
AbstractSplineScheme{N2, M2, T, L}

Supertype of the physical-domain local interpolation schemes over one shared stencil: the smooth LalescuSpline of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) and the $C^0$ LagrangeSpline, the classical Lagrange interpolant of the same nodes, matched to TurTLE's Lagrange_polys. Each acts on a field in physical representation, gathering and scattering its nodal values over a compact $(2n{+}2)^3$ stencil around the cell $c$ that contains a point $x_p$. With $t_a$ the point's fractional position along axis $a$ and $w$ the scheme's per-axis weight polynomials, interpolation is the tensor-product gather

\[f(x_p) = \sum_{i_1, i_2, i_3} w_{i_1}(t_1) w_{i_2}(t_2) w_{i_3}(t_3) f\bigl(x_{c + (i_1, i_2, i_3)}\bigr),\]

and anterpolation is its transpose, adding $w_{i_1}(t_1) w_{i_2}(t_2) w_{i_3}(t_3) q_p$ (with $q_p$ the value carried at $x_p$) onto each of those same nodes. Concretely a scheme is a tensor product of 1D weight polynomials: a compact stencil of $N_2 = 2n{+}2$ nodes, each weight a degree-$(M_2-1)$ polynomial in the fractional cell position $t \in [0, 1)$. The $N_2 \times M_2$ coefficient matrix ([node, power]) is precomputed once into an SMatrix, so it passes into the gather/scatter kernels by value (no device array). One of the two families of AbstractTransferScheme; the spectral-domain DirectDFT / NUFFT are the other. Only the spline family has a shared supertype: its members differ in their weights alone and so share a parametric interface, while a stateless DirectDFT and a plan-carrying NUFFT share no structure a supertype could hold.

Reference: C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._check_nufft_compatibility — Method
_check_nufft_compatibility(scheme::NUFFT, field::AbstractFieldSpectral)

Check that a NUFFT plan may be used on field: same base, Fourier layout, scalar type, storage family and tensor order.

The plan precomputes index maps, an oversampled work grid and a deconvolution for one specific context. Applying it to a field from another context is not a shape error that surfaces on its own — a same-order field on a different domain has identical storage, so it silently receives the wrong deconvolution and the wrong index maps.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._check_transfer_colocation — Method
_check_transfer_colocation(field, points, values)

Check that the three operands of a grid↔point transfer carry the same scalar type and live in the same storage family, throwing otherwise.

A transfer selects its launch backend from the field and then hands the point and value arrays to that one kernel, so a host/device mixture reaches a launch that can only see one of them, and a mixed precision reaches an internal locator that requires one scalar type. Both are public input errors, and without this check both surfaced from inside the implementation instead of at the call.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._hermite_weight_matrix — Method
_hermite_weight_matrix(n, m)

Build the $(2n{+}2) \times (2m{+}2)$ matrix of smooth-spline weight-polynomial coefficients (row $q$ is the weight polynomial for stencil offset $q - 1 - n \in \{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$; column $r+1$ is the $t^r$ coefficient). Each weight has degree $2m{+}1$; together they form the two-point Hermite interpolant reproducing the value and the order-$1, \dots, m$ derivatives at the two bracketing nodes (offsets $0$ and $1$), the derivatives taken from centred finite differences over a $2n{+}1$-node stencil about each bracketing node. See LalescuSpline; pinned bit-for-bit against TurTLE's beta_n{n}_m{m}.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._lagrange_weight_matrix — Method
_lagrange_weight_matrix(n)

Build the $(2n{+}2) \times (2n{+}2)$ matrix of Lagrange cardinal-polynomial coefficients over the stencil offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$ (row $q$ is the cardinal polynomial of node $q$; column $r+1$ is the $t^r$ coefficient). Each is the degree-$2n{+}1$ polynomial that is one at its own node and zero at the others (the inverse of the offsets' Vandermonde matrix). See LagrangeSpline.
