Interpolation

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation — Module
Interpolation

Transfer between the grid sections of Fields and the point sections of Points: interpolation evaluates a field at off-grid positions, and its adjoint anterpolation deposits per-point quantities back onto the grid. A PointSet holds the positions and an AbstractPointValues the per-point values.

Interpolation (interpolate) is the gather $W$, the linear map that evaluates a field $f$ at the points: its value $(Wf)_p = f(x_p)$ at the $p$-th point is $f$ at that point's position $x_p$. Anterpolation (anterpolate) is the scatter $W^\top$, the transpose of $W$, its adjoint for the unnormalized sums: it spreads per-point quantities $q$ (with $q_p$ the value carried at $x_p$) back onto the surrounding grid nodes with stencil weights, returning a whole field whose value $(W^\top q)(x)$ is what it deposits at grid position $x$. The two are exact transposes — pairing the gathered values against $q$ over the $M$ points equals pairing $f$ against the scattered field over the $N$ grid cells. Written with the normalized inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ (inner, the fiber contraction averaged over its base), the factors $M$ and $N$ undo its $1/M$ and $1/N$:

\[\begin{aligned} M \langle Wf, q \rangle_{\mathrm{pts}} &= \sum_p (Wf)_p \cdot q_p \\ &= \sum_x f(x) \cdot (W^\top q)(x) = N \langle f, W^\top q \rangle_{\mathrm{grid}}. \end{aligned}\]

The middle sum, over the $N$ grid nodes, is the physical-representation form. For the spectral schemes, whose scatter is the conjugate transpose, the same identity holds with the mode-wise pairing, one number by Parseval — which is why inner on a field in spectral representation returns the physical value.

Here the subscripts name the two mean-normalized inner products — over the $M$ sample points and over the $N$ grid nodes. This holds because each scheme's scatter applies its own gather's transpose — the same stencil weights for the local splines, the conjugate Fourier sum for the spectral pair. Because those weights sum to one (a partition of unity), anterpolation conserves the total it deposits — the property that coupling a quantity back onto the flow needs. That is a property of the local-spline schemes, whose weights sum to one at every position, and it holds for DirectDFT too, by a different route: a constant is band-limited, so the exact spectral gather reproduces it exactly, which makes the equivalent nodal weights sum to one; and the deposited zero mode is exactly $\sum_p q_p$, which is the conservation statement. Only NUFFT falls short, and only by its tolerance. Conservation holds only when both directions use the same weights; mismatched weights inject a spurious source (Sundaram & Collins 1996). The pair is symmetric in name and in adjointness, and asymmetric in three places, listed together because each is easy to meet as a surprise: a gather yields point values while its adjoint returns a field; the $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulation weights are taken by the scatter alone; and only the gather carries the callable sugar f(x), evaluation being pointwise where its adjoint is not. Each scheme's own docstring gives the gather and scatter weights it applies.

A transfer scheme (an AbstractTransferScheme) fixes the weights and the representation it acts on. Two families ship: the physical-domain local splines of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) (such as CatmullRom), which act on a field in physical representation, and the spectral-domain exact DirectDFT / fast NUFFT that act on a field in spectral representation. The point-value data types and their fiberwise algebra live in the Points module.

The parts are:

  • Interpolation (grid → point, gather; interpolate, $W$): interpolate and the in-place interpolate!, plus the callable f(x) (and its broadcast f.(xs)) as the ergonomic face of a single-point gather, which the physical-domain spline schemes carry and the spectral ones do not.
  • Anterpolation (point → grid, scatter; anterpolate, $W^\top$): anterpolate and the in-place anterpolate!, the latter with an $\alpha$/$\beta$ accumulate form ($\beta = 0$ overwrite, $\beta \ne 0$ accumulate, the mul! convention; accumulation is the common case for the scatter).
  • Traversal order. point_traversal_order builds a PointTraversalOrder, a permutation saying which point each work item of a transfer handles, so that neighbouring work touches neighbouring memory. It is an option on the four verbs above and never changes their result. Points move, and an order goes stale as they do, so point_traversal_order! rebuilds one in place against a PointTraversalOrderScratch, allocating nothing that scales with the point count.
  • Scheme persistence. write_scheme and read_scheme map a scheme to and from one self-describing HDF5 group, so a checkpoint restores not only where a point set samples but how (methods load with the HDF5 package, as a weak-dependency extension). A scheme is a parameter record rather than a section, so the group carries attributes and no dataset: the weights are derived and recomputed on read. NUFFT is excluded by design — it owns plans built against a reference field, so it is rebuilt rather than stored.

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.
  • S. Sundaram, L. R. Collins, Numerical considerations in simulating a turbulent suspension of finite-volume particles, J. Comput. Phys. 124(2) (1996) 337–350.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.interpolate! — Function
interpolate!(out::AbstractPointValues, field::AbstractFieldPhysical,
             points::PointSet, scheme::AbstractSplineScheme;
             traversal = nothing)
interpolate!(out::AbstractPointValues, field::AbstractFieldSpectral,
             points::PointSet, scheme::DirectDFT)
interpolate!(out::AbstractPointValues, field::AbstractFieldSpectral,
             points::PointSet, scheme::NUFFT; traversal = nothing)

Interpolate field to the off-grid points, writing the gathered values into out; returns out. This is the in-place home of the gather $W$, and for a single position there is the callable field(x). The Interpolation module docstring explains the pair.

Use this in-place form when out is already allocated; the allocating interpolate returns a fresh result instead.

Arguments

  • out — the point-value section written with the gathered values. It must match field in tensor order and cover the same number of points as points. The local-spline transfer serves any tensor order the field types hold, order 3 included.
  • field — the field to sample, in the representation the scheme acts on.
  • points — the PointSet of positions to evaluate at.
  • scheme — an AbstractTransferScheme, selecting the transfer kernel and the representation it acts on. The physical-domain local splines (CatmullRom and the other LalescuSpline / LagrangeSpline) gather the nodal values of a physical-space field over a compact stencil, while DirectDFT and NUFFT evaluate a field in spectral representation in the Fourier basis; each scheme's own docstring gives its weights.
  • traversal — an optional traversal order from point_traversal_order: a permutation of the point indices saying which point each work item handles. It changes only the sequence in which points are visited — the gather is bitwise unchanged — and is worth several times the throughput when the points are in an order unrelated to the grid. It must name every point exactly once and live in the same array family as the field; nothing, the default, visits them in index order.

The spline schemes are exact at grid nodes and for polynomials up to the scheme's reproduction degree and approximate otherwise; the spectral schemes reconstruct the band-limited field exactly (DirectDFT) or to a set tolerance (NUFFT).

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.anterpolate! — Function
anterpolate!(field::AbstractFieldPhysical, points::PointSet,
             quantities::AbstractPointValues,
             scheme::AbstractSplineScheme[, α::Real, β::Real];
             traversal = nothing)
anterpolate!(field::AbstractFieldSpectral, points::PointSet,
             quantities::AbstractPointValues, scheme::DirectDFT[,
             α::Real, β::Real])
anterpolate!(field::AbstractFieldSpectral, points::PointSet,
             quantities::AbstractPointValues, scheme::NUFFT[,
             α::Real, β::Real]; traversal = nothing)

Deposit (scatter) the per-point quantities onto field in place — the transpose of interpolate! under the same scheme, conjugated for a spectral one — computing $\texttt{field} = \alpha (W^\top q) + \beta \texttt{field}$; returns field. This is the in-place home of the scatter $W^\top$.

Use this in-place form when field is already allocated; the allocating anterpolate returns a fresh result instead.

Because it applies the same weights as the matching gather, anterpolation is the exact transpose; the Interpolation module docstring explains the pair. For a local-spline scheme the weights also sum to exactly one at any position (a partition of unity), so the total deposited quantity is conserved. Other communities name this operation spreading (immersed-boundary methods), deposition or charge assignment (particle-in-cell), projection (PSI-Cell, particle-source-in-cell), or gridding (nonuniform FFTs); anterpolation follows Brandt & Lubrecht (1990).

Arguments

  • field — the field deposited onto, in the representation the scheme acts on. It shares its tensor order with quantities.
  • points — the PointSet the quantities sit at, covering as many points as quantities.
  • quantities — the per-point values to deposit.
  • scheme — an AbstractTransferScheme, selecting the transfer kernel and the representation: the physical-domain local splines (CatmullRom and the other LalescuSpline / LagrangeSpline) scatter onto a physical-space field, while DirectDFT and NUFFT deposit into a field in spectral representation; each scheme's own docstring gives the weights.
  • α — scales the deposited contribution.
  • β — sets the accumulation: $\beta = 0$ (default) overwrites field, $\beta \ne 0$ accumulates onto it (the trailing weights mirror LinearAlgebra.mul!(C, A, B, α, β); accumulation is the common case for a two-way-coupling scatter — a deposit that accumulates onto a field the same points also sample).
  • traversal — an optional traversal order from point_traversal_order: a permutation of the point indices saying which point each work item handles. It changes only the sequence in which points are deposited, and since the accumulation is atomic its summation order was never fixed, so the result moves by no more than it already does between runs. It must name every point exactly once and live in the same array family as the field; nothing, the default, visits them in index order.

References

  • A. Brandt, A. A. Lubrecht, Multilevel matrix multiplication and fast solution of integral equations, J. Comput. Phys. 90(2) (1990) 348–370 (an early use of "anterpolation" for the adjoint of interpolation).

Traversal order

An order is an option on the transfer operations above, not an operation on the sections themselves: it changes the sequence in which the points are visited and never the result.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.PointTraversalOrder — Type
PointTraversalOrder(indices)

A PointTraversalOrder validates a traversal order: a permutation of 1:M, for M points, saying which point each unit of a transfer's work handles. Build one with point_traversal_order, or wrap your own permutation by calling this constructor, which checks it.

An order changes only the sequence in which points are handled, never the result, and it moves no data. Entry i names the point that the i-th unit of work takes, so the point set and any point-values indexed alongside it keep their own indexing, and every result lands in the slot it would have landed in anyway.

The constructor throws an ArgumentError unless indices names every point in 1:M exactly once. It accepts any integer vector, including a UnitRange such as 1:M, and keeps the array family it is given, since a transfer will not mix families. Use adapt to move one between families.

The guarantee is made once, at construction

Writing through indices afterwards can leave a duplicate or an out-of-range entry that no later call re-checks, and a transfer will then leave some points unhandled and others handled twice. Treat the field as read-only, and rebuild with point_traversal_order! rather than editing in place.

Fields

  • indices — the permutation itself, one point index per unit of work, in the array family the constructor was given.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.PointTraversalOrderScratch — Type
PointTraversalOrderScratch(grid::Grid3D, points::PointSet; tile_size = 4)

A PointTraversalOrderScratch holds storage for point_traversal_order!, so a rebuild allocates nothing that grows with the point count.

Build one alongside the order it will refill, and hold both for as long as the point set lives. It is storage and nothing else: it holds no order, no transfer ever reads it, and two orders over the same grid and point count can share one — though not at the same time, since a refill overwrites it.

Arguments

  • grid — the Grid3D the order will be built against. It is stored, and a refill against a different grid is refused.
  • points — a PointSet of the size and array family the refills will use. Only its point count, element type and array family are read.
  • tile_size — the block edge in grid cells, which fixes how many blocks the grid divides into and therefore how large the counters below are. A refill uses this value rather than taking one of its own.
It is sized for one point count and one block layout

A refill checks the point count and the block layout and refuses a mismatch rather than reading past an array. A point set that grows, or a different tile_size, needs a new scratch.

Fields

  • keys — the block each point falls in, one entry per point.
  • counts — how many points fall in each block.
  • offsets — the running total of counts, giving each block its starting slot.
  • seen — one mark per point, for the permutation check.
  • tiles — the number of blocks along each axis.
  • tile_size — the block edge the scratch was built with.
  • grid — the grid it was built for, compared against the rebuild's on every refill.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.point_traversal_order! — Function
point_traversal_order!(traversal::PointTraversalOrder, grid::Grid3D,
                       points::PointSet, scratch::PointTraversalOrderScratch)

Rebuild traversal in place for the current positions of points, and return it: a permutation of 1:M, for a set of M points, that groups points lying in the same block of grid cells.

Pass it as the traversal keyword of interpolate!, anterpolate! or their allocating siblings. A transfer's cost is dominated by where its points read and write rather than by the arithmetic on them, and points usually arrive in an order unrelated to the grid — the order they were seeded in, say — so consecutive units of work touch unrelated memory. Following a traversal order makes them touch neighbouring memory instead. The result is unchanged either way.

It is not always a gain. Points already in a lattice or a sweep order are local already, and ordering them adds a build for no benefit. Measure the pair on your own point set before adopting it.

Use this in-place form when traversal is already allocated; the allocating point_traversal_order returns a fresh result instead. The result is the one that form would have produced, up to the sequence of points within a block, which neither form fixes.

Reach for it when the points move. An order describes where they were when it was built, so it goes stale as they move — never wrong, only progressively less useful — and a caller that rebuilds it periodically would otherwise allocate about 15 MiB at $256^3$ with $10^6$ points on every rebuild. On a device that memory is freed by a collector which runs on host pressure and cannot see it, so it accumulates. Here the result is traversal's own storage and everything else is scratch, so nothing allocated per rebuild grows with the point count: about 1.2 KiB of KernelAbstractions launch bookkeeping, measured unchanged from $10^3$ points to $10^6$.

Arguments

  • traversal — the PointTraversalOrder to overwrite, whose length fixes the point count.
  • grid — the grid whose cells the blocks are cut from, and whose origin the points are measured from, which must be the grid the scratch was built for. Points lying outside it are clamped into the edge blocks rather than refused, since an order is a hint and cannot change a result.
  • points — the point set whose current positions decide the order. Its array family decides where the order is built and used.
  • scratch — a PointTraversalOrderScratch built for this grid, point count and tile_size. The block edge comes from it rather than from a keyword here, so there is one source of truth for it.

All four must agree in point count, element type and array family, and a mismatch is refused rather than converted.

The block edge tile_size (default 4), fixed when the scratch is built or passed to the allocating form as a keyword, is in grid cells, so each axis is divided into $\lceil N / \texttt{tile\_size} \rceil$ blocks for a grid of $N$ cells along it. Smaller blocks group points more tightly and cost more: the number of blocks grows as the cube of the reciprocal edge, and with it both the build time and the working memory — 2 MiB of counters at the default on a $256^3$ grid against 128 MiB at tile_size = 1. The best value follows the backend rather than the transfer scheme: a device is insensitive to it, while a CPU prefers coarser blocks, up to roughly 16.

A build or rebuild costs one pass over the points, a running total over the blocks, and the permutation check — $\approx 0.27$ ms on a device at $256^3$ with $10^6$ points, against $0.71$ ms for one ordered gather there, so about a third of a single transfer. On a CPU the same build is $\approx 9$ ms, so it pays back over more transfers; either figure is what sets how often a rebuild is worth doing.

Transfer-scheme types

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractTransferScheme — Type
AbstractTransferScheme

It is the supertype of all grid↔point transfer schemes, the parameter that fixes how the adjoint pair interpolate ($W$, the gather that evaluates a field at each point) / anterpolate ($W^\top$, its transpose, the scatter depositing per-point values onto the grid) is realized. A scheme is a choice of kernel and representation: the physical-domain local splines (AbstractSplineScheme — gather/scatter on the nodal values) and the spectral-domain transfers (the exact DirectDFT and the fast, approximate NUFFT — evaluate/deposit in the Fourier basis, acting on a field in spectral representation). They share the verbs, one adjointness contract and one documented non-uniformity, differing only in (kernel, representation).

One place that uniformity does not reach is the allocating anterpolate's leading argument, which names what the fresh destination is built from: a Grid3D suffices for the spline schemes, whose result is a field in physical representation, while the spectral schemes take a whole field in spectral representation, their destination needing that representation's layout, backend and element type too. Code polymorphic over the allocating form therefore has to branch; the in-place anterpolate! forms, where the caller owns the destination, are uniform.

Schemes divide the same way over array families. The splines and DirectDFT are plain values — weight coefficients in an SMatrix, or none at all — so a kernel captures them by value: one built on the host drives a transfer over device points with nothing copied, which is why adapt (documented with Fields) does not list them. NUFFT is the exception, and for the reason write_scheme also refuses it: it owns plans and buffers built from its reference field, so it already lives in that field's family.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LalescuSpline — Type
LalescuSpline(stencil_width; smoothness = stencil_width ÷ 2 - 1, T = Float64)

Smooth ($C^m$) local spline interpolation follows Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010), pinned to 1e-12 against the $\texttt{beta\_n}n\texttt{\_m}m$ weights of TurTLE, the reference pseudospectral turbulence solver with Lagrangian particle tracking (its interp_neighbours = n, smoothness = m). Over a compact stencil whose stencil_width is $2n{+}2$ nodes per axis, at offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$ ($n \ge 0$), the per-axis weights $w_q(t)$ for fractional cell position $t \in [0, 1)$ are the coefficients of the two-point Hermite interpolant of degree $2m{+}1$: the polynomial that reproduces the field value and the derivatives of order $1, \dots, m$ at the two bracketing nodes (offsets $0$ and $1$), each derivative estimated by a centred finite difference over a $2n{+}1$-node stencil about that node (Fornberg weights).

The smoothness $m$ is the order of continuity, $C^m$ with $0 \le m \le 2n$. The weights are stored in the $(2n{+}2) \times (2m{+}2)$ SMatrix coefficients ($w_q(t) = \sum_r \texttt{coefficients}[q, r{+}1] t^r$, the exponent $r$ running over the polynomial degrees).

It reproduces polynomials up to degree $\min(2m{+}1, 2n)$ (for $n \ge 1$) wherever the whole stencil samples one branch of that polynomial — a periodic base wraps the stencil at the boundary, and the wrapped samples come from the opposite edge, which no non-periodic polynomial continues. The default $m = n$ is the smallest smoothness reaching the stencil's full reproduction degree $2n$; a larger $m$ adds smoothness and no accuracy. Special cases: $m = 0$ is linear interpolation over the two central nodes (independent of the width), LalescuSpline(2) the linear scheme on the smallest stencil, and LalescuSpline(4) the Catmull–Rom spline (CatmullRom, quadratic precision). For the $C^0$ Lagrange hierarchy (degree $2n{+}1$, all $2n{+}2$ nodes interpolated) use LagrangeSpline. Used by interpolate (grid → point) and anterpolate (point → grid).

Arguments

  • stencil_width — the number of nodes per axis, an even integer $2n{+}2 \ge 2$.
  • smoothness — a keyword: the continuity order $m$, with $0 \le m \le 2n$, defaulting to $n$, half the width less one.
  • T — a keyword: the element type the weight coefficients are stored in, Float64 by default.

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.
  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Bramas, M. Rampp, M. Wilczek, An efficient particle tracking algorithm for large-scale parallel pseudo-spectral simulations of turbulence (TurTLE), Comput. Phys. Commun. 278 (2022) 108406; arXiv:2107.01104. Code: gitlab.mpcdf.mpg.de/TurTLE/turtle.
  • B. Fornberg, Generation of finite difference formulas on arbitrarily spaced grids, Math. Comp. 51(184) (1988) 699–706.

Fields

  • stencil_width — the nodes per axis, $2n{+}2$, as passed.
  • smoothness — the continuity order $m$.
  • coefficients — the weight polynomial's coefficients, derived from the width and the smoothness rather than stored independently, which is why write_scheme persists the two parameters and no dataset.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LagrangeSpline — Type
LagrangeSpline(stencil_width; T = Float64)

This is local, $C^0$ Lagrange interpolation: the unique polynomial through the nearest grid nodes per axis, $2n{+}2$ of them, the stencil_width (the stencil offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$, $n \ge 0$), of degree $2n{+}1$. The per-axis weights are the Lagrange cardinal polynomials, stored in the $(2n{+}2) \times (2n{+}2)$ SMatrix coefficients. The width fixes both the stencil, which reaches $n$ nodes below a point and $n{+}1$ above it, and the degree, so a wider stencil reproduces a higher degree at the cost of a wider gather.

It reproduces polynomials up to degree $2n{+}1$ exactly wherever the whole stencil samples one branch of that polynomial (a wrapped stencil draws from the opposite edge, which no non-periodic polynomial continues), but is only $C^0$ (the interpolant kinks at the nodes); for smooth derivatives use LalescuSpline instead. LagrangeSpline(2) is linear interpolation over the two bracketing nodes. Matches Lagrange_polys of TurTLE, the reference pseudospectral turbulence solver with Lagrangian particle tracking.

Arguments

  • stencil_width — the number of nodes per axis, an even integer $2n{+}2 \ge 2$; the interpolant has degree $2n{+}1$.
  • T — a keyword: the element type the weight coefficients are stored in, Float64 by default.

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.
  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Bramas, M. Rampp, M. Wilczek, An efficient particle tracking algorithm for large-scale parallel pseudo-spectral simulations of turbulence (TurTLE), Comput. Phys. Commun. 278 (2022) 108406; arXiv:2107.01104. Code: gitlab.mpcdf.mpg.de/TurTLE/turtle.

Fields

  • stencil_width — the nodes per axis, $2n{+}2$, as passed.
  • coefficients — the cardinal-basis weights, derived from the width rather than stored independently, which is why write_scheme persists the parameter and no dataset.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.DirectDFT — Type
DirectDFT()

The exact spectral-domain transfer scheme: it interpolates and anterpolates by the literal $O(NM)$ discrete Fourier sum over the field's stored Fourier modes $k$ and the $M$ points ($x_p$ the $p$-th point position, $q_p$ the value carried there), with $N = N_x N_y N_z$ the grid-point count — none of the fast scheme's apparatus (no spreading window, oversampled grid or FFT — the machinery of NUFFT, which it validates as the oracle). It defines the spectral-domain adjoint pair and is the transparent exact baseline; for many points or modes, prefer NUFFT. It acts on a field's Fourier coefficients, in spectral representation:

\[\begin{aligned} f(x_p) &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k} \hat f(k) e^{\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{\text{stored }k} \alpha(k) \mathrm{Re}\bigl(\hat f(k) e^{\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)}\bigr), \\ (W^\top q)(k) &= \sum_p q_p e^{-\mathrm{i} k\cdot (x_p - o)}, \end{aligned}\]

The second form reconstructs the real field ($o$ the grid origin) from the stored rfft half-spectrum with the Parseval weight $\alpha(k) = 1$ on the self-conjugate planes — the zero plane of the folded axis, the one the real transform halves, and on an even-length one its Nyquist plane — and $2$ elsewhere. The first equality reads an even axis's Nyquist amplitude split across $\pm k$ — its band-limited cosine form, which is what the stored-$k$ sum with the weight $\alpha(k)$ implements; sub-Nyquist, and at the grid nodes, the two sides agree with no convention needed.

Interpolation reproduces the band-limited field exactly, so it is pointwise divergence-free on the resolved band — the frequencies this grid represents, each even axis's Nyquist plane excluded — where the local splines are not, and matches to_physical at the grid nodes; anterpolation matches to_spectral from grid-node samples. It is stateless: pass DirectDFT() as the scheme.

Anterpolating off-node points does not give a real field's spectrum at an even axis's Nyquist

The deposit $(W^\top q)(k)$ is evaluated at every stored mode, the Nyquist planes included, and it is the exact adjoint of the gather there. But an even-length axis identifies its Nyquist mode with its own negative, so a real field's deposited coefficients must satisfy $\hat g(-k) = \overline{\hat g(k)}$ within that plane, and the sum above meets that condition only when every point sits on a grid node of that axis, $a$: off-node along it, the two sides differ by $e^{-2\mathrm{i} k_a x_{p,a}} \ne 1$. The deposited array is then the half-spectrum of no real field, and to_physical — which is the Hermitian projector — silently keeps only its Hermitian part, so a transform round trip changes it. The same hazard translation_kernel documents for its Nyquist factor is why NUFFT projects that plane out; DirectDFT deliberately does not, keeping the literal adjoint instead. The resolved band is unaffected, which is the band a de-aliased spectral computation uses.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.NUFFT — Type
NUFFT(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral;
      tol = 1e-10, oversampling = 2, width = 0, beta = 0)

The fast, approximate spectral-domain transfer scheme: the $O(N\log N + M)$ windowed-gridding (nonuniform FFT) counterpart of DirectDFT for evaluating a field in spectral representation at scattered points and depositing point values back, agreeing with DirectDFT to a tolerance tol. It runs the transfer through an oversampled work grid and a single FFT — spreading through a smooth compact window then transforming for the deposit, and the reverse for the evaluation, deconvolving the coefficients before the transform and gathering through the window after it — so it is far cheaper than the exact $O(NM)$ sum once the point and mode counts grow; for a few points or modes, DirectDFT is simpler and exact.

Build a plan once from a reference field in spectral representation, which fixes the grid, layout, element type, and tensor order, then pass it as the scheme to interpolate / anterpolate on fields like the reference.

Arguments

  • reference — a field in spectral representation, fixing the grid, layout, element type and tensor order the plan is built for. The order is fixed with the rest because the plan stores oversampled field templates, which carry the fiber; a plan built at one order does not serve another.
  • tol — the target accuracy; the window width and shape default to the values that reach it. It is a target, not a guaranteed bound: it must be finite and positive, and a value finer than the element type can resolve widens the window as far as the formula goes and then saturates at that resolution rather than erroring.
  • oversampling — the linear oversampling factor $\sigma$ of the work grid (default 2); must be at least 2. That is an implementation bound rather than a theoretical one — lower upsampling is standard with a correspondingly wider window — because the width and shape formulas here are fitted for $\sigma \ge 2$; a value of 1 leaves no spectral guard band, so the gridding breaks down.
  • width — the gridding stencil width in nodes; 0 (default) picks it from tol.
  • beta — the window shape parameter, unrelated to the accumulation weight $\beta$ the transfer verbs take; 0 (default) picks it from the width.

It agrees with DirectDFT (hence to_physical / to_spectral at the grid nodes) to tol on the resolved band (the rfft Nyquist plane is projected out), shares its adjoint relation, and like it reconstructs the band-limited field (divergence-free on the resolved band — the package's derivative wavenumbers are zeroed at an even axis's Nyquist frequency while this scheme evaluates the true phase there, so a discretely divergence-free field carrying Nyquist content is not pointwise divergence-free in the continuum).

A plan costs about eight times the field in memory, for its whole lifetime

A NUFFT plan holds an oversampled work grid of about $\sigma^3$ times the field's memory (eight times at the default oversampling $\sigma = 2$) for its whole lifetime, which matters on a memory-tight GPU.

References

  • A. H. Barnett, J. Magland, L. af Klinteberg, A parallel nonuniform fast Fourier transform library based on an exponential of semicircle kernel, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 41(5) (2019) C479–C504.

Fields

  • width — the gridding stencil width in nodes, as resolved from tol.
  • beta — the window shape parameter, as resolved from tol and width.
  • total_oversampling — the ratio of the oversampled point count to the reference grid's, which carries the transforms' normalization between the two grids.
  • reference_grid, reference_layout — the base and Fourier layout the plan was built for, kept so a field handed to it can be checked rather than assumed compatible.
  • oversampled_grid, oversampled_plans — the work grid and its transform plans.
  • deconvolution — the per-mode reciprocal of the window's transform, dividing out the gridding.
  • oversampled_physical, oversampled_spectral — the work buffers, whose contents are scratch: the physical one is a template copied per call and never mutated in place.

Scheme persistence

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.write_scheme — Function
write_scheme(parent::HDF5.H5DataStore, name::AbstractString,
             scheme::AbstractTransferScheme)

Persist a grid↔point transfer scheme — the choice of kernel and representation that fixes how interpolate and anterpolate are realized — as a new HDF5 group name under parent, returning the group it created. Use it when a checkpoint must restore not only the positions and values of a point set but also how that set samples a field, so a restarted run transfers identically rather than falling back to a default. Reload with read_scheme.

The group is self-describing and holds no dataset: a scheme's weight coefficients are derived from its parameters, so the group carries a type tag, a schema version, the weight element type, and the defining parameters — the stencil_width and, for LalescuSpline, the smoothness. Reading reconstructs through the ordinary constructor, which recomputes the weights and revalidates the parameters, so a group asserting an impossible combination is refused rather than producing a malformed scheme. The sugar parent[name] = scheme is equivalent. CatmullRom needs no identity of its own: it is LalescuSpline(4) and round-trips as one, comparing equal to the alias.

Because parent is a handle you opened and own, this never opens or closes a file, and the package attaches no notion of time — the caller bundles a snapshot and owns any time / iteration attributes.

Arguments

  • parent — an open HDF5 File or Group to create the new subgroup under.
  • name — the name of the group to create.
  • scheme — the scheme to write: a LalescuSpline, a LagrangeSpline, or DirectDFT.

Provided by a package extension, so it is available only when HDF5.jl is loaded.

NUFFT is deliberately not persistable

Every other scheme is its parameters, which is what makes a few attributes a faithful record. A NUFFT is not: it owns oversampled FFT plans and a deconvolution window built against a particular reference field, so a stored copy would be either a large duplicate of derived data or a set of parameters pretending to be the object. It is the same class as FieldTransformPlans, which is likewise rebuilt rather than moved when array families change. Rebuild it from the reference field the caller restores anyway — NUFFT(reference; tol) — and write the tol in the caller's own snapshot if it varies. Passing one here throws an ArgumentError naming this.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.read_scheme — Function
read_scheme(parent::HDF5.H5DataStore, name::AbstractString)

Reconstruct the transfer scheme written by write_scheme from the HDF5 group name under parent, dispatching on the stored type attribute to return it in the matching type. The reload is exact: the reconstructed scheme compares equal (==, and in fact ===) to the one written, because a scheme is fully determined by the parameters the group stores and the constructor recomputes the weights identically.

Here parent is an open HDF5 File or Group holding the group, and name is the name (or path) of that group under parent. The group's schema is checked, not merely read: a file from a newer schema version, a group missing a parameter its type needs, an unrecognized type tag, or a parameter combination the scheme's own constructor rejects all raise an ArgumentError rather than producing a scheme that is not what the file claims.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — the scheme supertype AbstractSplineScheme, the spline-weight generators (Fornberg finite differences plus a small linear solve), the periodic cell-finding, and the compatibility checks the transfer verbs route through.

Types

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractSplineScheme — Type
AbstractSplineScheme{N2, M2, T, L}

It is the supertype of the physical-domain local schemes over one shared stencil: the smooth LalescuSpline of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) and the $C^0$ LagrangeSpline, the classical Lagrange interpolant of the same nodes, matched to TurTLE's Lagrange_polys. Each acts on a field in physical representation, gathering and scattering its nodal values over a compact $(2n{+}2)^3$ stencil, n being the constructor's half-width argument, around the cell $c$ that contains a point $x_p$. With $t_a$ the point's fractional position along axis $a$ and $w$ the scheme's per-axis weight polynomials, interpolation is the tensor-product gather

\[\begin{aligned} f(x_p) &= \sum_{i_1, i_2, i_3} w_{i_1}(t_1) w_{i_2}(t_2) w_{i_3}(t_3) \\ &\qquad f\bigl(x_{c + (i_1, i_2, i_3)}\bigr), \end{aligned}\]

and anterpolation is its transpose, adding $w_{i_1}(t_1) w_{i_2}(t_2) w_{i_3}(t_3) q_p$ (with $q_p$ the value carried at $x_p$) onto each of those same nodes. A scheme is a tensor product of 1D weight polynomials: a compact stencil of $N_2 = 2n{+}2$ nodes, each weight a degree-$(M_2-1)$ polynomial in the fractional cell position $t \in [0, 1)$. The $N_2 \times M_2$ coefficient matrix ([node, power]) is precomputed once into an SMatrix that the kernels take by value. It is one of the two families of AbstractTransferScheme; the spectral-domain DirectDFT / NUFFT are the other. Only the spline family has a shared supertype, its members differing in their weights alone. In the signature T is the coefficient type and L the matrix length N2 * M2.

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.

Build the $(2n{+}2) \times (2m{+}2)$ matrix of smooth-spline weight-polynomial coefficients (row $q$ is the weight polynomial for stencil offset $q - 1 - n \in \{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$; column $r+1$ is the $t^r$ coefficient). GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._scan_offsets! — Method
_scan_offsets!(offsets, counts)

Write the inclusive running total of counts into offsets, and return offsets.

The generic method is cumsum!, which is a parallel scan on a device and a serial loop on the host. GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._scan_offsets! — Method
_scan_offsets!(offsets::Vector, counts::Vector)

It computes a threaded inclusive running total on the host: each chunk sums its slice, the chunk totals are scanned serially (there are as many as chunks), and each chunk then writes its slice offset by the total of everything before it.

The result is bitwise identical to cumsum!, and for integers a property, not a tolerance: addition is exact and associative, so regrouping the sums cannot change them. A threaded float reduction would need a fixed summation tree for thread-count invariance; this needs none.

Two details are load-bearing. The chunk accumulator is declared local, because a name already bound in the enclosing scope would be SHARED by every task rather than shadowed — a data race that produced wrong answers, and ran far slower than the serial loop, when this was first written. And the grand total is accumulated wide and checked against typemax, because cumsum! into a narrow integer is overflow-checked while a plain accumulation wraps: a wrapped total becomes a negative offset, which the placement kernel would use as an index inside @inbounds.
