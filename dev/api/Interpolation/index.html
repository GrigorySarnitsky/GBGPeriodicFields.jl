Interpolation

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation — Module
Interpolation

Transfer between the grid sections of Fields and the point sections of Points: evaluating a field at off-grid positions, and depositing per-point quantities back onto the grid. The two operations are formal adjoints of each other. The transfer uses the local spline interpolation of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) as portable gather/scatter kernels (CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU, via KernelAbstractions; the scatter accumulates atomically); the method, terminology, and GPU rationale are in docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §10, and the point-value data types and their fiberwise algebra live in the Points module.

API

  • Interpolation (grid to point, gather; interpolate $\equiv \mathcal I = W$): interpolate(f, xs[, scheme]) / in-place interpolate!(out, f, xs[, scheme]), plus the callable f(x) (and its broadcast f.(xs)) as the ergonomic face of interpolate.
  • Anterpolation (point to grid, scatter; anterpolate $\equiv \mathcal I^* = W^\top$): anterpolate(grid, xs, qs[, scheme]) / in-place anterpolate!(field, xs, qs[, scheme]), with a five-argument $\alpha$/$\beta$ form ($\beta = 0$ overwrite, $\beta \ne 0$ accumulate, the mul! convention; accumulation is the common case for the scatter).

interpolate / anterpolate! are the symmetric named pair (the operators $\mathcal I = W$ / $\mathcal I^* = W^\top$); only interpolation also carries the pointwise callable f(x), because evaluation is pointwise while its adjoint is not. Both directions share one scheme (the same weights) — which is what makes them an adjoint pair.

Why interpolation and anterpolation are adjoint: the function–measure duality

$\mathcal I : G \to P$ and $\mathcal I^* : P \to G$ satisfy $\langle \mathcal I f, q\rangle_P = \langle f, \mathcal I^* q\rangle_G$, a duality between functions (which you evaluate) and measures (which you integrate against):

  • evaluation is pairing with a Dirac mass, $f(x) = \langle \delta_x, f\rangle$, so $(\mathcal I f)_p = \langle \delta_{x_p}, f\rangle = f(x_p)$;
  • its adjoint cannot be pointwise — it returns a measure, a sum of point masses $\mathcal I^* q = \sum_p q_p\,\delta_{x_p}$, regularized by the stencil kernel to $\sum_p q_p\,\beta(\cdot - x_p)$, a point-source density.

Hence the API asymmetry: you evaluate functions (f(x) returns a value) but accumulate measures (anterpolate! adds a source density onto a field); there is no pointwise f(x) = q. Discretely, with $G = \mathbb R^N$ ($N$ grid nodes) and $P = \mathbb R^M$ ($M$ points), interpolation is the matrix $\mathcal I = W$ ($W_{pn} = \beta_n(x_p)$) and anterpolation its transpose $\mathcal I^* = W^\top$. With shared partition-of-unity weights ($\mathcal I\,\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 1$, i.e. $\sum_n W_{pn} = 1$), anterpolation is the exact transpose of interpolation and the total deposited quantity is conserved,

\[\sum_n (\mathcal I^* q)_n = \langle \mathbf 1, \mathcal I^* q\rangle = \langle \mathcal I\,\mathbf 1, q\rangle = \langle \mathbf 1, q\rangle = \sum_p q_p,\]

which is mandatory for the two-way-coupled parcel application (total water mass and latent heat conserved). This holds only when both directions use the same weights, so the design fixes anterpolation as the transpose/adjoint of whatever interpolation scheme is in use (mismatched weights inject a spurious source — Sundaram & Collins 1996). The inner products are a modelling choice: with the Euclidean (counting) products the adjoint is the bare transpose $W^\top$; with $L^2$/volume- weighted products it is $M_G^{-1} W^\top M_P$ (the immersed-boundary spreading/interpolation pair of Peskin 2002), conserving an energy pairing rather than the plain sum.

The Interpolation module is the grid↔point transfer layer: interpolation (grid → point, the gather $\mathcal I = W$) evaluates a field at off-grid positions, and its adjoint anterpolation (point → grid, the scatter $\mathcal I^* = W^\top$) deposits per-point quantities back onto the grid. The two are an L²-adjoint pair under a shared scheme; the scheme — an AbstractTransferScheme — is the choice of kernel and representation. Two families:

  • Physical-domain splines (AbstractSplineScheme) — the local high-order splines of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010): the smooth LalescuSpline (two-point Hermite, default CatmullRom, pinned bit-for-bit to TurTLE) and the $C^0$ LagrangeSpline. They act on the physical nodal values; interpolation and anterpolation are exact transposes and conserve the deposited total.
  • Spectral-domain exact transfer (DirectDFT) — the literal $O(NM)$ discrete Fourier sum, acting on a field in spectral representation: $f(x_p) = \tfrac1N \sum_k \hat f(k)\, e^{i k\cdot(x_p-o)}$ and its adjoint. It reproduces the band-limited field exactly (so it is pointwise divergence-free, matching to_physical at the grid nodes) and is the oracle for the fast NUFFT.
  • Spectral-domain fast transfer (NUFFT) — the $O(N\log N + M)$ windowed-gridding version of DirectDFT (spread → oversampled FFT → deconvolve), owned natively, exact to a tolerance on the resolved sub-Nyquist band. See docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §10 / decision 23.

The transfer kernels are portable across CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU (KernelAbstractions); the point-data types live in the Points module.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractTransferScheme — Type
AbstractTransferScheme

Supertype of all grid↔point transfer schemes — the parameter that fixes how the adjoint pair interpolate $= W$ / anterpolate $= W^\top$ is realized (the function–measure duality above). A scheme is a choice of kernel and representation: the physical-domain local splines (AbstractSplineScheme — gather/scatter on the nodal values) and the spectral-domain exact transfers (DirectDFT and NUFFT — evaluate/deposit in the Fourier basis, acting on a field in spectral representation). Same verbs, one adjointness contract; the schemes differ only in (kernel, representation). See docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §10.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.DirectDFT — Type
DirectDFT()

The exact spectral-domain transfer scheme: interpolation/anterpolation by the literal $O(NM)$ discrete Fourier sum — no window, no oversampling, no FFT. It is the definition of the spectral-domain adjoint pair interpolate $= W$ / anterpolate $= W^\top$, so it is at once a transparent exact baseline and the oracle that validates NUFFT. It acts on a field in spectral representation, interpolate(f̂, points, DirectDFT()) and anterpolate(reference, points, q, DirectDFT()):

\[f(x_p) = \frac1N \sum_{k} \hat f(k)\, e^{i k\cdot (x_p - o)} = \frac1N \sum_{\text{stored }k} \alpha(k)\, \mathrm{Re}\!\bigl(\hat f(k)\, e^{i k\cdot (x_p - o)}\bigr), \qquad (W^\top q)(k) = \sum_p q_p\, e^{-i k\cdot (x_p - o)},\]

reconstructing the real field ($o$ the grid origin) from the stored rfft half-spectrum with the Parseval weight $\alpha(k) = 1$ on the self-conjugate $k_x = 0$ plane (and the even-$N_x$ Nyquist plane), $2$ otherwise. The interpolation reproduces the band-limited field exactly — so it is pointwise divergence-free (unlike the local splines) and matches to_physical at the grid nodes; the adjoint matches to_spectral from grid-node samples. Adjoint pair: $M\,\langle W\hat f, q\rangle_{\mathrm{pts}} = N\,\langle \hat f, W^\top q\rangle$ (the measure factor, as for the splines). Stateless; $O(NM)$ — for many points/modes prefer NUFFT.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LagrangeSpline — Type
LagrangeSpline(n; T = Float64)

The $C^0$ Lagrange hierarchy of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010) (TurTLE's Lagrange_polys): the unique degree-$2n{+}1$ polynomial interpolating all $2n{+}2$ stencil nodes at offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$ ($n \ge 0$). The per-axis weights are the Lagrange cardinal polynomials, stored in the $(2n{+}2) \times (2n{+}2)$ SMatrix coefficients. Reproduces polynomials up to degree $2n{+}1$ exactly, but is only $C^0$ (kinks at the nodes); for smooth derivatives use LalescuSpline instead. $n = 0$ is linear interpolation.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.LalescuSpline — Type
LalescuSpline(n, m = 1; T = Float64)

Smooth ($C^m$) local spline interpolation of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010), pinned bit-for-bit to TurTLE's beta_n{n}_m{m} (TurTLE's interp_neighbours = n, smoothness = m). Over a compact stencil of $2n{+}2$ nodes at offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$ ($n \ge 0$, the finite-difference half-width), the per-axis weights $\beta_q(t)$ for fractional cell position $t \in [0, 1)$ are the coefficients of the two-point Hermite interpolant of degree $2m{+}1$: the polynomial that reproduces the field value and the derivatives of order $1, \dots, m$ at the two bracketing nodes (offsets $0$ and $1$), each derivative estimated by a centred finite difference over the $2n{+}2$-node stencil (Fornberg weights). Smoothness is $C^m$ with $0 \le m \le 2n$; the weights are stored in the $(2n{+}2) \times (2m{+}2)$ SMatrix coefficients ($\beta_q(t) = \sum_p \mathrm{coefficients}[q, p{+}1]\, t^p$).

It reproduces polynomials up to degree $\min(2m{+}1, 2n)$ (for $n \ge 1$). Special cases: $m = 0$ is linear interpolation over the two central nodes (independent of $n$); $n = 1, m = 1$ is the Catmull–Rom spline (the default CatmullRom, quadratic precision). For the $C^0$ Lagrange hierarchy (degree $2n{+}1$, all $2n{+}2$ nodes interpolated) use LagrangeSpline. Used by interpolate (grid → point) and anterpolate (point → grid).

References

  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Teaca, D. Carati, Implementation of high order spline interpolations for tracking test particles in discretized fields, J. Comput. Phys. 229(17) (2010) 5862–5869.
  • C. C. Lalescu, B. Bramas, M. Rampp, M. Wilczek, An efficient particle tracking algorithm for large-scale parallel pseudo-spectral simulations of turbulence (TurTLE), Comput. Phys. Commun. 278 (2022) 108406.
  • B. Fornberg, Generation of finite difference formulas on arbitrarily spaced grids, Math. Comp. 51(184) (1988) 699–706.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.NUFFT — Type
NUFFT(reference::AbstractFieldSpectral; tol = 1e-10, oversampling = 2, width = 0, beta = 0)

The fast spectral-domain transfer scheme — the $O(N\log N + M)$ windowed-gridding (NUFFT) counterpart of DirectDFT, exact to a tolerance tol. Built from a reference spectral field (it fixes the grid, layout, element type, and tensor order); the plan holds the oversampled (oversampling×) grid and its FieldTransformPlans, the deconvolution $1/\hat\psi$, and the coarse↔oversampled mode maps. width/beta default to the exponential-of-semicircle parameters for tol. Used like DirectDFT: interpolate(f̂, points, nufft) / anterpolate(points, q, nufft), on a field in spectral representation, of the reference's tensor order. The algorithm is

\[\text{type-2: deconvolve } \hat f / \hat\psi \to \text{zero-pad} \to \mathcal F^{-1} \to \text{window-gather}, \qquad \text{type-1: window-spread} \to \mathcal F \to \text{truncate} \to \text{deconvolve}.\]

It agrees with DirectDFT (hence to_physical/to_spectral at the nodes) to tol, shares its adjoint relation, and like it is divergence-free (it reconstructs the band-limited field). For a few points/modes DirectDFT is simpler; NUFFT wins as $M, N$ grow.

source
GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.CatmullRom — Constant
CatmullRom

The default interpolation scheme: the cubic $n = 1, m = 1$ LalescuSpline, a four-point $C^1$ stencil that reproduces the Catmull–Rom spline (and quadratic polynomials exactly).

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.anterpolate! — Function
anterpolate!(field::AbstractFieldPhysical, points::PointSet, quantities::AbstractPointValues,
             scheme = CatmullRom, α = true, β = false)

Deposit the per-point quantities onto field in-place — the adjoint (transpose) of interpolate! under the same scheme, $\mathcal I^* = W^\top$ — computing $\mathrm{field} = \alpha\,(W^\top q) + \beta\,\mathrm{field}$. Using the same stencil weights as the gather, each point $p$ (in cell $c$, fractional position $t$) atomically scatters its value $q_p$ onto the $(2n{+}2)^3$ surrounding nodes:

\[\mathrm{field}\bigl(x_{c + (i_1, i_2, i_3)}\bigr) \mathrel{+}= \alpha\; \beta_{i_1}(t_1)\, \beta_{i_2}(t_2)\, \beta_{i_3}(t_3)\; q_p\]

(after an initial $\times \beta$ prescale of the field). Sharing the gather weights makes this the exact transpose, so with the partition of unity $\sum \beta = 1$ the total deposited quantity is conserved. With $\beta = 0$ (default) the field is overwritten; with $\beta \ne 0$ the deposit is accumulated (the common case for the two-way-coupling scatter). field and quantities must share tensor order and points must cover the same number of points. Runs as a portable atomic-scatter kernel on the field's array backend.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.interpolate! — Function
interpolate!(out::AbstractPointValues, field::AbstractFieldPhysical, points::PointSet, scheme = CatmullRom)

Interpolate field (physical-space representation) to the off-grid points, writing into out; returns out.

Each point value is the tensor-product spline gather over the $(2n{+}2)^3$ stencil around the containing cell $c$, with $t_a$ the fractional position along axis $a$ and $\beta$ the scheme weights — exactly the weighted sum the kernel computes:

\[f(x_p) \approx \sum_{i_1, i_2, i_3} \beta_{i_1}(t_1)\, \beta_{i_2}(t_2)\, \beta_{i_3}(t_3)\; f\bigl(x_{c + (i_1, i_2, i_3)}\bigr).\]

The $\approx$ is genuine: the gather is exact at grid nodes and for polynomials up to the scheme's reproduction degree (CatmullRom quadratic, LalescuSpline(n,m) $\min(2m{+}1, 2n)$, LagrangeSpline(n) $2n{+}1$), approximate otherwise.

out must match field in tensor order and cover the same number of points as points. The transfer runs as a portable gather kernel on the field's array backend (CPU / CUDA / AMDGPU). Interpolation is defined for physical-space representation only; transform a spectral field to physical space first.

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation.AbstractSplineScheme — Type
AbstractSplineScheme{N2, M2, T, L}

Supertype of the local interpolation schemes — the two hierarchies of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010): the smooth LalescuSpline and the $C^0$ LagrangeSpline. A scheme is a tensor product of 1D weight polynomials over a compact stencil of $N_2$ nodes, each weight a degree-$(M_2-1)$ polynomial in the fractional cell position $t \in [0, 1)$. The $N_2 \times M_2$ coefficient matrix ([node, power]) is precomputed once into an SMatrix so it passes into the gather/scatter kernels by value (no device array). The physical-domain family of AbstractTransferScheme (its weights act on the physical nodal values).

GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._axis_weights — Method
_axis_weights(coefficients, t)

Evaluate the per-axis spline weights $\beta_q(t)$ for all N2 stencil nodes at fractional position $t$, returning an SVector{N2}. GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._hermite_weight_matrix — Method
_hermite_weight_matrix(n, m)

Build the $(2n{+}2) \times (2m{+}2)$ matrix of smooth-spline weight-polynomial coefficients (row q is the weight polynomial for stencil offset $q - 1 - n \in \{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$; column p+1 is the $t^p$ coefficient). Each weight has degree $2m{+}1$; together they form the two-point Hermite interpolant reproducing the value and the order-$1, \dots, m$ derivatives at the two bracketing nodes (offsets $0$ and $1$), the derivatives taken from centred finite differences over the $2n{+}2$-node stencil. GBGPeriodicFields.Interpolation._lagrange_weight_matrix — Method
_lagrange_weight_matrix(n)

Build the $(2n{+}2) \times (2n{+}2)$ matrix of Lagrange cardinal-polynomial coefficients over the stencil offsets $\{-n, \dots, n{+}1\}$ (row q is the cardinal polynomial of node q; column p+1 is the $t^p$ coefficient). Each is the degree-$2n{+}1$ polynomial that is one at its own node and zero at the others (the inverse of the offsets' Vandermonde matrix). See LagrangeSpline.
