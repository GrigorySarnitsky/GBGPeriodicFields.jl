DifferentialOperators

GBGPeriodicFields.DifferentialOperators — Module
DifferentialOperators

Differential operators on scalar, vector, and tensor fields over three-dimensional rectangular periodic domains, applied as spectral multipliers. A field in this module is its finite Fourier representation, so each operator is the exact term-by-term derivative of that trigonometric polynomial on the resolved band — the band a grid's resolution actually represents, which excludes the Nyquist frequency of each even-length axis — realized as a coefficientwise multiply by a wavenumber symbol with no finite-difference truncation. The two symbol families treat that Nyquist term differently and deliberately so (see the Nyquist split below), so it is not simply zeroed everywhere. The wavenumber symbols are held in a FieldSpectralMultipliers, built from a field with FieldSpectralMultipliers(field) or from its six wavenumber arrays. Every operator provides an allocating form and an in-place ! form, and each per-operator docstring gives both the physical-space meaning and the spectral symbol it applies.

The parts are:

  • gradient ($\nabla$) raises the tensor order: a scalar field becomes a vector field, and a vector field becomes a second-order tensor field.
  • divergence ($\nabla\cdot$) lowers the tensor order: a vector field becomes a scalar field, and a second-order tensor field becomes a vector field; an optional kernel argument composes a convolution into the same pass.
  • curl ($\nabla\times$) returns the curl of a vector field, a measure of its local rotation, and preserves the tensor order.
  • inverse_curl ($(\nabla\times)^{-1}$) returns the solenoidal preimage of a curl — the divergence-free field whose curl is the given one — and preserves the tensor order.
  • symmetric_gradient ($\operatorname{sym}\nabla$) returns the symmetric part of a vector field's gradient as a second-order tensor field, formed in one pass without the full gradient.
  • laplacian ($\Delta$) applies the second-derivative operator to every component and preserves the tensor order.
  • inverse_laplacian ($\Delta^{-1}$) returns the zero-mean solution of Poisson's equation and preserves the tensor order.
  • helmholtz_projection ($\mathbb P$) projects a vector field onto its divergence-free part in one pass; an optional kernel argument composes a convolution into the same pass.

The Nyquist frequency is treated differently by symbol: first-derivative wavenumbers are zeroed at the Nyquist frequency on even-length axes, while squared wavenumbers keep the true squared wavenumber there.

Compute the gradient (gradient, $\nabla$) of a field and write it into destination, returning destination. Differentiating raises the tensor order by one: a scalar field $\varphi$ gives the vector field with components $(\nabla\varphi)_i = \partial_{x_i}\varphi$, and a vector field $u$ gives the second-order tensor field with components $(\nabla u)_{ij} = \partial_{x_j} u_i$.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating gradient returns a fresh result instead.

Arguments

  • destination — the field that receives the result; it shares the domain of field and holds one tensor order higher.
  • field — the field to differentiate, in spectral representation.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber symbols for the field's grid.

Each output component is the source component multiplied coefficientwise by $\mathrm{i}$ times the derivative wavenumber of the differentiation axis. Below, $k$ labels the mode and $a$ is the derivative wavenumber family — the one zeroed at an even axis's Nyquist frequency, kept apart from the true squared wavenumber the Laplacian uses:

\[\begin{gathered} \widehat{(\nabla\varphi)_i}(k) = \mathrm{i} a_i \hat\varphi(k), \\ \widehat{(\nabla u)_{ij}}(k) = \mathrm{i} a_j \hat u_i(k). \end{gathered}\]

Compute the divergence (divergence, $\nabla\cdot$) of a field and write it into destination, returning destination. Differentiating lowers the tensor order by one: a vector field $u$ gives the scalar field $\nabla\cdot u = \partial_{x_i} u_i$, and a second-order tensor field $A$ gives the vector field with components $(\nabla\cdot A)_i = \partial_{x_j} A_{ij}$.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating divergence returns a fresh result instead.

Arguments

  • destination — the field that receives the result; it shares the domain of field and holds one tensor order lower.
  • field — the field to differentiate, in spectral representation.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber symbols for the field's grid.

The result is the coefficientwise contraction of the source with $\mathrm{i}$ times the derivative wavenumber vector, summed over the three axes. Below, $k$ labels the mode and $a$ is the derivative wavenumber family — zeroed at an even axis's Nyquist frequency, unlike the true squared wavenumber the Laplacian uses:

\[\begin{gathered} \widehat{\nabla\cdot u}(k) = \mathrm{i} (a_1 \hat u_1 + a_2 \hat u_2 + a_3 \hat u_3), \\ \widehat{(\nabla\cdot A)_i}(k) = \mathrm{i} (a_1 \hat A_{i1} + a_2 \hat A_{i2} + a_3 \hat A_{i3}). \end{gathered}\]

The result has exactly zero mean, as the divergence of a periodic field must: every derivative wavenumber is exactly zero at the zero mode, so the mean coefficient is computed as exactly zero rather than as round-off. This holds for multipliers built from the grid, which the convenience constructor of FieldSpectralMultipliers does; wavenumbers supplied through its explicit six-array constructor are not checked at the zero mode. In the kernel-composed form below with $\beta \ne 0$, the mean is $\beta$ times the destination's.

The kernel-composed form

Passing a kernel composes the divergence with a periodic convolution in the same fused pass. As a real physical object the result is the kernel-weighted local average of the divergence, $g \star (\nabla\cdot u)$ — a single operator, because convolution and divergence both act coefficientwise in spectral representation, so their symbols simply multiply. The additional arguments are:

  • kernel — the kernel's transfer function $\hat g$, in any storage class of a SpectralKernel — a StoredKernel holding one coefficient per mode, or a compressed class evaluated per mode (radial, separable, or their product) costing $O(N)$ for $N$ points per axis, not $O(N^3)$, exactly as convolve! takes it — including its reality condition, that $\hat g$ be Hermitian, $\hat g(-k) = \overline{\hat g(k)}$, without which the result is the spectrum of no real field; it may alias destination, since the product is coefficientwise.
  • α, β — real accumulation weights as in convolve!: the four-argument form overwrites ($\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 0$), and the six-argument form computes

\[\begin{aligned} \widehat{\texttt{destination}}(k) &= \alpha \hat g(k) \widehat{\nabla\cdot u}(k) \\ &\quad + \beta \widehat{\texttt{destination}}(k), \end{aligned}\]

and likewise per component for a second-order tensor field. The composed form replaces a divergence! followed by a convolve! — one fused pass instead of two, saving a full read and a full write of destination per call.

GBGPeriodicFields.DifferentialOperators.curl! — Function
curl!(
  destination::VectorFieldSpectral,
  field::VectorFieldSpectral,
  multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers,

Compute the curl (curl, $\nabla\times$) of a vector field $u$ and write it into destination, returning destination. The curl is the vector field whose components are, with $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ the Levi-Civita symbol, $(\nabla\times u)_i = \varepsilon_{ijk} \partial_{x_j} u_k$; written out cyclically,

\[\begin{gathered} (\nabla\times u)_1 = \partial_{x_2} u_3 - \partial_{x_3} u_2, \\ (\nabla\times u)_2 = \partial_{x_3} u_1 - \partial_{x_1} u_3, \\ (\nabla\times u)_3 = \partial_{x_1} u_2 - \partial_{x_2} u_1. \end{gathered}\]

It measures the field's local rotation at twice the angular rate — the rate itself is $\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\times u$. (A fluid-dynamics user calls $\nabla\times u$ the vorticity; the package keeps the neutral mathematical name, as it does for symmetric_gradient against "strain rate".)

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating curl returns a fresh result instead.

Arguments

  • destination — the vector field that receives the result; it shares the domain of field, and it may alias field freely: the single pass reads all three source coefficients of a mode before writing any component of it, so the aliased call needs no temporary copy and is bitwise-identical to the non-aliased one.
  • field — the vector field to differentiate, in spectral representation.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber symbols for the field's grid.

The spectral symbol is a coefficientwise multiply by $\mathrm{i}$ times the cross product with the derivative wavenumber vector, $\widehat{\nabla\times u}(k) = \mathrm{i} a \times \hat u(k)$, where $k$ labels the mode and $a$ is the derivative wavenumber family — zeroed at an even axis's Nyquist frequency, unlike the true squared wavenumber the Laplacian uses. Componentwise:

\[\begin{gathered} \widehat{(\nabla\times u)_1}(k) = \mathrm{i} (a_2 \hat u_3 - a_3 \hat u_2), \\ \widehat{(\nabla\times u)_2}(k) = \mathrm{i} (a_3 \hat u_1 - a_1 \hat u_3), \\ \widehat{(\nabla\times u)_3}(k) = \mathrm{i} (a_1 \hat u_2 - a_2 \hat u_1). \end{gathered}\]

Compute the symmetric gradient (symmetric_gradient, $\operatorname{sym}\nabla$) of a vector field $u$ and write it into destination, returning destination. It is the symmetric part of the gradient $\nabla u$, the second-order tensor field

\[S_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2} (\partial_{x_j} u_i + \partial_{x_i} u_j),\]

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating symmetric_gradient returns a fresh result instead.

Arguments

  • destination — the second-order tensor field that receives the result; it shares the domain of field and holds one tensor order higher.
  • field — the vector field to differentiate, in spectral representation.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber symbols for the field's grid.

The spectral symbol symmetrizes $\mathrm{i} a_j \hat u_i$ in its two indices, with $k$ labelling the mode and $a$ the derivative wavenumber family — zeroed at an even axis's Nyquist frequency, unlike the true squared wavenumber the Laplacian uses:

\[\widehat{S_{ij}}(k) = \tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2} \bigl(a_j \hat u_i(k) + a_i \hat u_j(k)\bigr).\]

GBGPeriodicFields.DifferentialOperators.laplacian! — Function
laplacian!(
  destination::AbstractFieldSpectral,
  field::AbstractFieldSpectral,
  multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers,
)

Compute the Laplacian (laplacian, $\Delta$) of a field and write it into destination, returning destination. The Laplacian is a local operator: it sums the second partial derivatives of a scalar field $f$ over the three axes, and it acts on each component of a vector or tensor field $u$ alike, so it preserves the tensor order:

\[\begin{gathered} \Delta f = \partial^2_{x_i x_i} f, \\ (\Delta u)_j = \partial^2_{x_i x_i} u_j. \end{gathered}\]

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating laplacian returns a fresh result instead.

Arguments

  • destination — the field receiving the result, on field's domain and tensor order.
  • field — the field to differentiate, in spectral representation.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber symbols for the field's grid.

The spectral symbol multiplies each mode's coefficient by $-|k|^2$, its true wavevector squared:

\[\begin{gathered} \widehat{\Delta f}(k) = -|k|^2 \hat f(k), \\ |k|^2 = k_1^2 + k_2^2 + k_3^2. \end{gathered}\]

GBGPeriodicFields.DifferentialOperators.inverse_laplacian! — Function
inverse_laplacian!(
  destination::AbstractFieldSpectral,
  field::AbstractFieldSpectral,
  multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers,
)

Compute the zero-mean inverse Laplacian (inverse_laplacian, $\Delta^{-1}$) of a field $f$ and write it into destination, returning destination. For a mean-free right-hand side the result is the zero-mean field $\psi$ solving Poisson's equation — on the periodic box, the integration domain, it is solvable only when $\int_\Omega f\,\mathrm{d}x = 0$, since any periodic $\psi$ has $\int_\Omega \Delta\psi\,\mathrm{d}x = 0$ — and because the Laplacian annihilates constants, a vanishing mean fixes the solution uniquely:

\[\begin{gathered} \Delta\psi = f, \\ \int_\Omega f\,\mathrm{d}x = 0, \\ \int_\Omega \psi\,\mathrm{d}x = 0. \end{gathered}\]

A general input is projected onto that compatible part: the result's zero Fourier mode is set to zero unconditionally, so the operator returns the zero-mean solution of $\Delta\psi = f - \langle f\rangle$ ($\langle f\rangle$ the input's mean); the per-mode form below is the unconditional statement.

For a vector or tensor field the same solve is applied to each component, so the tensor order is preserved.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating inverse_laplacian returns a fresh result instead.

Arguments

  • destination — the field that receives the result; it matches the domain and tensor order of field.
  • field — the field to invert, in spectral representation.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber symbols for the field's grid.

The spectral symbol divides by $-|k|^2$, each mode's true wavevector squared, and zeroes the zero mode:

\[\widehat{\Delta^{-1} f}(k) = \begin{cases} - \hat f(k) / |k|^2, & k \ne 0, \\ 0, & k = 0. \end{cases}\]

The inverse Laplacian is nonlocal

As the inverse of a differential operator, $\Delta^{-1}$ is nonlocal: unlike the Laplacian it has no local partial-derivative formula, and the value at any point depends on the whole field.

GBGPeriodicFields.DifferentialOperators.inverse_curl! — Function
inverse_curl!(destination::VectorFieldSpectral, field::VectorFieldSpectral,
              multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers)

Recover the divergence-free vector field whose curl is field, writing and returning destination — the inverse of curl! ($\nabla\times$) where that inverse exists.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating inverse_curl returns a fresh result instead.

Given a vorticity $w$ the equation $\nabla\times u = w$ fixes $u$ only up to a gradient, since $\nabla\times\nabla\phi = 0$. This returns the one representative that is itself divergence-free, $\nabla\cdot u = 0$, which is the gauge the choice amounts to and the one a velocity field satisfies. It also requires a solvable right-hand side: a curl is divergence-free, so $\nabla\cdot w = 0$ is a compatibility condition on the input, and a field violating it has its longitudinal part silently discarded rather than reported.

Arguments

  • destination — the vector field written with the result. It may alias field: every mode's three source coefficients are read before any of its components are written.
  • field — the vector field to invert, in spectral representation.
  • multipliers — the FieldSpectralMultipliers supplying the derivative wavenumbers, which must match the field's grid, layout, element type and storage family.

Because $\nabla\times(\nabla\times u) = \nabla(\nabla\cdot u) - \Delta u$ and the result is divergence-free, the operator is a Laplacian solve composed with a curl, and in spectral representation it is one coefficientwise pass,

\[\hat u(k) = \frac{\mathrm{i} a(k) \times \hat w(k)}{\lvert a(k)\rvert^{2}} ,\]

with $a$ the Nyquist-zeroed derivative wavenumbers and the $k = 0$ mode set to zero. Numerator and denominator share one family, so $\nabla\times(\nabla\times)^{-1}$ reproduces a divergence-free field exactly at every mode the warning does not zero, not only on the resolved band.

The zero mode has no preimage and is returned as zero

A constant field is the curl of nothing, so the $k = 0$ coefficient is written zero, as is each Nyquist corner where every derivative wavenumber vanishes. The recovered field has zero mean, the convention inverse_laplacian! uses for the same reason.

Projection operators

GBGPeriodicFields.DifferentialOperators.helmholtz_projection! — Function
helmholtz_projection!(
  destination::VectorFieldSpectral,
  field::VectorFieldSpectral,
  multipliers::FieldSpectralMultipliers,
)

helmholtz_projection!(destination, field, multipliers, kernel::SpectralKernel)
helmholtz_projection!(destination, field, multipliers,
                      kernel::SpectralKernel, α, β)

Compute the Helmholtz (Leray) projection (helmholtz_projection, $\mathbb P$) of a vector field $u$ and write it into destination, returning destination. The projection removes the longitudinal (curl-free) part of the field and keeps the divergence-free part, subtracting the gradient of the pressure-like potential that solves the divergence:

\[\mathbb P u = u - \nabla \Delta^{-1}(\nabla\cdot u),\]

an identity exact on the resolved band — the wavenumbers the grid resolves, excluding each even axis's Nyquist plane (the inverse Laplacian keeps the true squared wavevector $|k|^2$ while the projector uses the derivative family — the two coincide off the Nyquist planes). The implemented operator is the exact orthogonal projector onto the kernel of the discrete divergence: $\mathbb P^2 = \mathbb P$, and the divergence of the result vanishes, at every mode — the Nyquist planes included.

Use this in-place form when destination is already allocated; the allocating helmholtz_projection returns a fresh result instead.

Arguments

  • destination — the vector field that receives the result; it shares the domain of field, and it may alias field at no cost: the projection is computed in a single pass that reads all three components of a mode before writing any of them, so the aliased call needs no temporary.
  • field — the vector field to project, in spectral representation.
  • multipliers — the wavenumber symbols for the field's grid.

The spectral symbol subtracts the longitudinal part $a_i (a\cdot\hat u)/(a \cdot a)$ from $\hat u_i$, with $a$ the derivative wavenumber — the Nyquist-zeroed family the gradient and divergence use, equal to $k$ on the resolved band — and passes every mode with $a = 0$ unchanged (the zero mode, and the pure-Nyquist corners where the whole derivative wavenumber vanishes):

\[\begin{gathered} \widehat{(\mathbb P u)}_i(k) = \Bigl(\delta_{ij} - \frac{a_i a_j}{a \cdot a}\Bigr) \hat u_j(k), \\ \text{with } \delta_{ij} \text{ the Kronecker delta and } a \ne 0. \end{gathered}\]

The kernel-composed form

Passing a kernel composes the projection with a periodic convolution in the same fused pass. As a real physical object the result is the kernel-weighted local average of the projected field, $g \star (\mathbb P u)$ — a single operator, because convolution and projection both act coefficientwise in spectral representation, so the scalar symbol $\hat g$ multiplies the projection symbol. The additional arguments are:

  • kernel — the kernel's transfer function $\hat g$, in any storage class of a SpectralKernel — a StoredKernel holding one coefficient per mode, or a compressed class evaluated per mode (radial, separable, or their product) costing $O(N)$ for $N$ points per axis, not $O(N^3)$, exactly as convolve! takes it — including its reality condition, that $\hat g$ be Hermitian, $\hat g(-k) = \overline{\hat g(k)}$, without which the result is the spectrum of no real field; one scalar kernel weights all three components.
  • α, β — real accumulation weights as in convolve!: the four-argument form overwrites ($\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 0$), and the six-argument form computes

\[\begin{aligned} \widehat{\texttt{destination}}_i(k) &= \alpha \hat g(k) \widehat{(\mathbb P u)}_i(k) \\ &\quad + \beta \widehat{\texttt{destination}}_i(k). \end{aligned}\]

The composed form replaces a helmholtz_projection! followed by a convolve! — one fused pass instead of two, saving a full read and a full write of destination per call.

The projection is nonlocal in physical space

The inverse Laplacian $\Delta^{-1}$ is nonlocal, so $\mathbb P$ is a nonlocal operator in physical space even though it acts coefficientwise in spectral representation.

Internal API

These symbols are not part of the public interface; they are documented for contributors — the coefficientwise spectral kernels each operator applies per mode, and the gated CPU loops — "gated" meaning a loop that consults the threading setting before splitting — that mirror them expression for expression so a threaded run agrees with a device one bitwise.

_inverse_curl_modes!(backend, destination_1, destination_2, destination_3,
                     source_1, source_2, source_3,
                     wavenumber_1, wavenumber_2, wavenumber_3, imaginary_unit)

Apply the inverse curl to every mode, dispatching on backend: a KernelAbstractions launch of _inverse_curl_kernel! on a device, and a gated loop over slabs on a CPU. The two arms carry the same per-mode arithmetic expression for expression, so a threaded run agrees with a device one bitwise, which is what the GPU parity suite compares.
