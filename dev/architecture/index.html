Architecture — GBGPeriodicFields

This document records the architecture of the package and, more importantly, why it is the way it is. It exists so that future work (human or AI) extends the design instead of accidentally reverting it. CLAUDE.md carries the operational summary; this file carries the reasoning, the conceptual model (§2) and the writing and docstring conventions (the §2 documentation policy).

This file is self-contained: every piece of notation — a mathematical symbol (in LaTeX) or a code identifier — is introduced in plain language at its first use, the meaning stated before or in the same sentence as the symbol. So it reads top-to-bottom without opening the source or another document, as each entry of the separate decision log does. (Universally standard math — $\nabla$, $\Delta$, $\int$, $\mathbb R$ — may stand unglossed.) Keep it that way when editing.

1. Purpose and scope

The package is the field layer for pseudospectral simulation of incompressible fluid dynamics and turbulence on three-dimensional rectangular periodic domains. Its primary consumer is GBGHomogeneousTurbulence.jl, a direct numerical simulation of homogeneous isotropic turbulence built on it; the operation families below were shaped by what that solver needs, which is why a proposed addition without a consumer is deferred rather than built. It provides scalar, vector, and second-order tensor fields with two storage representations — physical and spectral (the spectral one stored non-redundantly as the real-to-complex / rfft half-spectrum) — plus FFT transform plans, spectral multipliers, storage-wise broadcasting, fiberwise algebra (FieldAlgebra), differential operators applied as spectral multipliers (DifferentialOperators), periodic convolution (Convolution), and the L²/measure reductions (Reductions). Interpolation and its adjoint anterpolation — the grid↔point transfer layer that serves consumer-side Lagrangian particle tracking — are in place (§10): the point-value sections, their fiberwise algebra, and the transfer schemes — local splines (Lalescu, Teaca & Carati 2010: the smooth Hermite and $C^0$ Lagrange hierarchies) and exact spectral-domain transfers (DirectDFT, NUFFT), unified under one AbstractTransferScheme. The same code must run on CPU Arrays and on GPU arrays (CUDA CuArray, AMDGPU ROCArray); development prioritizes GPU correctness and performance first. The target is a single node with shared-memory parallelism only — CPU multithreading (§9) and a single GPU device. Distributed-memory / multi-node execution (a cluster, MPI) and multi-GPU sharding are deliberately out of scope — a consumer's concern if ever needed, not this field layer's (every field is one host or device array, allocated via similar).

The package is a math backend — a library of field operations that turbulence and cloud solvers consume, not a solver itself. Its vocabulary is therefore domain-neutral (base/fiber, point/value), never particle or tracer (a consumer's interpretation), and it carries no concept of time: no advection, time integration, or temporal state. Trajectory dynamics belong to the consumer (§10).

2. The central principle: the architecture follows established mathematics

Every abstraction in this package is the code image of a standard mathematical object, and design decisions are settled by that mathematics rather than by implementation convenience. The primary source is differential geometry — the language of fiber bundles and their sections; the working rule is that a proposed abstraction must first be identifiable as a known mathematical object (or a faithful discretization of one) before it earns a place in the design. "For completeness" or "it would be convenient" is never a justification — indeed the recurring lesson below is that the absence of an operation is itself a precise mathematical statement.

The fiber-bundle model

A field, and a collection of values sampled at scattered locations, are both sections of a tensor bundle over a base. The domain is a flat periodic box (a 3-torus) with a single global frame, so the bundle is trivial — the product $E = B \times V$ of a base $B$ and a fiber $V$. The word "base" carries two related meanings. The continuum base is the torus $\Omega$ itself. The bundle is drawn over both: the trivial product exists over $\Omega$ and over each discrete base alike, and §10's pullback $\iota$ is what relates them. The discrete base is the finite set a section is actually stored on — a Grid3D of nodes or a PointSet of scattered locations, each a finite subset of $\Omega$ — and it is the one meant wherever the docs say a section is "over a base" or a reduction "integrates the base away". Neither reading is dispensable: the continuum carries the derivative and measure, the discrete set carries the data. The fiber is a tensor power of $\mathbb R^3$, fixed by the tensor order $R$: $V = \mathbb R$ ($R = 0$, scalar), $\mathbb R^3$ ($R = 1$, vector, FiberVector), $\mathbb R^3 \otimes \mathbb R^3 = \mathbb R^{3\times 3}$ ($R = 2$, second-order tensor, FiberTensor2), and higher (FiberTensor3/4).

That single global frame is what lets a derivative index be stored as a fiber index: $\nabla$ maps a section of $E$ to a section of $E \otimes T^{*}\Omega$, and the frame identifies $T\Omega$ — and, being orthonormal, $T^{*}\Omega$ with it — with the GENERATING space $\mathbb R^3$, the $R = 1$ fiber. It is not identified with $V$, which has rank $3^R$ and matches $T\Omega$ only at $R = 1$. So $E \otimes T^{*}\Omega$ has fiber $V \otimes \mathbb R^3 = (\mathbb R^3)^{\otimes (R+1)}$, derivative index LAST: $(\nabla u)_{ij} = \partial_{x_j} u_i$, the slot the divergence contracts, leaving $\nabla u$ one order higher. That single global frame is orthonormal and right-handed, so the fiber carries the Cartesian Euclidean structure: components refer to one fixed rectangular frame, indices are neither raised nor lowered, and there is no metric to carry because it is the identity. This is what makes the bundle trivial and flat rather than merely modelled as such, and it is why the bundle vocabulary here organizes the code without promising machinery on manifolds — the tensors are Cartesian tensors in the classical continuum-mechanics sense, save that cross products and curls flip sign under reflection. Do not introduce curvilinear-coordinate machinery, explicit metric tensors, or a vector/covector distinction unless the package design changes to need them: each would make the frame a variable the whole operation set has to carry, and none has a consumer. A section assigns a fiber value to each base point; the fiber-last data layout (§3) is exactly that section, stored base-indexed.

The package's structures then organize around that one object, rather than standing as separate worlds. Two of them are parameters of a section — what a section is built from — and three are operation families, maps acting on sections, each reaching only as far as the structure it needs:

  • The base is either the structured grid Grid3D — a field is a section sampled on the grid — or the unstructured point set PointSet — point-values are a section sampled at scattered points (§10). Same fibers, different base.
  • Fiberwise algebra is the vertical (within-fiber) structure: addition, real scaling, contraction with a constant fiber, dot/cross/tensor products, transpose, sym/skew/dev, norms, and an arbitrary fiber map (map!, the escape hatch) all act inside fibers, pointwise in the base, and are therefore base-independent — the very same operation on the very same fiber type, whether the base is a grid or a point set. This is the structural reason one set of generated kernels (§6) serves fields and point-values verbatim, and the mathematical motivation for treating the section as the primary abstraction: a shared AbstractFiberSection{T, R} supertype spanning both, in the FiberSections module (§10).
  • Differential operators are the complementary horizontal structure — gradient, divergence, curl, Laplacian are derivatives along the base, using the periodic box's flat connection — its one fixed frame, which lets vectors over different base points be compared directly (§4). Read that precisely, since a Grid3D is a finite set and has no tangent bundle of its own: the derivative lives on the continuum torus, and a grid section reaches it by standing for the trigonometric polynomial its coefficients define, of which each operator is the exact term-by-term derivative on the resolved band, the modes short of each even axis's Nyquist.

What distinguishes the two bases is therefore whether such an identification exists — a uniform periodic grid determines that polynomial, so it inherits the derivative; a PointSet of scattered points determines no such interpolant, so point-value sections carry the vertical algebra and no derivatives.

  • Base reductions are the third operation family — integrating the base away against a measure the base carries (Grid3D cell volume; PointSet counting): integral (extensive), the normalized inner/l2norm, and the mean/std/var moments. They map a section to a scalar or a fiber — with one sub-family deliberately excepted, since naming it here saves a reader the contradiction: the radial reductions radial_spectrum and radial_correlation return a profile, a pair of vectors over shells, not a single value. They are base-dependent (the measure is the base's), yet — like the vertical algebra — are shared by fields and point-values through AbstractFiberSection (§12, decision 18). The same measure concept recurs as the interpolation kernel (below) and a convolution multiplier (the Convolution module, decision 20): one L²/integration structure in three roles (the measure taxonomy, decision 19).
  • Representation is a choice of basis for a grid section's data (distinct from the fiber frame above): physical storage uses the nodal basis, spectral storage the Fourier basis, and the rfft is the linear isomorphism between them. "Basis" is the right intuition and slightly flatters the codomain: the transform is not an automorphism of one space, because the stored complex half-spectrum has strictly more real dimensions than the section it came from. It is an isomorphism onto the Hermitian-constrained subspace of that array — the constraint being what makes the map a bijection and the round trip exact (FORMALIZATION.md states it typed). A point-value section has no second representation — there is no periodic grid to transform over — so the point types are representation-free (§10).

(The vertical/horizontal language is the bundle-theoretic one — fiber directions versus the connection's base directions — applied here by analogy to operations: the fiberwise algebra is order-zero and base-preserving — it covers the identity on the base, acting inside the fiber over each point and touching no neighbour. It is deliberately not called fiber-preserving, which this document reserves — in a narrower sense than the standard bundle-theoretic one — for a map that leaves the fiber by the identity (the transfer maps, the fiber-preserving reductions); dot, norm, det and map! all change the fiber. The horizontal family, by contrast, is built from the connection's covariant derivative, order one. Built from rather than equal to: gradient, divergence, curl and symmetric_gradient are that derivative, but inverse_laplacian and helmholtz_projection invert or project with it and are nonlocal — no local partial-derivative formula, the value at a point depending on the whole field. Strictly, vertical ($\ker \mathrm{d}\pi$, the kernel of the bundle projection $\pi$'s differential — canonical) and horizontal (a connection-chosen complement) are subbundles of the total space's tangent bundle.)

Transfer between sections over different bases is the interpolation/anterpolation pair: pulling a grid section back to points (evaluation — pairing with a Dirac mass $\delta_x$ — $f(x) = \delta_x(f)$) and the adjoint pushforward of point masses onto the grid. The full function–measure duality is given in §10; the Interpolation module docstring carries its concrete face (the adjoint identity with the point and grid counts $M$/$N$); the interpolation weights are themselves a unit-mass kernel measure (signed for higher-order schemes), the base-changing sibling of the convolution multiplier — see the measure taxonomy (decision 19).

The architecture map

A field — or values sampled at scattered points — is a section $f$, a right inverse of the bundle projection $\pi$:

\[B \xrightarrow{ f } E \xrightarrow{ \pi } B, \qquad \pi \circ f = \mathrm{id}_B,\]

with base $B$ (the structured Grid3D or the unstructured PointSet), $E = B \times V$ the trivial tensor bundle, and $f$ the field (AbstractFiberSection): one fiber value per base point. Everything below operates on sections, plus the support mechanics — each explained in its own section, referenced here rather than repeated:

  • Representations — physical (nodal) and spectral (rfft, written $\hat f$) views of one section, exchanged by the isomorphism $\mathcal F$ (to_spectral/to_physical); the spectral machinery and the operator-as-multiplier square are §4.
  • Vertical — fiberwise algebra (FieldAlgebra on fields, Points on point-values): add/scale, the weighted combination combine!, contract (dot/cross), transpose, sym/skew/dev, the tr/det invariants, pointwise norms — generated once (§6) — plus map!, the arbitrary-φ escape hatch (any unnamed pointwise fiber map; decision 48).
  • Horizontal — differential operators (DifferentialOperators): gradient, divergence, curl, symmetric_gradient, laplacian, …, applied as spectral multipliers (§4).
  • Convolution (Convolution) applies the kernel multiply $\hat f \cdot \hat g$ in spectral representation (§11).
  • Measure / L² (Reductions) provides integral, inner, weighted_inner, l2norm, mean, the radial spectra (§12).
  • The grid↔point transfer (Interpolation): interpolation $W$ and its adjoint anterpolation $W^{\top}$ (§10).
  • Moving between storage families (adapt, decision 63): host and device are two families of the same object, and crossing between them is an explicit operation rather than something arithmetic does implicitly — invariant 14 refuses a mixed pair, and this is its named complement.
  • Support mechanics — types & memory layout (§3), validation design (§5), code generation (§6), aliasing (§7), broadcasting (§8), performance & threading (§9).

Reductions, convolution, and the grid↔point transfer are the same L²/integration structure in three roles — the measure taxonomy (decision 19). Which operation is valid in which representation, the naming rules, and the documentation policy follow.

The invariants

Fourteen rules constrain every change, and the rest of this document refers to them by number. They are collected here so a reader meeting "invariant 6" has somewhere to look; each is stated in one line, with a pointer to the section that develops it.

  1. The public method set is the spec. Deliberate absences encode representation validity — there is no field-times-field multiplication in spectral representation (the coefficientwise product is a convolution, offered under its true name in §11), no spectral division by a scalar field, no spectral pointwise norms. Operations linear in the field exist in both representations; nonlinear ones are physical-space only. A missing combination is never added for completeness (see the next subsection).
  2. A field is a real physical object, and physical and spectral are two representations of it. Complex arrays are storage detail, never user-facing — hence "a field in spectral representation", not "a spectral field".
  3. The data layout is fiber-last, $(N_x, N_y, N_z[, 3[, 3]])$, so component slices are contiguous; FFT batching and CPU threading both rely on it (§3).
  4. The transform normalization is AbstractFFTs': forward unnormalized, inverse normalized. Adding a physical constant in spectral representation therefore updates only the zero Fourier mode, scaled by the grid-point count, rather than every coefficient (§4).
  5. The Nyquist split is deliberate. On an even-length axis the derivative wavenumber is zeroed at the Nyquist frequency, so derivatives of real fields stay real, while the squared wavenumber keeps the true $k^2$, so the Laplacian and Poisson-type solves stay correct. The two families are not unified (§4).
  6. The package is GPU-portable by construction: storage is a generic AbstractArray, no package method scalar-indexes a device array, allocation goes through similar/similar_field, and fused broadcast assignments are preferred (§9).
  7. No type piracy: a Base or LinearAlgebra function is imported before it is extended.
  8. No fiber-fiber methods. The fiber types are StaticArrays aliases and already carry their own algebra, so the package does not redefine it.
  9. Aliasing is safe by construction, and a section owns its storage so that it is. Every fused pass reads an element's sources into registers before writing any of its outputs, so an in-place aliased call is computed directly and is bitwise-identical to the non-aliased one; the former guard family is retired. That argument holds only while one section's element $n$ is the other's element $n$, which is the ownership half of the invariant and not a detail of it: the constructors reject borrowed storage — a SubArray anywhere in the parent chain — leaving same-array as the only way two sections can share memory (decision 59). Ownership, not layout: an Array, a CuArray, a ROCArray and a reshape of any of them all pass. The mul! contract keeps LinearAlgebra's documented contract that the destination must not alias (§7).
  10. The $\alpha$/$\beta$ convention. A five-argument form converts both weights to the field's scalar type and branches on $\beta = 0$ (overwrite) versus $\beta \ne 0$ (accumulate); a three-argument form delegates to it with $\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 0$. Throughout, $\alpha$ scales the computed result and $\beta$ the destination accumulated onto. Two deliberate exceptions, and only these two: combine! is the weighted combination $\alpha a + \beta b$, so its two weights scale its two operands instead — which is what the operation is, and what its name announces — and exponential_convolve!'s two-operand forms carry the same combination, and so the same reading of the glyphs, into a fused convolution. Both are operations whose definition is a weighted combination; no operation may reuse the glyphs for a role other than that.
  11. The transform input contract. The allocating transforms preserve their input; the in-place ones may overwrite the source, and the inverse real transform uses its spectral input as scratch on some backends but not others. Never rely on a !-transform's source surviving — with one deliberate exception that guarantees it: the four-argument scratch arity of to_physical!, which consumes a caller-supplied scratch in the source's place (decision 64; §4).
  12. This is a math backend — no time, and no particles. The terminology stays domain-neutral (base and fiber, point and value), and the package introduces no advection, time integration, or per-step state; trajectory dynamics belong to the consumer (§10).
  13. Allocation-free is an enforced contract. Every in-place ! operator allocates no field-sized scratch when called on preallocated buffers with a destination distinct from its sources. This is load-bearing on a GPU: a per-call device temporary escapes Julia's garbage collector, which triggers on host pressure and cannot see the device buffer a dead array owns, so the pool grows every step until the run wedges (§9).
  14. Sections combine only within one precision and one storage family — never by conversion. An operation over two or more sections requires them to agree in element type and in storage family (Array with Array, CuArray with CuArray), and refuses anything else with a clear error rather than promoting, copying, or transferring. The refusal is a decision of the package, not an accident of a vendor library declining a mixed-pointer call or a kernel launcher declining mixed arrays. It binds every argument an operation takes, the destination and the non-section partners included — a spectral field and its multipliers must match too. Conversion is an explicitly-named operation, never something an arithmetic operation does implicitly: crossing storage families is adapt (decision 63), or adapt! into a destination that already exists (sections only; decision 69), and crossing precisions remains future work.

Representation validity lives in the method set

Seeing each representation as a basis for a grid section's data — the nodal basis (physical) and the Fourier basis (spectral), with the rfft the linear isomorphism between them — fixes exactly which operations are valid where: an operation exists in a representation only when it is the same operation on the underlying section there — equivalently, only when it commutes with the rfft isomorphism.

  • Linear in the field — addition/subtraction of fields, scaling by real numbers, contractions with constant fibers, transpose, sym/skew/dev are valid in both representations, the Fourier transform commuting with them.
  • Adding constants — in physical space, every grid value shifts; in spectral space, $\mathcal F(f + c) = \mathcal F(f) + c N \delta_{k,0}$ under the unnormalized-forward convention, with $N$ the total grid-point count (prod(grid_size)) and $k$ the wavenumber — so only the zero mode ($k = 0$) changes, scaled by $N$. This is why _spectral_constant_scale exists and why constant kernels in spectral representation touch a single coefficient (per component).
  • Nonlinear in the field — products of two fields, division by a scalar field, pointwise norms, contractions of two fields (dot, cross, tensor mul!, and the fiberwise tensor inner product dot! — not the inner reduction, which is Parseval-valid in spectral representation): physical-space only. The coefficientwise spectral product is not the Fourier representation of the pointwise product (that would be a convolution), so offering it as a product would silently compute the wrong physical operation — that coefficientwise product is convolution, exposed under its true name as convolve (the Convolution module, decision 20), never as *.

The pointwise product is not preserved by $\mathcal F$ — the convolution-theorem square ($\star$ denoting periodic convolution, the package's convolve):

\[\begin{CD} (f,g) @>{\star}>> f \star g \\ @V{\mathcal F}VV @VV{\mathcal F}V \\ (\hat f,\hat g) @>>{\cdot}> \hat f \cdot \hat g \end{CD} \qquad\qquad \mathcal F(f \star g) = \hat f \cdot \hat g .\]

The coefficientwise product downstairs is convolution upstairs (offered as convolve ($\star$), §11); the pointwise product $f \cdot g$ transforms to a convolution with no coefficientwise spectral form — exactly why those nonlinear operations are physical-space only.

The same finiteness makes a nonlinear result aliased. A product doubles the spectral band — the determinant (a triple product) triples it, a non-polynomial map such as the norm is unbounded — so content beyond the grid's Nyquist (highest stored) wavenumber folds back onto the resolved modes when the physical result is transformed to spectral representation. The grid point values themselves stay exact; the aliasing is a spectral-representation artifact, and de-aliasing — the sharp low-pass spectral_cutoff, or phase-shift via translation_kernel (§11) — is the consumer's choice, the package supplying the tools rather than the strategy.

The consequence is a hard project invariant: a missing method is a statement, not an omission. The 206-method public surface of FieldAlgebra is the machine-checkable encoding of the validity table above. Any refactor must reproduce the method set exactly; it was verified signature-for-signature against the original — M2 (98 methods: add!/subtract!/multiply!/divide!/+/-/*//) and then M3 (the dot/cross/mul!/transpose/norm/sym/skew/dev/trace contraction and decomposition families, plus their * operators), bringing the total to 198; the later trace tr/tr! addition brings it to 202, and the weighted combination combine! (2) with the determinant det/det! (2; decision 27) complete the 206-method surface, all parity-checked at runtime in test/method_parity.jl. The point-value algebra realizes the vertical operations on the unstructured base — it mirrors the physical field surface and is locked by its own separate baseline (§10).

Naming conventions

Operation names follow what the operation is — the same mathematics-first principle:

  • Reductions (section → scalar/fiber) take the quantity's noun: integral, inner, l2norm, lpnorm, radial_spectrum (the radial profile is still the quantity's noun).
  • Canonically named operators (section → section with a standard, often eponymous operator name and no natural verb) take that noun: gradient, divergence, curl, inverse_curl, laplacian, inverse_laplacian, helmholtz_projection.
  • Generic transformations (section → section, named by the action) take the verb: the elementwise algebra (add, subtract, multiply, divide), the representation changes (to_physical, to_spectral), and the kernel-measure maps interpolate / anterpolate and convolve.
  • In-place forms append ! uniformly (gradient!, interpolate!, convolve!).
  • Extensions of Base / LinearAlgebra / Statistics keep the source name, overriding the above (dot, cross, mul!, transpose, norm, map, map!, mean, std, var, cov, maximum, minimum, extrema); the continuum-mechanics decomposition abbreviations sym / skew / dev are likewise conventional. An extended generic is re-exported when its source is not already in scope, so that a caller need not know which standard library a verb lives in: the LinearAlgebra and Statistics names are re-exported by the top-level GBGPeriodicFields module, while the Base ones need no such treatment. Re-exporting a binding the package only extends is safe — two packages re-exporting the same binding do not collide, only two exporting different bindings of one name do.
  • Modules carry the family noun (Fields, DifferentialOperators, Reductions, Interpolation, Convolution); the operations inside follow the rules above — so Convolution / convolve mirrors Interpolation / interpolate.

The discriminator that places a new operation: a generic transformation with a natural verb is a verb (convolution → convolve); a canonically named operator is its noun (the Laplacian → laplacian). The operators gradient/laplacian/helmholtz_projection are nouns because they are the names of those operators, not by a noun preference.

Documentation policy

The @eval code generators (§6) emit methods only, never docstrings. Each operation has one docstring attached to the bare binding (function f end for package-owned generics; the bare imported name for Base/LinearAlgebra functions — never function f end there, since a missing import would silently create a new generic). Docstrings show a few canonical signatures plus prose about representation validity, including which combinations are intentionally absent. Exhaustive signature lists were dropped: they duplicated the tables and went stale instantly.

Mathematics is rendered LaTeX, with the symbol bound to the code. All math in docstrings and in these doc pages is written as Documenter LaTeX (KaTeX) in standard notation — inline $\langle f, g\rangle$, display math blocks — never Unicode-glyph approximations (angle brackets, a summation sign or an integral sign typed as characters). Because the package is grounded in mathematics (the principle of this section), each code object is explicitly identified with its mathematical symbol on first mention so the correspondence is never left implicit: gradient ($\nabla$), divergence ($\nabla\cdot$), curl ($\nabla\times$), laplacian ($\Delta$), inverse_laplacian ($\Delta^{-1}$), helmholtz_projection ($\mathbb{P}$), inner ($\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$), l2norm ($\lVert\cdot\rVert_2$), integral ($\int\,\mathrm{d}\mu$), convolve ($\star$), interpolate/anterpolate ($W$/$W^\top$), to_spectral/to_physical ($\mathcal F$/$\mathcal F^{-1}$), a field in spectral representation written $\hat f$. (In a """ docstring the LaTeX backslashes are escaped \\; in a .md page they are raw. Plain # comments stay Unicode — they are not rendered.) Reductions is the reference implementation.

3. Types and memory layout

Grid3D{T} holds domain geometry; RealFourierLayout records the rfft layout. Field types are parameterized by scalar type T<:AbstractFloat: ScalarFieldPhysical/ScalarFieldSpectral, VectorFieldPhysical/VectorFieldSpectral, TensorFieldPhysical{2}/TensorFieldSpectral{2}. Constant fiber values are StaticArrays aliases — FiberVector{T} = SVector{3,T}, FiberTensor2{T} = SMatrix{3,3,T,9} (plus FiberTensor3/4) — so fiber-fiber algebra comes from StaticArrays and LinearAlgebra. StaticArrays is a direct dependency, not just the type-only StaticArraysCore: the package indexes fibers (a[i], A[i, j]) and uses their algebra inside the generated kernels, so the core-types package alone leaves those operations undefined.

Data layout is fiber-last: (Nx, Ny, Nz) for scalars, (Nx, Ny, Nz, 3) for vectors, (Nx, Ny, Nz, 3, 3) for tensors; spectral arrays are Complex{T} with the axis the real transform halves being the first under the default RealFourierLayout, which may assign any of the three. With column-major storage, each component slice data[:, :, :, i] (or data[:, :, :, i, j]) is one contiguous block. That gives batched per-component FFTs, coalesced GPU access, pure streaming writes for elementwise kernels, and trivially chunkable index ranges for the CPU threading (§9). Component access goes through views (_vector_component_data, _tensor2_component_data); views are lazy, so splicing them into generated formulas costs nothing.

Storage is any AbstractArray, and the package names no vendor type and no memory kind. A field's storage family is the kind of array holding its values — Array, CuArray, ROCArray — and on CUDA the same CuArray may sit in device memory or in unified memory paged between the GPU and host RAM. Which of the two is CUDA.jl's configuration rather than the package's, and every allocating route follows it, so a CUDA run can hold fields larger than the card with no change to the code (decision 84, Running on a GPU).

4. Spectral machinery

FieldTransformPlans owns the FFT plans (per backend). Forward transforms are unnormalized; inverse transforms carry the normalization (AbstractFFTs convention) — this single convention choice propagates into the zero-mode constant updates described above.

Transform input-preservation contract. The in-place to_physical!/to_spectral! apply their plans through mul!, the no-copy path, so the source field may be overwritten as scratch. This is intrinsic to real↔complex FFTs: FFTW's complex-to-real (irfft) transform destroys its input by default, and the multidimensional case has no input-preserving algorithm at all. It is also backend-divergent, and which backend overwrites which direction is not uniform: FFTW preserves the forward to_spectral! but destroys the inverse to_physical!, while a GPU backend (rocFFT) can differ on either. The package therefore guarantees nothing about a plain !-transform's source on any backend, matching the to_spectral!/to_physical! docstrings — the one guaranteed-preserving ! call is the four-argument to_physical! scratch arity, which pays one explicit copy into a caller-supplied scratch for exactly that guarantee (decision 64); to keep a uniform, portable contract, the allocating to_physical/to_spectral copy their input before transforming, so non-! calls never mutate their argument (the Julia convention) while ! calls stay allocation-light and document that the source may be consumed.

FieldSpectralMultipliers holds six one-dimensional arrays: derivative wavenumbers wavenumber_1–wavenumber_3 and squared_wavenumber_1–squared_wavenumber_3, plus the grid and layout. It lives in Fields, as a sibling of FieldTransformPlans, because it is representation infrastructure (what the spectral axes mean), not a differential operator. Two constructors by design:

  • An explicit constructor taking all six arrays (lengths validated), testable in isolation and letting callers control the backend.
  • A convenience constructor from a field: wavenumbers $2\pi n/L$, with $n$ the integer mode index and $L$ the box length, are built on the host and copied to the field's backend (no scalar GPU writes).

The two differ in the type they produce, not only in convenience: a final Canonical type parameter records whether the arrays were derived from the grid (the convenience constructor) or supplied by the caller (the explicit one, which validates lengths and not values). Two canonical sets on a matching grid and layout therefore hold equal values by construction, which is what lets convolve collapse two radial kernels without a runtime test — see decision 61.

The Nyquist split is deliberate and must not be unified. On even-length axes:

  • Derivative multipliers are zeroed at the Nyquist frequency. The Nyquist mode of a real signal is a pure cosine; multiplying by $\mathrm{i} k_N$ would produce a component the real signal cannot represent, and the symmetric, real-preserving choice for its derivative is zero. This is standard pseudospectral practice and keeps derivatives of real fields exactly real.
  • Squared multipliers keep the true $k^2$ at Nyquist. The Laplacian of the Nyquist mode is perfectly well defined ($-k_N^2 \mathrm{mode}$); zeroing it would silently remove grid-scale dissipation and corrupt inverse_laplacian.

Reference tests pin this down: multipliers vs fftfreq/rfftfreq, transform round-trips, and a real-field Nyquist end-to-end test (the derivative zeroed at Nyquist vs the squared wavenumber kept) — see docs/TESTING.md.

Operators are multipliers — the intertwiner square. $\mathcal F$ (to_spectral, with inverse to_physical) is a linear isomorphism, and every translation-invariant operator $L$ — gradient ($\nabla$), divergence ($\nabla\cdot$), curl ($\nabla\times$), laplacian ($\Delta$) — is diagonalized by it into a pointwise multiplier $\hat L = m(k) \cdot$ ($\mathrm{i}a$, $\mathrm{i}a\cdot$, $\mathrm{i}a\times$, $-|k|^2$, writing $a$ for the derivative wavenumber and $k$ for the true one — the Nyquist split just above):

\[\begin{CD} f @>{\mathcal F}>> \hat f \\ @V{L}VV @VV{\hat L}V \\ Lf @>>{\mathcal F}> \widehat{Lf} \end{CD} \qquad\qquad \mathcal F\circ L = \hat L\circ\mathcal F .\]

This commuting square is invariant 2 (one object, two representations) and the entire design of DifferentialOperators: $L$ is never formed in physical space — the multiplier $\hat L$ is applied in spectral and conjugated by $\mathcal F^{\pm1}$. It is the worked case of §2's representation-validity rule: an operation may be offered in a representation iff its square commutes.

5. Validation design

Fields owns compatibility checking, built from atomic Boolean predicates (_same_scalar_type, _same_grid, _same_layout, _same_backend) composed into _check_same_domain. The last of those is what realizes invariant 14: it throws when two sections live in different storage families (an Array against a CuArray), and it sits in the domain check rather than below it, so the error does not depend on which operation the user happened to call. Tensor order is deliberately not among them — _same_tensor_order is checked one level up, in _check_field_compatibility only, because the domain check must let order differ for the operators that change it (gradient, divergence). Composed on top are the representation-dispatched _check_field_domain_compatibility (physical-physical and spectral-spectral methods — the dispatch barrier that prevents cross-representation mixing), the unexported _check_field_compatibility (un-prefixed, but documented with the internals), and _check_field_matches_context for transform plans.

History: FieldAlgebra used to carry near-duplicate checkers (_check_scalar_field_compatibility, _check_tensor2_vector_field_compatibility). These were removed in favor of direct calls to Fields._check_field_domain_compatibility — an explicit decision against alias names: one concept, one name, imported explicitly.

Check placement follows one rule: bodies that go through field broadcasting do not pre-check, because the broadcast machinery already enforces compatibility (shape/grid/style promotion); bodies that bypass broadcasting — copy-then-kernel paths, zero-mode updates — check explicitly. This is why, e.g. physical scalar±Real methods are bare broadcasts while their spectral counterparts call _check_field_compatibility before _copy_field_data!.

6. The code-generation architecture of FieldAlgebra

Why it emerged

The original FieldAlgebra was 3033 hand-written lines defining ~198 public methods, with per-component kernels unrolled by hand (three statements per vector operation, nine per tensor). The regularity was overwhelming: one mathematical operation expands into 8–32 near-identical methods (two representations × two argument orders × in-place/allocating × vector/tensor). Hand maintenance meant every new operation or convention change had to be edited consistently in dozens of places — a divergence machine. The refactor regenerates the same method set from small tables, so each formula and each convention is written exactly once, while the public surface (and its deliberate absences) stays bit-identical.

Layers

  • M1 — expression builders (the vertical-algebra builders in FiberSections, imported by FieldAlgebra): _vector_component(symbol, kind, i) / _tensor_component(symbol, kind, i, j) produce component-access expressions, with kind = :field (component view) or :fiber (StaticArrays indexing); _sum_terms and _product combine formulas; _vector_kernel_body / _tensor_kernel_body (with alpha_beta::Bool) and _scalar_kernel_body wrap formulas into kernel bodies ending in return destination. With alpha_beta = true the body branches on iszero(β_value) so the pure-overwrite path stays allocation-free.
  • M2 — elementwise families (done): flat @eval tables stamp out constant-fiber kernels (an update lambda (target, value) -> :($target .+= $value) applied per component via statement splats), field-field combination and operators, scalar-constant operators, field±fiber (one row per field type carries the kernels and yields eight methods), real scaling, and scalar-field×fiber (rows carry the destination tensor order for similar_field(…, Val(order))). Type parameters enter tables as expressions — :(TensorFieldPhysical{2, T}) with where {T<:AbstractFloat} on the emitted method. Irregular corners stay explicit, each with a comment saying why: the scalar±Real family (physical broadcasts vs zero-mode updates; reversed subtraction is its own operation), the four ambiguity-resolving multiply! signatures, and the physical-only divisions.
  • M3 — contraction kernels (done): one formula table per family — dot, cross, tensor-vector and tensor-tensor mul!, transpose, tensor inner products, pointwise norms, sym/skew/dev — fed through the kernel-body builders. Per-family flags: has_beta (α/β accumulation for the mul! families; five-argument forms convert T(α), T(β), three-argument forms delegate one(T), zero(T)) and the routing through the fused executor (cross and the unary index-shuffles — transpose, sym, skew, dev — run alias-safe by construction, an element's sources read before its outputs are written, decision 39; mul! documents the no-alias contract instead, per LinearAlgebra convention). The sym/skew/dev methods on bare FiberTensor2 remain explicit StaticArrays one-liners.
  • M4 — public wrappers/operators (done, folded into M3): the allocating forms and the * operators (tensor·vector, tensor·tensor) are generated alongside their mul!/dot/cross/… kernels, completing the 206-method surface.

The same builders are reused verbatim to generate the point-value algebra over the unstructured base (§10) — the formulas are base-independent (§2). They live in the shared FiberSections module (with the _assign! primitive); both FieldAlgebra and Points source them from there, so each fiberwise formula — including the norm/sym/skew/dev ones, via _section_scalar_type — is written exactly once.

Verifying codegen changes

With Julia available, verification is runtime introspection: load the package, compare per-generic methods(f) counts against the committed baseline (test/method_parity.jl), and run detect_ambiguities (from Test) over the package. The full M2+M3 baseline is 206 public methods (e.g. * 28, mul! 30, dot/dot! 10 each, add! 14, combine! 2, tr 2, det/det! 1 each). (During the no-Julia refactor phase, parity was checked by simulating table expansion and diffing normalized signatures against the original file; runtime introspection supersedes that.)

7. Aliasing policy

Alias safety is by construction: every fused pass — the one-pass differential operators (decisions 32 and 38's operator kernels) and the generic fiberwise executor behind the algebra (decision 39) — reads all of an element's source values into registers before writing any of that element's destinations, and elements are disjoint, so an in-place call (curl!(u, u, m), transpose!(A, A), sym!(A, A)) is computed directly and is bitwise-identical to the non-aliased call, with no temporary.

That argument carries one hypothesis, load-bearing rather than incidental: one section's element $n$ has to be the other's element $n$. Ownership supplies it — a section's constructor rejects borrowed storage, a SubArray anywhere in the parent chain, so same-array is the only way two sections can share memory (decision 59). Before that rule, two partially-overlapping views at different base indices broke the argument silently across fourteen operations; such a pair is now not merely undetected but unconstructible.

The former guard family (_guard_noalias! and its point-value twin), which ran an aliased call through a fresh field and a copy-back, is retired — decision 39 removed its last callers. The one remaining aliasing rule is contractual: mul! documents that the destination must not alias the inputs, matching LinearAlgebra.mul! semantics (its fused pass happens to be alias-safe too, but the documented contract is not widened).

8. Broadcasting

Fields broadcast storage-wise: a field in a broadcast stands for its data array, and the field broadcast style (owned by Fields) materializes into field storage. Style promotion is one-sided by design — define a single binary rule per style pair, never symmetric mixed-representation rules. The broadcast layer doubles as the compatibility enforcement for broadcast-mediated methods (§5).

The broadcast layer is internal machinery, not a public contract. A consumer can dot-broadcast fields — Julia's dispatch makes that unpreventable without hostile piracy against Base idiom — and the expression will fuse and return a field; but nothing about that surface is specified, verified, or guarded. In particular it can express what the method set deliberately withholds (invariant 1): a .* between two fields in spectral representation compiles and computes the banned coefficientwise product, with none of the named operations' checks. The public contract is the named method set; the broadcast layer exists to implement it (decision 35).

9. Performance model and CPU threading

Elementwise field operations are memory-bandwidth-bound streaming; FFTs dominate asymptotically. Both are now threaded on CPU (shared memory). On GPU, broadcasting and the device FFT libraries already launch parallel kernels, so the CPU threading is dispatched away (below). The full performance model (per operation class), the externally sourced best practices, the recorded benchmark findings, and the tracked benchmark suite live in the companion PERFORMANCE.md.

FFTs. FFTW threading is baked in at plan time, so the package's __init__ calls FFTW.set_num_threads(Threads.nthreads()) before any FieldTransformPlans is built (set_fft_threads! retunes it). Oversubscription caveat: FFTW's pthreads multiply with Julia's tasks — code that parallelizes across fields and transforms inside each task should build plans under set_fft_threads!(1). The common case (one field, transforms serial in a time loop) wants nthreads() FFTW threads, the default.

Elementwise/fiberwise ops. One internal primitive _assign!(destination, bc) in FiberSections. The generic method is Broadcast.materialize!, the safe default: GPU and unknown array wrappers keep the standard fused broadcast, so invariant 6 holds by dispatch, not discipline. The Union{Array, SubArray{<:Any, <:Any, <:Array}} specialization threads a single instantiated Broadcasted over contiguous chunks (one Broadcasted tree, no temporaries, one memory pass), bitwise-identical to serial because each output index is written once. The threaded path splits the destination's slab axis — the last among the largest, so a whole-vector-field write splits a grid axis, never the length-3 fiber — and runs the standard fused materialize! per slab over sliced views of the operands, so each slab executes the same SIMD kernel the serial path uses (decision 38; its per-element predecessor ran ~4.7× slower per core and inverted at low thread counts — PERFORMANCE.md §4 finding (l)). A serial cutoff (_SERIAL_CUTOFF, default $2^{14}$) keeps small ops — including the one-element zero-mode views — serial. Policy is hybrid: threaded when nthreads() > 1 and above the cutoff, with a task-local ScopedValue override (with_threading(enabled = …)) for nested-parallel callers.

Two plug points route work through _assign!: the field broadcast style's copyto! (all whole-field dotted ops and the field-level algebra), and the kernel assignments — the codegen body builders emit _assign!(target, …) (via _to_broadcasted, which rewrites a dotted formula into a lazy broadcasted tree at codegen time), and the hand-written FieldAlgebra / DifferentialOperators kernels use the @_assign! macro in place of .=. (The decision-39 fused executor is the third route: its values functions, each reading one element's inputs and returning its outputs, run inside _fiberwise_pass!'s kernel and slab loop under the same gate.) No types, signatures, or method tables change — the 206-method surface and the zero-ambiguity property are preserved. Discipline: a raw elementwise write must go through _assign!/@_assign!, never a bare .=, or it silently runs single-threaded.

Reductions (global norms, integrals — the L²/measure structure) are now provided in the Reductions module (decision 18), built on sum/dot/maximum rather than the threaded _assign!: those reorder floating-point adds (pairwise on the host, differently on threads/GPU), so they are tolerance-tested, not bitwise like the assignments here. (The only already-threaded part is a prior step in a couple of them: lpnorm for $p \ne 2$ builds the pointwise fiber magnitude $\lvert f\rvert$ via the vertical norm — an _assign!-routed fiberwise op, hence threaded — then reduces that with the plain sum/maximum. The reductions inner/l2norm/integral/mean build no such field, so they are a single, unthreaded dot/sum.) That remaining work landed as decision 40: every CPU reduction now runs a fixed midpoint tree, spawned at its top levels through the same gate, bitwise thread-count-invariant; the speedup saturates at socket bandwidth over single-core (typically 2–6×; more on multi-socket nodes, where thread pinning and NUMA first-touch matter — ThreadPinning.jl). Placement is not settled by similar, which reserves address space without touching pages: the first write decides, so a threaded _assign! fill distributes pages across domains while a serial one concentrates them. On the two-socket cluster node decision 37 names as the CPU path's target, that difference is the dominant bandwidth risk, and no measurement of it exists — a single-socket workstation cannot produce one.

The execution-route policy — which mechanism a new or rewritten operation takes. The routes below were each adopted by measurement (decisions 30, 38, 39, 40); together they form the standing rule, so a future operation is placed by its structure, not by precedent-mining:

  1. Single-output elementwise work whose traffic is already at the fused ideal takes the broadcast route (@_assign!): one fused expression, the threading gate and slab materialize! on CPU, the device's native broadcast elsewhere. (add!, combine!, laplacian!, the scalar convolve!.)
  2. Multi-output fiberwise work with shared source reads and no coordinate-dependent coefficient takes the fused executor (_fiberwise_pass!): a values function stamped from the shared formula tables, one KernelAbstractions kernel on a device, the gated linear @simd loop on CPU. (cross!, the mul! family, the shuffles and decompositions, det!, the scalar-field actions, the fiber cases of convolve!.)
  3. Elementwise work whose coefficient depends on the mode's cartesian index (wavenumbers) takes a decision-38-shape pair: a hand-written KA kernel for devices and a gated cartesian @simd loop for CPU, both from one set of per-mode expressions. (gradient!, curl!, symmetric_gradient!, helmholtz_projection!, and applying a kernel not held as one coefficient per mode — decision 50's axis-factored class and decision 51's radial one, which take it for the same reason: a broadcast device route paired with a gated CPU loop, rather than a hand-written kernel with one, as PERFORMANCE.md finding (o) measures. The exponential family's vector and tensor forms took this shape in decision 79: one exponential of $|k|^2$ per mode.)
  4. Reductions take the backend split of decisions 30/40: the fixed midpoint tree on CPU — gated, spawned at its top levels, bitwise thread-count-invariant — and the device's own parallel reduce elsewhere.
  5. Atomic accumulation or data-dependent irregular access — and only that — takes KernelAbstractions on every backend (the transfer gather/scatter, the shell binning): a gated loop cannot serve atomics or per-point stencils, so these are the only operations outside the threading policy, and the atomic ones are tolerance-tested where reordering perturbs last bits.

Whatever the route, three obligations are constant: a migration is verified bitwise against the implementation it replaces (or against an oracle when the arithmetic legitimately changes), the element-traffic test counts no reads or writes beyond the fused minimum or names why, and alias safety is either by construction (an element's reads precede its writes) or a documented contract (mul!).

An operand count the caller chooses is worked through in groups of a fixed size (decision 88). Four operations take as many operands as their caller passes: add! and subtract! over sections, map! over sources, displace! over terms, and weighted_inner over a tuple of weights. Whatever route one of them takes, it handles at most a fixed number of operands per pass — eight for the first three, four weights for the reduction — and within a pass every per-element step that indexes the operands is written out with a literal index per operand, generated per count, never a splat of an unbounded list, a map over a tuple, or a closure indexing a tuple of mixed types. The ceilings this keeps clear are the compiler's, not the mathematics': Julia may decline to specialize a function on the length of a trailing argument list unless its signature names the count (Vararg{T, N}); a tuple of 32 or more entries leaves Base's specialized tuple functions for a generic path, which allocates on a CPU and cannot run inside a device kernel; and a device kernel's arguments are limited in size. The group sizes are package constants rather than per-machine tunings, set well below all three. Grouping must not change a result: each group continues the previous one's left-to-right order, an operand that is also the destination is copied before a later group would read it, and the torus form of displace! wraps once, after the last group.

The kernel sites split into two classes for the threading policy — and two kernels are not bitwise. The fused operations of decisions 38/39 (gradient!, curl!, symmetric_gradient!, the fiberwise executor behind the algebra) are KernelAbstractions kernels on a device only; on a CPU array they run slab-threaded plain loops that pass through the same _use_threads gate as the broadcast route, so the cutoff and the with_threading override govern them.

Bitwise is a same-backend contract, and the reason it cannot cross a backend is compiler contraction. A device compiler folds x + c * v into a fused multiply-add, rounding once where the host's two instructions round twice, so the same source expression is not the same arithmetic. The difference is a last bit — for displace! it measures $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ in Float64 on coordinates of order one — but it is real, it is not noise, and it is why every cross-backend comparison in the suites is tolerance-tested while thread-count invariance is asserted exactly. Writing muladd on the host side to force the two into agreement was measured and declined: it buys no speed on either backend, improves the error bound without improving every element, and would rest a package guarantee on undocumented vendor contraction (PERFORMANCE.md finding (z)).

One pairing the route list above does not name outright, because it mixes two of its entries, is worth recording since decision 50 added the second instance: an operation may take the broadcast route on a device and a hand-written gated loop on a CPU. The helmholtz_projection! operator pairs a KernelAbstractions kernel with a gated CPU loop (decisions 38, 40); the axis-factored kernel of decision 50 pairs a broadcast with one. The reason is measured rather than aesthetic — a broadcast whose operands carry singleton axes indexes cartesian and launches flat, which is what a device wants, but does not vectorize on a CPU, where it ran at a fixed element rate independent of precision (PERFORMANCE.md finding (o)). Either way the pair is backend-dispatched, and a test checks both halves are reached, lest the CPU half be dead code.

Two groups of operations, however, are KA kernels on every backend — the gather/scatter transfer kernels (§10), and the radial shell binning (decisions 25, 34) — and on a CPU array these launch on the CPU() backend, which partitions the index range by Threads.nthreads() itself (helmholtz_projection! left this set when decision 40 gave it a gated loop twin, so what remains is exactly the atomic/irregular kernels — the exemption is structural, not historical). Two consequences follow for those two families, and neither is a defect to fix — they are the price of their kernel shapes. First, a KA launch never consults the _assign! policy above: the serial cutoff and the with_threading override do not reach those two groups, which thread by KA's rules at every size (the exclusion is stated on with_threading's own docstring). Second, the two kernels that accumulate through Atomix — the anterpolation scatter and the shell binning — sum in a nondeterministic order, and floating-point addition is not associative, so they are not bitwise-reproducible: not across thread counts, and not even between two runs at one thread count. Measured on a Ryzen 9800X3D over twelve trials at 8 and 16 threads, every trial differed, anterpolate! by 2–3 ulp of the field's scale and radial_spectrum only inside numerically-zero shells (max $\lvert \Delta \rvert$ = 1.4e-47). The remaining kernel with disjoint writes — the gather — is bitwise-reproducible, and was measured to be so across 1, 2, 4 and 8 threads, as is helmholtz_projection! on the gated loop it moved to. The threading suite (test/threading.jl) encodes exactly this split: bitwise equality asserted for the disjoint writers (including against a subprocess launched at a different thread count, the one comparison the in-process with_threading toggle cannot make for a KA kernel), a tolerance for the Atomix pair — the same tolerance-not-bitwise treatment the reductions above receive, and for the same reason.

10. Interpolation and anterpolation (the grid↔point transfer layer)

Lagrangian particle tracking by a consumer (§1) requires two transfer operations between the Eulerian fields and off-grid positions. They are formal adjoints of one another, and the package gives each a fixed name:

  • Interpolation — grid → point (the gather direction): evaluate a field f at the off-grid positions xs (a PointSet, $x_p$ the $p$-th position). Allocating interpolate(f, xs[, scheme]) / in-place interpolate!(out, f, xs[, scheme]), plus the callable f(x) (and its broadcast f.(xs)) as the ergonomic face of interpolate.
  • Anterpolation — point → grid (the scatter direction): deposit per-point quantities qs ($q_p$ the value at $x_p$) back onto a field. Allocating anterpolate(grid, xs, qs[, scheme]) / in-place anterpolate!(field, xs, qs[, scheme]).

Both carry the usual allocating / in-place (!) pair. The α/β accumulate form ($\beta = 0$ overwrite, $\beta \ne 0$ accumulate — the mul! convention, since they are the linear maps $W$ / $W^\top$) belongs to anterpolation alone: accumulation is what a scatter needs, and it is the direction that writes into a preallocated field, while a gather fills a fresh point-value section. So the pair is symmetric in name and adjointness, and asymmetric in three places: a gather yields point values while its adjoint returns a field; the accumulate weights are taken by the scatter alone; and only interpolation gets the callable f(x), because evaluation is pointwise while its adjoint is not (see the module docstring). The driving case is two-way coupling in the cloud-parcel DNS this package serves: the water-vapour mass and latent heat a droplet releases on condensation (or absorbs on evaporation) must be returned to the supersaturation and temperature fields.

The two operations are a conserving adjoint pair. Interpolation and anterpolation share the same stencil weights $w$, which form a partition of unity ($\sum w = 1$); anterpolation is then the exact adjoint of interpolation, and — since $\sum w = 1$ — the total deposited quantity is conserved: summed over the grid, the scattered field equals the input summed over the points, in each fiber component. Using mismatched weights injects a spurious source/dissipation term (Sundaram & Collins 1996). For the parcel application this is mandatory — total water and latent heat must be conserved as vapour passes between droplets and the fields — so the design fixes anterpolation as the adjoint of whatever interpolation kernel is in use. The function–measure duality underlying this adjointness — evaluation as pairing with a Dirac mass, $f(x) = \delta_x(f)$, and anterpolation as the pushforward of point masses against the normalized counting measure, $\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_p q_p \delta_{x_p}$ (regularized to $\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_p q_p w(\cdot - x_p)$, i.e. a regularized point source) — is given in full in the Interpolation module docstring.

Those two facts — adjointness and a partition-of-unity reproduction — are the commuting identities below. The two inner products are concrete pairings against the measure each base carries: on point-values $a, b$ the counting measure makes $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{pts}}$ a sum over the $M$ points, while on fields $f, g$ the cell-volume measure makes $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{grid}}$ a volume integral over the box $\Omega$ (with $\cdot$ the fiber contraction — the ordinary product for scalars, the dot product for vectors). Both are normalized by the total measure — divided by $M$ and by $|\Omega|$ respectively — which is what makes them intensive averages rather than extensive totals, and it is precisely that normalization the $N/M$ factor further down is accounting for:

\[\begin{gathered} \langle a, b\rangle_{\mathrm{pts}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{p=1}^{M} a_p\cdot b_p, \\ \langle f, g\rangle_{\mathrm{grid}} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega} f(x)\cdot g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{x} f(x)\cdot g(x), \end{gathered}\]

summed over the $M$ points and over the box $|\Omega|$ cut into $N$ cells, as inner computes them; the integral equals the grid sum for a resolved product; otherwise the sum defines it. Writing $\operatorname{sample}_{\mathrm{grid}}$ for sampling a function at the grid nodes, $\operatorname{eval}_{\mathrm{pts}}$ for evaluating it at the points, and $\phi$ for any polynomial in $\Pi_d$ (the polynomials up to the scheme's reproduction degree $d$), the adjointness and the polynomial reproduction read

\[\begin{gathered} \sum_{p} (W f)_p\cdot q_p = \sum_{x} f(x)\cdot(W^{\top} q)(x), \\ W \bigl(\operatorname{sample}_{\mathrm{grid}} \phi\bigr) = \operatorname{eval}_{\mathrm{pts}} \phi \quad (\phi \in \Pi_d), \end{gathered}\]

the left is the plain transpose $W^{\top}$ — the partition-of-unity scatter anterpolate applies; normalizing each side by its base count gives the measure-weighted $M \langle W f, q\rangle_{\mathrm{pts}} = N \langle f, W^{\top} q\rangle_{\mathrm{grid}}$ ($N$ grid nodes, $M$ points — the $N/M$ is just the two normalizations). Intrinsically the pair is a pullback–pushforward: writing $\iota : B_{\mathrm{points}} \to \Omega$ for the map from the point base $B_{\mathrm{points}}$ to the torus $\Omega$ that carries each point to its location $x_p$, pulling the trivial bundle back along $\iota$ keeps the fiber and changes only the base, $B_{\mathrm{points}} \times V = \iota^{*}(\Omega \times V)$ — a base-change, not a fiber operation (decision 19):

\[\begin{CD} B_{\mathrm{points}} \times V @>{\iota \times \mathrm{id}_V}>> \Omega \times V \\ @V{\pi}VV @VV{\pi}V \\ B_{\mathrm{points}} @>>{\iota}> \Omega \end{CD}\]

Interpolation is the pullback of the section, $W = \iota^{*} \otimes \mathrm{id}_V$ (fiber-preserving — the trivial bundle leaves the fiber by the identity; $(Wf)_p = f(x_p)$); anterpolation the pushforward of the dual measure, $\iota_{*}$ ($\iota_{*} q = \tfrac{1}{M}\sum_p q_p \delta_{x_p}$, the Dirac mass above regularized by the stencil — pushed forward against the normalized counting measure, the same one $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{pts}}$ uses, which is what puts the $1/M$ there and makes the identification below come out at $N/M$ rather than $N$). That pushforward lands in measures, while $(N/M) W^{\top} q$ is a section; the two are identified only after regularizing by the stencil and taking the density with respect to the normalized grid measure, and with those two steps understood $\iota_{*} = (N/M) W^{\top}$ for the uniform measures, the scalar rescaling that turns the implemented transpose $W^{\top}$ into the exact L²-adjoint. They have opposite variance — $\iota$ pulls a function back but pushes a measure forward — so the adjointness is their adjunction $\langle \iota^{*} f, q\rangle_{\mathrm{pts}} = \langle f, \iota_{*} q\rangle_{\mathrm{grid}}$. (Strictly $W$ is $\iota^{*}$ after a reconstruction — the spline or band-limited spectral interpolant lifting grid samples to a section over $\Omega$ — which the reproduction identity above shows is exact on $\Pi_d$.)

Two scheme families (decision 23). The scheme argument is an AbstractTransferScheme — the choice of kernel and representation that realizes $W$ / $W^\top$ (the commutative-diagram view above: a scheme is how the Dirac $\delta_{x_p}$ is realized). Two families share the same interpolate/anterpolate verbs and the same adjointness contract:

  • Physical-domain splines (AbstractSplineScheme) are local kernels on the physical nodal values (the gather/scatter kernels below); approximate, conserving, the production default.
  • Spectral-domain exact transfer acts on a field in spectral representation by the literal inverse/forward DFT $f(x_p) = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_k \hat f(k) e^{\mathrm{i} k\cdot(x_p-o)}$ and its adjoint $(W^\top q)(k) = \sum_p q_p e^{-\mathrm{i} k\cdot(x_p-o)}$ (here $N$ is the grid-point count, $o$ the grid origin, and $x_p$ the $p$-th sample point). The gather's $\sum_k$ runs over the full spectrum; the equivalent sum over the stored rfft half — carrying the Parseval weight $\alpha(k)$ (the per-mode multiplicity: $1$ where a stored coefficient represents only itself, $2$ where it stands for a conjugate pair the half-spectrum dropped) and a real part, which is what the code evaluates — is written out where DirectDFT is detailed below, and is worth reading before anyone reproduces the compact form against a half-spectrum array. DirectDFT is the exact $O(NM)$ form — the definition of the spectral-domain pair, a transparent baseline, the reference for divergence-free interpolation (it reproduces the band-limited field exactly, matching to_physical on nodes), and the oracle for the fast NUFFT (the $O(N\log N + M)$ windowed gridding version, owned natively — spread → FFT → deconvolve, reusing the spline kernels with a window, an oversampled FieldTransformPlans, and an assignment_window-style deconvolution; exact to a requested tolerance $\varepsilon$ on the resolved band). The adjoint relation carries the measure factor as for the splines: $M \langle W\hat f, q\rangle_{\mathrm{pts}} = N \langle \hat f, W^\top q\rangle_{\mathrm{grid}}$.

Method: the local splines (the physical-domain family). The package uses the local high-order spline interpolation of Lalescu, Teaca & Carati (2010), as implemented in the TurTLE code (Lalescu et al. 2022). At its core it is Hermite interpolation with the node derivatives supplied by finite differences, which makes the interpolant simultaneously local, high-order, and smooth; in the cubic four-point case it reduces to the Catmull–Rom spline (Catmull & Rom 1974). Concretely f(x) is a tensor-product weighted sum $\sum w_{i_1} w_{i_2} w_{i_3} f(c+i)$ over a compact $(2n{+}2)^3$ stencil ($n$ the per-axis finite-difference half-width) — $c$ the grid cell containing $x$ and $i$ the integer node offset within the stencil — with closed-form polynomial weights $w$. Lalescu's two hierarchies share this stencil: the smooth hierarchy (LalescuSpline(n, m)) is a two-point Hermite of degree $2m{+}1$ that matches the value and the first $m$ derivatives at the two bracketing nodes ($C^m$, $0 \le m \le 2n$; $m = 0$ is linear, $n = 1, m = 1$ is Catmull–Rom), and the Lagrange hierarchy (LagrangeSpline(n)) is the $C^0$ degree-$2n{+}1$ interpolant of all $2n{+}2$ nodes. Field derivatives needed for the velocity gradient / deformation tensor are obtained by differentiating the same local polynomial, not from a separate field. Three properties make it the right fit:

  1. Single representation, no global precompute. The weights act directly on the real-space nodal values, so interpolation acts only on the physical representation — unlike the spectral-domain schemes (DirectDFT, NUFFT; decision 23, below), which act on the spectral representation directly and reconstruct the band-limited field exactly. (The B-spline interpolation of van Hinsberg et al. (2012) — whose coefficients require a global transform that, in a spectral code, costs one FFT — is the higher-accuracy local alternative still in reserve.) PLANNED — the van Hinsberg global B-spline (needs a design discussion before coding): a coefficient pre-transform, then a compact local stencil — higher accuracy per stencil width, at the cost of a global solve that breaks the "single representation" property, so it needs a coefficient-field type and a transform step. It would slot under AbstractTransferScheme but changes the data flow (a global precompute), so it is a recorded future direction, not a drop-in scheme. (The other once-planned alternative — exact spectral transfer — is done: the $O(NM)$ DirectDFT oracle and the fast NUFFT, owned natively with no NonuniformFFTs.jl dependency, under AbstractTransferScheme; decision 23.) See decision 17 and the references below.
  2. GPU-friendly (the GPU-first invariant, §1): per-point interpolation is a gather of $(2n{+}2)^3$ neighbours plus a fused multiply–add — embarrassingly parallel, no FFT, no global solve, no communication beyond the periodic stencil wrap. Anterpolation is the matching scatter.
  3. Trivial, conservative anterpolation: the same closed-form $w$ deposit a point's source over its stencil with $\sum w = 1$ — exactly the conserving two-way-coupling feedback the parcel DNS needs.

The interpolation order is a tunable parameter (n, m), not hard-wired, so accuracy can be matched to the spectral resolution (standard practice for pseudospectral particle tracking). The transfer kernels are implemented as portable KernelAbstractions gather/scatter kernels (the scatter accumulates atomically); the smooth per-axis weights are generated for any (n, m) by a small linear solve — match the value and the order-1…m centred-finite-difference (Fornberg 1988) derivatives at the two bracketing nodes — and pinned to 1e-12 against TurTLE's beta_n1_m1-style weights (n ↔ interp_neighbours, m ↔ smoothness), with the closed-form Catmull–Rom (n = 1, m = 1) as an independent anchor. The smooth scheme reproduces polynomials to degree $\min(2m+1, 2n)$ for $n \ge 1$; the Lagrange scheme to degree $2n+1$. The bound needs that scope because the two-point n = 0 stencil is linear and reproduces degree 1, where the formula would read 0.

Data types — the unstructured base, in the package's own language. Interpolation needs to name two new objects: off-grid locations and the field values there. The package names them in its base/fiber vocabulary, not as particles — deliberately, because GBGPeriodicFields is a math backend (§1): "particle"/"tracer" is the consumer's interpretation (a location with physical attributes and an equation of motion), not a primitive here. The organizing principle reuses the existing base/fiber split: a field is a section — fibers (scalar, FiberVector, FiberTensor2) over a base — and so far the base has always been the structured Grid3D. Interpolation adds the unstructured base and its section, reusing the same fibers; the maps between sections over the two bases are interpolation $W$ and its adjoint anterpolation $W^\top$ (the same $W$/$W^\top$ used above).

  • Point{T} = SVector{3,T} is a single position; a base-space alias of FiberVector (same representation, different role). It is an alias, not a wrapper, so arithmetic stays StaticArrays-native (invariant 8); position vs. value is a distinction of role, not dispatch.
  • PointSet holds M positions in structure-of-arrays layout (M, 3) (point index first, coordinate last — each coordinate axis contiguous across points, mirroring the field's fiber-last (Nx,Ny,Nz,3) for coalesced GPU gather). It is grid-free: periodicity wraps any coordinate, so positions are untethered from a grid, the field supplying it at transfer time.
  • PointValues is the section over a PointSet, as three concrete types mirroring the field zoo — ScalarPointValues / VectorPointValues / TensorPointValues{R} under an abstract AbstractPointValues, SoA layout (M,) / (M,3) / (M,3,3). They are representation-free: a point value is always a physical-space quantity (even exact spectral interpolation produces physical values), so, unlike the field types, they have no physical/spectral split.

All wrap a generic AbstractArray and allocate via similar, so they are backend-generic (CPU, CUDA, AMDGPU); compatibility reduces to M-matching and matching tensor order (a check predicate in the Fields style). The types live in the Points module, importing Fields helpers via an explicit using ..Fields: ….

Implemented and tested. The point types, their full fiberwise algebra (M2 + M3, generated by reusing FieldAlgebra's per-component builders over an extended section interface — §6 — and locked by a separate 122-method parity baseline, the 206-method field surface byte-unchanged), and all transfer schemes (the local splines plus the spectral DirectDFT/NUFFT, decision 23) are implemented and covered on CPU (Float64/Float32) and the GPU (ROCm, allowscalar(false)).

Why the code says "anterpolation." The point→grid (scatter) operation is named differently in every community, with no dominant choice: spreading (immersed-boundary method, where it is explicitly the adjoint $S = J^*$ of interpolation, built from the same kernel — Peskin 2002; also NUFFT type-1), deposition / charge assignment (particle-in-cell — Hockney & Eastwood 1988; Birdsall & Langdon 1991), scatter (data-parallel computing), projection / Particle-Source-In-Cell (particle-laden-flow DNS — Crowe, Sharma & Stock 1977), gridding (MRI and radio interferometry), and, in cloud microphysics, no operator noun at all — only the source/sink term it produces and the two-way coupling / thermodynamic feedback it enacts. We adopt anterpolation (Brandt & Lubrecht 1990) because it is the single term whose literal definition is "the adjoint of interpolation" — it names the operation by the property that matters, independent of application. The binding carries a docstring listing the synonyms — adjoint of interpolation; spreading; deposition; scatter; projection / PSI-Cell; gridding; two-way-coupling source/feedback — so a reader from any of those fields finds it. "Extrapolation" is deliberately rejected: extrapolation means evaluating outside the convex hull of the data, whereas a sample point lies inside a grid cell and the operation is a weighted distribution; the loose use of "extrapolation" for it (seen in a few papers) is a misnomer.

Base-space motion: displace! and wrap! (decision 73). The transfer verbs above move field values between the grid and the points; nothing above moves the points. The consuming solver's trajectory update is exactly that missing operation — each position advanced along a weighted sum of vector values held over it, $x_p \leftarrow x_p + \sum_j c_j v_j(p)$, where $x_p$ is the position of point $p$, the $v_j$ are VectorPointValues sections (each holding one vector value per point), and each $c_j$ is one plain real number — one scalar per term of the sum, not a per-point array — and until this decision it could only be written by unwrapping the storage and broadcasting on raw arrays, outside every contract the layer states. The geometry says it is a legitimate operation and says where its boundaries are. On this package's flat periodic base with its one global orthonormal frame (§2), the tangent space at every point is canonically the fiber's Cartesian $\mathbb R^3$, so a vector value over a point defines a displacement with no connection machinery: displacement is coordinate addition. It is a named operation and not a + method, because positions are points of the periodic domain, not vectors — an algebra on positions would assert a linear structure the base does not have, and the position-versus-value role split stays intact. And it comes in two forms the type structure itself dictates: a PointSet deliberately carries no grid, so displace! without a grid cannot wrap and is displacement in the universal cover $\mathbb R^3$ — the unwrapped torus, where a coordinate keeps growing instead of folding back (winding preserved — a mean-square displacement needs it), while displace! with a trailing Grid3D is displacement on the torus proper, the wrap fused into the same pass. The call wrap!(points, grid) is the zero-term case kept as its own named verb: reduction to the canonical representative in the half-open domain $[o_a, o_a + L_a)$ from each axis's origin over its length — with the floating-point endpoint folded (mod can return the period for a coordinate just below the origin) and non-finite coordinates passed through non-finite, so a diverged trajectory is never silently rewritten into an in-box position. Sampling never requires the wrap — the transfer kernels wrap their stencil indices, so interpolation is invariant under shifting a coordinate by whole periods — but canonicalization is load-bearing for precision on long runs (absolute floating-point resolution degrades as a coordinate grows), which is why the wrap is a package contract and not consumer hygiene. The coefficients are part of what the operation is, so they are not the α/β accumulation scalars and the operation takes no trailing α/β (the combine! precedent); there is deliberately no allocating sibling, displacement being motion of an existing base. The $n$-term form runs in one fused traversal — positions read once, each section once, positions written once — which collapses a $K$-step Adams–Bashforth update from $K$ passes to one.

11. Convolution

The current shape of the kernel protocol is here, not in the decision log

The decision log records how the kernel design arrived — decisions 19, 20, 21, 50, 51, 56, 60, 61 and 62 each changed part of it, and several supersede earlier ones. Reconstructing the present state from that chain is not the intended reading. This section is the current state: a kernel is its own kind of object, never a field; SpectralKernel is the abstract supertype of four storage classes — StoredKernel (one coefficient per mode), SeparableKernel (per-axis factors), RadialKernel (a symbol of $\lvert k\rvert^2$) and ProductKernel (any two held unmultiplied); all four are interchangeable wherever a kernel is applied and agree bitwise on the host (on a device a complex-valued kernel agrees only to rounding), materialize_kernel expands any other class into the stored one (and materialize_kernel! into a StoredKernel that already exists), and inv and weighted_inner are the two entry points with narrower contracts.

Convolution is the kernel-multiplier role of the measure structure: periodic (circular) convolution of a field with a kernel, in spectral representation. By the convolution theorem (§2), $f \star g$ is the coefficientwise product $\hat f \cdot \hat g$; convolve/convolve! ($\equiv \star$, also exported as the infix operator ⋆) apply a kernel — a SpectralKernel, in any of its storage classes — whose transfer function $\hat g$ multiplies every fiber component (one method for scalar, vector, and tensor fields), with the α/β accumulate form and alias-safety of a coefficientwise op. It is the spectral sibling of DifferentialOperators (both are coefficientwise multipliers, §4) and the same-base counterpart of the Interpolation transfer (decision 19); offered under its true name convolve, never as * (invariant 1). Spectral representation only; physics (an LES filter, an integrating factor $\exp(-\nu |k|^2 \Delta t)$) stays a consumer-built kernel (invariant 12). Six neutral builders ship: spectral_cutoff (the isotropic sharp low-pass), spectral_band (its annular generalization), radial_kernel (any real symbol of $|k|^2$, held as a closure), tophat_window (the box average of edges $l_a$, $\prod_a \operatorname{sinc}(k_a l_a / 2)$; decision 87), assignment_window (the $\prod_a \operatorname{sinc}^2$ CIC deconvolution window, the top-hat at the grid spacing composed with itself), and translation_kernel (the shift theorem's phase $e^{-\mathrm{i} k\cdot h}$, which displaces a field by a physical offset $h$). The first five are real and even in the wavenumber $k$; translation_kernel is the one complex kernel, and the reality condition a complex kernel must meet — the Hermitian symmetry $\hat g(-k) = \overline{\hat g(k)}$, equivalently a real physical-space kernel $g$ — is what forces its value at the Nyquist frequency to be a real attenuation rather than a phase (decision 28). Two fused forms extend the family without a stored kernel or an extra pass (decision 33): exponential_convolve!/exponential_convolve apply the exponential radial kernel $\exp(c |k|^2 + s \hat w)$ evaluated lazily from the wavenumber arrays (for a real coefficient $c \le 0$ the periodic Gaussian $e^{-c\Delta}$; $\hat w$ an optional real per-mode exponent field entered as scale times field, $s$ its scale), in single-field and weighted-two-field forms; and the kernel-composed combine! method convolves a weighted combination $\hat g \cdot (\alpha \hat a + \beta \hat b)$ in the same fused pass — the decision-32 composition idiom applied to the combination. See decisions 20, 21, 22, 28, 33.

12. Reductions

Reductions is the L² / measure family — the third operation family, integrating a section over its base against the measure the base carries (Grid3D's uniform cell volume, PointSet's counting measure; decision 18). The integral ($\equiv \int\,\mathrm{d}\mu$) is the extensive sum; inner ($\equiv \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$), l2norm, and lpnorm are the normalized L² product and norms, and weighted_inner ($\equiv \langle\cdot, w \cdot\rangle$) is the spectral multiplier-weighted product $\langle f, w g\rangle$ — the reduction-dual of convolve, generalizing inner (decision 27); mean/std/var/cov (extending Statistics) and maximum/minimum/extrema (extending Base) the order statistics, all acting on fields in physical representation and point-values (representation-free); cov returns the vector second central moment as a symmetric $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)$ tensor, which a fiber-preserving var would reduce to its basis-dependent diagonal. The scalar-only std/var additionally read from spectral representation through Parseval (decision 49), built from $\langle f,f\rangle - \langle f\rangle^2$ — Bessel-scaled for var, its square root for std — both terms already spectral-available. The reductions inner/l2norm also act on a field in spectral representation via Parseval's theorem (equal to the physical inner, no transform; the rfft half-spectrum weighting), and integral/mean via the zero (DC) Fourier mode — $\hat f(0) = \sum_x f$ gives the mean $\hat f(0)/N$ and the integral $(|\Omega|/N) \hat f(0)$, no transform. The radial pair are spectral/physical duals: radial_spectrum (the shell-resolved inner — the power spectrum) and its Wiener–Khinchin dual radial_correlation (the two-point autocorrelation $C(r) = \mathcal F^{-1}(|\hat f|^2)/N$), each with a two-field co-spectrum form. Five of them — integral, mean, maximum, minimum and extrema — additionally have a mapped form taking a leading function — a map applied to the section's fiber value at every base point, so mean(det, A) averages a determinant and maximum(norm, u) takes a supremum of magnitude, each reduced in one pass with no intermediate section (decision 30). Physics (kinetic energy $\tfrac{1}{2}\langle u,u\rangle$, dissipation, rms) stays consumer-side. See decisions 18, 21, 22, 30.
