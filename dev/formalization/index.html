Formalization — the typed statements behind the prose

This page is the formal backing for the mathematical statements made informally in Architecture, the module docstrings, and the API pages. For each nontrivial operation it records a typed statement — every object given its space, every operator its domain and codomain — and checks each claim by expansion, with the definitions taken from the implementation (src/) and a link to the test that verifies the claim numerically.

It exists as a precision check, not as a page a user reads. Writing a statement formally forces every type and normalization into the open, which is exactly where imprecision in the prose becomes visible: the all-ones $\mathbf 1$ that was secretly two different vectors, or a section space written $\mathbb R^N$ that had quietly flattened the fiber away. These are hand-verified notes, not machine-checked proofs — the deterministic check is the linked test (the test suite plays the role of the compiler), so a statement here with no test is a coverage gap to close.

Coordinate forms live here

The coordinate-free preference (CLAUDE.md) governs the user-facing prose. This page is the sanctioned home for the coordinate/matrix/component expansions — a basis, the matrix entries $W_{pn}$, the all-ones $\mathbf 1_N$ / $\mathbf 1_M$ — because checking precision requires expanding into components. The statements here must still be correct; they need not be coordinate-free.

Declarations

Fixed throughout (definitions read from src/):

\[\begin{aligned} V &:= (\mathbb R^3)^{\otimes R} &&\text{fiber, tensor order } R\ (\dim 3^R)\\ \Gamma_G &:= \mathbb R^{N_x}\otimes\mathbb R^{N_y}\otimes\mathbb R^{N_z}\otimes V &&\text{grid sections, } N = N_x N_y N_z \text{ cells}\\ \Gamma_P &:= \mathbb R^M \otimes V &&\text{point sections, } M \text{ scattered points} \end{aligned}\]

A grid cell is a triple $(i, j, k)$, so the scalar field over the grid is the 3-axis tensor product $\mathbb R^{N_x}\otimes\mathbb R^{N_y}\otimes\mathbb R^{N_z}$ (the array $f_{ijk}$) — one real per cell. As a vector space this is $\mathbb R^N$ with $N = N_x N_y N_z$: a column-major flat index $n$ collapses $(i, j, k)$, and the global pairings below use that flat $n$ (the per-axis structure is irrelevant to them — it is what the tensor-product stencil weights $w_{i_1}w_{i_2}w_{i_3}$ and the differential multipliers $i k_a$ act on). $\otimes V$ then attaches the fiber, so a grid section is a $V$-value at each cell ($\cong V^N$; a single real only in the scalar case $R = 0$). The point base $\mathbb R^M$ is unstructured — $M$ scattered points, no product — which is exactly the structured-to-unstructured nature of the transfer. A point's position $x_p \in \mathbb R^3$ is a separate object from the value it carries: the positions are the PointSet geometry ($\mathbb R^M \otimes \mathbb R^3 \cong \mathbb R^{3M}$, themselves a vector-valued section over the points) and enter only through the transfer weights $w_n(x_p)$, whereas $\Gamma_P$ is the space of values — a $V$ per point. So $\Gamma_P$ is $\mathbb R^{3M}$ only when the sampled field is a vector ($V = \mathbb R^3$); in general it is $\mathbb R^M \otimes V$, just as the grid value space is not $\mathbb R^{3N}$.

The transform $\mathcal F$ (to_spectral, inverse to_physical) is the rfft isomorphism between the physical (nodal) and spectral representations of a grid section; $\hat f = \mathcal F f$. With the fiber contraction $\cdot : V \times V \to \mathbb R$ and the wavenumber $k$, the inner products are the package's normalized reductions (inner, src/Reductions.jl = dot(data, data) / _base_count):

\[\langle a, b\rangle_{\text{pts}} := \frac1M \sum_{p=1}^{M} a_p\cdot b_p, \qquad \langle f, g\rangle_{\text{grid}} := \frac1N \sum_{n=1}^{N} f_n\cdot g_n .\]

Representations and transforms (Fields, Architecture §4)

$\mathcal F$ is forward unnormalized, inverse carrying $1/N$ (AbstractFFTs convention, plan_rfft/plan_irfft):

\[\hat f(k) = \sum_{x} f(x)\,e^{-i k\cdot x}, \qquad f(x) = \frac1N \sum_{k} \hat f(k)\,e^{i k\cdot x}, \qquad N = N_x N_y N_z,\]

so $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F = \mathrm{id}$ (exact to round-off). Two consequences used elsewhere:

  • Half-spectrum + conjugate symmetry. $f$ real $\Rightarrow$ $\hat f(-k) = \overline{\hat f(k)}$, so only $k_x \ge 0$ is stored (rfft_shape: length $N_x/2{+}1$ on the rfft axis); the $k_x = 0$ plane, and on an even axis the Nyquist plane, are self-conjugate.
  • Adding a real constant $c$ touches only the zero mode, scaled by $N$: $\mathcal F(f + c) = \hat f + c\,N\,\delta_{k,0}$ (_spectral_constant_scale = prod(grid_size)), since the unnormalized forward sums $N$ copies of $c$ at $k = 0$. $\checkmark$

Backs Architecture §4 and the Fields transform docstrings. Checked by test/fields.jl — FFT round-trip, multipliers vs fftfreq/rfftfreq, the Nyquist behaviour, and transform input-preservation — and test/fieldalgebra.jl (the constant/zero-mode invariant).

Fiberwise algebra (FieldAlgebra / FiberSections, Architecture §2, §6)

The vertical operations act inside the fiber $V$ at each base point, identically over grid and point bases (one set of generated kernels, src/FiberSections.jl). A FiberVector is $\mathbb R^3$, a FiberTensor2 genuinely a $3\times3$ matrix, so component notation is the natural form here:

\[\begin{aligned} u\cdot v &= u_i v_i, & A : B &= A_{ij}B_{ij}, & (u\times v)_i &= \epsilon_{ijk}\,u_j v_k,\\ (Au)_i &= A_{ij}u_j, & (uA)_i &= u_j A_{ji}, & (AB)_{ij} &= A_{ik}B_{kj},\\ (A^{\top})_{ij} &= A_{ji}, & \lVert u\rVert &= \sqrt{u_i u_i}, & \lVert A\rVert &= \sqrt{A_{ij}A_{ij}}. \end{aligned}\]

The decompositions are $\operatorname{sym}(A)=\tfrac12(A+A^{\top})$, $\operatorname{skew}(A)=\tfrac12(A-A^{\top})$, $\operatorname{dev}(A)=A-\tfrac13(\operatorname{tr}A)\,I$, so $\operatorname{sym}+\operatorname{skew}=\mathrm{id}$ and $\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{dev}(A)=0$. The trace $\operatorname{tr}(A)=A_{ii}$ — the first tensor invariant that dev removes — is exposed as tr/tr!, and being linear it is representation-preserving (the trace of the spectral coefficients is the spectral representation of $\operatorname{tr}A$). The third invariant, the determinant $\det A=\epsilon_{ijk}A_{1i}A_{2j}A_{3k}=I_3$ (det/det!), is a cubic product of the components, hence nonlinear — physical-space only, like the norm — and multiplicative $\det(AB)=\det A\,\det B$; it reuses the one shared formula builder, so it mirrors tr onto both fields and point-values. $\checkmark$ the mul! family carries the α/β accumulate $\mathrm{dst}=\alpha(\cdot)+\beta\,\mathrm{dst}$, and the weighted combination $\alpha a+\beta b$ (combine!) is its linear two-field peer — coefficientwise, hence representation-preserving and alias-safe, with $\alpha=\beta=1$ the plain add!.

Backs Architecture §2, §6, decision 27, and the FieldAlgebra docstrings. Checked by test/fieldalgebra.jl (and test/pointvalues_algebra.jl for the identical point-value mirror) — dot/cross, tensor products with α/β, transpose, norms, the trace, the determinant (against a per-point LU oracle), the weighted combination, and the sym/skew/dev identities.

Differential operators (DifferentialOperators, Architecture §4)

Each operator $L$ is diagonalized by $\mathcal F$ into a pointwise multiplier $\hat L$ — the intertwiner square $\mathcal F\circ L = \hat L\circ\mathcal F$ — applied coefficientwise in spectral representation (src/DifferentialOperators.jl):

\[\begin{aligned} \nabla &\to ik, & \nabla\cdot &\to ik\cdot, & \nabla\times &\to ik\times,\\ \Delta &\to -|k|^2, & \Delta^{-1} &\to -|k|^{-2}\ (0\text{ at }k=0), & \mathbb P &\to I - \hat k\,\hat k^{\top}\ \ (\hat k = k/|k|). \end{aligned}\]

Nyquist split (even axes, deliberate — FieldSpectralMultipliers): the derivative wavenumber is zeroed at Nyquist (the Nyquist mode is a real cosine, so $ik_N$ would break reality), while the squared wavenumber keeps the true $k^2$ (correct $\Delta$ / $\Delta^{-1}$). Identities, each a one-line spectral check:

\[\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times u) = 0\ \ \bigl(k\cdot(k\times\hat u)=0\bigr), \qquad \nabla\times(\nabla\phi) = 0\ \ \bigl(k\times ik\hat\phi=0\bigr), \qquad \mathbb P^2 = \mathbb P, \qquad \langle \Delta\phi,\phi\rangle = -\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^2 \le 0. \quad\checkmark\]

($\mathbb P$ and $\Delta$ are self-adjoint — their multipliers $I-\hat k\hat k^{\top}$, $-|k|^2$ are real symmetric.)

The symmetric gradient $\operatorname{sym}\nabla$ (symmetric_gradient) is the fused symmetric peer of $\nabla\times$ (which takes the antisymmetric part of $\nabla u$): its multiplier is the symmetric part of $\nabla u$'s multiplier $ik_j\hat u_i$,

\[\hat S_{ij} = \tfrac{i}{2}\,(k_j\hat u_i + k_i\hat u_j), \qquad S = \operatorname{sym}(\nabla u), \quad\checkmark\]

formed without materializing the full gradient tensor and sharing the derivative-wavenumber Nyquist zeroing.

Backs Architecture §4, decision 27. Checked by test/differentialoperators.jl (analytic round-trips, the Nyquist split, $\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times)=0$, $\nabla\times\nabla=0$, and $\operatorname{sym}\nabla u=\operatorname{sym}(\nabla u)$) and test/adjointness.jl (self-adjointness, idempotent projection, negative-definite Laplacian).

Convolution (Convolution, Architecture §11)

By the convolution theorem, periodic convolution is the coefficientwise spectral product; convolve applies a scalar kernel $\hat g$ (a ScalarFieldSpectral, broadcast over the fiber) with α/β accumulate (src/Convolution.jl):

\[\mathcal F(f \star g) = \hat f\cdot\hat g, \qquad \widehat{\mathrm{dst}} = \alpha\,\hat f\,\hat g + \beta\,\widehat{\mathrm{dst}}. \quad\checkmark\]

This is exactly the product invariant 1 bans as $*$ — it is convolution, not the (absent) pointwise spectral product. The neutral kernel builders: spectral_cutoff is the sharp low-pass $\hat g(k)=1$ for $|k|\le k_c$ (else $0$), idempotent ($\hat g^2=\hat g$, a projection); spectral_band its annular $k_{lo}\le|k|\le k_{hi}$ generalization (cutoff is $k_{lo}=0$); squared_wavenumber_field the $|k|^2$ coordinate consumers build symbols from; and assignment_window the linear/CIC mass-assignment window

\[\hat W(k) = \prod_{a=1}^{3}\operatorname{sinc}^2\!\Bigl(\tfrac{k_a\,\Delta x_a}{2}\Bigr), \qquad \operatorname{sinc} y = \tfrac{\sin y}{y},\]

undone by dividing (deconvolution).

Backs Architecture §11, decisions 20–22. Checked by test/convolution.jl — the translation kernel $e^{-i k_a m\Delta} \Leftrightarrow$ circshift (an independent physical oracle), cutoff idempotence, the $\operatorname{sinc}^2$ window, and the physical-space intentional absence.

Reductions (Reductions, Architecture §12)

The L²/measure structure (src/Reductions.jl). integral is extensive (against the base measure — grid cell volume, point counting); inner is the normalized L² product (declared above); l2norm $=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle}$:

\[\int_\Omega f\,d\mu = \frac{|\Omega|}{N}\sum_n f_n\ (\text{grid}),\quad \sum_p f_p\ (\text{points}); \qquad \int_\Omega f\,d\mu = \text{measure}\cdot\text{mean}. \quad\checkmark\]

integral and mean in spectral representation (the zero mode, no transform): the unnormalized forward gives the DC coefficient $\hat f(0) = \sum_x f_x$ (real for a real field), so

\[\text{mean}(f) = \frac{\hat f(0)}{N}, \qquad \int_\Omega f\,d\mu = \frac{|\Omega|}{N}\,\hat f(0). \quad\checkmark\]

Both equal the physical reductions (since $\sum_x f_x = \hat f(0)$), read from the same zero-mode slot the constant-add writes; std/var/lpnorm/maximum have no single-coefficient form.

inner in spectral representation (Parseval, no transform): the discrete Parseval identity $\tfrac1N\sum_x f_x\cdot g_x = \tfrac1{N^2}\sum_{\text{all }k}\hat f(k)\,\overline{\hat g(k)}$ together with the rfft half-spectrum (each $k_x>0$ mode stands for its conjugate partner, so it counts twice; the self-conjugate $k_x=0$ and even-Nyquist planes count once) gives

\[\langle f, g\rangle = \frac{1}{N^2}\,\mathrm{Re}\!\Bigl(2\!\!\sum_{k_x\ge0}\!\!\hat f\,\overline{\hat g} \;-\;\sum_{k_x=0}\hat f\,\overline{\hat g}\;-\;\sum_{k_x=N_x/2}\hat f\,\overline{\hat g}\Bigr) \quad\checkmark\]

(the Nyquist sum only for even $N_x$). radial_spectrum bins this with the half-spectrum weight $\alpha(k)$ ($1$ on the self-conjugate planes, $2$ else) over $|k|$-shells, and radial_correlation is the Wiener–Khinchin dual ($\mathcal F^{-1}=$ to_physical, the normalized inverse):

\[E(\kappa_i) = \frac{1}{N^2}\!\!\sum_{\mathrm{round}(|k|/\Delta k)=i}\!\!\alpha(k)\sum_c |\hat f_c(k)|^2, \qquad C(r) = \frac1N\,\mathcal F^{-1}\!\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_c|\hat f_c|^2\Bigr)(r),\]

each refining inner exactly: $\sum_i E(\kappa_i) = \langle f,f\rangle$ and $C(0) = \langle f,f\rangle$. $\checkmark$ The two-field forms (cross-power $\sum_c\mathrm{Re}(\hat f_c\overline{\hat g_c})$, cross-correlation $C_{fg}(r)=\tfrac1N\mathcal F^{-1}(\overline{\hat f}\,\hat g)(r)$) refine the mixed $\langle f,g\rangle$ ($\sum E_{fg}=C_{fg}(0)=\langle f,g\rangle$), are symmetric in $f,g$, and may be negative.

weighted_inner applies a real Fourier multiplier $w(k)$ inside the same Parseval sum,

\[\langle f, w\,g\rangle = \frac{1}{N^2}\sum_k \alpha(k)\,w(k)\sum_c \mathrm{Re}\!\bigl(\hat f_c\,\overline{\hat g_c}\bigr), \quad\checkmark\]

generalizing inner (the $w\equiv1$ case) — the reduction-dual of convolve. For $w=|k|^2$ (the squared-wavenumber symbol, true $k^2$ at Nyquist) it is the Dirichlet energy $\langle f,-\Delta g\rangle$; and since $w$ is real it equals the naive $\langle w\star f, w\star g\rangle$ iff $w$ is idempotent ($w^2=w$, a sharp band), not for a general $w$. Symmetric in $f,g$, may be negative, spectral-only.

Backs Architecture §12, decisions 18/21/22/27. Checked by test/reductions.jl — $\langle\sin\rangle=0$, $\langle\sin^2\rangle=\tfrac12$, the spectral integral/mean against the physical reductions ($\langle c\rangle=c$, $\int c\,dx=c|\Omega|$), $\sum E=\mathrm{inner}$, $C(0)=\mathrm{inner}$ against an independent binned-circshift oracle, the co-spectrum signs, and weighted_inner against $\langle f,-\Delta g\rangle$ and the idempotent-band identity.

Interpolation / anterpolation (Interpolation, Architecture §10)

The base gather $W_b : \mathbb R^N \to \mathbb R^M$ has entries $(W_b)_{pn} = w_n(x_p)$ (the local stencil weights, src/Interpolation.jl), a partition of unity — for each point the row sums to one, $\sum_n (W_b)_{pn} = 1$, i.e. $W_b\,\mathbf 1_N = \mathbf 1_M$ with $\mathbf 1_N \in \mathbb R^N$ and $\mathbf 1_M \in \mathbb R^M$ (two distinct all-ones vectors). Both transfer maps are fiber-preserving (the trivial bundle leaves the fiber by the identity):

\[W := W_b \otimes \mathrm{id}_V : \Gamma_G \to \Gamma_P, \qquad W^{\top} := W_b^{\top} \otimes \mathrm{id}_V : \Gamma_P \to \Gamma_G .\]

Adjoint, raw (counting) form. For $f \in \Gamma_G$, $q \in \Gamma_P$:

\[\sum_p (Wf)_p\cdot q_p = \sum_p \sum_n (W_b)_{pn}\,(f_n\cdot q_p) = \sum_n f_n\cdot (W^{\top} q)_n \qquad\checkmark\]

Adjoint, normalized inner. Dividing the two sides by $M$ and $N$:

\[M\,\langle Wf, q\rangle_{\text{pts}} = \sum_p (Wf)_p\cdot q_p = \sum_n f_n\cdot(W^{\top}q)_n = N\,\langle f, W^{\top} q\rangle_{\text{grid}} \qquad\checkmark\]

So the bare $\langle Wf, q\rangle_{\text{pts}} = \langle f, W^{\top}q\rangle_{\text{grid}}$ is false unless $N = M$ — the two sides differ by $N/M$. This is the slip the formalization catches: it type-checks, but the expansion does not match. The clean adjunction uses the measure-adjoint $\iota_* := (N/M)\,W^{\top}$:

\[\langle f, \iota_* q\rangle_{\text{grid}} = \frac1N\sum_n f_n\cdot\tfrac NM\,(W^{\top}q)_n = \frac1M\sum_n f_n\cdot(W^{\top}q)_n = \langle Wf, q\rangle_{\text{pts}} \qquad\checkmark\]

Conservation (sum the deposit over the grid, per fiber component):

\[\sum_n (W^{\top} q)_n = \sum_n\sum_p (W_b)_{pn}\,q_p = \sum_p \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_n (W_b)_{pn}\Bigr) q_p = \sum_p q_p \;\in V \qquad\checkmark\]

(uses the partition-of-unity row sum $\sum_n (W_b)_{pn} = 1$).

Backs Architecture §10 and the Interpolation module docstring. Checked by test/interpolation_transfer.jl — the measure-weighted adjointness $M\,\mathrm{inner}(Wf, q) = N\,\mathrm{inner}(f, W^{\top}q)$, conservation (partition of unity), and polynomial reproduction.

Spectral transfer: DirectDFT / NUFFT (Interpolation, Architecture §10, decision 23)

The exact Fourier transfer between a field's spectral coefficients and off-grid points, and its fast windowed form — the spectral siblings of the spline $W$ / $W^{\top}$ above. DirectDFT is the literal $O(NM)$ sum (src/Interpolation.jl; $o$ the grid origin, $\alpha$ the half-spectrum Parseval weight, $1$ on the self-conjugate planes and $2$ else):

\[\text{type-2 (interpolate)}\quad f(x_p) = \frac1N\!\!\sum_{\text{stored }k}\!\!\alpha(k)\,\mathrm{Re}\!\bigl(\hat f(k)\,e^{i k\cdot(x_p-o)}\bigr), \qquad \text{type-1 (anterpolate)}\quad (W^{\top}q)(k) = \sum_p q_p\,e^{-i k\cdot(x_p-o)} .\]

Type-2 reproduces the band-limited field exactly (it matches to_physical at the grid nodes), and the pair satisfies the same measure-weighted adjoint as the splines, $M\,\langle W\hat f, q\rangle_{\text{pts}} = N\,\langle \hat f, W^{\top}q\rangle_{\text{grid}}$. $\checkmark$ NUFFT is its $O(N\log N + M)$ form — spread → oversampled ($\sigma=2$) FFT → deconvolve by $1/\hat\psi$ (window $\psi$, transform $\hat\psi$) — exact to a tolerance $\varepsilon$ against DirectDFT on the resolved sub-Nyquist band (the rfft Nyquist is projected out), an exact adjoint pair there.

Backs Architecture §10, decision 23. Checked by test/transfer_spectral.jl — interpolate == to_physical at the nodes, exact off-grid reconstruction, the adjoint relation ($R = 0, 1, 2$), and NUFFT vs DirectDFT to $\varepsilon$.
